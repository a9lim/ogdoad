The core arm C theorem and its forced-failure direction are correct.** The surrounding description misstates the extension degree and needs precise relative-splitting language. There is also a substantive arm D analogue—and it reveals that the excerpt’s claimed failure value \(m_p=6\) is wrong.

I checked §4 of the supplied file, the live half-angle and exceptional-target arguments in `writeups/excess.tex`, and the relevant cyclotomic-unit/class-group statements. The additional deductions below are mathematical arguments, not computational or Lean verification.

1. **The cyclotomic lift and inertness are correct.**

   Put \(h=3^k\), \(m=3^{k+1}\), and
   \[
   K=\mathbf Q(\xi_m)^+,\qquad \mathfrak q=2O_K.
   \]
   Since
   \[
   \operatorname{ord}_{m}(2)=2h,\qquad 2^h\equiv-1\pmod m,
   \]
   the image of \(2\) generates
   \[
   G=(\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}.
   \]
   Thus \(2\) is inert in \(K\), and \(O_K/\mathfrak q=\mathbf F_{2^h}\).

   The paper proves that its literal \(\zeta_k=\kappa_{3^k}\) is a primitive \(m\)-th root. All primitive \(m\)-th roots in characteristic two are Frobenius conjugate, because \(2\) generates the full group \((\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z)^\times\). Therefore
   \[
   \eta=\xi_m+\xi_m^{-1}
   \]
   reduces to \(\gamma_k\), after choosing the residue-field identification, and otherwise to a Frobenius conjugate. There is no unresolved root-selection issue here.

   The finite-field norm identity
   \[
   N_{\mathbf F_{2^{2h}}/\mathbf F_{2^h}}(1+\zeta_k)
      =(1+\zeta_k)(1+\zeta_k^{-1})=\gamma_k
   \]
   is correct. For a row prime \(p\), \(\operatorname{ord}_p(2)=h\), so \(p\neq3\); the norm preserves the power/nonpower test. Consequently the row fails exactly when \(\bar\eta\) is a \(p\)-th power. This tests **full \(p\)-primary order**, including when \(p^2\mid2^h-1\).

2. **The character is \(\psi(\sigma_2)=2\), with no inverse.**

   Write \(E=O_K^\times\), and use additive notation for
   \[
   \rho:E/E^p\longrightarrow
   \mathbf F_{2^h}^{\times}/(\mathbf F_{2^h}^{\times})^p.
   \]
   Arithmetic Frobenius satisfies
   \[
   \rho(\sigma_2u)=2\rho(u).
   \]
   Hence the target has character \(\psi(\sigma_2)=2\bmod p\), and \(\rho\) kills every other character component.

   Here \(h\mid p-1\), \(p\nmid h\), and \(K\) is totally real. The unit representation is the regular representation minus its trivial character. Its integral \(p\)-adic decomposition therefore gives
   \[
   \dim_{\mathbf F_p}e_\psi(E/E^p)=1.
   \]
   This establishes that reduction **can see only this one line**; it does not establish that reduction is nonzero on it.

   For expressions over \(\mathbf Z_p\), explicitly use the Teichmüller lift \(\widehat\psi\). Its value at \(\sigma_2\) is the \(h\)-th root of unity congruent to \(2\bmod p\), not the integer \(2\in\mathbf Z_p\). An inverse character appears in Kummer duality, not in this reduction map or the same-character Gras comparison.

3. **The cyclotomic-unit generation argument works.**

   Define, with half-exponents interpreted modulo \(m\),
   \[
   \xi_a=\xi_m^{(1-a)/2}\frac{1-\xi_m^a}{1-\xi_m}.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   \xi_{ab}=\xi_a\,\sigma_a(\xi_b),\qquad
   \xi_{-a}=-\xi_a.
   \]
   Every invertible residue class is \(\pm2^j\), so \(\xi_2\) generates the real cyclotomic units modulo signs as a \(\mathbf Z[G]\)-module. Moreover
   \[
   \sigma_2(\xi_2)=\xi_m+\xi_m^{-1}=\eta.
   \]
   Thus \(\eta\) is also a module generator.

   Washington’s Lemma 8.1 and Theorem 8.2 are being used correctly: for this prime-power conductor,
   \[
   [E:C^+]=h(K).
   \]
   Since \(K\) is already real, \(h(K)\) is cleaner notation than \(h^+(K)\). The cited generators and index formula are also explicitly recorded in [Replogle–Underwood, p. 79](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/82000).

4. **The forced-failure implication is valid and needs no class-number hypothesis.**

   Let
   \[
   U_\psi=e_{\widehat\psi}(E\otimes\mathbf Z_p),\qquad
   C_\psi=e_{\widehat\psi}(C^+\otimes\mathbf Z_p).
   \]
   Both have rank one, and \(C_\psi\) is generated by the projection of \(\eta\). Write
   \[
   C_\psi=p^{a_\psi}U_\psi
   \]
   after choosing a lattice generator. Then
   \[
   e_\psi[\eta]=0\text{ in }E/E^p
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_\psi>0.
   \]
   Since reduction kills all other components, \(a_\psi>0\) forces \(\rho(\eta)=0\).

   This is stronger than merely saying that \(p\mid h(K)\): divisibility in **another** character component does not force this row to fail.

   The Gras input is also correctly directed. For the natural Galois action, it identifies the orders of the **same-character** components:
   \[
   \#e_{\widehat\psi}((E/C^+)\otimes\mathbf Z_p)
   =
   \#e_{\widehat\psi}(\mathrm{Cl}(K)\otimes\mathbf Z_p).
   \]
   All needed semisimplicity hypotheses hold. A precise reference is [Greither, Corollary 4.15, p. 496](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/aif.1299.pdf), which gives equality in the Grothendieck group when \(p\nmid|G|\). This supplies equality of component lengths/orders, not an asserted canonical module isomorphism.

   **Gras is unnecessary for the unit-index forced-failure statement or the class-number-coprime splitting statement.** It is needed to translate the characterwise unit index into a characterwise class-group obstruction.

5. **The exact corrected statement is an unconditional disjunction.**

   Set
   \[
   F=K(\mu_p),\qquad L=F(E^{1/p}),\qquad
   A_\psi=e_{\widehat\psi}(\mathrm{Cl}(K)\otimes\mathbf Z_p).
   \]
   Then
   \[
   \boxed{\quad
   \text{row }p\text{ fails}
   \iff
   A_\psi\neq0
   \ \text{or}\
   \mathfrak q\text{ splits completely in }L/K.
   \quad}
   \]

   To verify the splitting clause, \(\mathfrak q\) already splits completely in \(F/K\): its residue field has size \(2^h\equiv1\pmod p\). At every prime of \(F\) above \(\mathfrak q\), unit Kummer extensions are unramified, and splitting is equivalent to the corresponding residue being a \(p\)-th power. Thus
   \[
   \mathfrak q\text{ splits completely in }L/K
   \iff \rho(E)=0
   \iff \rho|_{e_\psi(E/E^p)}=0.
   \]
   Multiplication of the two one-dimensional factors—projection of \(\eta\), followed by reduction—gives the boxed disjunction.

   In particular, \(A_\psi=0\) suffices for the claimed splitting equivalence. The hypothesis \(p\nmid h(K)\) is sufficient but stronger than necessary.

   Two presentation corrections are mandatory:

   - Say **“the prime \(\mathfrak q=2O_K\) splits completely in \(L/K\)”**. Rational \(2\) does not split completely in \(L/\mathbf Q\); it is already inert in \(K/\mathbf Q\).
   - The full extension is not cyclic of degree \(p\):
     \[
     [L:F]=p^{h-1}.
     \]
     A cyclic degree-\(p\) formulation uses only the radical line \(e_\psi(E/E^p)\), choosing an actual unit representing a nonzero class on that line.

   Similarly, the Wieferich comparison must concern the prime above \(p\) **relative to \(\mathbf Q(\mu_p)\)**, not complete splitting of rational \(p\) over \(\mathbf Q\).

   The class-group consequence is a restricted Weber-type statement: vanishing of the specified component at row primes. Calling it the “only global mechanism” is justified only within this factorization, not as a classification of all possible arithmetic explanations of failure.

6. **Arm D has a substantive analogous theorem.**

   This is a deduction beyond the supplied Theorem C. Put
   \[
   N=5\cdot3^{k+1},\qquad K_D=\mathbf Q(\xi_N)^+.
   \]
   One has
   \[
   [K_D:\mathbf Q]=4h,\qquad
   \operatorname{ord}_N(2)=4h,\qquad -1\notin\langle2\rangle.
   \]
   Therefore \(2\) generates the real Galois group and is inert in \(K_D\).

   Let \(c=4\), regarded as an element of \(\mathbf F_{16}\), and put \(t=\zeta_k/c\). Since \(c\notin\mathbf F_4\), its order has a factor \(5\); the \(3\)-part of \(\zeta_k\) cannot cancel against that of \(c\). Thus \(t\) is a primitive \(N\)-th root.

   Choose the cyclotomic reduction \(\bar\xi_N=t\), and take the real circular unit
   \[
   v=(1-\xi_N)(1-\xi_N^{-1}).
   \]
   It is a unit because \(N\) has two distinct prime factors. Its reduction satisfies
   \[
   \bar v=t+t^{-1}
          =\frac{(\zeta_k+c)^2}{c\zeta_k}.
   \]
   For a D row prime, \(\operatorname{ord}_p(2)=2h\), the root-of-unity factor \(c\zeta_k\) has order prime to \(p\). Consequently \(\bar v\), \(\zeta_k+4\), and the paper’s \(M_k\) have identical power/nonpower status.

   Define \(\psi_D(\sigma_2)=2\bmod p\). Its Dirichlet conductor is exactly \(N\): its factor at \(5\) is the quadratic character, and its factor at \(3^{k+1}\) sends \(2\) to \(-2\bmod p\), of order \(h\). Hence every proper-conductor circular generator has zero \(\psi_D\)-projection. The conjugates of \(v\) generate the remaining circular-unit component after \(p\)-adic localization.

   The same rank-one argument and Gras theorem therefore give
   \[
   \boxed{
   \text{D row }p\text{ fails}
   \iff
   e_{\widehat\psi_D}\mathrm{Cl}(K_D)_p\neq0
   \ \text{or}\
   2O_{K_D}\text{ splits completely in }
   K_D(\mu_p,E_D^{1/p})/K_D .
   }
   \]
   Here use the general circular-unit theorem, **not** Washington’s prime-power Theorem 8.2 directly.

7. **The D lift exposes a separate error: the two six-element classes are tied.**

   For every \(c\in\mathbf F_{16}\setminus\mathbf F_4\), the element \(\zeta_k/c\) is primitive of order \(N\). Because
   \[
   (\mathbf Z/N\mathbf Z)^\times=\{\pm2^j\},
   \]
   any two such elements differ by Frobenius and possibly inversion. But
   \[
   1+t^{-1}=t^{-1}(1+t),
   \]
   and \(t^{-1}\) is a \(p\)-th power whenever \(p\nmid N\).

   Hence **all twelve translates by nimbers \(4,\ldots,15\) have the same row-prime power status**. The excerpt’s four classes are orbits under its chosen subgroup, not the complete power-status equivalence classes.

   Therefore:

   - A D row failure forces **\(m_p\ge16\)**, not \(m_p=6\).
   - If a C row fails both its \(1\)-class and its \(4\)-class, it likewise has **\(m_p\ge16\)**.
   - No argument here proves equality at \(16\).
