\title{Quadratic Forms over the Full Nimber Field}
\author{a9lim}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Let $K$ be a perfect field of characteristic $2$ for which the
Artin--Schreier map $x\mapsto x^2+x$ is surjective.  Every nonsingular
finite-dimensional quadratic form over $K$ is hyperbolic, and therefore
$W_q(K)=0$.  More generally, every quadratic form is classified by
\[
  \bigl(\dim V,\dim\rad B,\dim\rad Q\bigr),
\]
where $B$ is the polar form and
$\rad Q=\{x\in\rad B:Q(x)=0\}$.  Conway's full field of nimbers
$\On$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $2$, so the theorem applies
to $\On$: there is no transfinite Arf coordinate, and the Clifford class of
every nonsingular form is split.  Finite-field Arf invariants remain valid
relative invariants over a declared finite nim-subfield, but scalar extension
from $\F_{2^d}$ to $\F_{2^{2d}}$ kills them.  We conclude by separating this
full-field theorem from computations in a partial ordinal-nimber backend,
which neither represents all of $\On$ nor constructs every required root.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Nim addition and nim multiplication make the class of ordinals into
Conway's algebraically closed field $\On$ of characteristic $2$
\cite{conway1976,lenstra1978}.  Quadratic-form invariants over finite nimber
fields therefore raise a natural base-change question: what remains of the
Arf invariant after extension to the full nimber field?  The answer is that
the nonsingular invariant vanishes.  The singular theory retains only polar
rank and one radical flag.

This conclusion is an application of standard characteristic-$2$ quadratic
form theory \cite{arf1941,baeza1978,elmankarpenkomerkurjev2008}.  Its role
here is to identify the correct ground field and to separate two assertions
that should not be conflated:
\begin{enumerate}
\item over full $\On$, every required square root and Artin--Schreier root
      exists, so the classification is complete;
\item a finite implementation of ordinal nim arithmetic may classify a form
      over a certified finite subfield without being able to construct the
      isometry over $\On$.
\end{enumerate}
Set-sized quadratically closed initial segments of the nimbers have also been
studied in \cite{lenstra1977,risnoveanu2026}; the theorem below
requires only the field hypotheses stated explicitly and then uses algebraic
closure for the full-$\On$ corollary.

\section{Quadratic forms in characteristic two}

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $2$ and $V$ a finite-dimensional
$K$-vector space.  A quadratic form is a map $Q:V\to K$ satisfying
\[
  Q(av)=a^2Q(v),\qquad
  B(u,v)=Q(u+v)+Q(u)+Q(v),
\]
where the polar form $B$ is bilinear.  The form $B$ is alternating.  We put
\[
  R=\rad B=\{v:B(v,w)=0\text{ for all }w\in V\},
  \qquad
  \rad Q=\{r\in R:Q(r)=0\}.
\]
The quadratic form is \emph{nonsingular} when $R=0$.  This is the standard
characteristic-$2$ convention \cite[Chapter II, Section 7]
{elmankarpenkomerkurjev2008}.

Write
\[
  \wpmap:K\longrightarrow K,\qquad \wpmap(t)=t^2+t.
\]
For a nonsingular form and a symplectic basis $(e_i,f_i)_{i=1}^r$, its Arf
class is represented by
\[
  \sum_{i=1}^r Q(e_i)Q(f_i)\pmod{\wpmap(K)}.
\]
Over a finite field, absolute trace identifies $K/\wpmap(K)$ with $\F_2$.
Over a field on which $\wpmap$ is surjective, the quotient is zero.

Let $H$ denote the hyperbolic plane, with basis $e,f$ satisfying
\[
  Q(e)=Q(f)=0,\qquad B(e,f)=1.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Splitting a symplectic plane]\label{lem:plane}
If $\wpmap$ is surjective, every nonsingular quadratic plane over $K$ is
hyperbolic.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $e,f$ with $B(e,f)=1$ and write $a=Q(e)$ and $b=Q(f)$.  If $a=0$,
then $e$ is isotropic.  If $a\ne0$, choose $t$ with $t^2+t=ab$ and put
$v=(t/a)e+f$.  Then
\[
  Q(v)=\frac{t^2+t+ab}{a}=0.
\]
Thus in either case there is a nonzero isotropic vector $v$.  Choose $w_0$
with $B(v,w_0)=1$ and set $w=w_0+Q(w_0)v$.  Since $Q(v)=0$,
\[
  Q(w)=Q(w_0)+Q(w_0)B(w_0,v)=0,
  \qquad B(v,w)=1.
\]
Hence $(v,w)$ is a hyperbolic basis.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nonsingular}
If $\wpmap$ is surjective, every nonsingular quadratic space of dimension
$2r$ is isometric to $H^{\perp r}$.  Consequently $W_q(K)=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A nondegenerate alternating form has a symplectic plane.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:plane}, the restriction of $Q$ to that plane is hyperbolic.
Its polar-orthogonal complement is nonsingular, so induction splits off all
$r$ planes.  Every nonsingular form is therefore Witt-trivial.
\end{proof}

The remaining information lies on the polar radical.  Perfection is used
precisely here.

\begin{lemma}[Zero-polar forms]\label{lem:radical}
Suppose $K$ is perfect and $R$ is an $s$-dimensional space on which the polar
form of $Q$ vanishes.  There is a unique linear functional
$\ell:R\to K$ such that $Q(r)=\ell(r)^2$.  Up to isometry,
\[
  Q|_R\cong
  \begin{cases}
    0^{\perp s},&\ell=0,\\
    \langle x^2\rangle\perp0^{\perp(s-1)},&\ell\ne0.
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a basis $r_1,\dots,r_s$, perfection gives unique $c_i$ with
$c_i^2=Q(r_i)$.  Define
$\ell(\sum_i x_i r_i)=\sum_i c_i x_i$.  Since the polar form vanishes,
\[
  Q\left(\sum_i x_i r_i\right)
    =\sum_i x_i^2Q(r_i)
    =\ell\left(\sum_i x_i r_i\right)^2.
\]
Injectivity of Frobenius gives uniqueness.  If $\ell\ne0$, choose a basis in
which $\ell$ is the first coordinate.
\end{proof}

\section{Classification over an Artin--Schreier-surjective field}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:classification}
Let $K$ be a perfect field of characteristic $2$ with surjective
Artin--Schreier map.  Let $Q$ be a quadratic form on $V$, let $B$ be its
polar form, and write
\[
  \rank B=2r,\qquad \dim\rad B=s.
\]
Then exactly one of the following normal forms holds:
\[
 (V,Q)\cong
 \begin{cases}
  H^{\perp r}\perp0^{\perp s},
    &Q|_{\rad B}=0,\\
  H^{\perp r}\perp\langle x^2\rangle\perp0^{\perp(s-1)},
    &Q|_{\rad B}\ne0.
 \end{cases}
\]
In the second case $s\ge1$.  Two such forms are isometric if and only if
their triples
\[
  \bigl(\dim V,\dim\rad B,\dim\rad Q\bigr)
\]
agree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a vector-space complement $W$ to $R=\rad B$.  The restriction
$B|_W$ is nondegenerate: a vector in its radical is orthogonal to both $W$
and $R$, hence lies in $W\cap R=0$.  Therefore
\[
  (V,Q)=(W,Q|_W)\perp(R,Q|_R).
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:nonsingular} identifies the first summand with
$H^{\perp r}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:radical} gives the two radical summands.
In the notation of that lemma, $\rad Q=\ker\ell$, so its dimension is $s$
in the first case and $s-1$ in the second.  These dimensions recover the
normal form and are invariant under isometry.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The full nimber field]\label{cor:on2}
Every nonsingular finite-dimensional quadratic form over $\On$ is
hyperbolic, and
\[
  W_q(\On)=0.
\]
Every singular form over $\On$ has exactly one of the two normal forms in
Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}.  In particular, no additional transfinite
Arf invariant occurs.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The field $\On$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $2$
\cite{conway1976,lenstra1978}.  Algebraic closure makes both Frobenius and
$x\mapsto x^2+x$ surjective, so Theorem~\ref{thm:classification} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Set-sized reduction]
Only finitely many coefficients and finitely many roots occur in the proof of
a finite-dimensional instance.  They lie in a set-sized algebraically closed
subfield of $\On$.  Thus the corollary does not require performing linear
algebra over a proper-class carrier.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite nim-subfields and the Arf invariant}

Let $K_d=\F_{2^d}\subset\On$.  For a nonsingular quadratic form over $K_d$,
the Arf class lies in
\[
  K_d/\wpmap(K_d)\cong\F_2,
\]
with the isomorphism given by absolute trace.  This is a relative invariant:
it depends on the ground field over which changes of basis are allowed.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:finite-extension}
Scalar extension from $K_d$ to $K_{2d}$ induces the zero map
\[
  W_q(K_d)\longrightarrow W_q(K_{2d}).
\]
Consequently the directed colimit of the finite-field quadratic Witt groups
inside $\On$ is zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $c\in K_d$, the polynomial $X^2+X+c$ either splits over $K_d$ or is an
irreducible quadratic, in which case it splits over $K_{2d}$.  Thus every
class in $K_d/\wpmap(K_d)$ becomes zero in
$K_{2d}/\wpmap(K_{2d})$.  The finite-field classification by the Arf class
\cite{arf1941,dickson1901} gives the Witt-group statement.  Every element
at every finite stage therefore dies at a later stage, which is exactly the
vanishing of the colimit.
\end{proof}

For example, the plane with $Q(e)=Q(f)=B(e,f)=1$ is anisotropic over $\F_2$.
If $t^2+t+1=0$ in $\F_4$, then $te+f$ is isotropic, so the same plane is
hyperbolic over $\F_4$.  The original Arf bit correctly records failure to
split over $\F_2$; it is not invariant under unrestricted scalar extension.

\section{Clifford consequence}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:clifford}
For every nonsingular finite-dimensional quadratic form $Q$ over $\On$, the
graded Morita class of $\Cl(Q)$ is split on the characteristic-$2$
Clifford--Brauer--Wall surface.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:on2}, $Q\cong H^{\perp r}$.  Orthogonal sum corresponds
to graded tensor product of Clifford algebras
\cite[Chapter~IX]{elmankarpenkomerkurjev2008}.  For a hyperbolic basis $e,f$,
\[
  e^2=f^2=0,\qquad ef+fe=1,
\]
so $\Cl(H)$ is a graded matrix algebra and is graded Morita-trivial.  Hence
$[\Cl(Q)]=0$.
\end{proof}

This statement concerns Clifford algebras of nonsingular quadratic forms.
For singular $Q$, the full Clifford algebra need not be graded central simple,
so the singular radical flag in Theorem~\ref{thm:classification} is
quadratic-form data rather than a Brauer--Wall coordinate.

\section{Executable and formal boundary}

The mathematical theorem concerns full $\On$.  Ogdoad's executable
\texttt{Ordinal} scalar is instead a partial, checked representation of an
initial Kummer region.  Its multiplication rejects computations that leave
the verified window.  For a finite metric, the routine
\texttt{ordinal\_common\_finite\_}\allowbreak
\texttt{subfield\_degree} returns a common finite
degree only when the necessary excess data and checked degree arithmetic are
available.  The finite-field Arf and Brauer--Wall reports are then relative
to that recorded degree, exactly as Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-extension}
requires.

None of these routines asserts that the represented scalar is algebraically
closed.  Nor do they promise to construct the square roots and
Artin--Schreier roots used by Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}.  A failure to
produce an in-window witness is therefore an implementation boundary, not a
third mathematical isometry class.

The companion Lean module \texttt{formal/Ogdoad/Off.lean}, distributed with
Ogdoad \cite{ogdoad2026}, checks the following algebraic ingredients
over a set-sized algebraically
closed characteristic-$2$ field: surjectivity of Frobenius and the
Artin--Schreier map, conversion of a normalized symplectic pair to a
hyperbolic pair, simultaneous conversion of a supplied symplectic family,
and the zero-polar normal form $Q=\ell^2$.  It uses the standard symplectic
decomposition of a finite alternating form as an interface and does not
encode Conway's proper-class field or the partial Rust arithmetic.

\section{Conclusion}

Perfectness and Artin--Schreier surjectivity remove the nonsingular
characteristic-$2$ obstruction completely.  Over full $\On$, every
nonsingular quadratic form is hyperbolic, $W_q(\On)=0$, and its associated
Clifford class is split.  A singular form retains only the dimensions of its
polar and quadratic radicals.  Finite-field Arf bits remain meaningful when
their finite ground field is recorded, but they vanish after a quadratic
field extension and hence in the directed limit.  This field-theoretic
classification is independent of whether a partial ordinal backend can
construct the corresponding change of basis.
