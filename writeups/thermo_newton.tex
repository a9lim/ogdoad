\begin{abstract}
Let $A_u(G)=G.u$ be Norton multiplication of a short partizan game by a
positive dyadic number $u=m/2^k$ in canonical form.  Put
$\delta_u=2^{-k}$, with $\delta_u=1$ for integral $u$, and
$a_u=u-\delta_u$.  For every nonnumeric short game,
\[
  \mean(A_u(G))=u\mean(G),\qquad
  \temp(A_u(G))=u\temp(G)+a_u.
\]
For a number $x$ with canonical mesh $\epsilon_x=1/\den(x)$, the image has
mean $ux$ and temperature $-1$ or $a_u-u\epsilon_x$, according as the latter
quantity is negative or nonnegative.  Hence each positive numeric Norton
operator induces an additive transport between thermic residue groups.
These transports do not form an action of the multiplicative dyadics: their
exact degree defect is
\[
 \Delta(u,v)=v(1-\delta_u)-\delta_v+\delta_{uv}\ge0,
\]
and we classify all pairs for which it vanishes.  Temperature thus supplies
a useful filtered, tropical-like shadow of Norton multiplication, but not a
Newton-polygon-style graded ring.  The freezing step is not tropical,
nonnumeric Norton units do not descend modulo colder games, and the
temperature-zero residue retains nonzero $2$-torsion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Temperature is the filtration parameter of thermography: it measures the
amount of cooling required before a short game freezes to a number.  Norton
multiplication $G.U$ is an additive operation in $G$ attached to a positive
game $U$ \cite[Chapter 8]{berlekampconwayguy1982}; modern accounts and
related applications appear in \cite{johnson2011b,mesdal2009}.
This paper determines exactly how the thermic filtration transforms when the
unit $U$ is a positive number.

The result is affine rather than homogeneous.  If $u$ is a canonical
positive dyadic and $G$ is nonnumeric, then the thermic degree changes by
\[
  \tau\longmapsto r_u(\tau)=u\tau+u-\delta_u.
\]
The denominator mesh $\delta_u$ is essential.  It also produces an exact
composition defect, proving that these additive transports cannot be the
scalar maps of an associative graded algebra whose dyadic coefficients
multiply ordinarily.

The final section compares this result with Newton polygons.  The comparison
is deliberately limited: both theories have filtrations and extremal
piecewise-linear envelopes, but game thermography includes a nontropical
freezing operation and has no compatible internal product on Conway values.
This sharpens, rather than identifies, the analogy suggested by the standard
temperature and valuation formalisms \cite{bandokenmorikawa2020,dumas1906}.

\section{Norton and thermographic conventions}

We work with short normal-play partizan games modulo Conway equality.  Numbers
have temperature $-1$.  For every nonnumber $G$, its left and right
thermographic walls meet at a finite height $\temp(G)\ge0$ and then freeze to
the common mast $\mean(G)$.  We use the standard thermographic conventions of
\cite{siegel2013,bandokenmorikawa2020}.

Norton multiplication depends on the selected form of its positive unit, so
throughout a numeric unit is taken in its canonical short-game form.  Write a
positive dyadic in lowest terms as $u=m/2^k$ and set
\begin{equation}
  \delta_u=\frac{1}{\den(u)}=2^{-k},\qquad a_u=u-\delta_u.
\end{equation}
For an integer $u$, this gives $\delta_u=1$.  A nonintegral canonical dyadic
has options $u-\delta_u$ and $u+\delta_u$, while a positive integer has the
sole left option $u-1$.  Every Norton incentive is consequently $a_u$, and
the defining recursion becomes
\begin{equation}
 A_u(n)=nu\quad(n\in\mathbb Z),\qquad
 A_u(G)=\{A_u(G^L)+a_u\mid A_u(G^R)-a_u\}
 \quad(G\notin\mathbb Z).
\end{equation}

For a number $x$, put $LS_t(x)=RS_t(x)=x$.  For a nonnumber $G$, let
\begin{align}
 L_G^{\mathrm{raw}}(t)&=\max_{G^L}RS_t(G^L),&
 R_G^{\mathrm{raw}}(t)&=\min_{G^R}LS_t(G^R),\\
 LS_t^{\mathrm{sc}}(G)&=L_G^{\mathrm{raw}}(t)-t,&
 RS_t^{\mathrm{sc}}(G)&=R_G^{\mathrm{raw}}(t)+t.
\end{align}
The scaffold walls are followed until their first meeting, after which both
are replaced by the common constant mast.  The raw gap is
\begin{equation}
 D_G(t)=L_G^{\mathrm{raw}}(t)-R_G^{\mathrm{raw}}(t)-2t.
\end{equation}
It is nonincreasing, and $\temp(G)$ is its first zero.  We shall also use the
standard facts that left walls are nonincreasing, right walls are
nondecreasing, and wall slopes lie between $-1$ and $1$.

\section{The exact thermic law}

The recursion stops at integers, not at all numbers.  Numeric inputs therefore
form a separate branch of the theorem.

\begin{lemma}[Numeric inputs]\label{lem:numbers}
Let $x$ be a short-game number and put $\epsilon_x=1/\den(x)$.  Then
\[
 \mean(A_u(x))=ux,
 \qquad
 \temp(A_u(x))=
 \begin{cases}
   -1,&a_u-u\epsilon_x<0,\\
   a_u-u\epsilon_x,&a_u-u\epsilon_x\ge0.
 \end{cases}
\]
In particular, $\temp(A_u(x))<a_u$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Induct on the exponent of the exact dyadic denominator of $x$.  Integers are
the base case of the Norton recursion.  If $x$ is nonintegral, then
$x=\{x-\epsilon\mid x+\epsilon\}$ with
$\epsilon=\epsilon_x$.  The option images have means
$u(x-\epsilon)$ and $u(x+\epsilon)$ and, by induction, freeze strictly below
$a_u-u\epsilon$ whenever that value is nonnegative.  At
$c=a_u-u\epsilon\ge0$, the parent scaffold values are
\[
 u(x-\epsilon)+a_u-c=ux,\qquad
 u(x+\epsilon)-a_u+c=ux.
\]
Immediately below $c$ their gap is $2(c-t)>0$, so the first meeting is at
$c$ and has mast $ux$.  If $c<0$, the two numeric bounds in the Norton
recursion lie on opposite sides of $ux$; the simplicity rule selects
the number $ux$.
\end{proof}

The central wall identity is affine covariance of the raw gap.

\begin{lemma}[Raw-gap transport]\label{lem:gap}
Suppose the wall formula
\[
 LS_{a_u+ut}(A_u(X))=uLS_t(X),\qquad
 RS_{a_u+ut}(A_u(X))=uRS_t(X)
\]
holds for every option $X$ of a nonintegral game $G$.  Then
\begin{align}
 L_{A_u(G)}^{\mathrm{raw}}(a_u+ut)
   &=uL_G^{\mathrm{raw}}(t)+a_u,\\
 R_{A_u(G)}^{\mathrm{raw}}(a_u+ut)
   &=uR_G^{\mathrm{raw}}(t)-a_u,
\end{align}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
 D_{A_u(G)}(a_u+ut)=uD_G(t).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A left option of $A_u(G)$ is $A_u(G^L)+a_u$, and numeric translation shifts
both walls by $a_u$.  Taking the maximum in the definition of the raw left
wall proves its displayed transform; the right-option minimum gives the raw
right transform.  Substitution into the raw-gap definition, with
$s=a_u+ut$, cancels the two parent shifts and yields the displayed covariance.
\end{proof}

When $\temp(G)>0$, raw-gap covariance immediately locates the
first meeting.  The only subtle case is $\temp(G)=0<a_u$: one must exclude a
premature mast below $a_u$.  We isolate the strategy argument needed for that
case.

\begin{lemma}[No premature mast]\label{lem:corridor}
Let $X$ be a canonical nonnumber and $m$ a number.  Assume the wall formula of
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} for every proper follower of $X$.  If, for some
$b<a_u$,
\[
 RS_s(A_u(X))=um-a_u+s\qquad(b\le s\le a_u),
\]
then Right wins $X-m$ moving first.  Dually, if
$LS_s(A_u(X))=um+a_u-s$ on the same interval, then Left wins $X-m$ moving
first.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We prove the right-wall statement by birthday induction; negation gives the
dual statement.  The displayed wall has maximal slope $1$, so it is an
unfrozen right scaffold on $[b,a_u)$.  By the Norton recursion,
\[
  \min_{Y\in X^R}LS_s(A_u(Y))=um
  \qquad(b\le s\le a_u).
\]
Choose a right option $Y$ attaining this minimum at $b$.  Left walls are
nonincreasing and every candidate is at least $um$ throughout the interval,
so $LS_s(A_u(Y))=um$ on $[b,a_u]$.

If $Y$ is numeric, Lemma~\ref{lem:numbers} shows that it has frozen below
$a_u$ with mast $uY$, hence $Y=m$.  Otherwise, for every left option $Z$ of
$Y$, the parent scaffold inequality gives
\[
 RS_s(A_u(Z))+a_u-s\le um.
\]
At $s=a_u$, the induction hypothesis implies $RS(Z)\le m$.  If the inequality
is strict, the standard stop criterion says that Right wins $Z-m$ moving
first.  If equality holds, the corridor equality excludes numeric $Z$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:numbers}: a numeric image is frozen strictly below $a_u$,
whereas this wall has slope one on a nontrivial interval.  The slope bound
therefore forces equality throughout the corridor, and birthday induction
gives the same conclusion.  Right moves
from $X-m$ to $Y-m$ and answers every left reply using this strategy.  Number
avoidance shows that a move in the numeric component is no better
\cite[Part II]{siegel2013}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Thermic regrading]\label{thm:thermic}
For every positive canonical dyadic $u$ and every nonnumeric short game $G$,
\begin{equation}
 \mean(A_u(G))=u\mean(G),\qquad
 \temp(A_u(G))=u\temp(G)+a_u.
\end{equation}
More strongly, for every $t\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 LS_{a_u+ut}(A_u(G))=uLS_t(G),\qquad
 RS_{a_u+ut}(A_u(G))=uRS_t(G).
\end{equation}
For numeric $G$, Lemma~\ref{lem:numbers} is the exact replacement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proceed by birthday induction, using Lemma~\ref{lem:numbers} for numeric
followers.  Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} then applies to $G$.  Put
$\tau=\temp(G)$.  If $\tau>0$, then
$D_{A_u(G)}(a_u)=uD_G(0)>0$, so monotonicity excludes an earlier meeting;
raw-gap covariance places the first zero at
$a_u+u\tau$.  If $\tau=0=a_u$, the same identity places it at zero.

Suppose $\tau=0<a_u$, and write
$LS(G)=RS(G)=m$.  Raw-gap covariance makes the transformed
scaffolds meet at $(a_u,um)$.  If $A_u(G)$ had frozen at some $b<a_u$, its
walls would equal $um$ on $[b,a_u]$.  For each left option $X$ of $G$, either
$RS(X)<m$, in which case Right wins $X-m$ by the stop criterion, or the slope
bound forces
\[
 RS_s(A_u(X))=um-a_u+s
\]
on the corridor, and Lemma~\ref{lem:corridor} gives the same conclusion.
Thus Right wins $G-m$ playing second.  The dual argument for every right
option shows that Left also wins $G-m$ playing second, whence $G=m$, contrary
to the assumption that $G$ is nonnumeric.  Premature freezing is impossible.

Before the first meeting, the raw left-wall transform and the scaffold
definition give
\[
 LS_{a_u+ut}^{\mathrm{sc}}(A_u(G))
 =uL_G^{\mathrm{raw}}(t)+a_u-(a_u+ut)
 =uLS_t^{\mathrm{sc}}(G),
\]
and similarly on the right.  At and above the meeting both sides freeze to
the mast $u\mean(G)$.  This proves both assertions.
\end{proof}

\section{Thermic residue transport}

Let $F_{<0}=\Numbers$ be the subgroup of short numbers.  For $\tau\ge0$, let
$F_{\le\tau}$ be the subgroup of games of temperature at most $\tau$, including
$F_{<0}$, and let $F_{<\tau}$ be the union of lower layers.  The standard
inequality
\begin{equation}
  \temp(G+H)\le\max\{\temp(G),\temp(H)\}
\end{equation}
shows that this is an additive filtration
\cite[Theorem II.5.18]{siegel2013}.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:residue}
For every $\tau\ge0$, Norton multiplication by $u$ induces an additive map
\[
 \overline A_{u,\tau}:
 F_{\le\tau}/F_{<\tau}
 \longrightarrow
 F_{\le u\tau+a_u}/F_{<u\tau+a_u}.
\]
For a positive integer $n$, the shifted thermal height
$h(G)=\temp(G)+1$ satisfies $h(A_n(G))=nh(G)$ on nonnumeric layers.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Norton multiplication by a fixed positive unit is additive
\cite{berlekampconwayguy1982,mesdal2009}.  If $G-H$ has temperature
$\sigma<\tau$, Theorem~\ref{thm:thermic} sends it to temperature
$u\sigma+a_u<u\tau+a_u$.  If $G-H$ is numeric,
Lemma~\ref{lem:numbers} puts its image strictly below $a_u$.  Hence the map is
well defined on the quotient.  For integral $n$, $a_n=n-1$.
\end{proof}

\section{The composition defect}

Write $r_u(\tau)=u\tau+u-\delta_u$.  Successive thermic transports differ
from transport by the ordinary dyadic product by a degree-independent term.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:defect}
For positive dyadics $u,v$ and every $\tau\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 r_v(r_u(\tau))=r_{uv}(\tau)+\Delta(u,v),
 \qquad
 \Delta(u,v)=v(1-\delta_u)-\delta_v+\delta_{uv}\ge0.
\end{equation}
The defect is zero exactly in the following cases:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $u$ is nonintegral and $v=2^{-\ell}$ for some $\ell\ge0$;
\item $u$ is an odd positive integer and $v$ is arbitrary;
\item $u$ is an even positive integer and $v$ is an integer.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the affine maps gives
\[
 r_v(r_u(\tau))-r_{uv}(\tau)
 =v(1-\delta_u)-\delta_v+\delta_{uv}.
\]
If $u$ is integral, then $\delta_u=1$ and
$\Delta=\delta_{uv}-\delta_v$.  Multiplication by an integer cannot increase
a dyadic denominator.  It preserves every denominator when $u$ is odd; for
even $u$, it preserves the denominator exactly when $v$ is integral.  This
proves the integral cases and their nonnegativity.

Now let $u=m/2^k$ in lowest terms with $k>0$.  If $v$ is nonintegral, then
$\delta_{uv}=\delta_u\delta_v$, and
\[
  \Delta=(1-\delta_u)(v-\delta_v)\ge0.
\]
Equality holds precisely when the numerator of $v$ is $1$.  If $v=n$ is an
integer, write $n=2^sq$ with $q$ odd.  For $s\ge k$,
$\delta_{uv}=1$ and the defect is positive unless $n=1$.  For $s<k$,
\[
  \Delta=n-1-\frac{n-2^s}{2^k},
\]
which is zero at $n=1$ and positive for $n>1$.  This completes the
classification.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:no-action}
The residue transports of Corollary~\ref{cor:residue} cannot be the scalar
maps of an associative action whose positive dyadic coefficients multiply
ordinarily and whose equivalence preserves thermic degree.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $u=1/2$, $v=2$, and $G=*$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:defect} gives
$\Delta(1/2,2)=1$, so
\[
 \temp(A_2(A_{1/2}(*)))=1,
 \qquad
 \temp(A_1(*))=0.
\]
Associativity would identify these two classes, but an equivalence preserving
thermic degree cannot do so.
\end{proof}

\section{The tropical shadow and its limits}

The filtration inequality above is analogous to the nonarchimedean
inequality, and the raw option operators are
pointwise max-plus and min-plus folds.  Cooling adds the affine terms
$-t$ and $+t$.  This is the exact tropical content of thermography.

The subsequent freeze is different: one compares the two envelopes, finds
the first zero of $D_G$, and replaces both walls by one constant mast.  Nor is
the thermograph a congruence for disjunctive sum.  The games
$*=\{0\mid0\}$ and $\uparrow=\{0\mid *\}$ have the same constant-zero walls,
but $*+*=0$ is numeric while $\uparrow+*$ is a nonnumber of temperature zero.
Thus no binary operation on thermographs alone recovers the thermograph of
every sum.

Newton polygons, by contrast, arise from valuations of coefficients and
transform functorially under polynomial multiplication; Dumas's theorem is a
classical form of this product behavior \cite{dumas1906}.  The game side has
an additive filtration and the external transports of
Corollary~\ref{cor:residue}, but no analogous internal product on all Conway
game values.

The restriction to numeric Norton units is also necessary.  At degree zero,
$*$ and $*+1$ differ by the cold number $1$.  For the positive nonnumeric
unit $U=\uparrow$, additivity gives
\[
 A_U(*+1)-A_U(*)=A_U(1)=\uparrow,
\]
whose temperature-zero class is nonzero.  Thus $A_U$ does not descend modulo
cold numbers.  Finding coarser equivalences compatible with Norton
multiplication is a separate problem of the kind posed by
\cite[Problem 6.10]{altmanlipparini2026}.

Finally,
\[
 \gr_0(\Games)=F_{\le0}/F_{<0}\cong\Inf,
\]
the additive group of infinitesimals \cite{calistrate1996,moews2002}.  It
contains $[*]\ne0$ with $2[*]=0$.

\begin{proposition}[Dyadic torsion obstruction]\label{prop:torsion}
There is no faithful module containing $\gr_0(\Games)$ for which multiplication
by a coefficient $2$ agrees with game doubling and $2$ has a multiplicative
inverse representing $1/2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $s$ be the image of $[*]$.  Compatibility with game doubling gives
$2s=0$.  If $2$ is invertible, then $s=(1/2)(2s)=0$, contradicting
faithfulness.
\end{proof}

This obstruction does not exclude integer coefficients, quotients that kill
the incompatible torsion, or finer game equivalences.  It excludes the
specific faithful full-dyadic graded-ring reading.

\section{Implementation and verification boundary}

The Ogdoad implementation contains the standard recursive operations
\texttt{norton\_multiply} and \texttt{overheat}, together with three exact
closed-form routines:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{numeric\_norton\_regrade};
\item \texttt{numeric\_norton\_mean\_temperature};
\item \texttt{numeric\_norton\_composition\_defect}.
\end{itemize}
Unit tests compare the
closed formulas with recursively materialized products over bounded families
of short games and positive dyadic units, and an independent transcription of
the Norton recursion checks its noninteger branches.  These are regression
oracles for the universal proofs above, not their premises.  The source is
distributed with Ogdoad \cite{ogdoad2026}.

The implementation also exposes the tropical folds and the ordinary freeze
tail separately.  It therefore encodes the same boundary as the paper: the
max-plus/min-plus structure belongs to the option envelopes, while the meeting
and freezing operation remains ordinary thermography.

\section{Conclusion}

Positive numeric Norton multiplication acts on thermic degree by the exact
affine map
\[
  \tau\longmapsto u\tau+u-\delta_u,
\]
with a strictly lower numeric branch.  It consequently induces additive maps
between thermic residue groups.  The nonnegative defect
$\Delta(u,v)$ measures exactly why those maps fail to form a multiplicative
dyadic action.  The tropical comparison is therefore a filtered one:
thermography has extremal envelopes and a nonarchimedean temperature
inequality, but its freeze, its nonnumeric Norton operators, and its
temperature-zero torsion prevent a faithful Newton-style graded ring.
