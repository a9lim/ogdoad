\title{The FIFO Linking Conjecture:\\
Affine Response and the Cut-Space Frontier}
\author{a9lim}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Let a finite graph be played by opening vertices into a FIFO queue and later
closing the front. A close flips a bit exactly when the front has odd degree
into the untouched set. The FIFO linking conjecture asserts that one isolated
dummy lets either designated seat force even terminal parity on every graph.

We convert the conjecture into an exact affine problem. Every complete history
has a disjointness vector in the $\F_2$ edge space of the complete graph. For a
fixed attacker strategy, universal harmlessness is equivalent to zero lying in
the affine hull of the compatible response vectors. At a front $f$, the edge
space splits into the cut star at $f$ and the edge space after deleting $f$;
the quotient is the two-graph coboundary chart. The missing proof is therefore
a causal, strategy-relative affine contraction across successive front levels.

We prove the transition and affine reductions, the parity-cell and cut-space
subclasses, complete contraction against close-first attackers, the
conditioned close-first tail, the stopped defender-first root theorem, and
complete fixed-front prefix contraction.  Minimum-tree extraction supplies
neutral punctured tails and excludes the zero-charge/even-untouched
predecessor branch.  The remaining odd--odd spike is reduced to a relative
Schur class supported away from its selected front; live-star incidence shows
that cancelling it requires earlier siblings, while explicit response trees
rule out a descendant-local or fixed-fan repair.  Exact minimax verifies the
conjecture through eight real vertices plus the dummy, but no finite census is
used as a universal argument.  The remaining theorem is the simultaneous
cancellation of the cut, continuation, pruning-frontier, and frozen ko-wall
moments by a strategy-relative odd multibranch chain.
\end{abstract}

\section{The reduced game}

Let $G=(R,E)$ be a finite simple graph on the \emph{real} vertices, and adjoin
one isolated vertex $d$.  A state is $(U,Q,\mathsf{ko})$, where $U$ is the set
of untouched vertices, $Q$ is an ordered FIFO queue of open vertices, and
$\mathsf{ko}$ records the one-move prohibition after a vertex is opened onto an
empty queue.  A legal move is
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item $\operatorname{OPEN}(x)$ for any $x\in U$, moving $x$ to the back of
      $Q$; or
\item $\operatorname{CLOSE}(f)$ for the front $f$ of $Q$, unless
      $\mathsf{ko}$ is set.
\end{enumerate}
A close of $f$ scores
\[
  \deg_U(f)\pmod 2.
\]
If no move is legal, the forced pass only clears $\mathsf{ko}$.  This can occur
only after $U$ is empty, when no later close can score.  The players have
opposite targets for the parity of the total score.

Every vertex opens and closes exactly once.  FIFO makes the opening
and closing orders identical.  Consequently the activity intervals
$I_x=[o(x),c(x)]$ never properly nest.  For an edge $xy$ with $o(x)<o(y)$, the
edge contributes to the flip score precisely when $c(x)<o(y)$, i.e. precisely
when $I_x$ and $I_y$ are disjoint.  Thus the total score is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-score}
  F_G(h)=\sum_{xy\in E(G)}[I_x\cap I_y=\varnothing]\pmod2.
\end{equation}
The proper-interval vocabulary is standard; the adversarial parity problem is
not a standard queue-layout problem, because the queue stores vertices and the
play itself constructs their intervals.

There is also an exact static encoding of the schedule which is useful for
checking any proposed involution.  Let $N=|R|+1$, including the dummy, and let
$\pi=(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_N)$ be the common opening and closing order.  Put
\[
 r_b=\#\{\hbox{closes strictly before }O_{\pi_b}\}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[permutation--threshold normal form]
\label{prop:threshold}
Complete legal schedules are in bijection with pairs $(\pi,r)$ in
which $\pi$ is a permutation and
\[
 0=r_1\le r_2\le\cdots\le r_N,\qquad 0\le r_b\le b-1,
\]
subject to the ko condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threshold-ko}
 r_b=b-1\text{ and }b<N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_{b+1}=b-1.
\end{equation}
For $a<b$ one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threshold-D}
 \pi_a\pi_b\in D(h)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\le r_b.
\end{equation}
If $r_N=N-1$, the unique forced pass occurs after $O_{\pi_N}$ and before its
close; otherwise the encoding is pass-free.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
FIFO identifies the opening and closing permutations.  The number of closes
before successive opens is nondecreasing, and before the $b$th open at most
$b-1$ vertices have opened.  Equality $r_b=b-1$ says that $\pi_b$ opens onto
an empty queue.  Ko then forbids closing it before the next open, which is
exactly \eqref{eq:threshold-ko}.  Conversely, before each $O_{\pi_b}$ close
the first $r_b-r_{b-1}$ queued vertices.  The displayed bounds make these
closes available, and \eqref{eq:threshold-ko} is precisely the only possible
ko obstruction.  After the last open, close the remaining queue, inserting
the forced pass exactly in the stated singleton case.  Finally, when $a<b$,
$I_{\pi_a}$ and $I_{\pi_b}$ are disjoint exactly when $\pi_a$ is among the
$r_b$ vertices already closed before $O_{\pi_b}$, proving
\eqref{eq:threshold-D}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[maximal-block score]
\label{cor:block-score}
Consider one maximal nonempty-queue block with opening order
$v_1,\ldots,v_b$, and let $S$ be the vertices still untouched when the block
ends.  If $t_i$ is the number of block vertices opened before $C_{v_i}$, then
either $b=1$, $S=\varnothing$, and the block is the terminal
open--pass--close macro, or $b\ge2$ and
\[
 i+1\le t_i\le b\ (i<b),\qquad t_b=b,\qquad
 t_1\le\cdots\le t_b,
\]
and these conditions characterize maximal blocks.  The charge of the $i$th
close and the total block score are
\begin{align}
 c_i&=\deg_S(v_i)+\sum_{j>t_i}[v_iv_j\in E(G)],\label{eq:block-charge}\\
 F_{\rm block}
 &=e_G(\{v_1,\ldots,v_b\},S)
   +\sum_{\substack{i<j\\j>t_i}}[v_iv_j\in E(G)]\pmod2.
   \label{eq:block-score}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The singleton case is exactly the ko-forced terminal macro.  Assume $b\ge2$.
After $C_{v_i}$ the queue has length $t_i-i$.  It must remain positive for
$i<b$ and become zero for $i=b$, giving the bounds; monotonicity records that
opens accumulate as the FIFO closes proceed.  Conversely, open the next
vertices and perform $C_{v_i}$ as soon as the count reaches $t_i$, resolving
ties in FIFO order.  The bounds make the queue positive until the last close,
so this constructs the unique canonical block word.  At $C_{v_i}$ the
untouched vertices are $S$ together with exactly the later block vertices
$v_j$ for which $j>t_i$.  This proves \eqref{eq:block-charge}; summing over
$i$ gives \eqref{eq:block-score}.
\end{proof}

The block formula exposes a sharp boundary for the broad prevention/debt
menu.  A defender close cannot create debt under that menu: at debt zero only
an even close is admitted, while at debt one an odd close discharges and an
even close transports.  Hence a block ends odd precisely when its last odd
$c_i$ belongs to the attacker; its final close always belongs to the defender,
so necessarily $c_b=0$.  This condition is not by itself an exclusion.  On
the path $a-f-u$ plus isolated $d$, the completely legal line
\[
 O_a,O_d,C_a^{(1)},C_d^{(0)},O_f,O_u,C_f^{(0)},C_u^{(0)}
\]
ends with odd debt.  The losing defender fork is the first reply $O_d$: the
two-vertex block $\{a,d\}$ has score $\deg_{\{f,u\}}(a)=1$.  If an initial
real opener has odd degree, handshaking instead supplies another odd real
vertex; replying with it makes the immediate close--close branch even.  This
repairs the displayed fork but does not constrain longer block continuations.

\section{The vector score}

Write
\[
  \mathcal E_R=\F_2^{\binom{R}{2}}
\]
for the vector space whose coordinates are unordered pairs of real vertices.
For a completed history $h$, define its \emph{disjointness vector}
\[
  D(h)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq R}
       [I_x\cap I_y=\varnothing],xy\in\mathcal E_R.
\]
Pairing $D(h)$ with the adjacency vector $A_G$ recovers
\eqref{eq:scalar-score}.

There is an exact endpoint formula for the cut boundary of this score.  It is
useful because it isolates the part of any prospective local coefficient
system which constant-coefficient topology cannot see.  Temporarily put
$V=R\sqcup\{d\}$ and let $\widehat D(h)\in E(K_V)$ include the dummy
coordinates as well.  Number only OPEN and CLOSE events, omitting the unique
possible forced pass, and write $o_v,c_v\in\{1,\ldots,2|V|\}$ for the two
event positions of $v$.

\begin{proposition}[endpoint-controller boundary]
\label{prop:endpoint-boundary}
For every complete legal FIFO history and every $v\in V$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-boundary}
 \deg_{\widehat D(h)}(v)=|V|+o_v+c_v\pmod2.
\end{equation}
Consequently, on the real edge space,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-endpoint-boundary}
 (\partial D(h))_v
 =|V|+o_v+c_v+\widehat D(h)_{vd}.
\end{equation}
If $\bar o_v,\bar c_v$ are the positions of the real events after deleting
$O_d,C_d$ from the event word, whether or not the resulting word is legal,
then equivalently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-endpoint-boundary}
 (\partial D(h))_v=|R|+\bar o_v+\bar c_v.
\end{equation}
Thus the cut boundary records exactly whether the two endpoint events of each
real interval are inherited by the same or opposite projected controllers.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $v$ have opening rank $b$, let $r$ earlier vertices have closed before
$O_v$, and let $t$ vertices have opened by $C_v$.  FIFO makes $v$ the $b$th
vertex to close, so
\[
 o_v=b+r,\qquad c_v=b+t.
\]
Exactly the $r$ already-closed earlier vertices and the $|V|-t$ not-yet-opened
later vertices have intervals disjoint from $I_v$.  Hence
\[
 \deg_{\widehat D}(v)=r+|V|-t
 =|V|+o_v+c_v\pmod2,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:endpoint-boundary}.  Deleting the dummy coordinate
from $\widehat D$ gives \eqref{eq:real-endpoint-boundary}.

Finally, deleting $O_d,C_d$ changes the parity of an endpoint position exactly
when that endpoint lies strictly between the two dummy events.  Properness of
the FIFO intervals says that exactly one endpoint of $I_v$ lies there when
$I_v$ overlaps $I_d$, and zero modulo two lie there when the intervals are
disjoint.  Therefore
\[
 \bar o_v+\bar c_v
 =o_v+c_v+1+\widehat D_{vd}.
\]
Since $|V|+1=|R|$ modulo two, substitution proves
\eqref{eq:projected-endpoint-boundary}.
\end{proof}

At any moment let $L=U\cup Q$ be the live set.  For a real $x\in L$, put
\[
  s_L(x)=\sum_{y\in (L\cap R)\setminus\{x\}}xy,
\]
and set $s_L(d)=0$.  These are the vertex stars, or simplicial
coboundaries, of the live complete graph.

\begin{theorem}[vector live-star identity]\label{thm:live-star}
Every completed history satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:live-star}
  D(h)=\sum_{x\text{ opened}}s_{L_x}(x),
\end{equation}
where $L_x$ is the live set at the instant $x$ opens.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a real pair $xy$ and suppose $x$ opens first.  The vertex $y$ is live when
$x$ opens, so $xy$ occurs once in $s_{L_x}(x)$.  If the activity intervals
overlap, $x$ is still live when $y$ opens and the coordinate occurs a second
time in $s_{L_y}(y)$; the two occurrences cancel over $\F_2$.  If the intervals
are disjoint, $x$ has already closed when $y$ opens and there is no second
occurrence.  The coordinate of the right-hand side is therefore exactly the
coordinate of $D(h)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[scalar live-degree identity]
For every graph $G$,
\[
  F_G(h)=\sum_{x\text{ opened}}\deg_{G[L_x]}(x)\pmod2.
\]
\end{corollary}

The threshold normal form does not permit a contraction one opening
permutation at a time.

\begin{proposition}[fixed-permutation contraction fails]
\label{prop:fixed-pi}
There is a conditioned deterministic attacker strategy whose four
response leaves have the same opening permutation but whose projected real
disjointness vectors have affine hull excluding zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take real vertices $a,b,c$ and dummy $d$.  After the prefix $O_a,O_d$, put the
attacker in the even positions.  If the defender's third move is $O_b$, the
attacker plays $C_a$; if it is $C_a$, play $O_b$.  On move six, after the
defender chooses $O_c$ or $C_d$, play the other.  The four resulting leaves
are
\begin{align*}
 B&=O_aO_dO_bC_aO_cC_dC_bC_c,&
 C&=O_aO_dO_bC_aC_dO_cC_bC_c,\\
 D&=O_aO_dC_aO_bO_cC_dC_bC_c,&
 E&=O_aO_dC_aO_bC_dO_cC_bC_c.
\end{align*}
They are legal single blocks, all with opening order $(a,d,b,c)$.  Their two
free thresholds $(r_3,r_4)$ are respectively
$(0,1),(0,2),(1,1),(1,2)$, and direct use of
\eqref{eq:threshold-D} gives
\[
 D(B)=D(C)=ac,\qquad D(D)=D(E)=ab+ac.
\]
The affine hull is $ac+\Span\{ab\}$ and excludes zero.  The unpruned threshold
lattice has one further point $(0,0)$ with zero moment; the attacker's move
$C_a$ prunes exactly that correction.  Thus any global contraction must couple
different opening-permutation branches.
\end{proof}

This upgrades the earlier scalar potential calculation to an identity before
a graph is chosen.  The graph enters only through the final linear functional
$z\mapsto\langle A_G,z\rangle$.

\section{Affine response is exactly the theorem}

Fix which seat seeks odd parity, and fix a deterministic strategy $S$ for that
attacker.  Move legality is graph-independent, so $S$ can be regarded as a
strategy in the schedule game before an adjacency vector is chosen.  Let
$\mathcal H_S$ be the set of terminal histories compatible with $S$ and with
arbitrary legal responses by the other player.

\begin{theorem}[affine separation]\label{thm:affine}
The strategy $S$ forces odd score for some graph on $R$ if and only if
\[
  0\notin\Aff_{\F_2}\{D(h):h\in\mathcal H_S\}.
\]
Consequently, the general linking theorem is equivalent to the following
graph-free statement for both choices of attacker seat:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-target}
  \text{for every }S,\qquad
  0\in\Aff_{\F_2}\{D(h):h\in\mathcal H_S\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $S$ forces odd score for a graph with adjacency vector $A$, then
$\langle A,D(h)\rangle=1$ for every $h\in\mathcal H_S$.  The whole affine hull
therefore lies in the affine hyperplane $\langle A,z\rangle=1$, which excludes
zero.

Conversely, write the affine hull as $a+W$.  If it excludes zero, then
$a\notin W$.  Elementary linear separation over $\F_2$ supplies a linear
functional $\ell$ with $\ell(W)=0$ and $\ell(a)=1$.  Every linear functional on
$\mathcal E_R$ is pairing with the adjacency vector of a simple graph on $R$.
For that graph, $\ell(D(h))=1$ for every response history, so $S$ forces odd
score.
\end{proof}

The separating graph can also be used to remove an apparent source of
generality from a hypothetical counterexample.  Say that a scalar policy is
\emph{positional} when its prescribed attacker move depends only on the full
public state $(U,Q,\mathsf{ko},\mathsf{turn},g)$, including the accumulated
score bit, and not on the history which reached that state.

\begin{proposition}[positional scalar reduction]
\label{prop:positional-reduction}
If \eqref{eq:affine-target} fails for either attacker seat, then it
fails for a strategy which is positional after scalarization by one graph
$B$.  More precisely, a counterexample is equivalent to a finite set
$\mathcal W$ of full public states, containing the initial state, such that
every terminal state in $\mathcal W$ has score one, every defender state in
$\mathcal W$ has all of its legal children in $\mathcal W$, and every attacker
state in $\mathcal W$ has at least one legal child in $\mathcal W$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If affine contraction fails, Theorem~\ref{thm:affine} supplies a graph $B$ for
which the original history-dependent policy forces terminal score one.  On
the finite acyclic public-state graph, let $W(s)$ mean that the attacker can
force terminal score one from $s$.  Backward induction on the number of
remaining legal events gives the Bellman alternatives: at an attacker state
$W(s)$ holds exactly when it holds at some legal child, while at a defender
state it holds exactly when it holds at every legal child.  Choose one winning
child once and for all at every winning attacker state.  The resulting policy
is positional and the set of its winning states is the required
$\mathcal W$.

Conversely, choose one child in $\mathcal W$ at every attacker state.  Closure
at defender states makes every compatible terminal history have scalar score
one.  Pairing its disjointness vector with $B$ is therefore one on every leaf,
so their affine hull lies in the hyperplane
$\langle B,z\rangle=1$ and excludes zero.
\end{proof}

Thus history-dependent pruning is not itself the essential obstruction.  A
proof may work with a Markov Bellman certificate, but it must retain the score
sheet: two paths which reconverge after forgetting score can arrive on
opposite sheets.

Equivalently, one must select an odd-cardinality formal multiset of response
histories whose disjointness vectors XOR to zero.  This is an
$\F_2$-flow problem: attacker nodes route all incoming weight along the move
prescribed by $S$, while defender nodes may split it among an odd number of
outgoing moves.  Uniformly summing all response leaves does \emph{not} work in
general; the flow must be selected.

There is a useful exact homogeneous recursion.  At a history $h$, let
$Z(h)\subseteq\F_2\oplus\mathcal E_R$ be the span of
$(1,D_h(\lambda))$ over response leaves $\lambda$ below $h$, where $D_h$ is
the future disjointness increment.  Give a close of a real front $f$ the
charge $c=\sum_{u\in U\cap R}fu$, and give an open, dummy close, or pass charge
zero.  This sums to $D(h)$ because a pair becomes disjoint exactly when its
earlier vertex closes while the later one is untouched.  Put
\[
  \tau_c(a,z)=(a,z+ac).
\]
Then a terminal node has $Z=\Span\{(1,0)\}$; an attacker node, unary after
fixing $S$, has $Z(h)=\tau_c Z(h')$; and a defender node has
\[
  Z(h)=\Span_m\tau_{c_m}Z(h_m).
\]
The desired affine response is exactly $(1,0)\in Z(\text{root})$.  Thus the
open problem is a label-preserving contraction of this recursion, not an
ordinary contraction of the response tree: ordinary boundaries have even
augmentation, while the required leaf chain has odd augmentation.

The augmentation parity can be resolved explicitly rather than hidden inside
the homogeneous span.  This is the exact operation needed when an odd flow
must split among several defender children.

\begin{proposition}[parity-resolved continuation convolution]
\label{prop:parity-convolution}
At a history $h$, with its residual history-dependent attacker
policy retained, let $\Lambda_h$ be the compatible terminal leaves and put
\[
 \mathcal C_h^\epsilon
 =\{D_h(c):c\in\F_2[\Lambda_h],\ \operatorname{aug}(c)=\epsilon\},
 \qquad \epsilon\in\F_2.
\]
Then $W_h:=\mathcal C_h^0$ is a linear space and
$A_h:=\mathcal C_h^1$ is a nonempty affine $W_h$-coset.  At a terminal node,
$\mathcal C_h^0=\mathcal C_h^1=\{0\}$.  If an attacker node uses its fixed
move of charge $c$ and has child $h'$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parity-attacker}
 \mathcal C_h^\epsilon=\epsilon c+\mathcal C_{h'}^\epsilon.
\end{equation}
At a defender node with legal moves $m$, charges $c_m$, and children $h_m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parity-defender}
 \mathcal C_h^\epsilon
 =\bigcup_{\sum_m\epsilon_m=\epsilon}
   \sum_m\bigl(\epsilon_m c_m+\mathcal C_{h_m}^{\epsilon_m}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, choose $a_m\in A_{h_m}$, put $b_m=c_m+a_m$, and fix one
reference move $m_0$.  Then
\begin{align}
 W_h&=\sum_mW_{h_m}
      +\Span\{b_m+b_{m_0}:m\ne m_0\},\label{eq:parity-W}\\
 A_h&=b_{m_0}+W_h.\label{eq:parity-A}
\end{align}
In particular a target $t$ is feasible with odd augmentation exactly when
$t+b_{m_0}$ belongs to the space on the right of
\eqref{eq:parity-W}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A formal leaf chain splits uniquely among the disjoint child leaf sets.  Its
augmentation and moment are respectively the sums of the child augmentations
and of the translated child moments, giving
\eqref{eq:parity-attacker} and \eqref{eq:parity-defender}.  Two chains of the
same augmentation differ by an even chain, so $A_h$ is a $W_h$-coset.  At a
defender node, every odd child-parity profile is obtained from the profile
supported on $m_0$ by toggling pairs of child parities.  The even corrections
inside each child give $\sum_mW_{h_m}$, and toggling $m_0$ together with $m$
adds $b_{m_0}+b_m$.  This proves \eqref{eq:parity-W} and
\eqref{eq:parity-A}.  The rank
\[
 \rho(h)=3|U|+|Q|+1_{\mathsf{ko}}
\]
strictly decreases under every OPEN, CLOSE, or forced pass, so the recursion
is well founded.  Its domain is the pair consisting of the history and the
residual policy: two histories may reconverge to the same public
$(U,Q,\mathsf{ko})$ state while a history-dependent attacker retains different
continuations.
\end{proof}

The same convolution can be flattened across an arbitrary decorated
frontier; this is the exact bookkeeping needed below when prefixes have
already accumulated different cut charges.

\begin{corollary}[decorated-frontier decomposition]
\label{cor:decorated-frontier}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a finite antichain in the residual response tree
which meets every terminal branch exactly once.  For $q\in\mathcal F$, let
$\Gamma_q$ be the disjointness charge accumulated from the root to $q$, and
write the odd continuation coset at $q$ as
\[
 A_q=a_q+W_q.
\]
Then
\begin{align}
 A_{\rm root}
   &=\Aff\{\Gamma_q+a_q:q\in\mathcal F\}
     +\sum_{q\in\mathcal F}W_q,
     \label{eq:decorated-frontier-A}\\
 W_{\rm root}
   &=\sum_{q\in\mathcal F}W_q
     +\Span\{(\Gamma_q+a_q)+(\Gamma_{q_0}+a_{q_0}):
               q\ne q_0\}
     \label{eq:decorated-frontier-W}
\end{align}
for any fixed $q_0\in\mathcal F$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every root leaf lies below a unique $q\in\mathcal F$.  Splitting a formal
leaf chain by these disjoint descendant sets shows that its total moment is
\[
 \sum_q\bigl(\epsilon_q(\Gamma_q+a_q)+w_q\bigr),
 \qquad w_q\in W_q,
\]
where $\epsilon_q$ is the augmentation of the $q$-part.  The root chain is
odd exactly when $\sum_q\epsilon_q=1$.  The odd sums of the decorated points
$\Gamma_q+a_q$ form their affine hull, while the $w_q$ vary independently;
this proves \eqref{eq:decorated-frontier-A}.  The even sums of the decorated
points are generated by their pairwise differences from one reference point,
which gives \eqref{eq:decorated-frontier-W}.
\end{proof}

There is one particularly useful relative specialization.  It states exactly
what is lost when an absolute prefix contraction forgets which minimum-bad
leaf it was meant to carry.

\begin{proposition}[relative spine quotient]
\label{prop:relative-spine-quotient}
\PROVED{} Fix a deterministic attacker strategy and a node $h$ in its response
tree.  Follow the root-to-$h$ spine, and let $\mathcal F_h$ consist of $h$
together with every off-spine child of every defender node on that spine.
(Attacker nodes are unary after the strategy is fixed.)  Then $\mathcal F_h$
is an antichain meeting every terminal branch once.

For $q\in\mathcal F_h$, put $b_q=\Gamma_q+a_q$, using the notation of
Corollary~\ref{cor:decorated-frontier}, and define
\begin{align}
 E_{\mathrm{off}}(h)
   &=\left\{\sum_{q\ne h}\epsilon_qb_q:
             \sum_{q\ne h}\epsilon_q=0\right\},\\
 M_h&=\sum_{q\in\mathcal F_h}W_q+E_{\mathrm{off}}(h).
\end{align}
The moments of the odd root leaf chains whose frontier coefficient at $h$ is
one are exactly the affine coset
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-spine-coset}
 b_h+M_h.
\end{equation}
Consequently such a chain has zero moment if and only if $b_h\in M_h$.
This condition is independent of the chosen representatives $a_q$.

If $B$ is a scalar separating certificate for a counterstrategy and $h$ is
the selected minimum-bad node, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-spine-separation}
 \langle B,b_h\rangle=1,
 \qquad
 M_h\subseteq B^\perp.
\end{equation}
Thus proving $b_h\in M_h$ for the normal forms (A) and (B$'$) is sufficient
for the full linking theorem, and every hypothetical counterexample violates
precisely that membership.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every terminal branch either leaves the spine for the first time at a unique
off-spine defender child or reaches $h$, which proves the frontier assertion.
Split a formal root leaf chain at this frontier.  If its coefficient at $h$
is one, odd total augmentation says that the sum of all off-spine
coefficients is zero.  Corollary~\ref{cor:decorated-frontier} then gives
exactly
\[
 b_h+\sum_qW_q+
 \left\{\sum_{q\ne h}\epsilon_qb_q:
              \sum_{q\ne h}\epsilon_q=0\right\},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:relative-spine-coset}.  Replacing $a_q$ by another point
of the same coset adds an element of $W_q$, already contained in $M_h$.

For a scalar counterstrategy, every absolute terminal moment pairs to one
with $B$.  Hence each $b_q$ pairs to one and every $W_q$, being generated by
differences of terminal moments below $q$, pairs to zero.  An even sum of the
off-spine $b_q$ also pairs to zero, proving
\eqref{eq:relative-spine-separation}.
\end{proof}

The isolated dummy also gives an exact affine model of one OPEN fan, but only
as long as the next attacker operator is common across its children.

\begin{proposition}[virtual OPEN and the pruning obstruction]
\label{prop:virtual-open-pruning}
Let $U=R\sqcup\{d\}$ with $d$ isolated, and let
$\epsilon\in\F_2^U$ have odd augmentation.  Its real virtual vertex
\[
 \nu(\epsilon)=\sum_{r\in R}\epsilon_r r
\]
determines $\epsilon$ uniquely, since
$\epsilon_d=1+\sum_{r\in R}\epsilon_r$.  If every selected OPEN child is
followed by the same front close $C_f$, then the aggregate prefix charge is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virtual-open-common-close}
 \sum_{z\in U}\epsilon_zc_z
 =\sum_{r\in R}fr+f\nu(\epsilon).
\end{equation}

This affine rule does not commute with a history-dependent attacker policy.
On real vertices $0,1,2$ and dummy $d$, let the attacker first play $O_0$.
After defender replies $O_1$ or $O_2$, it plays ``OPEN the least untouched
vertex, otherwise CLOSE''; after $O_d$, it plays ``CLOSE if legal, otherwise
OPEN the least untouched vertex''.  The three continuation affine sets, in
coordinates $01,02,12$, are exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virtual-open-counterexample}
 \mathcal A_1=\mathcal A_2=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_d=01+02+\langle12\rangle.
\end{equation}
Hence the nontrivial odd superposition of all three OPEN children has
continuation coset
\[
 \mathcal A_1+\mathcal A_2+\mathcal A_d
 =01+02+\langle12\rangle,
\]
which excludes zero despite the common zero OPEN charge.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Odd augmentation makes the displayed formula for $\epsilon_d$ immediate.
After $O_z$, a common $C_f$ has charge
\[
 c_z=\sum_{r\in R}fr+fz\quad(z\in R),
 \qquad
 c_d=\sum_{r\in R}fr.
\]
Summing with coefficients $\epsilon_z$ proves
\eqref{eq:virtual-open-common-close}.

For the policy obstruction, all compatible terminal histories are the
following; thus the verification is a finite branch argument rather than a
force-set computation:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l|c}
 &\text{history}&D_R\\ \hline
O_1&O_0O_1O_2O_dC_0C_1C_2C_d&0\\
   &O_0O_1O_2C_0O_dC_1C_2C_d&0\\ \hline
O_2&O_0O_2O_1O_dC_0C_2C_1C_d&0\\
   &O_0O_2O_1C_0O_dC_2C_1C_d&0\\ \hline
O_d&O_0O_dC_0O_1C_dO_2C_1C_2&01+02\\
   &O_0O_dC_0O_2C_dO_1C_2C_1&01+02\\
   &O_0O_dC_0C_dO_1O_2C_1C_2&01+02\\
   &O_0O_dC_0O_1C_dC_1O_2PC_2&01+02+12\\
   &O_0O_dC_0O_2C_dC_2O_1PC_1&01+02+12.
\end{array}
\]
In a real child, after the attacker's next OPEN the defender can only OPEN
the remaining dummy or close $0$, giving the two listed rows.  In the dummy
child the forced $C_0$ leaves the defender the three choices $O_1,O_2,C_d$;
the first two each split once more between an OPEN and the next front CLOSE,
while the last creates the unique ko-forced row.  This exhausts the policy
tree.  Direct interval inspection gives the last column and proves
\eqref{eq:virtual-open-counterexample}.
\end{proof}

The example is the smallest nontrivial odd OPEN superposition: with at most
two children every odd subset is a singleton.  It is not a root
counterexample, because either singleton real branch already supplies the
zero moment.  What fails is precisely the attempt to replace the decorated
family $z\mapsto a_z+W_z$ by one virtual vertex before applying the
branch-dependent attacker continuations; symplectic Gram--Schmidt on the
alternating adjacency form alone cannot make that replacement.

Sibling coupling is essential already at three real vertices.  Let $d=3$ and
consider the defender fan after the attacker opens $0$.  One deterministic
residual attacker has the three branch spaces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:essential-three-fan}
 A_1=02+\langle12\rangle,
 \qquad A_2=01+\langle12\rangle,
 \qquad A_d=01+02+\langle12\rangle,
 \qquad W_1=W_2=W_d=\langle12\rangle.
\end{equation}
The compatible schedules are replayed in
\path{experiments/linking_game.py}.  Direct interval calculation gives the
displayed labels.  Zero is absent from every individual $A_m$, even after all
three even spaces are added, but the odd parity profile $(1,1,1)$ works because
\[
 02+01+(01+02)=0.
\]
Thus no induction can send the odd target into one chosen defender child.
This is minimal in real-vertex count: when the real edge space has dimension at
most one, either the combined even space is the whole edge space, making a
unit child feasible, or it is zero, in which case equal nonzero child offsets
make every odd sum nonzero.

There is a useful dual form.  Pair $\F_2\oplus\mathcal E_R$ with itself by
\[
 \langle(\alpha,B),(a,z)\rangle=\alpha a+\langle B,z\rangle
\]
and put $N_h=Z(h)^\perp$.  For a move charge $c$, define the involution
\[
 \sigma_c(\alpha,B)=(\alpha+\langle B,c\rangle,B).
\]

\begin{proposition}[dual obstruction descent]
\label{prop:dual-descent}
The exact dual recursion is
\begin{align*}
 N_h&=\{(0,B):B\in\mathcal E_R\}&&\text{at a terminal node},\\
 N_h&=\sigma_cN_{h'}&&\text{at a fixed attacker move},\\
 N_h&=\bigcap_m\sigma_{c_m}N_{h_m}&&\text{at a defender node}.
\end{align*}
Hence a counterexample is exactly a pair $(1,B)\in N_{\rm root}$.

More generally, let an odd formal flow from a node $h$ reach a frontier
$\mathcal F$ with coefficients $\epsilon_q$ and path charges $\Gamma_q$.
If $(r_h,B)\in N_h$ and $(r_q,B)\in N_q$ are the inherited dual bits, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-frontier}
 \sum_{q\in\mathcal F}\epsilon_qr_q
 =r_h+\left\langle B,
       \sum_{q\in\mathcal F}\epsilon_q\Gamma_q\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
Thus every odd frontier flow of actual $B$-charge zero from a bad node
$r_h=1$ has an odd number of bad exits.

For a fixed counterexample choose a bad reachable node lexicographically
minimal in
\[
 \bigl(\,|(U\cup Q)\cap R|,\ \rho\,\bigr).
\]
It is an attacker node, its prescribed move is an odd real CLOSE, and its
child has dual bit zero.  It cannot have a balanced ordered front pair.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Orthogonality against $\tau_c(a,z)=(a,z+ac)$ replaces $\alpha$ by
$\alpha+\langle B,c\rangle$, proving the three dual recurrences.  Along a
selected path this gives
$r_h=\langle B,\Gamma_q\rangle+r_q$; multiply by $\epsilon_q$, sum, and use
$\sum_q\epsilon_q=1$ to obtain \eqref{eq:dual-frontier}.

At a bad defender node with $U\ne\varnothing$, every OPEN child has charge
zero, preserves the number of live real vertices, and lowers $\rho$.  If
$U=\varnothing$, every CLOSE or pass has charge zero and
gives the same contradiction.  A lexicographically minimum bad node is
therefore controlled by the attacker.  Its prescribed child would be bad
unless the move had charge one; only an odd real CLOSE can have that charge.
Finally Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced-front-transport} supplies an odd
frontier whose consecutive front closes have equal scalar charge and whose
transported coefficient $\kappa$ is $B$-isotropic.  Equation
\eqref{eq:dual-frontier} would then produce a lower-rank bad exit, excluding a
balanced front at the minimum node.
\end{proof}

The predecessor of that minimum node has a much more rigid form.

\begin{proposition}[minimum-bad predecessor normal form]
\label{prop:minimum-bad-predecessor}
Let $h$ be lexicographically minimum as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-descent}.  Write its prescribed move as the odd
close $C_x$.  Its immediate defender predecessor is exactly one of the
following two types.

\begin{enumerate}
\item A CLOSE predecessor
\[
 r_g=1+q_y\ \xrightarrow{\ C_y,\;q_y=\deg_U(y)\ }\
 r_h=1\ \xrightarrow{\ C_x,\;\deg_U(x)=1\ }\ r=0.
\tag{A}
\]
Here the predecessor queue begins $(y,x,\ldots)$.  If $q_y=1$, this is the
good--bad--good odd--odd CLOSE spike.  If $q_y=0$, then $g$ is also bad and
$|U_h|$ is necessarily even.

\item A ko-protected domination state after the dummy has already been
touched:
\[
 r_g=1,\qquad Q_g=(x),\qquad \mathsf{ko}=1,
 \qquad \varnothing\ne U_g\subseteq R,
\tag{B}
\]
where $|U_g|$ is even and $B(x,u)=1$ for every $u\in U_g$.  The defender is
forced to open some $u$; every such child is minimum bad, and the attacker
immediately plays $C_x$ with odd charge $|U_g|-1$.

This second type has a forced one-step ancestry.  Let $j$ be the empty-queue
attacker node whose prescribed $O_x$ reaches $g$, and let $e$ be the preceding
defender node.  Then
\[
 Q_e=(y),\qquad
 e\xrightarrow{\ C_y,\;\deg_{U_e}(y)=1\ }j
 \xrightarrow{\ O_x,\;0\ }g,
\tag{B$'$}
\]
and $r_e=0$.  Thus (B), like the odd subcase of (A), is created from a good
defender node by an odd singleton-front CLOSE; it merely inserts a
zero-charge empty-queue OPEN before the minimum bad checkpoint.
\end{enumerate}
In particular an untouched isolated dummy rules out every OPEN predecessor
of a minimum bad node.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The node $h$ is not the root, since its prescribed move is a CLOSE.  A pass
cannot precede it because $U_h\ne\varnothing$.  Suppose first that the
defender reached it by opening $z$ at a node $g$.  OPEN has zero charge, so
$g$ is bad.  Every alternative OPEN $w\in U_g$ has a bad child with the same
lexicographic rank as $h$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-descent} forces the
attacker at each such child to close the common real front $x$ oddly.  If
$q=\deg_{U_g}(x)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimum-open-predecessor}
 q+B(x,w)=1\qquad(w\in U_g).
\end{equation}
If the dummy $d$ were untouched, the equation for $w=d$ would give $q=1$,
whereas the equations for all real $w$ would make every $B(x,w)=0$ and hence
$q=0$, a contradiction.

Thus every vertex of $U_g$ is real.  Summing
\eqref{eq:minimum-open-predecessor} over $w$ gives
\[
 q=|U_g|(1+q).
\]
The cardinality $|U_g|$ is therefore even, $q=0$, and
$B(x,w)=1$ for all $w\in U_g$.  If ko were clear, the defender could instead
play the even close $C_x$; its child would be bad with one fewer live real
vertex.  Hence ko is set, which at a reachable position forces
$Q_g=(x)$.  This is (B).

The remaining possibility is a defender close $C_y$ from a queue beginning
$(y,x,\ldots)$.  Put $q_y=\deg_U(y)$.  If $q_y=0$, then $g$ is bad and any
defender OPEN child is bad.  Moreover
\[
 \sum_{v\in U}\bigl(B(y,v)+B(x,v)\bigr)=q_y+\deg_U(x)=1,
\]
so the set of $v$ which make the two front charges equal after $O_v$ is odd.
If $|U|$ were odd, each such child would have an even untouched set and a
balanced front.  Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced-front-transport} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-descent} would then give a bad exit deleting
$y,x$, with fewer live real vertices than $h$ (whether or not $y=d$), a
contradiction.  Hence $q_y=0$ forces $|U|$ even.  If $q_y=1$, the defender
recurrence instead gives $r_g=0$.  Together with the odd $C_x$ and its good
child, these are exactly the two subcases of (A).

It remains to prove the ancestry assertion for (B).  Since the dummy has
already been touched and $U_g$ is nonempty, the empty-queue predecessor $j$
is not the root and cannot follow a pass.  Its preceding move is therefore a
defender close $C_y$ from a ko-clear singleton queue at a node $e$.  Put
$A=\{x\}\sqcup U_g$ and $q_y=\deg_A(y)$.  The fixed $O_x$ has charge zero,
so $r_j=r_g=1$, while the defender recurrence at $e$ gives
\[
 r_e=1+q_y.
\]
Suppose $q_y=0$.  Then $e$ is bad, and every alternative defender OPEN
$O_v$, $v\in A$, leads to a bad attacker checkpoint with queue $(y,v)$.
In the even-order live graph on $\{y\}\sqcup A$, the full-degree parity class
of $y$ has even cardinality.  After deleting $y$, it therefore supplies an
odd nonempty set of vertices $v$ for which the two front charges at $(y,v)$
agree.  The untouched set $A-\{v\}$ is even, so both $\eta_{yv}$ fibres are
even and Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced-front-transport} applies.  Its odd
zero-charge flow has a bad exit by Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-descent}; that
exit deletes $y,v$.  If $y$ is real it starts with one more live real vertex
than $g$ and deletes two, while if $y=d$ it starts with the same number and
deletes the one real vertex $v$.  Either way the exit has one fewer live real
vertex than $g$, hence also fewer than the chosen minimum node $h$.  This
contradicts the primary minimality of $h$.  Hence $q_y=1$ and $r_e=0$,
proving (B$'$).
\end{proof}

The zero-charge half of the CLOSE-predecessor alternative can now be removed
entirely.  This is a genuine use of the complete fixed-front theorem rather
than another local degree comparison.

\begin{proposition}[the even-$U$ branch of (A) is impossible]
\label{prop:a-even-u-impossible}
\PROVED{} The alternative $q_y=0$ in normal form~(A) cannot occur.  Hence
every minimum-bad CLOSE predecessor of type~(A) is the odd--odd spike
$q_y=1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
First $y$ is real.  If $y=d$ were the isolated dummy, then after any defender
OPEN $O_v$ at $g$ the child would still be bad (OPEN has charge zero), would
have the same number of active real vertices as $h$, and would have smaller
$\rho$: relative to $g$, $C_y$ lowers $\rho$ by one whereas $O_v$ lowers it
by two.  This contradicts the choice of $h$.

Fix $v\in U$.  We claim that after the defender plays $O_v$, the attacker
cannot answer with another OPEN $O_w$.  Such an answer reaches a
defender-controlled state with the same fixed clear front $y$ and untouched
set $S=U-\{v,w\}$.  Stop every continuation when $y$ closes.  The queue was
already nonempty before either OPEN, so ko remains clear; $C_y$ is legal
throughout, every branch reaches this frontier, and no pass occurs.  All
moves before the frontier are OPENs and hence have charge zero, so the
inherited dual bit immediately before each terminal $C_y$ is still $r_g=1$.
If $R\subseteq S$ is its untouched remainder, the close has charge
\[
 c_y(R)=\deg_R(y)=\sum_{u\in R}B(y,u).
\]
Because $y$ is real, the state after this delayed $C_y$ has the same
active-real count as $h$; because at least $v,w$ were opened first, its
$\rho$ is strictly smaller.  Minimality of $h$ therefore makes it good.  The
dual recurrence
$r_{\rm after}=r_{\rm before}+c_y(R)$ forces $c_y(R)=1$ on every leaf.

This is impossible by Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-fixed-front-prefix}: apply it
at the displayed defender root and pair its odd zero-remainder flow with the
linear functional $R\mapsto\deg_R(y)$.  Equivalently, the kernel-checked
theorem \texttt{not\_closureDefenderForces\_one} says directly that no
attacker policy from a defender-rooted fixed-front deletion game can put
every leaf on sheet one.  Thus the attacker must answer every $O_v$ by
$C_y$.

That immediate close has charge $B(y,v)$ because $\deg_U(y)=0$.  Its child
has dual bit $1+B(y,v)$, the same active-real count as $h$, and smaller
$\rho$; hence $B(y,v)=1$.  The child is good and defender-controlled with
clear front $x$.  Its legal $C_x$ has charge
\[
 \deg_{U-\{v\}}(x)=1+B(x,v).
\]
If this were one, its child would be bad after deleting the real vertex $x$,
with fewer active real vertices than $h$.  Hence $B(x,v)=1$.  Since $v$ was
arbitrary, every member of $U$ lies in the joint $11$ fibre of the two front
rows.  But $|U|$ is even in the $q_y=0$ subcase, so
\[
 \deg_U(x)=\sum_{v\in U}B(x,v)=|U|=0,
\]
contradicting the defining condition $\deg_U(x)=1$ of~(A).
\end{proof}

The ancestry statement cannot be strengthened by requiring one odd unit from
every immediate OPEN child.  The following counterexample is entirely
strategy-theoretic; no computed force set is used.

The remaining CLOSE-predecessor alternative has a parallel first-response
restriction.

\begin{proposition}[(A) immediate-close fibre]
\label{prop:a-immediate-close-fibre}
\PROVED{} In the odd-spike subcase of (A), so
\[
 r_g=0\xrightarrow{C_y,1}r_h=1
       \xrightarrow{C_x,1}0,
 \qquad \deg_U(y)=\deg_U(x)=1,
\]
consider a defender alternative $O_v$ at $g$.  If the attacker's prescribed
first response is $C_y$, then necessarily
\[
 B(y,v)=B(x,v)=1.
\]
Thus every $v$ outside the joint $11$ fibre of the two front rows forces the
attacker to continue with an OPEN.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After $O_v$, the charge of $C_y$ is $1+B(y,v)$.  If $B(y,v)=0$, this close
creates a bad state with the same live-real count as $h$ but strictly smaller
secondary rank, contradicting the choice of $h$.  Hence $B(y,v)=1$, and the
$C_y$ child is good.  It is defender-controlled with clear front $x$, whose
legal close has charge
\[
 \deg_{U-\{v\}}(x)=1+B(x,v).
\]
If $B(x,v)=0$, that close creates a bad child after deleting the real front
$x$, with fewer live real vertices than $h$.  Primary minimality excludes
this, so $B(x,v)=1$ as claimed.
\end{proof}

The same argument extends from the first response to every transition in an
off-spine subtree.

\begin{proposition}[the odd spike exports neutral punctured trees]
\label{prop:a-neutral-punctures}
\PROVED{} In the remaining case~(A),
\[
 r_g=0\xrightarrow{C_y,1}r_h=1\xrightarrow{C_x,1}0,
 \qquad \deg_U(y)=\deg_U(x)=1,
\]
fix any $v\in U$ and let $q_v$ be the child of $g$ after the defender OPEN
$O_v$.  The entire strategy subtree below $q_v$ is scalar-neutral: every
prescribed attacker transition and every legal defender transition has
$B$-charge zero, and every terminal residual is zero.

Consequently, if the first prescribed move below $q_v$ is $C_y$, then
$B(y,v)=B(x,v)=1$.  If it is $O_w$, then
\[
 B(y,v)+B(y,w)=1.
\]
More generally, at every later defender checkpoint before $y$ closes its
untouched $y$-degree is zero.  Once $y$ closes, the remaining subtree is
still completely neutral.  If the attacker closes $y$ after an odd OPEN word
with opened set $P$, the defender's legal next $C_x$ gives in addition
$B(x,P)=1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
OPEN has charge zero, so $r_{q_v}=r_g=0$.  Until $y$ closes the FIFO front
remains $y$ and every preceding move is an OPEN.  At any eventual delayed
$C_y$, at least $v$ has moved from the untouched set to the queue.  The close
removes the real vertex $y$, so its child has the same active-real count as
$h$ and strictly smaller $\rho$.  A unit charge would change residual zero
to residual one and produce a lexicographically smaller bad node.  Hence
every such $C_y$ has charge zero.  Its child has residual zero, and every
strict descendant has no more active real vertices and smaller rank than
$h$.  Any later unit transition would therefore create a smaller bad node.
Thus all later transitions and terminal residuals are zero, proving
neutrality of the whole punctured subtree.

For an immediate $C_y$ after $O_v$, zero charge says
$1+B(y,v)=0$.  The next node is defender-controlled with front $x$; its
legal $C_x$ must also have charge zero, so $1+B(x,v)=0$.  If the attacker
instead opens $w$, the defender may immediately close $y$, and neutrality
gives $1+B(y,v)+B(y,w)=0$.  The same argument at an arbitrary later defender
checkpoint gives zero untouched $y$-degree.  Finally, when the attacker
itself closes $y$ after opening $P$, the resulting node is
defender-controlled and neutral; its legal $C_x$ has charge
$\deg_{U-P}(x)=1+B(x,P)$, which must vanish.
\end{proof}

This is the strongest possible descendant-only statement: even these entire
neutral trees do not force the selected relative coefficient.

\begin{proposition}[the odd-spike coefficient is not descendant-local]
\label{prop:a-descendant-no-go}
\PROVED{} Proposition~\ref{prop:a-neutral-punctures}, even together with the
complete fixed-front subtree and the neutral selected tail, does not imply
$b_h\in M_h$.  This fails on three real vertices plus the isolated dummy by
an explicit legal response tree.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take real vertices $y,x,a$, isolated dummy $d$, support edges $ya,xa$, and
the defender state
\[
 g:\qquad U=\{a,d\},\qquad Q=(y,x),\qquad \mathsf{ko}=0.
\]
On the selected branch the defender plays $C_y$, the attacker plays $C_x$,
and thereafter use any strategy on the edgeless residual board $\{a,d\}$.
Both closes have charge one and every later transition has charge zero.  Thus
$g$ has residual zero, the intermediate node $h$ has residual one, and the
child of $C_x$ is completely neutral.

After the off-spine reply $O_a$, prescribe $C_y$.  The only untouched vertex
is the isolated dummy, so this close and every later close have charge zero.
After the other reply $O_d$, prescribe $O_a$; the untouched set is then empty
and again every later close has charge zero.  Hence both punctured trees are
completely neutral and $h$ is the only bad node in this local response tree.

Now use the universal real edge space, not merely the scalar support.  At the
relative frontier $\{h,q_a,q_d\}$, every terminal below $h$ has moment
$ya+xa$: $C_y$ separates $y$ from the still-untouched $a$, $C_x$ separates
$x$ from $a$, and the residual board has only one real vertex.  Every
terminal below either off-spine child has moment zero: in the $O_a$ branch
$a$ overlaps both old fronts, and in the $O_d,O_a$ branch all real vertices
are open before any closes.  Therefore
\[
 b_h=ya+xa,\qquad b_{q_a}=b_{q_d}=0,
 \qquad W_h=W_{q_a}=W_{q_d}=0.
\]
The even off-spine space is zero, so $M_h=0$ and $b_h\notin M_h$.
Absolute contraction at $g$ still holds by either zero off-spine leaf.  Thus
the missing odd-spike relation must use at least one sibling strictly earlier
than $g$; no theorem over $g$ and its descendants can suffice.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the mandatory (B$'$) OPEN fan is false]
\label{prop:bad-open-fan}
Let
\[
 y=0,\qquad x=1,\qquad A=\{2,3,4,5\},
\]
and use the primary and diagnostic graphs
\[
 B=\{01,12,13,14,15\},
 \qquad L=\{02\}.
\]
At the clear defender state
\[
 e:\qquad U=\{1,2,3,4,5\},\qquad Q=(0),\qquad g_B=0,
\]
there is one history-dependent attacker policy below the six defender moves
with the following constant terminal scores:
\[
 (B,L)(\mathcal A_1)=\{(0,1)\},\qquad
 (B,L)(\mathcal A_s)=\{(0,0)\}\quad(2\le s\le5),
 \qquad
 (B,L)(\mathcal A_{C_0})=\{(0,1)\}.
\]
Here $\mathcal A_s$ is the terminal continuation set below $O_s$, and
$\mathcal A_{C_0}$ is the corresponding set below $C_0$.
Consequently
\[
 L(\mathcal A_1+\mathcal A_2+\mathcal A_3+
   \mathcal A_4+\mathcal A_5)=\{1\},
\]
so an odd flow constrained to use every immediate OPEN face cannot contract.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We first record the elementary star strategy used in every branch.  Once the
center $1$ is open, let $k$ be the number of queued leaves ahead of it and
let $m$ be the number of untouched leaves.  The only remaining $B$-charge is
the charge $m$ of $C_1$.  At an attacker turn, play
\[
 \begin{cases}
 C_1,&k=0, m\text{ even},\\
 \text{OPEN an untouched leaf},&k=0, m\text{ odd},
       \text{ or }k>0, m>0,\\
 \text{CLOSE the front},&k>0, m=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
This forces an even future $B$-charge from every attacker turn, and from every
defender turn satisfying $k>0$ or $m$ even.  Indeed, mutual induction on
$k+m$ is immediate: a defender OPEN gives the attacker $(k,m-1)$, a defender
prefront CLOSE gives $(k-1,m)$, and at $k=0$ a defender $C_1$ is already even
when $m$ is even.  Each prescribed attacker move either finishes evenly or
strictly decreases the same rank.  Fix the least-labelled leaf whenever the
rule asks for an OPEN.

Now consider the six first moves at $e$.  After $O_1$, the attacker plays
$C_0$, of charge $(B,L)=(0,1)$, and invokes the star strategy at the defender
state $(k,m)=(0,4)$.  After $O_2$, it plays $O_1$; the intervals of $0,2$
already overlap, and the star strategy begins at the defender state
$(k,m)=(2,3)$.  These give respectively the first two displayed score sets.

Let next $s\in\{3,4,5\}$ and write $\{a,b\}=A-\{2,s\}$.  The attacker first
plays $O_2$, fixing the $L$-score at zero.  If the defender now opens $1$, use
the star strategy; if it opens $a$ or $b$, open $1$ and then use that strategy.
If instead the defender closes $0$, that close has $B$-charge one; answer by
$C_s$, also of $B$-charge one.  From the resulting defender state, an OPEN is
answered by opening $1$ if necessary and then using the star strategy.  The
only other move is $C_2$, which supplies a third unit $B$-charge and leaves
the attacker at an empty queue with $U=\{1,a,b\}$.  Play $O_1$; ko forces the
defender to open $a$ or $b$, and $C_1$ then sees the one remaining untouched
leaf and supplies the fourth unit.  All later $B$- and $L$-charges vanish.
Thus every $O_s$ branch has total $(B,L)=(0,0)$.

Finally, the defender move $C_0$ itself has charge $(1,1)$.  The attacker
opens $1$ at the empty queue, ko forces the defender to open one of
$2,3,4,5$, and the attacker closes $1$ with $B$-charge three and $L$-charge
zero.  No later edge can score, so this branch totals $(0,1)$.  The policies
on the six disjoint child trees combine into the asserted single
history-dependent policy.
\end{proof}

This refutes only the mandatory all-five transversal, not the global affine
contraction.  Under the same policy every face $s=2,3,4,5$ contains a literal
zero-moment leaf; compatible continuations are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
2&O_2O_1O_3O_4O_5C_0C_2C_1C_3C_4C_5\\
3&O_3O_2O_1O_4O_5C_0C_3C_2C_1C_4C_5\\
4&O_4O_2O_1O_3O_5C_0C_4C_2C_1C_3C_5\\
5&O_5O_2O_1O_3O_4C_0C_5C_2C_1C_3C_4.
\end{array}
\]
All OPENs precede all CLOSEs, so their disjointness moment is zero.  A
one-face odd flow therefore already contracts; the example supplies no
odd-winning strategy and no root counterexample.

The minimum-bad hypotheses do impose more structure on the first response,
but that structure still has one exact obstruction.

\begin{proposition}[the (B$'$) first-response forest criterion]
\label{prop:bprime-response-forest}
At a (B$'$) predecessor put
\[
 A=N\sqcup Z,\qquad
 N=\{v:B(y,v)=1\},\qquad Z=\{v:B(y,v)=0\}.
\]
Thus $|A|$ and $|N|$ are odd and $|Z|$ is even.  Mark a vertex
$v\in N$ as \emph{grounded} when the attacker answers $O_v$ by the immediate
zero close $C_y$.  For every other canonical branch, suppose its first
attacker response is $O_{w_v}$ and the defender then plays $C_y$.  Minimum
badness forces $v,w_v$ to have opposite $y$-colours.  Form the bipartite
multigraph $H$ on $A$ with edges $\{v,w_v\}$, and regard the grounded
vertices as marked components.

The resulting canonical good $C_y$ exits contain an odd chain whose prefix
decoration is zero if and only if every component of $H$ containing no
grounded vertex has even order.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $p=\sum_{a\in A}a$.  The prefix decorations are
\[
 \Gamma_v=y(p+v)\quad(v\text{ grounded}),
 \qquad
 \Gamma_v=y(p+v+w_v)\quad(v\text{ an edge branch}).
\]
For branch coefficients $\alpha_v$, their sum is zero exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bprime-response-incidence}
 p=\sum_{v\ {m grounded}}\alpha_vv
   +\sum_{v\ {m edge}}\alpha_v(v+w_v).
\end{equation}
Conversely, any solution of \eqref{eq:bprime-response-incidence} already has
odd branch augmentation.  Taking total coordinate augmentation says that an
odd number of grounded units is selected, because $|A|$ is odd.  Summing only
the $N$-coordinates then says that an even number of edge branches is
selected, because every edge crosses $N\mid Z$ and $|N|$ is odd.

Inside one connected component, its edge-incidence vectors span exactly the
even-weight subspace.  A grounded unit enlarges this to the full coordinate
space of that component.  Hence the all-one vector $p$ is representable
precisely when every component without such a unit has even order, proving
the criterion.
\end{proof}

The criterion is not automatic from (B$'$).  Take
\[
 A=\{x,z,z'\},\qquad
 E(B)=\{yx,xz,xz'\},
\]
with no grounded branch and first responses
\[
 x\longmapsto z,qquad z\longmapsto x,qquad z'\longmapsto x.
\]
Here $N=\{x\}$, $Z=\{z,z'\}$, and $x$ dominates the even set
$\{z,z'\}$ exactly as required in (B$'$).  The response graph is one
ungrounded component of odd order three, while the three decorations are
\[
 \Gamma_x=yz',\qquad \Gamma_z=yz',\qquad \Gamma_{z'}=yz.
\]
Only the even pair $\Gamma_x+\Gamma_z$ cancels.  Thus even an alternating
two-cycle may carry an odd in-tree defect.  This is a no-go for a proof using
only the first-response map: descendant continuation spaces and the other
defender replies remain available, so it is not a counterexample to the
linking theorem.

The obstruction is not a failure of the complete fixed-front prefix.  If one
allows the response tree to run until the old front closes, rather than
truncating after the first response, all of its front-star decorations do
affinely cancel.  The exact limitation is that the cancelling chain need not
carry the distinguished immediate CLOSE which leads into (B).

\begin{theorem}[complete fixed-front prefix contraction]
\label{thm:complete-fixed-front-prefix}
\PROVED{} Let $y$ be the clear front, let $S$ be the finite untouched set,
and fix an arbitrary deterministic attacker strategy until $y$ closes.  Stop
each branch at that close and write $R_\lambda\subseteq S$ for its untouched
remainder.  Then
\[
 0\in\operatorname{Aff}\{R_\lambda:\lambda\text{ is a defender response}\}.
\]
Equivalently, there is an odd response flow through the complete prefix whose
terminal front-star moment is zero:
\[
 \sum_\lambda\epsilon_\lambda=1,
 \qquad
 \sum_\lambda\epsilon_\lambda\,yR_\lambda=0.
\]
No graph, parity, or dummy hypothesis is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to exclude affine separation.  Fix a linear functional
$\ell$ on the subset space of $S$.  For $T\subseteq S$, let $A(T)$ mean
that some branchwise attacker policy, starting with the attacker to move,
makes $\ell(R)=1$ at every eventual $C_y$ leaf.  Let $D(T)$ denote the same
assertion with the defender to move.  Before $y$ closes, an OPEN merely
deletes its chosen element from $T$.  Hence the exact recurrences are
\begin{align*}
 A(T)&\iff \ell(T)=1\ \text{ or }\
       \bigl(\exists w\in T\;D(T-\{w\})\bigr),\\
 D(T)&\iff \ell(T)=1\ \text{ and }\
       \bigl(\forall v\in T\;A(T-\{v\})\bigr).
\end{align*}
The existential choices in different defender branches are independent, so
these formulas describe one deterministic history-dependent policy, not a
different game.

Simultaneous strong induction on $|T|$ gives
\[
 D(T)=\mathsf{false},\qquad A(T)\iff\ell(T)=1.
\]
Indeed all smaller $D$-terms vanish, which proves the formula for $A(T)$.
If $D(T)$ held, then $\ell(T)=\ell(T-\{v\})=1$ for every $v\in T$.
Writing $a_v=\ell(\{v\})$ gives $a_v=0$ for every $v\in T$, contradicting
$\ell(T)=1$; for $T=\varnothing$ the contradiction is immediate from
$\ell(0)=0$.  Thus no attacker policy can put every defender-response leaf
in an affine hyperplane $\ell=1$.  Finite-dimensional affine separation over
$\F_2$ proves the first assertion, and multiplication by the fixed symbol
$y$ proves the front-star form.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the distinguished prefix leaf cannot be prescribed]
\label{prop:fixed-front-relative-failure}
\PROVED{} Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-fixed-front-prefix} cannot in general be
strengthened by requiring coefficient one on the defender's immediate
$C_y$ leaf.  More precisely, let $\ell(S)=1$.  There is an attacker policy
for which every leaf below a non-CLOSE first defender move has
$\ell(R)=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After the defender has declined the immediate close, use the following
policy.  At an attacker node with $\ell(T)=0$, close $y$.  At an attacker
node with $\ell(T)=1$, open any $w\in T$ with
$\ell(\{w\})=1$.  The latter vertex exists.  Its removal hands the defender
a set of $\ell$-value zero.  If the defender closes, the leaf is zero; if the
defender opens a zero-weight vertex, the attacker closes; and if the defender
opens a unit-weight vertex, the remaining set has value one and the attacker
removes another unit-weight vertex.  In the last case another such vertex
exists because the defender started from an even number of unit weights.
The set strictly shrinks whenever play continues, so every non-immediate
leaf has value zero.

The immediate leaf has remainder $S$ and value one.  Applying $\ell$ shows
that its vector cannot be the XOR of an even number of the other leaf
vectors.  That is exactly the relation which an odd zero-moment chain with
coefficient one on the immediate leaf would require.
\end{proof}

The sharpest concrete instance takes $|S|$ odd and makes the attacker close
$y$ immediately after every defender OPEN.  The exit labels are $S$ and
$S-\{v\}$ for $v\in S$.  Their unique odd zero-moment chain is
\[
 \sum_{v\in S}(S-\{v\})=0,
\]
so it uses every one-OPEN exit and excludes the immediate close.  Taking $y$
adjacent to every member of $S$ supplies the separating functional in the
proposition.  The smallest (B$'$)-compatible instance has
$S=\{x,z,z'\}$ and edges
\[
 yx,yz,yz',xz,xz'.
\]
After the excluded $C_y$ exit and $O_x$, the front $x$ dominates the even set
$\{z,z'\}$, exactly the ko-protected state (B).

Thus the full prefix itself is affinely harmless, while the relative
contraction needed by the minimum-bad argument is genuinely decorated: it
must carry a specified odd-CLOSE branch and cancel that branch using
post-$C_y$ continuation spaces and/or still earlier defender siblings.  The
odd ungrounded component above is the first truncation at which that relative
defect is visible.

There is a complementary minimum which can be taken inside the actual scalar
strategy tree rather than over all public states.  This removes a subtle but
important quantifier error: a smaller residual-one state elsewhere in the
game graph says nothing about the fixed counterstrategy.

\begin{proposition}[strategy-tree charged-close extraction]
\label{prop:strategy-tree-neutral-tail}
\PROVED{} Suppose a fixed player has a strategy forcing terminal score one
from a score-zero state.  Among the score-zero nodes in that explicit
strategy tree, choose one minimizing the strictly descending game rank.  It
is controlled by the odd-seeking player, its prescribed move is a
charge-one CLOSE (hence a real CLOSE in the isolated-dummy game),
and, after translating the child's score sheet by one, the same physical
player has a continuation tree in which every legal or prescribed transition
has charge zero and the terminal score is zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A minimum node cannot be terminal.  If it were controlled by the defender,
then every legal child would belong to the strategy tree.  An OPEN child has
the same score and smaller rank whenever an untouched vertex remains; when
none remains, every legal CLOSE or pass also preserves score.  Either case
contradicts minimality.

At an attacker node the selected child has smaller rank.  Its score cannot
remain zero, so the selected move has charge one.  OPEN and pass have charge
zero, hence the move is a CLOSE.  In the isolated-dummy application it cannot
close the dummy, whose charge is always zero.  Every strict descendant of the child
has score one: a score-zero descendant would be a smaller selected/universal
node of the original strategy tree.  Translating all those score sheets by
one therefore makes every transition score-neutral and every terminal score
zero.  The membership relation in the explicit existential/universal
strategy tree, this proof, and the neutral translated subtree are
kernel-checked by
\texttt{oddStrategy\_extract\_minimalCloseNeutral} in
\path{formal/Ogdoad/Fifo.lean}.
\end{proof}

The strategy-tree extraction aligns exactly with the scalar minimum; no
additional all-punctures hypothesis is needed.

\begin{proposition}[dual-minimum neutral fan]
\label{prop:dual-minimum-neutral-fan}
\PROVED{} Fix a positional scalar counterstrategy $S$, its separator $B$, and
the lexicographically minimum bad node $h$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-descent}.  The score-one child of the selected odd
CLOSE at $h$, translated by one, carries a completely score-neutral strategy
for the same physical player.

In case (B), let $U=U_g$.  For every forced defender OPEN $O_z$, $z\in U$,
the selected $C_x$ child translates to a neutral singleton tail
\[
 U-\{z\},\qquad Q=(z),\qquad \mathsf{ko}=0,
\]
with the defender to move.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bprime-eulerian-tail}
  \deg_{U-\{z\}}(z)=0\qquad(z\in U),
\end{equation}
so the graph induced by the even set $U$ is Eulerian.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a reachable response-tree node $q$, let $\Gamma_q$ be its accumulated
vector charge and put
\[
 r_q=1+\langle B,\Gamma_q\rangle,
 \qquad g_q=\langle B,\Gamma_q\rangle.
\]
The dual recursion gives $(r_q,B)\in N_q$, while $g_q$ is the accumulated
scalar score.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-residual-complement}
 g_q+r_q=1;
\end{equation}
in particular a node is bad precisely when it is on score sheet zero in the
explicit odd strategy tree.

Let $k$ be the selected $C_x$ child of $h$.  The close is real, so the number
of live real vertices drops by one at $k$ and can never increase afterward.
Every node below $k$ therefore has fewer live real vertices than $h$.
Primary minimality makes all of them good, and
\eqref{eq:score-residual-complement} puts all of them on score sheet one.
Translating those sheets by one makes every included transition neutral:
at attacker nodes retain $S$'s selected edge, at defender nodes retain all
legal edges, and terminal score one becomes zero.  This is the formal
interface kernel-checked by
\texttt{oddStrategy\_one\_subtree\_translates\_neutral}.

In case (B), Proposition~\ref{prop:minimum-bad-predecessor} says that every
$O_z$ child is itself minimum bad and selects the odd $C_x$.  Applying the
same argument to each child gives the displayed neutral singleton tails.
The defender may immediately close $z$ in each one; neutrality forces its
charge to vanish, proving \eqref{eq:bprime-eulerian-tail}.  This last
implication is independently kernel-checked by
\texttt{treeNeutral\_singletonTails\_eulerian}.
\end{proof}

This is a genuine strengthening of (B), but it does not contradict it: a cone
from $x$ over any even-order Eulerian graph has exactly these local parities.

Write $A=\{x\}\sqcup U_g=U_e$.  Minimum badness also excludes one apparent
escape from an off-spine
$O_v$ child of (B$'$).  If the attacker immediately closed $y$ oddly, put
$q=\deg_{A-\{v\}}(v)$.  The defender's immediate $C_v$ child has fewer live
real vertices than the selected minimum, so it is good and $q=1$.  For every
$w\in A-\{v\}$, the alternative $O_w$ reaches a bad attacker node of exactly
the selected minimum rank; its prescribed odd $C_v$ gives
$q+B(v,w)=1$.  Hence every $B(v,w)$ vanishes, contradicting
$q=\sum_wB(v,w)=1$.  Thus an off-spine policy cannot use an immediate odd
$C_y$.

The unconditional Eulerian conclusion also sharpens every first response.

\begin{proposition}[(B$'$) first responses cross the $y$-colour]
\label{prop:bprime-cross-colour}
\PROVED{} In case (B$'$), put $U=U_g$, $A=\{x\}\sqcup U$, and
$Y_v=B(y,v)$.  Thus $U$ is even and Eulerian, $x$ is universal on $U$, and
$\sum_{v\in A}Y_v=1$.  After any defender $O_v$ with $v\in U$, the attacker
cannot answer $C_y$.  Its first response is an OPEN $O_w$ for some
$w\in A-\{v\}$, and necessarily
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bprime-cross-colour}
 Y_w=1+Y_v.
\end{equation}

Unless
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bprime-cap-colour}
 Y_x=1,\qquad Y_v=0\quad(v\in U),
\end{equation}
the defender can choose $v\in U$ with $Y_v=Y_x$.  Then the forced response
$w$ lies in $U$ and has the opposite $y$-colour.  After the defender's
zero-charge $C_y$, the attacker faces the ordered front $(v,w)$.  Its
$C_v$ is zero exactly when $B(v,w)=1$; when $B(v,w)=0$, minimum badness
forces the attacker to continue OPENing.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After $O_v$, the charge of $C_y$ is $1+Y_v$.  If this is one, the immediate
odd-CLOSE argument above excludes the move.  If it is zero, $C_y$ reaches a
good defender singleton with front $v$.  Since $U$ is Eulerian and $x$ is
universal on $U$,
\[
 \deg_{A-\{v\}}(v)=\deg_A(v)=1.
\]
The defender's legal $C_v$ would therefore create a bad node with fewer live
real vertices than $h$, again impossible.  Thus the attacker must OPEN some
$w$.

If $Y_w=Y_v$, the defender's $C_y$ has charge one and reaches a bad attacker
state with queue $(v,w)$ at exactly the lexicographic rank of $h$.  When
$w\in U$, both fronts have charge $1+B(v,w)$ and are balanced, contrary to
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-descent}.  When $w=x$, the front $v$ has charge
$\deg_A(v)+B(v,x)=0$, whereas a minimum bad attacker node must select an odd
CLOSE.  Both cases are impossible, proving
\eqref{eq:bprime-cross-colour}.

Write $t=Y_x$.  If \eqref{eq:bprime-cap-colour} fails, there is a
$v\in U$ with $Y_v=t$: for $t=1$ this follows from failure of the displayed
exception, while for $t=0$ it follows because the even set $U$ cannot have
all $Y_v=1$ and odd total $\sum_UY_v=1$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:bprime-cross-colour} then excludes $w=x$, so $w\in U$.
The intervening $C_y$ has charge $1+Y_v+Y_w=0$.  At the resulting front,
\[
 \deg_{A-\{v,w\}}(v)=\deg_A(v)+B(v,w)=1+B(v,w).
\]
If this charge were one, selecting $C_v$ would create a smaller bad node;
therefore that branch must continue with an OPEN.  This proves the final
assertion.
\end{proof}

The exceptional colour class \eqref{eq:bprime-cap-colour} is precisely the
sharp cap in which $y$ has sole live neighbour $x$.  The following example
shows why even the strengthened conclusions do not close that ancestry.

\begin{proposition}[sharp neutral (B$'$) cap]
\label{prop:bprime-neutral-cap}
\PROVED{} A complete family of strategy-tree neutral tails, Eulerian remainder
\eqref{eq:bprime-eulerian-tail}, and exclusion of an immediate off-spine odd
$C_y$ are jointly consistent.  Take
\[
 A=\{x,a,b\},\qquad Q_e=(y),\qquad
 E(B|_{\{y,x,a,b\}})=\{yx,xa,xb\}.
\]
The selected branch $C_y,O_x,O_z,C_x$, for $z=a$ or $b$, has charges
$1,0,0,1$ and then a neutral tail.  On every off-spine defender child the
attacker has one strategy all of whose future charges are zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After $O_x$, play $C_y$, whose charge is zero.  If the defender closes $x$,
its charge is two; if it opens one of $a,b$, open the other before $x$ can
score.  After $O_a$, play $O_x$.  The defender may now close $y$, at charge
zero because only $b$ is untouched, or open $b$; in the first case open $b$.
The $O_b$ branch is symmetric.  Once the untouched set is empty all remaining
charges vanish.

The cap is compatible with an actual odd prefix, not merely with isolated
degree equations.  Adjoin an already processed real vertex $p$ and the
isolated dummy $d$, add the single edge $py$, and use
\[
 O_p,O_d,C_p,O_y,C_d.
\]
This legal prefix reaches $e$, charges one at $C_p$, and closes $p,d$.
The displayed cap preserves that score on every leaf.  Its selected residual
bits are
\[
 0\xrightarrow{C_y,1}1\xrightarrow{O_x,0}1
  \xrightarrow{O_z,0}1\xrightarrow{C_x,1}0,
\]
while every off-spine subtree remains on residual zero.  Thus the example
meets the complete three-event local minimum and neutral-tail hypotheses.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:bprime-neutral-cap} is conditioned: at the initial root
the defender may answer $O_p$ by opening a real vertex instead of $d$.
Accordingly it is not a counterexample to linking.  It proves that the
remaining contradiction must use those still earlier root-to-$e$ siblings;
neither the three-event (B$'$) cap nor scalar neutrality below it contains
enough incidence information.

For the cap's four-real core graph, the necessary earlier response can be given
uniformly rather than found by search.

\begin{proposition}[star plus isolates, both seats]
\label{prop:star-root}
\PROVED{} Let $B$ be a star with center $x$ and any finite leaf set $L$,
together with a nonempty finite set $I$ of isolated vertices, one designated
as the dummy $d$.  From the empty root, the
zero-seeking player has a strategy from either seat under which every realized
CLOSE has charge zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Once $x$ is open, let $k$ be the number of neutral queued vertices ahead of
$x$ (leaves or isolates), let $m$ be the number of untouched star leaves,
and let $i$ be the number of untouched isolates.  The only move which can
carry charge is $C_x$, with charge $m$.  Use the mutual star
induction established in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:bad-open-fan}:
at a controller turn,
close $x$ when $k=0$ and $m$ is even, open a leaf when $m>0$ and either
$k>0$ or $m$ is odd, and close a neutral prefront when $k>0,m=0$.
At an opponent turn the same induction is valid whenever $k>0$ or $m$ is
even.  An opponent OPEN or prefront CLOSE strictly lowers
\[
 k+m+i,
\]
and an available $C_x$ has even charge.  The ko-set singleton-$x$ case is
entered only through the root prescriptions below; its forced OPEN lowers the
same rank, while the terminal singleton pass/drain has zero charge.

If the controller moves first, open $x$.  Ko forces the opponent to open a
leaf or an isolate; the resulting controller state enters the unconditional half of
the induction.  If the controller moves second, answer an initial $O_x$ by
opening a leaf when $|L|$ is odd and by opening any isolate when $|L|$ is even;
answer an initial leaf or isolate OPEN by $O_x$.  The resulting opponent state
has either even $m$ or positive $k$, so again lies in
the induction domain.  (When $L$ is empty the first prescription uses $d$.)
Before $x$ opens no edge can score, and afterward the induction makes $C_x$
even.  Hence every CLOSE has charge zero.
\end{proof}

In particular the four-real star $E=\{yx,xa,xb\}$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:bprime-neutral-cap}, with leaves $\{y,a,b\}$, is a
proved both-seat root class after adjoining $d$.  This does not include the
separate processed vertex $p$ and edge $py$ used only to exhibit a reachable
odd prefix.  What remains is not to defeat the isolated core cap again, but to
show that extra ancestry edges which sustain its earlier sibling branches
necessarily contribute the missing generators to $M_h$.

They do force one complete coordinate block.  This is the first genuinely
global relative incidence statement in the (B$'$) analysis.

\begin{proposition}[relative live-star incidence]
\label{prop:relative-live-star-incidence}
\PROVED{} Retain the notation of (B$'$), let $h=h_s$ be the distinguished
minimum child of the ko fan at $g$, and let $h_z$ be its child after the
defender OPEN $O_z$, $z\in U$.  Put $A=\{x\}\sqcup U$ and let
$P_y$ project an edge moment to the coordinates $\{ya:a\in A\}$, identified
with $\F_2^A$.

For each $z$, choose a terminal representative $n_z$ below the neutral tail
following the prescribed $C_x$.  If $r\ne s$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bprime-all-but-one-fan}
 d_r:=\sum_{z\ne r}b_{h_z}
 =\Gamma_e+yA+xr+\sum_{z\ne r}n_z
 \in b_h+M_h,
 \qquad P_y(d_r)=A.
\end{equation}
Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bprime-live-star-incidence}
 A\in P_y(M_h).
\end{equation}
Consequently, after choosing $m_y\in M_h$ with $P_y(m_y)=A$, the class
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bprime-away-schur}
 \omega_r=[d_r+m_y]\in
 \ker(P_y)/(M_h\cap\ker(P_y))
\end{equation}
is independent of $m_y$ and vanishes exactly when $b_h\in M_h$.
Thus the remaining (B$'$) obstruction is strictly away from the live
$y$-star.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The path from $e$ to $h_z$ first closes $y$ against $A$, then closes $x$
against $U-\{z\}$.  At $e$ every vertex of $A$ is still untouched and $y$
has not closed, so $P_y(\Gamma_e)=0$; no later event after $C_y$ contains a
$y$-coordinate.  Hence
\[
 b_{h_z}=\Gamma_e+yA+x(U-\{z\})+n_z.
\]
Give coefficient one to every ko child except $h_r$.  Since $|U|$ is even,
this is an odd child flow; it contains $h_s$ and uses an even number of the
other off-spine children.  It therefore represents the relative coset
$b_h+M_h$.  Summing the displayed formula and using
$\sum_{z\ne r}x(U-\{z\})=xr$ proves
\eqref{eq:bprime-all-but-one-fan}.

Suppose that \eqref{eq:bprime-live-star-incidence} fails.  Choose a linear
functional $\ell$ on $\F_2^A$ which annihilates $P_y(M_h)$ and satisfies
$\ell(A)=1$.  Every continuation space $W_q$ at the relative-spine frontier
lies in $M_h$, so $\ell P_y$ is constant on the terminal affine set below
$q$.  The excluded ko child $h_r$ is an off-spine reference with
$P_y(b_{h_r})=A$.  For every other off-spine frontier state $q$,
$b_q+b_{h_r}\in E_{\rm off}(h)\subseteq M_h$, and therefore
\[
 \ell P_y(b_q)=\ell(A)=1.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:bprime-all-but-one-fan} gives the same value at the
distinguished state $h$.  Since this frontier meets every terminal branch,
every terminal history in the fixed attacker response tree has
$\ell P_y(D)=1$.

Replay that history-indexed policy on the support graph whose only real edges
are $ya$ for which $\ell(a)=1$; all other real vertices remain isolated,
including the designated dummy.  Legality is graph-independent, so the same
response-history tree is obtained, and its terminal scalar score is always
one.  But the new graph is a star plus isolates.  Proposition~\ref{prop:star-root}
gives the opposing player an all-zero-charge strategy from the same seat,
a contradiction.  This proves \eqref{eq:bprime-live-star-incidence}.

Finally $d_r+m_y$ has zero $P_y$-projection.  Two choices of $m_y$ differ by
an element of $M_h\cap\ker(P_y)$, so \eqref{eq:bprime-away-schur} is
well-defined.  It vanishes precisely when $d_r\in M_h$, which, because
$d_r\in b_h+M_h$, is equivalent to $b_h\in M_h$.
\end{proof}

The complete first-response forest still cannot kill
\eqref{eq:bprime-away-schur}.  If an $e\to O_v$ branch first selects
$O_{w_v}$ and the defender then closes $y$, its live-star column is
$v+w_v$.  Every such column has even coordinate augmentation, whereas $A$
has odd augmentation.  Hence an even combination of these truncated branches
cannot produce $A$: the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-live-star-incidence} necessarily uses deeper
continuation directions or genuinely earlier defender siblings.

The next marginal is only two-dimensional, but it is genuinely relative.
Put
\[
 K_y=\ker P_y,\qquad M_0=M_h\cap K_y,
\]
choose $m_*\in M_h$ with $P_y(m_*)=A$, and let $P_x$ project to the live
coordinates $\{xu:u\in U\}$.  Since $\Gamma_e$ has no live $A$--$A$
coordinate and every $n_z$ begins after $x$ closes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bprime-second-star}
 P_x(d_r)=e_r,qquad
 \xi_r=[e_r+P_x(m_*)]\in\F_2^U/P_x(M_0).
\end{equation}
For distinct off-spine ko siblings $z,w\ne s$,
\[
 b_{h_z}+b_{h_w}\in M_0,qquad
 P_x(b_{h_z}+b_{h_w})=e_z+e_w.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bprime-even-x-star}
 \operatorname{Even}(U-\{s\})\subseteq P_x(M_0).
\end{equation}
Every dual functional on the quotient in
\eqref{eq:bprime-second-star} is therefore constant, say $t$, on
$U-\{s\}$, with one independent value $b$ at $s$.

This two-bit description respects the full relative space, not only its
projection.  If such a functional $\psi$ separates $\xi_r$, then
$\psi P_x$ annihilates $M_0$, so its restriction to $M_h$ factors through
$P_y(M_h)$.  Extend that factor to a functional $\lambda$ on the whole
$y$-star space.  Then
\[
 B_*=\psi P_x+\lambda P_y\in M_h^\perp,
 \qquad B_*(b_h)=1.
\]
If $c$ is its common value on every off-spine frontier cylinder, comparison
with any ko sibling gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bprime-two-bit-colour}
 c=1+b+t.
\end{equation}
When $b=t$, separation forces $b=t=1$: this is the global all-$U$ $x$-star,
or universal-cone colour.  When $b\ne t$, the distinguished cylinder has
value one and every off-spine cylinder value zero, so the root replay used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-live-star-incidence} gives no contradiction.

Equivalently, with
\[
 \bar X=\F_2^U/\operatorname{Even}(U-\{s\}),\qquad
 J=(P_y,\bar P_x),
\]
the next joint incidence target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bprime-joint-two-bit}
 (A,\bar e_r)\in J(M_h).
\end{equation}
The live-star theorem proves only its first marginal; the neutral tails map
to $(0,0)$ and ko-sibling differences only to $(0,\operatorname{Even})$.
Thus repeated marginal star elimination does not descend to a smaller board.
An earlier sibling carrying the $y$-correction $A$ together with the selected
$x$-bit $\delta_s$ is exactly the missing second-stage coefficient.  Even
after \eqref{eq:bprime-joint-two-bit}, coordinates away from both stars would
still have to be contracted.

This joint coefficient is not forced by the response-tree shape or the local
(B$'$) neutral-cap data, even when its two-colour support graph is a solved
root class.

\begin{proposition}[the joint coefficient is not tree-local]
\label{prop:bprime-joint-not-local}
\PROVED{} There is a legal fixed attacker response tree with a selected
(B$'$) spine and neutral punctured tails for which
\[
 b_h\notin M_h.
\]
The separating support consists of one edge, hence is matching plus isolates
and has a both-seat zero strategy.  Thus root safety of the support graph does
not imply \eqref{eq:bprime-joint-two-bit}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take real vertices $p,q,y,x,s,r$ and isolated dummy $d$.  Put the attacker on
the odd positions of the selected spine
\[
 O_p,O_q,C_p,O_d,C_q,O_y,C_d,C_y,O_x,O_s,C_x.
\]
This word is legal: the first and penultimate OPENs set ko, the following
defender OPEN clears it, and every displayed CLOSE is then the FIFO front.
At $e$ the queue is $(y)$ and $A=\{x,s,r\}$; the distinguished ko child opens
$s$, and $C_x$ occurs while $r$ is untouched.

Use the single edge $xr$ as a linear functional.  Below the distinguished
cylinder, $x$ and $r$ are disjoint, so every terminal has value one.  After
the first defender deviation, let the attacker force them to overlap.  If
the deviation opens one of $x,r$, open the other immediately.  If neither is
open, open $x$ and then open $r$ at the next attacker turn unless the defender
has already done so.  The intervening defender cannot close $x$: if $x$ was
opened onto an empty queue, ko forbids the close, and otherwise an older FIFO
front precedes it.  Hence every off-spine terminal has $xr$-value zero.

It follows that $xr$ annihilates every continuation-difference space $W_q$
and every even off-spine sum, while it pairs to one with $b_h$.  Therefore it
annihilates $M_h$ and separates $b_h$ from it.

For completeness, on the local separator
\[
 B_0=py+yx+xs+xr
\]
the selected closes $C_p,C_y,C_x$ have charges $1,1,1$.  At the cap, $y$ has
sole live neighbour $x$, while $x$ is universal over the even edgeless set
$\{s,r\}$; either ko child has a neutral singleton tail.  What fails is the
global hypothesis: this history policy does not force odd score under $B_0$
on every earlier defender sibling.  Root safety chooses such a zero
off-spine cylinder; it does not furnish a relative chain carrying $h$.
\end{proof}

Consequently any valid form of \eqref{eq:bprime-joint-two-bit} must use the
original separator's all-terminal-one Bellman constraint beyond the extracted
minimum normal form.  Conditioned on an actual global counterstrategy and its
minimum, proving that coefficient is simply the remaining (B$'$) relative
contraction problem; presenting it as a boundary-potential lemma would not
reduce the obligation.  Case (A) remains a separate relative contraction.

The first earlier sibling already exposes why ordinary smaller-board
induction does not do this.  In the genuine degree-one ancestry, consider
the attacker state
\[
 Q=(p,y),\qquad U=\{d\}\sqcup\{x\}\sqcup H,
\]
where $p$ has unique active neighbour $y$, $y$ has unique neighbour $x$ in
$\{x\}\sqcup H$, $x$ is adjacent to every vertex of the even set $H$, and
$d$ is isolated.  On the attacker branch $C_p$, the close has charge zero.
If the defender chooses $O_x$, the next attacker fan reduces exactly as
follows:
\begin{align*}
 C_y&\xrightarrow{\ C_x\ }
       (Q=(),\ U=\{d\}\sqcup H),\\
 O_z\ (z\in H)&\xrightarrow{\ C_y\ }
       (Q=(x,z),\ U=\{d\}\sqcup(H-\{z\})),\\
 O_d&\xrightarrow{\ C_y\ }
       (Q=(x,d),\ U=H).
\end{align*}
Every displayed charge is zero.  The first line is a smaller isolated-dummy
root.  The last is not: closing $x,d$ exposes the empty no-dummy game on the
even graph $H$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-minimum-neutral-fan} makes $H$
Eulerian and gives a neutral strategy from every defender singleton
$S_z^H$; in the notation of
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-full-fan}, it proves $P(H)$.  That proposition
reduces exclusion of an odd empty-root strategy to the single possibility of
a universal full row
\[
 Q_{xy}(H)=\F_2\qquad(y\ne x).
\]
This is still not enough by itself.  At $Q=(x,d),U=H$, the attacker may OPEN
vertices in pairs before choosing the stopping move $C_x$.  Since $x$ is
universal and the untouched set stays even after each response pair, every
stop $C_x,C_d$ is neutral and exposes the drafted $H$ checkpoint.  Hence the
exact endpoint assertion is an online draft-and-stop lift: after every attack
$O_z$ the defender must choose an $O_w$ so that every possible later stopping
checkpoint is safe.  Ordinary safety of the empty root $T(H)$ does not supply
this online choice; a safe drafted checkpoint can require a CLOSE as its only
safe reply to an OPEN, while that CLOSE is unavailable behind $x,d$.

Thus neither Eulerianity nor the bare even-board root theorem makes the
endpoint elementary.  Theorem~\ref{thm:euler-complete} shows that the Euler
ordered-pair form is already complete, and the online draft lift is the
strategy-relative strengthening required here.  This is only the
$C_p,O_x$ branch, not a proof that $O_x$ handles every earlier sibling.  It
identifies the exact dummy/rotated-queue endpoint which the offset ladder must
couple to the short (B$'$) cap.

The static parity-cell certificate, when it exists, closes this stronger
online endpoint as well as the empty root.

\begin{proposition}[parity cells close the universal-dummy endpoint]
\label{prop:cells-universal-dummy}
\PROVED{} Let $H$ have an even-cross perfect pairing $\mathcal M$.  Adjoin a
vertex $x$ universal to $H$ and an isolated vertex $d$.  From the clear state
\[
 Q=(x,d),\qquad U=V(H),
\]
with the odd-seeking attacker to move and score zero, the defender has a
strategy under which every response round has total charge zero.  In
particular the attacker cannot force future charge one, even when it drafts an
arbitrary number of vertices before closing $x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
While $x$ is front, answer every attacker OPEN by opening its mate in
$\mathcal M$.  The untouched set remains a union of whole cells and the
drafted suffix is a concatenation of whole cells.  If the attacker closes
$x$, its charge is $|U|=0$ in $\F_2$; close the isolated $d$ in reply.  If all
cells are drafted first, the same two CLOSEs are forced and still neutral.

After $C_x,C_d$, both the queue and untouched set are unions of whole cells,
and the attacker moves.  Continue with the parity-cell strategy of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cells}: answer an OPEN by opening its mate and answer a
front CLOSE by closing the next, matched front.  When a cell $C$ closes, its
two charges sum to
\[
 \sum_{D\subseteq U} e_H(C,D)=0.
\]
Ko causes no exception, because an OPEN onto an empty queue is answered by
the mate OPEN.  Thus every response round is neutral through termination.
\end{proof}

There is an exact dual form of this obstruction.  It shows why deleting the
Euler front does not give an induction on smaller Euler graphs.

\begin{proposition}[boundary-charged front quotient]
\label{prop:boundary-charged-front}
\PROVED{} Let $L=\{f\}\sqcup A$, let $B$ be an Euler graph on $L$, put
$K=B|_A$, and define $p_i=B(fi)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-charged-front}
 p=\partial K,
 \qquad
 \langle B,z\rangle=\langle K,T_fz\rangle
 \quad(z\in E(L)).
\end{equation}
Thus front deletion replaces the Euler separator by the arbitrary graph $K$
with a ghost front $f$ whose CLOSE charge against $U\subseteq A$ is
$p(U)$.  Re-basing at a later real front $r$ updates the boundary to
\[
 p'_i=p_i+K(ri)=\partial(K-r)_i.
\]
The resulting ghost recursion closes only by retaining the neutral and
target force sets at arbitrary ordinary FIFO states of $K$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Eulerianity at $i\in A$ gives
\[
 p_i=B(fi)=\sum_{j\in A-\{i\}}K(ij)=(\partial K)_i.
\]
On expanding $\langle K,T_fz\rangle$, the coefficient of $z_{ij}$ is
$K(ij)=B(ij)$, while the coefficient of $z_{fi}$ is
$\sum_{j\ne i}K(ij)=p_i=B(fi)$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:boundary-charged-front}.  If $f$ closes against $U$, its star pairs
with $B$ as $p(U)$.  Deleting a subsequent front $r$ removes the $r$-row of
$K$ and toggles the ghost boundary by that row, giving the displayed update.

While the ghost remains front, a neutral player node is the disjunction of
the zero-charge ghost CLOSE into the corresponding ordinary opponent state
and all ghost-preserving OPEN children; at an opponent node the same clauses
are conjunctive.  For a target force set the CLOSE clause is translated by
$p(U)$, and OPEN clauses again take union at a player node and intersection at
an opponent node.  Closing the ghost therefore calls the ordinary recursion
at the same arbitrary ordered queue.  These equations are well founded in
the usual FIFO rank, but they retain the complete ordinary Bellman table; they
are not a smaller Euler-board hypothesis.
\end{proof}

The need for the full ordinary exit data is real, not a weakness of the
formulation.  On the graph with sole edge $02$, the ordinary state
\[
 U=\{2,3\},\qquad Q=(0,1),\qquad\mathsf{ko}=0
\]
is unsafe for the zero target: the attacker closes $0$, scores one, and
deletes the only edge which could change the score later.  Its empty root with
an isolated dummy is nevertheless safe.  Hence a blanket safety assertion on
the transition-closed quotient class is false.  Enlarging the charged-ghost
induction enough to describe every ordinary exit restores precisely the
continuation cosets in $M_h$; it does not bypass their correlated incidence.

There is a tempting sharper strategy-theoretic subtarget inside the scalar
part.  Say
that an attacker has a \emph{neutral tree} at a state when one selected move
at each attacker node and every legal move at each defender node preserve
zero charge all the way to a zero terminal sheet, as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:strategy-tree-neutral-tail}.

\TESTED{} One neutral tree is not exclusive even on an Euler graph.  On
vertices $0,\ldots,9$, let
\[
\begin{split}
E(H)=\{&01,02,03,04,05,07,08,09,12,13,14,15,16,18,19,\\
       &25,26,38,39,45,47,48,49,57,59,68,69,79,89\}.
\end{split}
\]
Its degree sequence is $(8,8,4,4,6,6,4,4,6,8)$, so $H$ is Eulerian.  At
the canonical attacker state
\[
 Q=(2,9),\qquad U=V(H)-\{2,9\},\qquad \mathsf{ko}=0,
\]
exact recursion gives both a neutral tree and a strategy forcing future
charge one for the same physical player.  The proposed exclusivity does hold
through order eight, so order ten is its first observed Euler failure.  Off
the Euler locus it fails already with four live vertices, queue $(0,1)$,
untouched $\{2,3\}$, and sole edge $02$.

The neutral singleton tree at $S_x^H$ does restrict to a neutral tree at every
$Q_{xy}(H)$, while a universal full row gives target-one strategies there.
The displayed witness shows that these two facts are not contradictory at one
ordered pair.  Any proof of the universal-full-fan kernel must use the
\emph{simultaneous family} of punctured neutral trees, or their correlated
continuation spaces, rather than determinacy or Eulerianity at an individual
pair.

The simultaneous family does retain one exact row-wise coherence which the
counterexample lacks.

\begin{proposition}[neutral row contraction]
\label{prop:neutral-row-contraction}
\PROVED{} Let $H$ be Eulerian and suppose the attacker has a neutral tree at
the defender singleton $S_x^H$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item the defender's $C_x$ child is a neutral attacker tree at the odd root
      $T(H-x)$;
\item for every $y\ne x$, the defender's $O_y$ child is a neutral attacker
      tree at $Q_{xy}(H)$; and
\item the selected first move of this $Q_{xy}$ tree is either $C_x$, in which
      case $B(x,y)=0$, or an OPEN $O_z$, in which case
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:neutral-row-colour}
       B(x,z)=B(x,y),
      \end{equation}
      and the defender's subsequent $C_x$ child is a neutral tree at
      $Q_{yz}(H-x)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At $S_x^H$ the opponent controls the node, so every legal move belongs to the
neutral tree.  Eulerianity makes $C_x$ neutral, and its child is exactly
$T(H-x)$.  Every $O_y$ is also legal and neutral, giving the second claim.

At $Q_{xy}$ the attacker controls the node.  Pass is not legal, so the
selected move is a CLOSE or an OPEN.  The charge of $C_x$ is
\[
 \deg_{H-\{x,y\}}(x)=B(x,y),
\]
because $x$ has even degree in $H$.  Hence a selected neutral $C_x$ forces
$B(x,y)=0$.  If the selected move is $O_z$, its child is defender-controlled,
so the legal $C_x$ reply belongs to the neutral tree.  Its charge is
\[
 \deg_{H-\{x,y,z\}}(x)=B(x,y)+B(x,z),
\]
which must vanish.  This proves \eqref{eq:neutral-row-colour}, and the child
state after $C_x$ is visibly $Q_{yz}(H-x)$.  The generic fact that every legal
opponent child of a neutral tree remains neutral is kernel-checked by
\texttt{TreeNeutralWins.answer\_child}.
\end{proof}

Thus a full punctured family supplies neutral odd roots $T(H-x)$ and a
same-$x$-colour contraction in every ordered row.  What remains open is to
combine these independently selected row contractions with a hypothetical
target-one full row.  The order-ten pairwise flex witness shows why one row
cannot be detached from that simultaneous convolution.

The still weaker proposal that just one neutral odd root forces a parity-cell
completion is false.  \TESTED{} Let $H$ be the fourteen-vertex Euler graph
with edges
\[
\begin{split}
\{&02,03,0\,10,0\,13,12,1\,13,24,29,3\,13,46,58,5\,11,69,\\
  &7\,10,7\,13,8\,13,9\,12,9\,13,10\,11,10\,12\}.
\end{split}
\]
Exact recursion gives both a neutral tree at $T(H-0)$ and a neutral singleton
tree at $S_2^H$, but exact matching enumeration gives no even-cross pairing
of $H$.  The full family does detect the obstruction: the singleton $S_0^H$
is not neutral, and a minimum-height refutation begins with the opponent move
$O_4$.  Thus neither the $C_x$ child nor even one complete singleton tree can
be promoted to a static cell certificate; simultaneity across all punctures
is essential.

Adding two isolates to this same $H$ gives a sharp unresolved stress test for
the simultaneous conjecture.  Proposition~\ref{prop:cells-even-isolates}
proves that the sixteen-vertex extension still has no parity-cell pairing.
\TESTED{} Exact bounded neutral recursion finds no opponent refutation at any
of its sixteen singleton roots through depth twelve, and selected formerly
bad, formerly good, and isolated roots survive through depth fifteen.  Full
recursion exceeded the available state memory, so this is neither a neutral
strategy certificate nor a counterexample; it records that shallow local
obstructions do not settle the all-punctures implication.

The full family also supplies exactly the coherent short strategy needed by
the offset ladder.  Fix a hypothetical universal target-one row $x$.  The
$C_x$ child of the neutral tree at $S_x^H$ is a neutral attacker tree
$N_0$ at $T(H-x)$.  Its first move is some $O_y$.  After that move the short
state is
\[
 Q=(y),\quad U=H-\{x,y\},\quad \mathsf{ko}=1,
 \quad\hbox{defender to move},
\]
whereas the target-one row supplies a long attacker strategy at
\[
 Q=(x,y),\quad U=H-\{x,y\},\quad \mathsf{ko}=0,
 \quad\hbox{attacker to move}.
\]
These are precisely the opposite-mover one-front-offset states of
Proposition~\ref{prop:one-front-offset-ladder}, but they begin on its
singleton ko wall rather than in its clear interior.  If the long target-one
strategy first selects an OPEN $O_z$, the common OPEN repairs that wall and
enters the ordinary recurrence.  A selected initial $C_x$ has no simultaneous
short mirror, but after it the two copies have the same queue and untouched
set and differ only in ko.  Any common legal defender OPEN $O_z$ then
reconverges them diagonally, with scalar prefix difference $B(x,y)$; when no
such $z$ exists the terminal wall drain is immediate.  Thus this branch lands
directly in the same-state continuation-flex problem below.  Once in the
clear recurrence, a paired
OPEN contributes the token edge $xz$, crossed CLOSEs change the token without
changing the corrected offset moment, and a token CLOSE in the long copy
diagonalizes the states.  Eulerianity gives
\[
 \sum_{z\ne x}B(x,z)=0.
\]
The remaining incidence problem is causal rather than scalar: the pruned
coupled ladder need not use every $z$ once, and no proof yet forces its
selected token--OPEN edges to have even $B$-sum.  Establishing that relative
incidence, with the short neutral policy retained, would exclude the universal
full row without invoking the false pairwise exclusivity.

There is a useful exact combinatorial form of one coupled trunk.  Start with a
formal seed edge $xy$.  Vectorially
\[
 xU+xy=x(H-x)=\delta_H(\{x\}),
\]
so the seed and initial correction agree only modulo the cut space, and hence
have the same pairing with the Euler edge set $B$.  Whenever a previously untouched vertex $z$ is
OPENed in both copies while the long-only offset token is $a$, record the
parent edge $az$.  Crossed CLOSEs merely replace the token by the next short
front and record no edge.  Every subsequently encountered vertex is therefore
attached once to an already encountered vertex.  The seed and recorded edges
form a rooted ancestry tree $T$ on the vertices opened by that trunk (a
spanning tree of $H$ only when the trunk drains every untouched vertex).  The
offset recurrence gives, at the first diagonal or drain checkpoint, the exact
scalar holonomy

\begin{equation}\label{eq:offset-tree-holonomy}
 \langle B,\Delta_{\mathrm{checkpoint}}\rangle=\langle B,T\rangle .
\end{equation}

Thus a neutral short strategy and a hypothetical universal target-one row do
not produce a parity-forbidden object from one traversal: an Euler graph can
meet one rooted tree oddly.  Moreover two fixed deterministic strategy trees
select only one cross-fed trunk.  If the long strategy closes the offset
token, the copies diagonalize at a generally nonterminal public state; their
two same-player continuations may diverge again, so their different terminal
targets do not imply that the checkpoint tree pairs oddly.  This is exactly
the flexibility exhibited by the order-ten witness.

To obtain the required global cancellation one must first define a recursive
comparison object which continues past every diagonal and also promotes the
unused defender children to new seeds.  The hoped-for exchange conclusion is
an odd formal family whose symmetric difference is cut-valued; Eulerianity
would then annihilate its scalar pairing.  The ancestry-tree construction and
checkpoint holonomy identity are the proved one-front recurrence in
combinatorial dress.  Existence of that recursive response family, and of the
required odd cut-valued sum inside it, are both \OPEN{}.

Even one spanning tree for every seed would not make the exchange conclusion
formal.  On $V=\{0,1,2,3\}$ take the Euler edge set
$B=\{01,12,23,03\}$, root $x=0$, and
\[
 T_1=\{01,12,23\},\qquad
 T_2=\{02,01,13\},\qquad
 T_3=\{03,02,13\}.
\]
Each $T_y$ is a spanning tree containing its seed $0y$, and each has
$\langle B,T_y\rangle=1$.  Therefore every odd subfamily still pairs to one
and cannot be cut-valued; explicitly
$T_1+T_2+T_3=\{12,23,03\}$.  The missing exchange closure must consequently
come from the actual game-semantic response product, not from rooted-tree
incidence alone.

Nor does unrestricted recursion after a diagonal create a smaller automatic
invariant.  If the two same-state continuation trees have odd terminal cosets
$A_\sigma=a_\sigma+W_\sigma$ and
$A_\tau=a_\tau+W_\tau$, their paired odd-flow differences are exactly
\[
 A_\sigma+A_\tau
 =(a_\sigma+a_\tau)+(W_\sigma+W_\tau).
\]
After quotienting by cuts, this coset meets zero exactly when the two projected
continuation cosets intersect.  That is the decorated-frontier common-coset
problem again.  Operationally, two different selected moves at the diagonal
create either another CLOSE--OPEN front defect or an unrestricted pair of
different untouched sets.  Closing the product under all such restarts thus
recovers the original response-tree affine problem.  The offset tree alone
cannot prove the theorem; the earlier off-spine sibling spaces in $M_h$ must
enter essentially.

This is a genuine monotone reduction, but not yet the theorem.  Case (A)
cannot be extended backwards into a corridor of odd CLOSE pairs by local
reasoning.  For example, take $U=\{a,d\}$, $Q=(z,y,x)$, with $d$ isolated
and the only displayed separator edges $xa,ya$.  There is a residual policy
with the exact dual pattern
\[
 0\xrightarrow{C_z,\,0}0\xrightarrow{C_y,\,1}1
  \xrightarrow{C_x,\,1}0:
\]
after the defender alternatives $O_a$ and $O_d$, the attacker can respectively
use the now-even $C_y$ and the zero OPEN $O_a$, and all later charges vanish.
Thus the unresolved global task has two sharply related terminal patterns.
The odd subcase of (A) and all of (B) begin at a good defender singleton whose
odd close creates badness; (B) then carries that bad bit through one
empty-queue OPEN before the ko-protected domination fan.  The remaining even
subcase of (A) can occur only with an even untouched set.  One must exclude
these patterns by coupling earlier bad siblings; neither endpoint can be
discarded from its public state alone.

There is a sharper target when the separating graph is Eulerian.  It removes
exactly the directions which no Euler adjacency vector can see.

\begin{theorem}[Euler quotient separation]
\label{thm:euler-quotient}
Fix a deterministic attacker strategy $S$ on a vertex set $V$, and
write its response affine set as
\[
 \mathcal A_S=\Aff\{D(h):h\in\mathcal H_S\}=a+W
 \subseteq E(K_V).
\]
There is no Eulerian graph on $V$ for which $S$ forces odd score if and only
if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cut-affine-target}
 \mathcal A_S\cap\Cut(V)\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad a\in W+\Cut(V).
\end{equation}
After choosing any front $f$, this is equivalently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-affine-target}
 0\in\Aff\{T_fD(h):h\in\mathcal H_S\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under the edge-space dot product, Eulerian graphs are the cycle space
$Z(K_V)$, and
\[
 Z(K_V)=\Cut(V)^\perp.
\]
If \eqref{eq:cut-affine-target} holds, an Euler adjacency vector cannot pair
to one on every response score.  Conversely, if $a\notin W+\Cut(V)$, binary
linear separation supplies
\[
 A\in(W+\Cut(V))^\perp\subseteq Z(K_V),
 \qquad \langle A,a\rangle=1.
\]
This $A$ is the adjacency vector of an Eulerian simple graph, annihilates
$W$, and therefore pairs to one on all of $a+W$.  The final equivalence
follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:front-split}, whose kernel is exactly
$\Cut(V)$.
\end{proof}

Thus the Euler kernel does not require the graph-free target $0\in\mathcal
A_S$: a cut-valued odd response flow is enough.  This distinction is
substantial.  For even $|V|$ the bicycle space
$Z(K_V)\cap\Cut(V)$ has dimension $|V|-2$; demanding that response
differences span those invisible directions is strictly stronger than the
game.  The remaining problem is the quotient contraction
\eqref{eq:quotient-affine-target}, not full edge-space spanning.

\emph{Computational evidence.} The existing exhaustive graph census through eight real vertices
plus dummy, both attacker seats, implies \eqref{eq:affine-target} in those
dimensions by Theorem~\ref{thm:affine}.  Independent graph-free policy searches
also found no affine offset through eight real vertices; selected structured
policies at nine and ten real vertices likewise had zero offset.  These are
finite checks, not a proof of \eqref{eq:affine-target}.

\section{A solved parity-cell subclass}

The true/false-twin argument in the earlier draft can be weakened
substantially.

\begin{theorem}[parity-cell pairing]\label{thm:cells}
Suppose all vertices, including the dummy if present, admit a perfect
partition $\mathcal M$ into two-element cells such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cell-condition}
  e_G(A,B)=0\pmod2
  \qquad\text{for every distinct }A,B\in\mathcal M.
\end{equation}
Then the second player forces even flip parity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
When the first player opens one member of a cell $A$, the second opens its
mate.  When the first player closes the FIFO-front member of $A$, its mate is
the next queue front and the second closes it.  Thus untouched vertices are
always a union of whole cells, and the queue is a concatenation of whole cells.
Ko causes no exception because the forced second opening completes the first
cell.

When the two members $a,a'$ of $A$ close consecutively, their two flip bits sum
to
\[
  \deg_U(a)+\deg_U(a')
  =\sum_{B\subseteq U}e_G(A,B)=0
\]
by \eqref{eq:cell-condition}.  Every flip is contained in one such consecutive
pair, so the total parity is even.
\end{proof}

Twin pairs satisfy \eqref{eq:cell-condition}, but the condition only asks for
even cross-parity between cells; no automorphism is required.

\emph{Computational evidence.} This theorem is still not general.  Among the $1{,}044$
isomorphism classes on seven real vertices, $64$ boards obtained by adjoining
the isolated dummy have no such partition.  The first witness in the local
ordering has real edges
\[
  02,04,06,14,15,23.
\]
The general linking theorem nevertheless passes exact minimax on every one of
those boards.  Seidel switching cannot repair the pairing obstruction: between
two two-cells $A,B$, switching a set $S$ toggles
\[
 |A\cap S||B\setminus S|+|A\setminus S||B\cap S|
 \equiv ab+ab=0\pmod2.
\]

\section{FIFO gives a cut-space filtration}

Let $L\subseteq R$ be a live real set and let $f\in L$ be the FIFO front.  Let
$E(L)=\F_2^{\binom{L}{2}}$ and let
\[
  \Cut(L)=\Span\{s_L(x):x\in L\}.
\]
This is the cut space of the complete graph on $L$.

\begin{theorem}[front splitting]\label{thm:front-split}
There is a canonical direct sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:front-split}
  E(L)=\Cut(L)\oplus E(L\setminus\{f\}).
\end{equation}
The projection to the second summand is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Tf}
  (T_fz)_{ij}=z_{ij}+z_{fi}+z_{fj},
  \qquad i,j\neq f.
\end{equation}
It kills every live star and is the identity on the canonical
$E(L\setminus\{f\})$ summand.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $x=f$, the two incident coordinates in \eqref{eq:Tf} cancel; for
$x=i\neq f$, the $ij$ and $fi$ coordinates of $s_L(i)$ cancel.  Hence $T_f$
kills all generators of $\Cut(L)$.  If $z$ is supported away from $f$, then
$T_fz=z$.  Finally,
\[
 \dim\Cut(L)+\dim E(L\setminus\{f\})
 =(|L|-1)+\binom{|L|-1}{2}=\binom{|L|}{2},
\]
so the two subspaces are complementary and exhaust $E(L)$.
\end{proof}

There is a front-independent description of the same quotient.  Regard
$z\in E(L)$ as a simplicial $1$-cochain on the full simplex on $L$, and put
\[
  (\delta z)_{ijk}=z_{ij}+z_{ik}+z_{jk}.
\]
Then $\ker\delta=\Cut(L)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-graph-chart}
  (T_fz)_{ij}=(\delta z)_{fij}.
\end{equation}
Thus $T_f$ is the chart based at $f$ of the odd-triple two-graph of $z$;
graphically it is Seidel complementation at $f$ followed by deletion of $f$
\cite{mallows-sloane,limouzy}.  Equivalently, if $B$ is the alternating form
with Gram matrix $z$ and
\[
  E_0=\{x\in\F_2^L:\textstyle\sum_i x_i=0\},\qquad
  b_i^{(f)}=e_i+e_f,
\]
then $T_fz$ is the Gram matrix of $B|_{E_0}$ in the basis
$\{b_i^{(f)}:i\ne f\}$.  Changing the basepoint from $f$ to $g$ gives
\[
 b_f^{(g)}=b_g^{(f)},\qquad
 b_i^{(g)}=b_i^{(f)}+b_g^{(f)}.
\]
In particular, after restricting away from distinct $f,g$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T-absorption}
  T_g(T_fz)=T_gz.
\end{equation}
This absorption law says that earlier quotient gauges cancel exactly; it does
not say that their affine payoff offsets cancel.

While $f$ remains front, opens do not change the live set.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:live-star}, all their contributions lie in the current
$\Cut(L)$ layer.  Once FIFO deletes the real front $f$, every later contribution
is supported on $E(L\setminus\{f\})$.  Repeating the splitting along the forced
closure order gives a triangular decomposition of the complete play score.
This is the missing structural use of FIFO: the earlier scalar potential was
valid even if any queued vertex could close and therefore could not prove the
theorem.

The dummy needs a companion state in this induction.  Its star is zero, but
closing it does not shrink the real edge space.  A proof must use that zero
star to adjust affine augmentation and then transfer the odd response flow to
the unchanged real live set.  Treating the dummy as an ordinary deleted front
silently loses precisely the exceptional case.

\section{What the cone homotopy does and does not contract}

Fix a real front $f$ and a terminal continuation $\lambda$.  Let
$p_f(\lambda)\in\F_2^{L\setminus\{f\}}$ be the indicator of the real vertices
still untouched when $f$ closes, and let $W_f(\lambda)$ be the away-$f$
disjointness vector accumulated by the whole continuation.  Directly from the
definition of disjoint intervals,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:correlated-moment}
 D_h(\lambda)=\sum_i p_f(\lambda)_i\,fi+W_f(\lambda),
 \qquad
 T_fD_h(\lambda)=W_f(\lambda)+\delta p_f(\lambda),
\end{equation}
where $(\delta p)_{ij}=p_i+p_j$.  Hence for every formal response-leaf chain
$c$, of either augmentation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-cancellation}
 D_h(c)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p_f(c)=0\ \text{ and }\ W_f(c)=0.
\end{equation}
The two-graph chart therefore replaces the desired edge moment by a
\emph{joint} cut/continuation moment; it does not discard the correlation.

One half of that joint target does contract cleanly at the root.

\begin{lemma}[initial cut-moment contraction]\label{lem:cut-contract}
Suppose the attacker opens a real initial front $f$, so the isolated
dummy remains untouched and ko forbids the defender's immediate close.  For
every fixed attacker strategy up to the close of $f$,
\[
 0\in\Aff\{p_f(\lambda):\lambda\text{ is a compatible pre-close response}\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If zero were absent, separation would give weights $a_i\in\F_2$ on the
remaining real vertices such that the attacker forced
$a\mathbin{\cdot}p_f=1$.  Give the dummy weight zero, and let $s$ be the total
weight of the still-unselected vertices.  On the ko-forced first response, the
defender selects the dummy when $s=0$, and selects a weight-one real vertex
when $s=1$; either choice leaves $s=0$.  On every later response, close $f$
when $s=0$, and select a weight-one vertex when $s=1$.  If the attacker selects
weight zero, $s$ remains zero and the defender closes.  If the attacker selects
weight one from a zero total, the number of remaining weight-one vertices was
positive and even, so another remains and the defender restores $s=0$.
Whenever the attacker closes $f$, therefore, $a\mathbin{\cdot}p_f=0$, a
contradiction.
\end{proof}

The exact residue after this contraction is a Schur complement, rather than an
automatically exact spectral-sequence page.  Let $V_h=\F_2[\Lambda_h]$ at a
fixed real front $f$, extend $p_f$ and $W_f$ linearly, and suppose
$c_0\in V_h$ has
\[
 \operatorname{aug}(c_0)=1,
 \qquad p_f(c_0)=0.
\]
Put $K_f=\ker(\operatorname{aug},p_f)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schur-obstruction}
 \omega_f(S)=[W_f(c_0)]\in
 E(L-\{f\})/W_f(K_f)
\end{equation}
is independent of the chosen $c_0$, and vanishes exactly when $c_0$ can be
corrected to an odd zero-moment chain.  Indeed, any two choices differ by
$K_f$, while \eqref{eq:joint-cancellation} says that an admissible correction
$k\in K_f$ must solve $W_f(k)=W_f(c_0)$.  This is the honest first filtered
obstruction; attacker pruning gives no chain complex whose ambient simplicial
$\delta^2=0$ would make it vanish automatically.

The first phase-pivot differential is visible in one commuting real diamond.
If $x$ is front and $z$ untouched, the two orders OPEN--CLOSE and
CLOSE--OPEN have charge vectors
\[
 \Gamma_{OC}=s_x(U-\{z\}),
 \qquad \Gamma_{CO}=s_x(U),
 \qquad \Gamma_{OC}+\Gamma_{CO}=xz.
\]
Their $p_x$ values differ by $e_z$, and
$T_x(xz)=\delta e_z$ is the cut star of $z$ on the residual live set.
Successive matching shifts therefore create rooted-triangle holonomy.

Two exact four-real-vertex witnesses delimit this filtration.  First, the
compatible pair
\begin{align*}
h_1&=O_0O_1O_dC_0O_2C_1O_3C_dC_2C_3,\\
h_2&=O_0O_dO_1C_0O_3C_dC_1C_3O_2PC_2
\end{align*}
has equal $p_0=e_2+e_3$, but
\[
 D(h_1)=02+03+13,\qquad
 D(h_2)=02+03+12+23,\qquad
 W_0(h_1+h_2)=12+13+23.
\]
Thus the first surviving class may be the genuine triangle rather than a cut.
This order is minimal because after deleting $f$ a non-cut class requires at
least three real vertices.

More sharply, one deterministic policy admits three leaves
\begin{align*}
g_1&=O_0O_1O_2O_dC_0O_3C_1C_2C_dC_3,\\
g_2&=O_0O_2O_3O_dC_0O_1C_2C_3C_dC_1,\\
g_3&=O_0O_dO_2C_0O_1C_dC_2C_1O_3PC_3.
\end{align*}
Their respective $(p_0,W_0)$ values are
\[
 (e_3,0),\qquad(e_1,0),\qquad
 (e_1+e_3,13+23).
\]
The odd sum cancels $p_0$, and its residual $13+23$ pairs trivially with the
unique residual triangle $12+13+23$, so both the first cut and the triangle
quotient vanish; nevertheless its full moment is the nonzero next-front cut
$13+23$.  The three histories occupy different residual phases, so the
initial cut contraction cannot simply be reapplied to their sum as one child
state.  Vanishing of two ambient filtration layers is therefore a strict
false positive; the parity-resolved convolution must couple further siblings
across levels.

The scalar separation proof has a useful two-round refinement.  It repairs the
first explicit failure of degree-only pairing, although it still does not
iterate through an arbitrary FIFO prefix.

For a finite graph $H$, put
\[
 p_v=\deg_H(v)\pmod2,
 \qquad
 b_v=\sum_{u\sim_H v}p_u\pmod2,
 \qquad
 c(v)=(p_v,b_v).
\]
Thus $b_v$ is the parity of the odd-degree neighbours of $v$.

\begin{lemma}[two-bit handshake refinement]\label{lem:two-bit}
If $H$ has even order, every one of the four colour classes
$c^{-1}(i,j)$ has even cardinality.  In particular every vertex has a
distinct same-colour mate.

If $c(v)=c(w)$ and $A=\{v,w\}$, put
$r_x=e_H(x,A)\pmod2$ for $x\notin A$.  Then each of the four sets
\[
 \{x\notin A:(p_x,r_x)=(i,j)\},\qquad (i,j)\in\F_2^2,
\]
also has even cardinality.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $O=\{v:p_v=1\}$ and $E=V(H)\setminus O$.  Handshaking gives $|O|=0$
in $\F_2$.  Moreover
\[
 \sum_{v\in O}b_v=2e_H(O)=0,
\]
so both $b$-fibres in $O$ are even.  The cut $e_H(O,E)$ is even, since
\[
 e_H(O,E)=\sum_{v\in O}\deg_H(v)=|O|=0
\]
in $\F_2$.  Hence the $b=1$ fibre in $E$ is even; $|E|$ is even because
$|V(H)|$ and $|O|$ are even, so the remaining fibre is even as well.

For the second assertion, the four parity moments of the map
$x\mapsto(p_x,r_x)$ on $V(H)\setminus A$ vanish:
\[
 \sum 1=0,
 \quad
 \sum p_x=0,
 \quad
 \sum r_x=p_v+p_w=0,
 \quad
 \sum p_xr_x=b_v+b_w=0.
\]
The internal edge $vw$, when present, contributes
$p_v+p_w=0$ to the last two displayed identities.  The four fibre
indicators are the four polynomials
$(1+p)(1+r),(1+p)r,p(1+r),pr$ over $\F_2$; their sums therefore vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what the second bit buys]
Suppose an attacker opens $v$ and the defender opens a same-colour mate $w$.
If the attacker closes $v$, closing $w$ makes the two flip charges cancel.
If the attacker instead opens $x$, Lemma~\ref{lem:two-bit} supplies a $y$
with both $p_y=p_x$ and $e_H(y,A)=e_H(x,A)$.  Thus the new cell matches the
opening-charge parity and is orthogonal to the first FIFO cell.  The equality
$b_v=b_w$ is exactly the missing joint moment in the first degree-only
counterexample.

This is a two-round statement, not an induction: the new cell can split the
four balanced fibres unevenly.  A later response may have to close rather
than open, which is the correlated continuation obstruction in
Question~\ref{q:contraction}.
\end{remark}

\emph{Computational evidence.} Exact minimax on every graph isomorphism class through seven real
vertices plus the dummy gives a stronger root fact: whenever the same-colour
mate of Lemma~\ref{lem:two-bit} applies, \emph{every} such reply is winning
for the second-seat even player.  When the real graph has even order and the
attacker opens the unmatched dummy, every real second opening is winning
through six real vertices.  The first-seat even player also wins by opening
the dummy throughout the seven-vertex census.  The optional
\texttt{refinement} stage of \path{experiments/linking_game.py} reproduces
these checks.  They support the two-bit prefix but do not prove that its
balanced fibres survive later FIFO switches.

\section{Scalar response faces and the least counterexample}

It is useful to isolate exactly what an induction on a smaller even board
would have to prove.  Fix the player seeking odd parity and, for any game
state $h$, let $R(h)\subseteq\F_2$ be the set of future charge bits that this
attacker can force.  If a move of charge $c$ leads to $h'$, write
$c+R(h')=\{c+r:r\in R(h')\}$.  Backward induction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:force-recursion}
 R(h)=
 \begin{cases}
  \displaystyle\bigcup_m(c_m+R(h_m)),&h\text{ is an attacker node},\\[6pt]
  \displaystyle\bigcap_m(c_m+R(h_m)),&h\text{ is a defender node},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad R(\text{terminal})=\{0\}.
\end{equation}
Here a forced pass is the sole move from its state and has charge zero.
For a live vertex set $A$, use the following canonical roots:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
&Q&U&\text{mover / ko}\\ \hline
T(A)&()&A&\text{attacker / false}\\
K_x(A)&(x)&A-\{x\}&\text{defender / true}\\
S_x(A)&(x)&A-\{x\}&\text{defender / false}\\
Q_{xy}(A)&(x,y)&A-\{x,y\}&\text{attacker / false}.
\end{array}
\]
The same letters denote their force sets.  The boundary values are
$T(\varnothing)=K_x(\{x\})=\{0\}$.  Away from the empty-union boundary,
directly from
\eqref{eq:force-recursion},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-faces}
 T(A)=\bigcup_{x\in A}K_x(A)\quad(A\ne\varnothing),\qquad
 K_x(A)=\bigcap_{y\ne x}Q_{xy}(A)\quad(|A|\ge2),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-face}
 S_x(A)=K_x(A)\cap
 \bigl(\deg_{A-\{x\}}(x)+T(A-\{x\})\bigr).
\end{equation}
Thus the scalar even-board statement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:even-board}
 E(A):\qquad 1\notin T(A)\quad (|A|\text{ even}).
\end{equation}
It says that a first-seat odd attacker cannot force odd parity; by finite
determinacy, the second player can force even parity.

The four possible force sets have an exact Boolean form.  Write
$r_h(t)=1_{t\in R(h)}\in\F_2$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boolean-force-recursion}
 r_h(t)=
 \begin{cases}
  1+\displaystyle\prod_m\bigl(1+r_{h_m}(t+c_m)\bigr),
     &h\text{ an attacker node},\\[5pt]
  \displaystyle\prod_m r_{h_m}(t+c_m),
     &h\text{ a defender node}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boolean-canonical-faces}
 K_x(t)=\prod_{y\ne x}Q_{xy}(t),\qquad
 T(t)=1+\prod_x(1+K_x(t)),\qquad
 S_x(t)=K_x(t)T_{A-x}\bigl(t+\deg_{A-x}(x)\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[the universal-full-fan residual]
\label{prop:universal-full-fan}
For an even graph $H$, put
\[
 P(H):\qquad 0\in S_z^H(V)\quad(z\in V).
\]
Then
\[
 P(H)\iff
 \left\{
 \begin{array}{ll}
 0\in Q_{zy}(H)&(z\ne y),\\
 \deg_H(z)\in T(H-z)&(z\in V).
 \end{array}\right.
\]
If $P(H)$ and $1\in T(H)$, there is a vertex $x$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-full-row}
 Q_{xy}(H)=\F_2\qquad(y\ne x).
\end{equation}
Consequently $P(H)\Rightarrow T(H)=\{0\}$ is equivalent to excluding the
universal full row \eqref{eq:universal-full-row}.
If, in addition, $H$ has an even-cross perfect pairing as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:cells}, then $T(H)=\{0\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equations~\eqref{eq:S-face} and \eqref{eq:canonical-faces} give the first
equivalence.  If $1\in T(H)$, some $K_x$ contains $1$.  Under $P(H)$ the
same $K_x$ also contains zero, so it equals $\F_2$; its intersection formula
forces every $Q_{xy}$ in that row to equal $\F_2$.  Conversely such a row
gives $1\in K_x\subseteq T$.  Finally the parity-cell strategy forces even
score from the empty-queue second-seat root, so $1\notin T(H)$, while $P(H)$
already gives $0\in T(H)$.
\end{proof}

Eulerianity by itself cannot supply the pairing premise, for a dimension
reason rather than only by census.

\begin{proposition}[Euler graphs need not have parity cells]
\label{prop:euler-no-cells}
\PROVED{} There is an Euler graph on sixteen vertices with no even-cross
perfect pairing.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $V=\F_2^{16}$ with coordinate basis $e_1,\ldots,e_{16}$ and all-ones
vector $\mathbf 1$.  Euler graphs are exactly the alternating forms $B$ on
$V$ for which $B\mathbf 1=0$, hence exactly the alternating forms on the
fifteen-dimensional quotient $V/\langle\mathbf 1\rangle$.

Fix a perfect pairing $\pi$.  Its eight cell vectors $e_a+e_b$ span an
eight-dimensional space $L_\pi$ containing $\mathbf 1$, so their image
$\bar L_\pi$ in the quotient has dimension seven.  The pairing is even-cross
exactly when $\bar L_\pi$ is totally $B$-isotropic.  Restriction of
alternating forms to $\bar L_\pi$ is surjective, so this imposes
$\binom72=21$ independent linear equations.  Thus the fraction of Euler
forms admitting this fixed pairing is $2^{-21}$.

There are
\[
 \frac{16!}{2^8 8!}=2{,}027{,}025<2^{21}=2{,}097{,}152
\]
perfect pairings.  The union bound therefore leaves at least one Euler form
outside every pairing locus.  Its coordinate matrix is the required simple
Euler graph.
\end{proof}

The obstruction persists after adding neutral coordinates; static cells
cannot use isolated vertices as an unlimited repair resource.

\begin{proposition}[even isolated extension preserves parity cells]
\label{prop:cells-even-isolates}
\PROVED{} Let $I$ be an even set of new isolated vertices.  Then
$H\sqcup I$ has an even-cross perfect pairing if and only if $H$ does.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One direction pairs the vertices of $I$ among themselves.  Conversely,
consider an even-cross pairing of $H\sqcup I$.  The number of mixed cells
$\{d_i,a_i\}$, with $d_i\in I$ and $a_i\in H$, is even.  Two mixed cells
give
\[
 e(\{d_i,a_i\},\{d_j,a_j\})=B(a_i,a_j)=0,
\]
and a mixed cell against an old real cell $C$ gives $B(a_i,C)=0$.
Pair the mixed real partners $a_i$ arbitrarily among themselves and do the
same with the mixed isolates.  The new real cells are mutually cross-even
and cross-even to every unchanged real cell, while every new isolate cell is
trivial.  This produces an even-cross pairing of $H$.
\end{proof}

There is a useful exact flag form of the static certificate.  It says what
the neutral response trees would have to construct coherently, rather than
only one row at a time.

\begin{proposition}[isotropic pairing flag]
\label{prop:isotropic-pairing-flag}
\PROVED{} Let $B$ be an Euler graph on an even coordinate set $V$.  After
choosing disjoint cells $\{a_j,b_j\}$ for $j\leq i$, put
\[
 d_j=e_{a_j}+e_{b_j},\qquad
 D_i=\Span\{d_1,\ldots,d_i\},
\]
and let $S_i$ be the unpaired coordinates.  For each syndrome
$\lambda\in D_i^*$, write
\[
 C_\lambda=\{v\in S_i:B(-,v)|_{D_i}=\lambda\}.
\]
Assume that $D_i$ is $B$-isotropic and every $C_\lambda$ has even
cardinality.  Define the joint signature
\[
 \sigma_i(v)=
 \left(B(-,v)|_{D_i},
       \bigl(B(v,\mathbf 1_{C_\lambda})\bigr)_\lambda\right).
\]
Then $a,b\in S_i$ may be adjoined as the next cell while preserving both
invariants if and only if
\[
 \sigma_i(a)=\sigma_i(b).
\]
Consequently an even-cross perfect pairing is equivalent to a complete
sequence of equal-signature choices.  Every ordering of the cells of an
even-cross pairing gives such a sequence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equality of the first signature coordinate says that
$d=e_a+e_b$ lies in $D_i^\perp$, so $D_i+\langle d\rangle$ remains
isotropic.  Inside an old fibre $C_\lambda$, the new syndrome bit is
$B(d,v)$.  The parity of its one-subfibre is
\[
 B(d,\mathbf 1_{C_\lambda})
 =B(a,\mathbf 1_{C_\lambda})+B(b,\mathbf 1_{C_\lambda}),
\]
which vanishes exactly when the second signature coordinates agree.  Removing
$a,b$ does not alter that parity: they lie in the same old fibre and
$B(d,a)=B(a,b)=B(d,b)$.  The zero-subfibre is then even as well because the
old fibre, minus either zero or two points, is even.  This proves the extension
criterion and, by iteration, its sufficiency.

Conversely, order the cells of an even-cross pairing arbitrarily.  Their cell
vectors span a totally $B$-isotropic space.  Every remaining cell has equal
syndrome at its two endpoints, so every old fibre is a union of whole cells.
For the next cell vector $d$, orthogonality to every remaining cell vector
gives $B(d,\mathbf 1_{C_\lambda})=0$ in every fibre.  Its endpoints therefore
have equal joint signature, proving necessity.
\end{proof}

A locally optimal pairing makes the obstruction to extending this flag
equally concrete.

\begin{proposition}[minimum-defect cell switch]
\label{prop:minimum-defect-cell-switch}
\PROVED{} For a perfect pairing $\mathcal M$, let $\Gamma_{\mathcal M}$ be
the graph on its cells with
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathcal M}(A,C)=e_B(A,C),
\]
and choose $\mathcal M$ minimizing the ordinary integer edge count
$e(\mathcal M)=|E(\Gamma_{\mathcal M})|$.  If $A,B$ are adjacent defective
cells, the unique re-pairing of their four vertices which removes their
mutual defect must create defects from both new cells to some old cell $C$
which was adjacent to neither $A$ nor $B$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The mutual cross bits of the three pairings of four vertices sum to zero in
$\F_2$.  Since the current bit is one, exactly one of the two alternative
pairings has bit zero; call it $\mathcal M'$.  For every other cell $C$, let
$n_C\in\{0,1,2\}$ be the ordinary number of its adjacencies to $A,B$, and
define $n'_C$ for the two replacement cells.  Their total cell vector is
unchanged, so
\[
 n'_C-n_C\in\{-2,0,2\}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 e(\mathcal M')-e(\mathcal M)
 =-1+\sum_C(n'_C-n_C).
\]
Minimality makes this odd integer nonnegative, hence at least one.  Some
summand must consequently be $+2$, which says precisely that
$n_C=0$ and $n'_C=2$ for a blocker cell $C$.
\end{proof}

\TESTED{} The implication $P(H)\Rightarrow T(H)=\{0\}$ holds on the complete
graph census through order eight; the numbers of $P$-classes in even orders
$2,4,6,8$ are respectively $1,4,18,262$, and every one admits an even-cross
pairing.  This is not yet a proof.  The most obvious Boolean parity repairs
already fail: on the path $01,03,12$, all $S_z=\{0\}$ but the row at $0$ is
$\{0\},\{0\},\F_2$; even the same-degree subrow can have odd full-set count
at order six.  Nor does Eulerianity alone supply the static certificate:
Proposition~\ref{prop:euler-no-cells} proves this abstractly, and an exact
order-fourteen Eulerian graph already has no even-cross perfect pairing.  Thus the missing step is a
joint identity internal to the ordered $Q$-subtrees, not a rowwise M\"obius
parity or a static pairing induction.  Proposition~\ref{prop:isotropic-pairing-flag}
identifies it as the synchronization of all old syndrome-fibre parities;
Proposition~\ref{prop:minimum-defect-cell-switch} gives the equivalent local
picture in which every defect repair branches into a new blocker fan.

\begin{lemma}[least-counterexample odd routing]\label{lem:odd-routing}
Suppose $A$ is a least-order even counterexample to
\eqref{eq:even-board}.  Then there is an $x\in A$ with $1\in K_x(A)$.  Put
\[
 Y=\{y\in A-\{x\}:\deg_A(y)=\deg_A(x)\pmod2\}.
\]
The set $Y$ has odd cardinality.  For every $y\in Y$, every target-$1$
strategy witnessing $1\in Q_{xy}(A)$ begins by opening a third vertex
$z_y$; it cannot begin by closing $x$.

At the resulting defender state $(x,y,z_y)$, such a strategy must satisfy
both
\begin{equation}\label{eq:routing-faces}
 1+\deg_{A-\{x,y,z_y\}}(x)\in Q_{y z_y}(A-\{x\})
\end{equation}
and target-$1$ forceability after every defender opening
$w\in A-\{x,y,z_y\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct play proves $E(A)$ for $|A|=0,2$: on two vertices the forced opening
pair is followed by two zero-charge closes.  Hence a least counterexample has
order at least four, and Equation~\eqref{eq:canonical-faces} supplies $x$
without meeting either boundary case.  Each degree-parity class
in an even-order graph has even cardinality, so deleting $x$ leaves the
odd set $Y$.

Fix $y\in Y$.  If the attacker closes $x$ from $Q_{xy}(A)$, the charge is
\[
 c=\deg_{A-\{x,y\}}(x),
\]
and the child is $S_y(A-\{x\})$.  By minimality,
$T(A-\{x,y\})\subseteq\{0\}$, so \eqref{eq:S-face} gives
\[
 S_y(A-\{x\})
 \subseteq \{\deg_{A-\{x,y\}}(y)\}.
\]
But equality of the full degree parities gives
\[
 \deg_{A-\{x,y\}}(x)=\deg_{A-\{x,y\}}(y),
\]
where the possible edge $xy$ cancels from both sides.  The remaining target
after the close is $1+c$, not $c$, so this branch cannot force target $1$.
The attacker must therefore open some $z_y$.  The defender may now close
$x$ or open any remaining $w$.  Applying \eqref{eq:force-recursion} to
those children gives \eqref{eq:routing-faces} and the stated universal open
conditions.
\end{proof}

The odd routing is a real strengthening over the handshake lemma: it couples
an odd family of sibling histories.  It still cannot be collapsed one sibling
at a time.  A tempting twisted induction would label
$C=A-\{x,y\}$ by $a(u)=B(x+y,u)$ and ask for a $w$ with
\[
 1+a(z)+a(w)\notin Q_{zw}(C).
\]
\emph{Computational evidence.} This is false first at $|C|=4$.  Take the path
$5-2-3-4$, put $(a_2,a_3,a_4,a_5)=(1,0,1,0)$, and $z=5$.
Exact recursion gives
\[
 T(C)=\{0\},\qquad
 Q_{52}=\{0\},\quad Q_{53}=\{0,1\},\quad Q_{54}=\{0\},
\]
while the three shifted targets are $0,1,0$.  All three survive.  This is
realized inside the six-vertex graph
\[
 E=\{03,12,13,14,23,25,34\},\qquad (x,y)=(0,1).
\]
Hence ordinary smaller-board induction, even augmented by one complete cut
bit, does not prove Lemma~\ref{lem:odd-routing}'s required joint sibling
cancellation.

The lemma cannot simply be followed by an independent quotient induction.
On real vertices $0,1,2$ plus $d$, fix a second-seat attacker so that the line
\[
 O_d,O_1,C_d,O_0,C_1,O_2,C_0,C_2
\]
is forced below the displayed defender choices.  At the $C_1$ branch from
$Q=(1,0),U=\{2\}$, one has $p_1=e_2$, $W_1=0$, $D=12$, and
$T_1D=02$.  The subtree below that branch has one leaf.  The fictitious $02$
terms in $W+\delta p$ cancel only after the cut and quotient pieces are
recombined; no compatible continuation chain realizes either term separately.
This is the smallest broken commuting diamond behind the false branchwise
induction.

\section{Obstructions to deterministic local induction}

The live-degree pairing heuristic is correct only conditionally.  At a turn of
the block initiator the queue has even length.  If the initiator opens $x$, the
responder would like to open an untouched $y$ with the matching live-degree
parity.  If no such $y$ exists and the old queue is nonempty, closing the old
front rotates
\[
 (a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_r,b_r,x)
 \longmapsto
 (b_1,a_2,\ldots,a_r,b_r,x).
\]
Neither operation is a complete strategy.

\emph{Computational evidence.} The smallest useful witnesses are exact minimax states, not
counterexamples to the linking theorem.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item On $K_2+d$, after
\[
 A:O_d,\qquad D:O_0,\qquad A:C_d,
\]
the defender faces $Q=(0)$ and $U=\{1\}$.  The winning move is $O_1$; the
apparently natural mirror close $C_0$ loses.
\item On the real path $3-0-1-2$ plus $d$, after
\[
 A:O_0,\qquad D:O_1,\qquad A:O_d,
\]
the defender faces $Q=(0,1,d)$ and $U=\{2,3\}$.  Both available opens have the
wrong live-score parity, and closing $0$ loses.  The unique winning move is the
proactive wrong-parity open $O_3$, which creates an even odd-front corridor and
stores a debt before the local trap appears.
\end{enumerate}
Thus a state descriptor consisting only of current debt, front parity, parity
classes in $U$, and one FIFO rotation is insufficient.  The missing datum is a
recursive repair certificate in the untouched graph.  This is exactly what the
affine flow is allowed to encode globally.

\section{Local obstruction theory}

The scalar cut potential gives a complete local classification, provided its
limitation is stated explicitly.  Regard the queue as a set when counting
edges and put
\[
  P(Q,U)=e_G(Q,U)\pmod2.
\]
For a move $m$, let $\chi(m)$ be the change in $P$.  If
$R=Q\cup U$, direct edge accounting gives
\[
 \chi(O_x)=\deg_{G[R]}(x),\qquad
 \chi(C_f)=\deg_U(f),\qquad
 \chi(\mathrm{pass})=0.
\]
The middle term is the actual flip.

\begin{proposition}[charge-matching and the poison state]
Suppose $P=0$, $|U|$ is even, and the position is nonterminal.
After an opponent open, there is always a distinct legal open with the same
$\chi$.  If a reply is due after an opponent close of charge $c$, there is a
legal response of charge $c$ except in exactly the following case:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poison}
 \begin{aligned}
 c&=0,\qquad U\ne\varnothing,\qquad
 \deg_{R'}(u)=1\pmod2\quad\text{for every }u\in U,\\
 &Q'=\varnothing\quad\text{or}\quad
 \deg_U(\operatorname{front}Q')=1\pmod2.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $Q'=Q\setminus\{f\}$ and $R'=Q'\cup U$ after the close.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At $P=0$,
\[
 \sum_{u\in U}\deg_R(u)=e(U,Q)=0\pmod2,
\]
because edges internal to $U$ are counted twice.  Hence the odd-charge opens
form an even set; since $|U|$ is even, the even-charge opens do too.  The charge
class containing the opponent's opener therefore contains another vertex.
Opening never has a ko obstruction, and the live set $R$ is unchanged by an
open, so this is a legal matching response.

If the opponent closes $f$ with charge $c$, then
\[
 \sum_{u\in U}\deg_{R'}(u)=e(U,Q')=c\pmod2.
\]
For $c=1$ an odd-charge open exists.  For $c=0$ an even-charge open exists
unless every untouched vertex has odd charge; in that exceptional case a
matching response still exists precisely when the new FIFO front exists and
has even degree into $U$.  A close clears ko, so that front close is legal.
This is exactly the complement of \eqref{eq:poison}.  When $U$ is empty all
remaining closes, including the close after the unique terminal pass, have
charge zero.
\end{proof}

Matching $\chi$ restores $P=0$, but does \emph{not} by itself pair the flip
score: a mixed odd-close/odd-open round contains one actual flip.  This is the
debt which the global chain must transport.  The dummy prevents
\eqref{eq:poison} while it is untouched (it is an even-charge open) or is the
new front (it is an even close), but it may be buried in the queue or already
closed.  The proposition therefore isolates the local obstruction without
pretending to solve its recurrence.

One portion of that recurrence is nevertheless well founded.  Let $g$ be the
accumulated flip debt and keep $P=e_G(Q,U)$ as above.

\begin{lemma}[fixed-front poison descent]\label{lem:poison-descent}
Suppose it is the defender's turn, $g=1$, and the front $f$ has
positive even degree into $U$; ko may be set or clear.  The defender can open
a neighbour of $f$ so that either the debt is discharged within the next
attacker--defender round or the degree drops by exactly two.  If the result is
still positive the lemma may be repeated; if it is zero, the descended
anticomplete front can be closed but need not discharge the debt.  Hence the
fixed-front loop terminates.
Moreover
\[
 H=g+P
\]
is unchanged by every close.  Thus $g=1,H=0$ excludes a singleton
anticomplete front, although $H$ is not generally preserved by opens.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Opening one neighbour changes $\deg_U(f)$ from even to odd and clears ko,
since the queue was already nonempty.  If the attacker
closes $f$, that odd close discharges the debt.  If the attacker opens a
nonneighbour, the front remains odd and the defender closes it, again
discharging the debt.  The only remaining attacker move is to open a second
neighbour; then the front is even again, the defender faces the same local
problem when its degree remains positive, and exactly two of its untouched
neighbours have been removed.  At degree zero the neighbour-open loop stops;
ko is clear at this descended boundary, so the even close is legal and carries
no flip.

A close of charge $c$ changes both $g$ and $P$ by $c$, so it fixes $H$.
If $g=1$ and $H=0$, then $P=1$, whereas a singleton queue whose front is
anticomplete to $U$ has $P=0$.  Finally an open $x$ changes $H$ by
$\deg_{G[Q\cup U]}(x)$, which may be odd; therefore this exclusion is a local
corridor condition, not a global invariant.
\end{proof}

The fixed-front data can be packaged much more richly and still fail to
determine the continuation.  For $Q=(q_0,\ldots,q_{m-1})$, define the
\emph{front signature}
\[
 \mathcal F(U,Q)=\left(
 B|_{Q\times Q},
 \left\{\!\left\{
  \bigl(B(u,q_0),\ldots,B(u,q_{m-1});\deg_{G[U]}(u)\bmod2\bigr):u\in U
 \right\}\!\right\}
 \right).
\]
It retains the ordered queue graph, every live front degree, each untouched
vertex's live-degree parity, and its full adjacency word into the queue; only
the internal adjacency correlations among untouched vertices are forgotten,
while their degree parities are retained.

\begin{proposition}[front-signature insufficiency]
The debt, mover, ko bit, and $\mathcal F(U,Q)$ do not determine
zero-normalized safety.  This already fails with five active vertices and a
nonempty even queue.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $Q=(0,1)$, $U=\{2,3,4\}$, debt zero, ko clear, and let the attacker move.
Compare
\[
 E(G_A)=\{23,04,14\},\qquad
 E(G_B)=\{23,24,02,12\}.
\]
Neither graph has edge $01$, and in both cases the untouched signature
multiset is
\[
 \left\{\!\left\{(00;1),(00;1),(11;0)\right\}\!\right\}.
\]
For $G_A$ the defender has the following first replies:
\[
 C_0\mapsto C_1,\qquad
 O_2\mapsto O_3,\qquad O_3\mapsto O_2,\qquad O_4\mapsto O_2.
\]
The first branch leaves the edge $23$ plus isolated vertex $4$ on an empty
queue; pair $O_2/O_3$, and after $O_4$ use $O_2$, opening the last vertex
before any unmatched close.  The middle two branches leave queue pairs
$(0,1)$ and $(2,3)$ (in one order or the other): mate-closing gives equal
charges, while an attack opening $4$ makes every later close zero.  In the
last branch, after $Q=(0,1,4,2),U=\{3\}$, answer $C_0$ by $C_1$ and $O_3$ by
$C_0$; from $Q=(4,2),U=\{3\}$ answer $C_4$ by $O_3$ and $O_3$ by $C_4$.
Every displayed response has charge equal to the preceding attack, and the
remaining closes have charge zero.  Thus $G_A$ is safe.

For $G_B$, the attack $C_0$ has charge one, forcing the sole normalized reply
$C_1$, also of charge one.  The queue is now empty.  The attacker opens $2$;
ko forces the defender to open $3$ or $4$.  In the first case $C_2$ has charge
one into $\{4\}$, and in the second it has charge one into $\{3\}$; both legal
defender replies then have charge zero.  Hence $G_B$ is unsafe despite the
identical signature.
\end{proof}

The first obstruction to same-action pairing does have a complete local
repair.  Write $B(u,v)$ for adjacency in the current graph.

\begin{lemma}[two-switch tail]\label{lem:two-switch-tail}
Suppose the defender moves just after an attacker close, the score
debt is zero, and
\[
 U=\{x,y\},\qquad Q=(v_1,\ldots,v_{2r+1}).
\]
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-pairs}
 B(v_{2i-1},y)=B(v_{2i},y)\quad(1\le i\le r),
 \qquad B(v_{2r+1},y)=B(x,y),
\end{equation}
then the defender forces even final score with at most two changes of action
type relative to the attacker's preceding move.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The defender first opens $x$, so the even queue is paired as
$(v_1,v_2),\ldots,(v_{2r+1},x)$ and every pair has equal adjacency to the sole
untouched vertex $y$.  While the attacker closes, the defender closes the next
front; the two flip bits in each pair cancel by \eqref{eq:tail-pairs}.  If the
attacker opens $y$, the defender closes the front.  Now $U$ is empty and every
later close has charge zero.  If the attacker never opens $y$ before the pairs
are exhausted, $y$ is the harmless terminal singleton.  The initial
close/open and the possible later open/close are the two switches.
\end{proof}

\emph{Computational evidence.} Same-action adaptive pairing first fails, without a dummy, on two
of the seven exceptional order-eight graphs: \verb|GCZN^{| and
\verb|GEjt~{|.  Call them $G_1,G_2$ in the table below.
Exact minimax gives minimum worst-case switch count two on each.  Their worst
paths enter Lemma~\ref{lem:two-switch-tail} at
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
&Q&U&(x,y)\\ \hline
G_1&(1,2,4,3,5)&\{6,7\}&(6,7)\\
G_2&(1,2,3,4,5)&\{6,7\}&(6,7).
\end{array}
\]
In both graphs $7$ dominates the remaining live graph, so
\eqref{eq:tail-pairs} is immediate.  This proves the tail repair, not the
still-missing prefix theorem that would force such a tail on every board.

\section{The zero-normalized kernel and dynamic queue matchings}

The order-eight search reveals a stronger scalar target than
\eqref{eq:even-board}.  Sample play after every defender move, with the
odd-seeking player about to move and accumulated flip score zero.  Away from
the unique terminal-pass boundary, the queue then has even length.  Put
\[
 \rho(U,Q)=|U|+\frac{|Q|}{2};
\]
every two-move block decreases $\rho$ by one.

\begin{proposition}[zero-normalized repair recursion]
\label{prop:repair-forest}
Let $Q$ have even length.  There is a strategy returning the flip
score to zero after every defender move if and only if the following recursive
predicate $\mathsf{Safe}(U,Q)$ holds.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item $\mathsf{Safe}(\varnothing,())$ holds.
\item $\mathsf{Safe}(\{z\},())$ holds as the terminal macro
      $O_z$, forced zero-charge pass, $C_z$.  The pass is the defender's move;
      after it the score is still zero, and the last close also has charge
      zero.
\item After an attacker open $O_z$, a legal zero-charge reply is either
      $O_w$ for some $w\in U-\{z\}$, leading to
      \[
       \mathsf{Safe}(U-\{z,w\},Qzw),
      \]
      or, when $Q=(f,Q_0)$ and
      $\deg_{U-\{z\}}(f)=0$, the close $C_f$, leading to
      \[
       \mathsf{Safe}(U-\{z\},Q_0z).
      \]
\item After an attacker close $C_f$ of charge
      $\epsilon=\deg_U(f)$ from $Q=(f,h,Q_0)$, a same-charge reply is
      $C_h$ when $\deg_U(h)=\epsilon$, leading to
      $\mathsf{Safe}(U,Q_0)$.  When $\epsilon=0$, the defender may instead
      open $w\in U$, leading to
      $\mathsf{Safe}(U-\{w\},hQ_0w)$.
\end{itemize}
Except inside the displayed terminal macro, every legal attacker move must
have at least one listed reply whose child is safe.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
All opens and passes have flip charge zero, and the only nonzero move charge is
the displayed untouched degree of a FIFO front.  Consequently the list
contains exactly the replies whose charge equals the preceding attacker
charge.  The singleton macro is the only time the defender has no ordinary
reply: opening the last untouched vertex onto an empty queue creates ko, so
the forced pass clears it and leaves one final zero-charge close.  Every other
child has rank $\rho-1$.  Induction on $\rho$, treating the macro as a terminal
leaf, proves sufficiency by selecting the recorded child after every attacker
move.  Conversely, record the first reply of any strategy which restores zero
after each defender move, and apply the same argument to every reachable
child.  This produces the recursion.
\end{proof}

The resulting certificate is a ranked, branch-dependent repair forest.  It is
not yet a structural existence proof: defining the forest by the winning
strategy would be circular.  It does, however, state exactly what the finite
search certifies and prevents weaker local invariants from being mistaken for
the theorem.

The operational semantics has now also been made logically two-sided.  Let
$\mathsf{EvenWins}(s)$ be the existential/universal strategy tree above, and
define $\mathsf{OddWins}(s)$ by exchanging the two seats and requiring terminal
score one.

\begin{proposition}[finite strategy duality]
\label{prop:strategy-duality}
Every state admits exactly one of $\mathsf{EvenWins}$ and
$\mathsf{OddWins}$.  Consequently the linking theorem is pointwise equivalent
to the nonexistence of an odd-forcing counterstrategy.  This proposition,
including the explicit dual trees, determinacy, incompatibility, and the
equivalence, is formalized in \path{formal/Ogdoad/Fifo.lean} without axioms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The move rank strictly decreases.  Induct on it.  At a terminal state the
score chooses exactly one tree.  At a nonterminal state, if the designated
seat moves, choose an even child when one exists; otherwise every child is odd
by induction.  If the other seat moves, exchange ``even'' and ``odd''.  The
two trees cannot coexist: at a choice node follow the chosen child through the
opposite tree's universal node, and continue by induction.  The terminal
scores then contradict one another.
\end{proof}

There is an exact terminal corridor inside the repair forest.  Suppose
$U=\{b\}$, the attacker moves, and the even queue is
$Q=(v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_{2r-1})$.  Put $c_i=B(v_i,b)$.  Scan consecutive cells
$c_{2i}c_{2i+1}$ from the front.  The state is zero-normalized safe exactly
when the first cell which is not $11$, if one exists, is not $10$.
Indeed, an attacker OPEN of $b$ empties $U$.  Against $C_{v_0}$, a zero first
bit is answered by $O_b$; a one first bit has the unique normalized form
$C_{v_1}$, which succeeds exactly when the second bit is also one and then
recurses on the tail.  This is a two-step induction on $Q$.  The sufficient
strategy, together with the all-touched terminal tail, is formalized as
\path{SingletonTail} and \path{evenWins_singletonTail} in the same Lean file.

This exact tail does \emph{not} promote a bounded-defect matching invariant to
the full game.

\begin{proposition}[unbounded absorbable defect fan]
\label{prop:defect-fan}
For every $r\ge1$ there is a normalized reachable checkpoint whose
queue consists of $r$ balanced cells, but after the attacker opens the dummy,
every normalized reply makes all $r$ old cells defective at once.  The branch
is nevertheless zero-normalized safe in one more response square.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take
\[
 Q=(x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_r,y_r),\qquad U=\{a,b,d\},
\]
where $d$ is isolated, and put in exactly the edges $x_i a$ and $y_i b$.
Opening the displayed queue in order reaches the checkpoint, and every cell
has charge $11$ into $U$.  After the attacker plays $O_d$, closing $x_1$ has
charge one, so a normalized defender must play $O_a$ or $O_b$.  After $O_a$
the residual set is $\{b\}$ and all old cells read $01$; after $O_b$ they all
read $10$.  Thus the defect count is $r$, with either orientation.

Choose $O_a$.  The attacker can now only open $b$ or close $x_1$, whose charge
has become zero.  Answer $O_b$ by $C_{x_1}$ and answer $C_{x_1}$ by $O_b$.
Either way $U$ becomes empty with score zero, so every later close is neutral.
This is precisely the first-cell-$01$ exit of the singleton corridor.
\end{proof}

Therefore clean consecutive matching, a single transported defect, monotone
defect position, and every uniformly bounded defect count are false induction
objects.  What must be transported is a \emph{factored} repair fan: arbitrarily
many local defects can share one complementary OPEN/CLOSE absorber.  The open
factor-extension theorem must recognize such shared absorbers while still
surviving attacker pruning.

\emph{Computational evidence.} Every even-order graph through order eight has
$\mathsf{Safe}(V,())$.  On all $12{,}346$ order-eight isomorphism classes, the
second player can therefore keep the \emph{actual} flip score zero after every
one of her moves.  The first failures of the stronger same-action rule occur
on \verb|GCZN^{| and \verb|GEjt~{|; the zero-normalized strategy survives by
using a close/open or open/close rotation.  Static selectors fail more
sharply: on \verb|G?bFf[| an opener's unique same-$(p,b)$ mate is unsafe, and
on \verb|GCz^vw| an odd-degree opener's only safe reply has even degree.
These are failures of proposed selectors, not of
Proposition~\ref{prop:repair-forest} or the linking theorem.

The normalized repair forest cannot itself be promoted to the Euler quotient
target of Theorem~\ref{thm:euler-quotient}.  \emph{Computational evidence.} On eight vertices, let
the primary and secondary Euler graphs have edge sets
\begin{align*}
 A=\{&02,03,06,07,13,15,23,24,26,35,36,37,47,56,57\},\\
 B=\{&01,04,06,07,12,13,15,16,17,24,25,27,35,45,47,56,57,67\}.
\end{align*}
Both degree vectors vanish modulo two, and $A$ has a zero-normalized repair
forest.  Nevertheless, the exact deterministic attack generated by
\path{experiments/linking_game.py::normalized_secondary_moment} has $88$
complete $A$-normalized response histories and every one satisfies
\[
 \langle B,D(h)\rangle=1.
\]
Therefore the affine hull of that normalized response set misses
$\Cut(V)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:euler-quotient}.  This does not refute scalar
zero normalization for $A$; it refutes the stronger move of first pruning by
one graph's zero-score replies and then asking the surviving carrier for a
graph-independent cut-valued contraction.  A proof must use the scalar target
without treating normalization as a label-independent chain map.

The rotations have an exact ordered-matching interpretation.  At a checkpoint
write
\[
 Q=(v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_{2r-1})
\]
and match consecutive queue vertices.  Pair opens extend this matching and
pair closes delete its front edge.  If the attacker appends
$z=v_{2r}$ and the defender gives the legal zero close $C_{v_0}$, the new
matching is shifted by one place.  Let
\[
 P(W)=\sum_{i=0}^{2r-1}[v_i,v_{i+1}],
 \qquad W=(v_0,\ldots,v_{2r}).
\]
Then the old and shifted matching chains differ by $P(W)$ and
\[
 \partial P(W)=e_{v_0}+e_z.
\]
More precisely, with the triangle fan
\[
 F(W)=\sum_{i=1}^{2r-1}[v_0,v_i,v_{i+1}],
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-fan}
 P(W)+[v_0,z]=\partial F(W).
\end{equation}
For the adjacency $1$-cochain $B$, therefore,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-holonomy}
 \langle B,P(W)\rangle
 =B(v_0,z)+
 \sum_{i=1}^{2r-1}\delta B(v_0,v_i,v_{i+1}).
\end{equation}
The second term is genuine two-graph holonomy.  Endpoint exchange, a queue
matching span, or a symplectic basis invariant sees only $B(v_0,z)$ and is
therefore insufficient on an arbitrary graph.

The triangle shears are coherent but not flat.  If
$\tau(i,j,k)=\delta B(i,j,k)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tetrahedron-coherence}
 \tau(j,k,l)+\tau(i,k,l)+\tau(i,j,l)+\tau(i,j,k)=0,
\end{equation}
which is $\delta^2B=0$.  Thus the ambient complete simplex fills the fan in
\eqref{eq:phase-fan} consistently.  The adversarial obstruction is in its
coefficients: after fixing an attacker strategy, compatible histories are not
closed under deleting an interior letter.  Defender coefficients may split,
but an attacker coefficient must follow one prescribed child.  Projection to
compatible histories consequently fails to commute with the simplicial
boundary.

There is a stronger obstruction before attacker pruning.  On the
score-forgotten public-state graph give a real close $C_f$ from untouched set
$U$ the universal charge
\[
 c(U,f)=\sum_{u\in U\cap R}fu\in\mathcal E_R,
\]
and give every other move charge zero.  On
$M=\F_2\oplus\mathcal E_R$ transport along a move of charge $c$ by
$T_c(a,z)=(a,z+ac)$.

\begin{proposition}[universal public-state holonomy]
\label{prop:universal-holonomy}
Let $Q=(f,Q_0)$ be ko-clear, let $Q_0\ne()$, and let
$z\in U\cap R$.  The legal paths
\[
 O_z;C_f\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad C_f;O_z
\]
end in the same score-forgotten public state, but their transports differ by
$T_{fz}$.  Every real basis edge $fz$ occurs on a reachable such square.
Consequently, if $p:\mathcal E_R\to W$ is a linear quotient on which the
induced transports are flat on every schedule square, then $p=0$.  In
particular scalarization by a graph $B$ is flat on all squares if and only if
$B$ is edgeless.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The close-before-open path has charge $c(U,f)$, whereas the
open-before-close path has charge $c(U-\{z\},f)$.  Their difference is exactly
$fz$.  The identity $T_cT_{c'}=T_{c+c'}$ proves the transport assertion, and
the two paths visibly leave the same untouched set, queue, ko bit and mover.

For distinct real $f,z$, first play $O_f,O_d$, where $d$ is the isolated
dummy.  The resulting queue is $(f,d)$, ko is clear, and $z$ is untouched, so
the displayed square is reachable and has curvature $fz$.  These $fz$ form a
basis of $\mathcal E_R$.  Flatness after applying $p$ therefore gives
$p(fz)=0$ basiswise and hence $p=0$.  Pairing with a scalar adjacency vector
$B$ sends this curvature to $B(f,z)$, which vanishes for all pairs exactly
when $B$ has no edges.
\end{proof}

The qualification $Q_0\ne()$ is exact.  At a singleton queue the two paths
have different ko bits, so there is no square to fill.  Squares involving the
dummy have zero real curvature, and the missing singleton square is precisely
where the dummy cannot cone off a nonzero $fz$.  Hence adding ordinary
curvature fillers to the public-state complex either fails to define a flat
complex or, after quotienting, erases the entire terminal invariant.  The
Lean model kernel-checks both the charge identity and the complete operational
square, including reachability of every real basis coordinate, in
\texttt{Ogdoad/Fifo.lean}.

Eulerian support makes this curvature into an honest Euler complex.  Recall
that a $3$-oik is a multiset of three-element rooms in which every two-element
wall occurs with even multiplicity \cite{vegh-von-stengel}.

\begin{theorem}[Euler curvature oik]
\label{thm:euler-oik}
Let $G$ be an Eulerian graph of even order $n$, with adjacency
$1$-cochain $B$, and put
\[
 \tau(i,j,k)=B(i,j)+B(i,k)+B(j,k).
\]
The triples on which $\tau=1$ form a $3$-oik.  Moreover this binary room
multiset has a coherent oriented integral multiset lift: there is an integral
$2$-cycle $z$ in the full simplex whose coefficient on each triangle is
congruent to $\tau$ modulo $2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a wall $\{i,j\}$, its room incidence modulo two is
\[
 \sum_{k\ne i,j}\tau(i,j,k)
 =(n-2)B(i,j)+(\deg i-B(i,j))+(\deg j-B(i,j))=0,
\]
because $n$, $\deg i$, and $\deg j$ are even.  This is exactly the oik wall
condition.

For the oriented lift, choose an orientation on every triangle and regard the
$0/1$ room set as an integral $2$-chain $t$.  The wall condition says that
$\partial t$ is even, so write $\partial t=2b$.  Then $\partial b=0$.
The full simplex has $H_1(-;\mathbb Z)=0$, hence $b=\partial c$ for an
integral $2$-chain $c$.  The chain $z=t-2c$ is an integral cycle and satisfies
$z\equiv t\equiv\tau\pmod2$.  Replacing each nonzero coefficient by that many
signed copies gives the claimed coherent oriented multiset.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:phase-holonomy} now says exactly that the phase defect is
the parity of rooms of this $3$-oik occurring in the triangle fan $F(W)$.
This is a genuine bridge to complementary pivoting, but ordinary oik parity
does not close the game.  A fan overlaps in vertices and is not a room
partition; attacker pruning is irreversible and need not give every wall even
incidence; and a pivot path pairs endpoints, producing augmentation zero over
$\F_2$, whereas the required response chain has augmentation one.  The
curvature also cannot be oriented on Markov states alone.  The two legal words
$C_fO_z$ and $O_zC_f$ end in the same state when both orders are legal, but
their charges differ by $B(f,z)$.  Thus a successful use of
Theorem~\ref{thm:euler-oik} needs a relative, strategy-pruned,
charge-decorated oik contraction, not the constant-coefficient room-partition
theorem.

The full simplex nevertheless gives an exact dichotomy.  Write $J$ for the
all-edge cochain and $\epsilon(R)=\langle J,R\rangle$ for the number of edges
in a binary chain modulo two.

\begin{theorem}[odd isotropic cycle dichotomy]
\label{thm:odd-cycle-dichotomy}
Let $G$ be an Eulerian graph of positive even order, with adjacency
chain $B$.  Exactly one of the following holds.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item There are distinct vertices $i,j,k$ spanning an even number of edges of
$G$, or equivalently
\[
 \tau(i,j,k)=B(i,j)+B(i,k)+B(j,k)=0.
\]
Thus the single triangle $F=[i,j,k]$ and its boundary $R=\partial F$ obey
\[
 \epsilon(F)=\epsilon(R)=1,
 \qquad
 \langle\delta B,F\rangle=\langle B,R\rangle=0.
\]
\item There is an odd set $S\subseteq V$ such that
\[
 B=J+\delta1_S,
 \qquad
 G=K_S\sqcup K_{V\setminus S}.
\]
Both cliques have odd order.  This exceptional family satisfies the
parity-cell hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:cells} for every partition into
pairs, and hence every prescribed ordered-pair checkpoint is safe.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume first that $|V|\ge4$.  The coboundary $\delta J$ is one on every
triangle.  If the first alternative fails, then $\delta B=1=\delta J$ on
every triangle, so $\delta(B+J)=0$.  The $1$-cocycles of the full simplex are
exact, hence $B+J=\delta1_S$ for some $S\subseteq V$.  Conversely, that
identity gives $\delta B=\delta J=1$ on every triangle, so the alternatives
are exclusive.  When the first alternative holds, the displayed claims for
$F$ and $R$ are the augmentation identity for one triangle and Stokes.

Put $s_v=1_S(v)$.  Because $|V|$ is even, at every vertex
\[
 \deg_B(v)
 =\sum_{u\ne v}(1+s_u+s_v)=1+|S|\pmod2.
\]
Eulerianity therefore makes $|S|$ odd.  Moreover
$B(u,v)=1+s_u+s_v$ is one exactly when $u,v$ lie on the same side of the
cut, proving the two-clique description; its complement also has odd order.
At order two, Eulerianity forces the empty graph, which has the same form for
either singleton $S$.

Finally, in the exceptional family, if $C,D$ are any two-element cells, then
\[
 e_G(C,D)
 =|C\cap S||D\cap S|
  +|C\cap S^c||D\cap S^c|=0\pmod2,
\]
because a two-element cell has equal intersection parities with $S$ and
$S^c$.  Any perfect matching containing a prescribed pair therefore invokes
Theorem~\ref{thm:cells} from that ordered-pair checkpoint.
\end{proof}

Outside the solved two-clique family, Theorem~\ref{thm:odd-cycle-dichotomy}
supplies exactly the odd, zero-curvature full-simplex chain that ordinary oik
parity was missing---in fact, one even-curvature triangle suffices.  It still
does not solve the game: that face need not be compatible with a fixed
attacker strategy.  The remaining theorem is now sharply one of promoting
such a triangle through attacker pruning while preserving odd augmentation
and its cut-valued terminal moment.

The wall parity itself has a precise game-facing form.  It supplies an odd
fan of locally cancelling OPEN replies, but the continuation coefficients are
still the unresolved datum.

\begin{lemma}[same-curvature odd wall fan]
\label{lem:same-curvature-fan}
Let $G$ be an even-order Eulerian graph, fix distinct $x,y$, and put
\[
 \tau_{xy}(v)=B(x,y)+B(x,v)+B(y,v)
 \qquad(v\notin\{x,y\}).
\]
Each of the two $\tau_{xy}$ fibres in $V-\{x,y\}$ has even cardinality.
Consequently, after an attacker opens $z$ from the ordered-pair checkpoint
$Q=(x,y)$, the set
\[
 W_z=\{w\notin\{x,y,z\}:\tau_{xy}(w)=\tau_{xy}(z)\}
\]
has odd cardinality.  For every $w\in W_z$, if the defender opens $w$ and the
attacker next closes $x$, then closing $y$ gives the same charge and cancels
that close pair.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because $|V|$, $\deg x$, and $\deg y$ are even,
\[
 \sum_{v\ne x,y}\tau_{xy}(v)
 =(|V|-2)B(x,y)+(\deg x-B(x,y))+(\deg y-B(x,y))=0.
\]
Thus the one-fibre is even; its complement in the even set $V-\{x,y\}$ is
even as well.  Removing $z$ from its own fibre leaves the odd set $W_z$.

After $z,w$ have opened, put $U=V-\{x,y,z,w\}$.  Eulerianity gives
\begin{align*}
 \deg_U(x)+\deg_U(y)
 &=B(x,z)+B(y,z)+B(x,w)+B(y,w)\\
 &=\tau_{xy}(z)+\tau_{xy}(w)=0.
\end{align*}
These are exactly the charges of the consecutive closes of $x$ and $y$, so
they agree and cancel.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:same-curvature-fan} is the local $3$-oik wall promised by
Theorem~\ref{thm:euler-oik}; it is not a contraction of the response tree.
The order-eight open-completion witness below has an attacker open for which
every OPEN child can be made losing while the unique safe reply is the
phase-changing close.  A valid odd flow must therefore choose a
strategy-dependent subset across OPEN and CLOSE children and across earlier
front levels, rather than assigning coefficient one to the whole odd wall.

The wall does, however, transport an odd flow through an arbitrarily long
run of OPEN moves while one ordered front pair is fixed.  The right hypothesis
is slightly more general than Eulerianity.  For a checkpoint
$Q=(x,y,Q_0)$, put
\[
 \eta_{xy}(u)=B(x,u)+B(y,u)\qquad(u\in U),
\]
and call the front pair \emph{balanced} when both $\eta_{xy}$ fibres in $U$
have even cardinality.

\begin{theorem}[balanced-front odd-flow transport]
\label{thm:balanced-front-transport}
Fix a balanced checkpoint with the attacker to move and fix an
arbitrary deterministic attacker strategy until $x$ closes.  There is a
canonical response flow of odd augmentation with the following rules:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item after an attacker OPEN $O_z$, put coefficient one on every reply
      $O_w$ with $\eta_{xy}(w)=\eta_{xy}(z)$;
\item after the attacker closes $x$, reply by closing $y$ and exit the
      fixed-front phase.
\end{itemize}
Every exit has equal charges on $C_x,C_y$, so the phase contributes zero
scalar score.  This assertion and the flow construction also allow one of
$x,y$ to be the isolated dummy, with every dummy edge projected to zero.  If
$U_\lambda$ is the untouched set at an exit $\lambda$, its future
disjointness increment on the deleted pair is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa-front}
 \kappa_{xy}(U_\lambda)
 =\sum_{u\in U_\lambda}(xu+yu).
\end{equation}
When both front vertices are real this chain is Eulerian; in every case its
real projection is $B$-isotropic:
\[
 x,y\in R\Longrightarrow\partial\kappa_{xy}(U_\lambda)=0,
 \qquad
 \langle B,\kappa_{xy}(U_\lambda)\rangle=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If the attacker opens $z$, its $\eta$-fibre had even size, so after removing
$z$ the displayed reply set is odd and nonempty.  Every selected child removes
two vertices of one fibre; hence both fibres remain even and the construction
may be repeated.  Each OPEN round removes two untouched vertices, so the
process terminates.  Attacker nodes route their coefficient to the one child
prescribed by the strategy, while every defender fan has odd augmentation;
the exit flow therefore has augmentation one.

At an exit,
\[
 \deg_{U_\lambda}(x)+\deg_{U_\lambda}(y)
 =\sum_{u\in U_\lambda}\eta_{xy}(u)=0,
\]
so the consecutive close charges agree.  A residual untouched vertex is
disjoint from both deleted front intervals, whereas every vertex already in
the queue overlaps them.  This proves \eqref{eq:kappa-front}.  If $x,y$ are
real, the residual set has even cardinality, so every vertex in the displayed
complete bipartite chain has even degree.  Pairing the real projection with
$B$ gives the same even $\eta$-sum whether or not one front vertex is the
dummy, proving the last two assertions with the stated qualification.
\end{proof}

At an Euler ordered-pair checkpoint, balancedness follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:same-curvature-fan}, since
$\eta_{xy}=\tau_{xy}+B(x,y)$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced-front-transport}
is therefore a genuine strategy-pruned local coefficient transport, not merely the
one-round wall count.  Its nonzero $\kappa$ is the local coefficient which
must be carried to the next FIFO front.

That coefficient has an exact cell-cut calculus.  For arbitrary disjoint sets
$P,X$ in a residual live graph $H$, write
\[
 K(P,X)=\sum_{p\in P,\,x\in X}px.
\]
It is an Eulerian edge chain when both sets are even.
The application below uses two-cells $P_i=\{a_i,b_i\}$ whose endpoints have
equal degree parity in $H$.

\begin{proposition}[cell-cut transport]
\label{prop:cell-cut-transport}
Let the residual queue be
$P_1P_2\cdots P_r$, where $P_i=\{a_i,b_i\}$ and
$\deg_H(a_i)=\deg_H(b_i)\pmod2$ for every $i$, and let $U$ be untouched.
For every prefix
$A_k=P_1\cup\cdots\cup P_k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cell-prefix-cut}
 \sum_{i\le k}K(P_i,U)
 +\sum_{i\le k<j}K(P_i,P_j)
 =\delta_H(A_k).
\end{equation}
Consequently, with
\[
 \Delta_i=\langle B,K(P_i,U)\rangle,
 \qquad c_{ij}=\langle B,K(P_i,P_j)\rangle,
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cell-prefix-charge}
 \sum_{i\le k}\Delta_i=\sum_{i\le k<j}c_{ij}.
\end{equation}
In particular the number of cells with $\Delta_i=1$ is even.  Moving a new
two-cell $R$ from $U$ to the back of the queue obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cell-open-transport}
 K(P,U-R)=K(P,U)+K(P,R).
\end{equation}
Thus OPEN converts untouched $\kappa$-mass into a queued four-cycle, while
successive CLOSE pairs, with intervening OPEN transfers accounted for by
\eqref{eq:cell-open-transport}, move the same defect across the phase cuts in
\eqref{eq:cell-prefix-cut}; after the entire queue closes only a cut remains.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both sides of \eqref{eq:cell-prefix-cut} are exactly the complete bipartite
edge chain from $A_k$ to $H-A_k$: the first sum contains its edges to $U$ and
the second its edges to later queue cells.  Pairing with $B$ proves
\eqref{eq:cell-prefix-charge}, because
\[
 \langle B,\delta_H(A_k)\rangle
 =\sum_{i\le k}\bigl(\deg_H(a_i)+\deg_H(b_i)\bigr)=0.
\]
More explicitly, $\deg_H(a_i)+\deg_H(b_i)=0$ gives
\[
 \Delta_i=\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ij};
\]
summing over $i$ cancels every $c_{ij}$ twice and proves the even-defect
claim.  Equation~\eqref{eq:cell-open-transport} is the disjoint union
$P\times U=(P\times(U-R))\sqcup(P\times R)$ over $\F_2$.
\end{proof}

For the Euler ordered-pair exits of
Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced-front-transport}, the homogeneity hypothesis is
automatic.  Put $H=G-\{x,y\}$.  Every wall-created cell $P_i=\{z_i,w_i\}$
has $\eta_{xy}(z_i)=\eta_{xy}(w_i)$, while Eulerianity gives
\[
 \deg_H(v)=B(v,x)+B(v,y)=\eta_{xy}(v).
\]
Moreover $U$ is even, so each $K(P_i,U)$ is itself Eulerian.  This application
does not extend merely from balancedness at an arbitrary non-Euler checkpoint;
the cell-degree hypothesis is an additional datum there.

The tempting next induction---that the first residual cell is balanced after
summing the canonical wall exits---is false.  \emph{Computational evidence.} On the Euler graph
\[
 E=\{02,03,04,07,24,34,36,37,45,46,47,57\},
\]
start at $(x,y)=(0,1)$.  The nonzero $\eta_{01}$ fibre is
$\{2,3,4,7\}$ and the zero fibre is $\{5,6\}$.  Let the attacker open $2$;
after replies $3,4,7$, respectively, let it follow
\[
 C_0,\qquad O_6O_7C_0,\qquad O_5C_0.
\]
The canonical transport exits at
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 &Q&U\\ \hline
(a)&(2,3)&\{4,5,6,7\}\\
(b)&(2,4,6,5,7,3)&\varnothing\\
(c)&(2,7,5,6)&\{3,4\}.
\end{array}
\]
Their first-cell imbalances are $0,0,1$.  In the last exit both cells $27$
and $56$ are imbalanced and $c_{27,56}=1$: the defect is not lost, but sits
on the intermediate phase cut exactly as
\eqref{eq:cell-prefix-charge} predicts.  Hence the remaining lemma must
causally lift these cross-cell four-cycles through legal OPEN/CLOSE matching
shifts; aggregate front balance is not the invariant.

Even the complete ordered cell-cut datum is not a Markov state for this
lifting.  On seven real vertices, with isolated $d=7$, put
\begin{align*}
 E_{\rm bad}={}&\{01,02,04,12,13,14,16,23,24,26,34,35,45,56\},\\
 Q={}&(6,1,5,4),\qquad U=\{0,2,3,7\}.
\end{align*}
All four individual queue charges are one, all four queued vertices have odd
full degree, both cell defects $\Delta_i$ vanish, and
$c_{61,54}=0$.  Nevertheless the checkpoint is unsafe even for the
unrestricted terminal-score game.  The attacker opens the isolated dummy.
Replies $O_0$ and $O_3$ are met by the odd close $C_6$, while $C_1$ and every
remaining OPEN have charge zero.  After reply $O_2$, the attacker closes $6$
evenly; the three normalized replies $O_0,O_3,C_1$ are defeated respectively
by short continuations beginning $C_1,C_1,C_5$.  In the $O_0$ subbranch the
forced charge-one responses continue through $C_5,C_4$.  Direct evaluation
gives one on each displayed spoiler and zero on every available response; the
exact tree is replayed by the checker.

This is not an arbitrary unreachable carrier.  Add old fronts $x=8,y=9$,
make $x$ isolated, and join $y$ to
$\{0,1,2,4,5,6\}$, the odd-degree set of $E_{\rm bad}$.  The resulting
ten-vertex graph is Eulerian.  From $Q=(x,y)$ the canonical balanced-wall
branch
\[
 O_6,O_1,O_5,O_4,C_x,C_y
\]
exits exactly at the displayed bad checkpoint with zero accumulated charge.
Thus no proof may continue every canonical wall exit independently.

For comparison, the labelled graph
\[
 E_{\rm good}=\{04,06,13,14,15,23,35,45\}
\]
has the same $Q,U$, the same four individual charges, full-degree colours,
$\Delta$ vector, cross-cell parity, and formal chains
$K(P_i,U),K(P_1,P_2)$, but exact zero-normalized recursion marks it safe.
\emph{Computational evidence.} The graph6 records are respectively \texttt{Fz\{ZG} and
\texttt{FBp[?}; \path{experiments/linking_game.py} verifies both decisions
and the Euler lift.  Hence vertex-resolved continuation data below the cell
aggregation is indispensable.

Nor is the Eulerian case a simplifying restriction of the scalar kernel.

\begin{theorem}[Euler ordered-pair kernel is complete]
\label{thm:euler-complete}
Suppose that for every even-order Eulerian graph $G$ and every
ordered pair of distinct vertices $(x,y)$, the checkpoint
\[
 U=V(G)-\{x,y\},\qquad Q=(x,y)
\]
is safe in the zero-normalized recursion.  Then every even-order graph $H$
satisfies $\mathsf{Safe}(V(H),())$.
Equivalently, any even-board counterexample produces an Eulerian ordered-pair
counterexample two vertices larger.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $H$ have nonempty even vertex set $V$, put
$p(v)=\deg_H(v)\pmod2$, and let $O=\{v:p(v)=1\}$.  Handshaking makes $|O|$
even.  Choose any odd set $A\subseteq V$ and put $C=A\mathbin\triangle O$.
Adjoin $x,y$, the edge $xy$, all edges from $x$ to $A$, and all edges from
$y$ to $C$.  For $v\in V$,
\[
 \deg_G(v)=p(v)+1_A(v)+1_C(v)=0\pmod2,
\]
while $\deg_G(x)=1+|A|$ and
$\deg_G(y)=1+|C|=1+|A|+|O|$ are even.  Thus $G$ is Eulerian of even order.

At the displayed checkpoint, the attacker may close $x$ with charge
$|A|=1$.  Zero normalization forbids an open reply, and FIFO leaves only the
close of $y$, whose charge $|C|$ is also one.  The two charges cancel and the
resulting state is exactly $U=V$, $Q=()$, attacker to move, with score zero in
the original graph $H$.  Safety of the Eulerian checkpoint therefore implies
$\mathsf{Safe}(V,())$; contraposition gives the final assertion.
\end{proof}

This is not repaired by ordinary topology of injective words.  Those words
form a Boolean cell complex with
\[
 \partial[v_0\cdots v_k]
 =\sum_i[v_0\cdots\widehat v_i\cdots v_k]
 \quad\text{over }\F_2
\]
\cite{jonsson-welker,gan}.  Define the strategy carrier here as the Boolean
subcomplex generated by the attacker-compatible pre-close tail words.  Even
with a dummy, attacker pruning need not leave this carrier acyclic.  On
$K_2+d$, let the attacker open a real front $f$ and, after the forced-open
reply is chosen as $O_a$ or $O_d$, close $f$ immediately.  Before the first
close the carrier consists of the two isolated vertices $[a]$ and $[d]$; the
joining cells $[ad]$ and $[da]$ have been pruned.  Its reduced
$H_0$ is nonzero.  The linking response still exists because the two close
charges differ.  Hence any successful carrier theorem must retain the charge
and continuation labels; ordinary shellability or a constant-coefficient cone
is not enough.

Three further exact obstructions delimit the remaining Boolean contraction.
First, the two-bit colour is a prefix moment, not a winning selector.
\emph{Computational evidence.} On the eight-vertex graph
\[
\begin{split}
 E=\{&01,03,05,07,12,14,15,17,24,26,27,34,\\
     &37,45,46,47,57,67\},
\end{split}
\]
vertices $6,7$ have the same $(p,b)$ colour, but $Q_{67}$ is unsafe while
$Q_{76}$ is safe.  The unique first spoiler in $Q_{67}$ is $O_5$.
Vertices $6,7$ have odd degree while $5$ has even degree, so the
local unsafe-$Q$ hypotheses used by odd routing do not themselves force the
witness choice $y\mapsto z_y$ to preserve $Y$.  Functional-digraph cycle
parity would first need a genuinely least-counterexample-specific closure
theorem.
There is also an opener with no safe same-colour mate: in graph6 class
\verb|G?ben[|, with
\[
 E=\{04,05,15,25,06,16,36,56,07,17,27,47,57,67\},
\]
vertex $2$ has sole same-colour mate $7$, yet $Q_{27}$ is unsafe.  Its
unique first spoiler is $O_3$; the reverse $Q_{72}$ is safe.  Thus neither
orientation nor existential choice within one colour class proves the root
theorem.

Second, a safe residual strategy cannot always be lifted by insisting on an
open response.  On
\[
\begin{split}
 E=\{&01,02,03,04,05,06,12,13,14,17,23,26,\\
     &27,35,37,56,57\},
\end{split}
\]
the checkpoint
\[
 Q=(4,3),\qquad U=\{0,1,2,5,6,7\}
\]
is safe in the zero-normalized recursion.  After attacker $O_6$, the unique
safe reply is the zero close $C_4$, leading to
$Q=(3,6)$ and $U=\{0,1,2,5,7\}$.  The five open replies by
$0,1,2,5,7$ are all unsafe; respective short spoilers may be chosen as
$C_4,C_4,O_7,O_7,O_1$.  Hence the phase-changing OC branch is not an
artifact of the finite search: it is indispensable even when $|U|$ was even.

Finally, time reversal does not repair the strategic pruning.  For a
pass-free complete event word put
\[
 \rho(e_1\cdots e_m)=\bar e_m\cdots\bar e_1,
 \qquad \overline{O_v}=C_v,\quad \overline{C_v}=O_v.
\]
This is a legal FIFO schedule, swaps the two seats, and preserves
$D$ coordinatewise.  Indeed, if both opening and closing order in the original
word are $v_1,\ldots,v_n$, both orders after reversal are
$v_n,\ldots,v_1$, so FIFO is preserved.  A pass-free word has no adjacent
singleton block $O_vC_v$, hence reversal creates no ko violation.  Position
$i$ moves to $2n+1-i$, which swaps seat parity, while each activity interval
$[o_v,c_v]$ reflects to
$[2n+1-c_v,2n+1-o_v]$; interval disjointness, and therefore $D$, is
unchanged.

Let $\mathcal L_{\rm pf}$ be the full set of legal pass-free complete
schedules and extend $\rho$ linearly to $\F_2[\mathcal L_{\rm pf}]$.  For at
least two vertices, $\rho$ is fixed-point-free: a fixed word would
have its common FIFO opening/closing order equal to its reverse, impossible
for distinct labels.  Thus every $\rho$-invariant chain in this full space is
a sum of two-element orbits, hence lies in the image of $1+\rho$.  For every
chain $c$,
\[
 (\operatorname{aug},D)((1+\rho)c)=(0,0).
\]
Reversal averaging therefore produces a homogeneous zero-moment chain, never
the required affine certificate $(1,0)$.  Even a hypothetical causal reversal
would preserve the odd payoff rather than complement it, so composing two
odd-target strategies would give no contradiction.

This identity does not extend to a strategy contraction.  If one attempts to
set $\rho(P)=P$, a terminal singleton component $O_x,P,C_x$ moves to the
beginning; when other vertices remain untouched there, its pass is not forced
and is illegal.  Even without passes, fixed strategies are anti-causal under
$\rho$.  Put the original attacker in the even positions.  One deterministic
attacker strategy contains both histories below: after $O_0$ it prescribes
$O_d$; after the defender's choice $C_0$ or $O_1$ it prescribes respectively
$O_1$ or $C_0$; and after $C_d$ it prescribes $C_1$.  The smallest such local
witness has real vertices $0,1$ plus $d$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
h_C=O_0O_dC_0O_1C_dC_1&D(h_C)=01,\\
h_O=O_0O_dO_1C_0C_dC_1&D(h_O)=0.
\end{array}
\]
The two histories reconverge across the commuting $C_0/O_1$ diamond, whose
label difference is the edge coordinate $01$.  Reversal gives a common
prefix $O_1O_d$ but demands different moves, $C_1$ and $O_0$, at the same
odd-position attacker node.  Hence the reversed pair cannot lie under one
deterministic reversed attacker strategy.  Separately, the diamond involution
$\iota$ exchanging $C_0$ and $O_1$ swaps $h_C$ and $h_O$, so
\[
 \F_2\{h_C,h_O\}^{\iota}=\{0,h_C+h_O\}.
\]
Both invariant chains have even augmentation (and
$D(h_C+h_O)=01$), whereas the valid odd zero-moment certificate is the
nonsymmetric singleton $h_O$.  Diamond symmetrization therefore destroys the
odd augmentation rather than proving it.

The dummy does have one exact phase interpretation when it is opened onto an
empty root.

\begin{proposition}[dummy-first draft and switch]
\label{prop:dummy-draft-switch}
Suppose $R\ne\varnothing$ and the first move is $O_d$.  After the
ko-forced first real OPEN, the play until $C_d$ is precisely an alternating
draft of distinct real vertices: on each turn the player either OPENs an
untouched real vertex or plays the zero-charge switch $C_d$.  At the switch,
ordinary no-dummy FIFO play begins at score zero from the nonempty drafted
queue, with ko clear and with the other player to move.  If all real vertices
are drafted before the switch, $C_d$ is forced.  Conversely every such
draft-and-switch word is legal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Opening $d$ onto the empty queue sets ko, so the next move is a real OPEN.
Until $d$ closes it remains the FIFO front.  Hence the only legal CLOSE is
$C_d$, while every other legal move is an OPEN of a fresh real vertex.  All
these OPENs and $C_d$ have charge zero.  Closing $d$ clears ko and leaves,
in its opening order, the nonempty queue of drafted real vertices.  These
observations also prove the converse and the exhausted-draft clause.
\end{proof}

Thus the dummy is a genuine one-use phase switch at a dummy-first root, but
not an empty-root reduction.  In particular, the stopped close-first theorem
cannot be invoked at the switch: its hypothesis is a defender-first empty
no-dummy root, whereas Proposition~\ref{prop:dummy-draft-switch} exposes a
nonempty queue and either player may have taken the switch.  The nonempty
drafted prefix, rather than the dummy event itself, remains part of the global
ancestry problem.

For a general history, deleting the dummy has one further, exactly localized
defect.

\begin{proposition}[dummy deletion and the unique ko wall]
\label{prop:dummy-deletion-wall}
Let $h$ be a complete legal history with isolated dummy $d$.  Form
the raw projected event word by deleting $O_d,C_d$ and any pass, while
retaining the real events in order.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item every real CLOSE has the same charge before and after deletion;
\item relative to the raw projected positions, the controller parity of
      precisely the real events strictly between $O_d$ and $C_d$ is
      reversed;
\item after inserting the unique forced pass exactly when required at a
      terminal singleton, the projected word is a legal real FIFO history
      except possibly at one internal ko wall; and
\item if that wall occurs, its consecutive real events are
      $O_v,C_v,O_w$.  Replacing them by $O_v,O_w,C_v$ gives a legal real
      history $\widetilde h$ and
      \[
        D_R(\widetilde h)=D_R(h)+vw,
        \qquad
        F_G(\widetilde h)=F_G(h)+B(v,w).
      \]
\end{enumerate}
The wall occurs exactly when $d$ is the sole open vertex immediately before
$O_v$, and both $C_d$ and $C_v$ occur before the next real OPEN $O_w$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $d$ is isolated, removing it from the untouched set changes no real
CLOSE charge.  Deleting two event positions preserves controller parity
outside their open interval and reverses it inside.

Deletion preserves the real opening order, closing order, and FIFO condition.
Suppose nevertheless that a projected $O_v$ opens an empty real queue and is
followed by $C_v$ while another real vertex remains untouched.  The original
$O_v$ could not have opened an actually empty queue, since its ko would have
made $C_v$ illegal.  Thus $d$ was the unique open vertex before $O_v$.
FIFO then forces $C_d$ before $C_v$, and no other real event can intervene
before the next real OPEN $O_w$.  Once this happens $d$ is closed, so there
can be no second internal wall.  If no such $w$ exists, the projected block
is terminal and inserting its forced pass is the unique repair.

In the internal case, moving $O_w$ immediately before $C_v$ clears ko and
preserves FIFO legality.  The adjacent exchange changes no real interval
relation except that of $v,w$: their intervals were disjoint and now overlap.
This proves both displayed identities and the characterization of the wall.
\end{proof}

Consequently the dummy cannot simply be erased to make a shadow copycat game.
If $\pi$ deletes $O_d,C_d$, then
$\pi(h_C)=O_0C_0O_1C_1$ is illegal: after $O_0$, ko forbids $C_0$ while
$O_1$ remains available.  Even when a schedule and its dummy deletion are
both legal, controllers are not preserved.  For
\[
 k=O_dO_0O_1C_dC_0C_1,\qquad \pi(k)=O_0O_1C_0C_1,
\]
the projected real events were controlled in order by $B,A,A,B$, not by
alternating seats.  With only one real vertex, deletion instead requires the
state-dependent terminal pass from
Proposition~\ref{prop:dummy-deletion-wall}.  Hence dummy deletion preserves
neither legality, turn ownership, nor the pass boundary; it is not a game-tree
morphism.

The failure is concentrated at an exact ko wall.  Suppose $d$ is untouched,
$Q=(f,Q_0)$ has real front $f$, and ko is clear.  The adjacent words
\[
 O_dC_f,\qquad C_fO_d
\]
have the same real score and end with the same untouched set and queue
$Q_0d$.  Their ko bits agree unless $Q_0$ is empty; at that singleton wall
the first word ends with ko clear and the second with ko set.  Dually, from
$Q=(d,Q_0)$ and untouched $x$, the words $C_dO_x$ and $O_xC_d$ end in the
same queue $Q_0x$ with equal real score, but at $Q_0=()$ their ko bits are set
and clear respectively.  This follows directly because a close clears ko,
whereas an open sets it exactly when the pre-open queue is empty.

The first wall is already label-essential.  The legal word
\[
 O_0O_dC_0O_1C_dC_1
\]
has $D_R=01$, whereas deleting the dummy gives the illegal word
$O_0C_0O_1C_1$.  The only legal real FIFO word with the same real opening and
closing orders is $O_0O_1C_0C_1$, whose disjointness score is zero.  A virtual
pass cannot repair the former word while vertex $1$ remains untouched.
Thus bubbling or deleting the dummy cannot be made both causal and
label-preserving by retaining only a role-switch or ko marker:
Proposition~\ref{prop:dummy-deletion-wall} shows that repairing the ko wall
necessarily transports its labelled edge moment.  The global odd flow may
cancel such terms across several histories, but neither the exact
draft-and-switch reduction nor a single-history dummy normalization proves
the theorem.

The same wall is the only exceptional cell in a useful two-copy transport.
It gives an operational realization of the relative quotient above, although
not yet the required odd incidence theorem.

\begin{proposition}[one-front-offset ladder]
\label{prop:one-front-offset-ladder}
\PROVED{} Consider two reachable coherent, ko-clear public states with the
same untouched set $U$, opposite movers, and queues
\[
 a::Q\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad Q.
\]
Let $\Delta$ be the XOR difference of their already accumulated vector
moments, write $aU=\sum_{u\in U}au$, and put
\[
 \Omega=\Delta+aU.
\]
Thus $a\notin U$, $a\notin Q$, and the entries of $a::Q$ are distinct.
Cross-feed an attacker move in one copy as a defender move in the other
whenever the following clear-state rules apply.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item A paired OPEN $O_z$ preserves the one-front offset and sends
      $\Omega$ to $\Omega+az$.
\item If $Q=q::Q'$ and the attacker closes $q$ in the short copy, answer by
      closing $a$ in the long copy.  The copies exchange roles, the offset
      token changes from $a$ to $q$, and $\Omega$ is unchanged.
\item If the attacker closes $a$ in the long copy, the two public states
      diagonalize.  Their vector-moment difference at the diagonal is
      exactly $\Omega$.
\end{enumerate}
At $Q=()$, a paired $O_z$ creates precisely the singleton ko wall: the long
copy has queue $(a,z)$ and is clear, while the short copy has queue $(z)$ and
ko set.  There are two continuations.  Another paired $O_w$ restores clear
offset states and contributes the ordinary updates $az+aw$.  If instead the
attacker closes $a$ in the long copy, choose the same defender OPEN $O_w$ in
both copies.  Relative to their common predecessor the suffixes are
\[
 C_a,O_z,O_w\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad O_z,C_a,O_w;
\]
they reconverge diagonally and differ by the frozen wall edge $az$.  When no
such $w$ remains after $O_z$, the forced pass and the two terminal CLOSEs form
a separate zero-charge drain.  Every ladder is finite.  Its endpoint holonomy
is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:offset-ladder-holonomy}
 \Omega_{\rm initial}+\sum_i a_i z_i,
\end{equation}
where $a_i$ is the current offset token when $O_{z_i}$ crosses the offset.
This includes the first wall OPEN and excludes the common repair OPEN after
the token has closed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An OPEN removes $z$ from $U$ and changes no accumulated moment, so
$aU$ changes by $az$.  In the second rule the two CLOSEs add respectively
$qU$ and $aU$ to $\Delta$; after exchanging the long and short copies the
new correction is $qU$, and the three new terms cancel to leave the old
$\Omega$.  In the third rule the long $C_a$ adds $aU$ to $\Delta$, giving
the displayed diagonal difference.  At the empty-$Q$ wall, two successive
paired OPENs apply the first rule twice.  The close branch is exactly the
three-event singleton-wall reconvergence square, including its edge label;
the terminal alternative has empty untouched set after its first OPEN, so no
later CLOSE can add an edge.  Finally each cross-fed event strictly decreases
the sum of the two game ranks, so the process terminates and summing the
token-crossing OPEN updates proves
\eqref{eq:offset-ladder-holonomy}.
\end{proof}

For the predecessor/child copies in (B$'$), the initial $\Omega$ is zero.
Thus paired CLOSEs are flat; every surviving obstruction is an explicit
token--OPEN edge from \eqref{eq:offset-ladder-holonomy}.  An odd family of
ladders incident to the distinguished $C_y$ branch would give a concrete
representation of $b_h$ by the continuation and off-spine generators of
$M_h$.  What is not proved is that arbitrary score-sensitive strategy pruning
gives such a family with even internal incidence.  Scalar minimality only
annihilates the displayed holonomies under $B$; it does not make their vector
sum zero.

\section{The remaining global lemma}

The proof target can now be stated without Gold forms, graph enumeration, or a
particular local menu.

\begin{question}[global FIFO affine contraction]\label{q:contraction}
\emph{Conjecture.} Fix either attacker seat and a deterministic attacker strategy $S$ in
the schedule game with one isolated dummy.  Does there always exist an odd
$\F_2$-flow through the defender branches whose terminal disjointness moment is
zero, with the flow allowed to couple different defender children and different
levels of the front filtration?
\end{question}

An affirmative answer proves the general linking theorem by
Theorems~\ref{thm:affine} and~\ref{thm:front-split}.  The tempting stronger
claim---that projected continuation hulls of different defender children meet
one common coset---is false.  At the $P_4+d$ state above, fix a
history-dependent attacker which follows different continuations after $O_2$
and $O_3$.  Under the front projection $T_0$ to the edge coordinates
$12,13,23$, exact recursion gives the two open-child continuation hulls
\[
  \{0\},
  \qquad
  \Aff\{23,12+23\}.
\]
They are disjoint.  Exhaustive recursion over the smaller states with two real
open children shows that this is the first such childwise failure.

Thus a valid contraction must transport nonzero target cosets and cancel them
\emph{across} defender children; it cannot solve every projected child as an
independent zero-target subproblem.  In particular, an induction classified
only by dummy presence, live-set parity, attacker seat, and front position is
false on reachable states.  The local relations
\[
  s_L(d)=0,
  \qquad
  \sum_{x\in L\cap R}s_L(x)=0
\]
provide the cone and the cut dependence, but not the required global coupling.

There is a second exact encoding of the causal obstruction.  Call a complete
winning line \emph{zero-normalized} when the cumulative flip score is zero
after every defender move (or forced defender pass).

\begin{proposition}[response-factor normal form]
\label{prop:response-factor}
First suppose the history is pass-free.  Let $I$ pair the two event
occurrences $O_v,C_v$ of each vertex, and pair each attacker event with the
immediately following defender event to obtain a response matching $R$.
If the defender moves second, $I\cup R$ is a disjoint union of alternating
even cycles.  If the defender moves first, it is a union of alternating
cycles and one alternating path from the defender's initial OPEN to the
attacker's terminal zero-charge CLOSE.  Projecting $R$ to vertex labels gives
a loopless $2$-regular multigraph in the second-seat case and the analogous
path-plus-cycles factor in the first-seat case.

If a forced pass occurs in the terminal singleton macro $O_v,P,C_v$, delete
that entire macro, retain the actual charge labels on the prefix, and apply
the pass-free statement on $V-\{v\}$.  The vertex $v$ is then a marked
zero-charge terminal singleton outside the factor.  For a second-seat
defender the prefix again gives cycles.  For a first-seat defender it gives
cycles plus a path from the initial OPEN to the zero-charge attacker CLOSE
immediately preceding the deleted macro, unless the prefix is empty, in which
case only the marked singleton remains.

A response edge has zero normalization defect exactly when it is an
OPEN--OPEN edge, a CLOSE--CLOSE edge whose two charges agree, or a mixed edge
whose unique CLOSE has charge zero.  Thus a complete zero-normalized line is
equivalently a causal charge-balanced response factor of the stated type,
together with the marked zero terminal singleton when a pass occurs.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both $I$ and, in the second-seat case, $R$ are perfect matchings on the $2N$
event occurrences, so their union consists of alternating even cycles.  In
the first-seat case $R$ leaves only the initial and terminal occurrences
unmatched, producing one alternating path in addition to cycles.  Every
vertex has its OPEN and CLOSE occurrence in $I$ and therefore two projected
$R$ incidences, except at those path endpoints.  A projected loop would
require two consecutive legal events on the same vertex, which FIFO and ko
forbid.  Immediately before every attacker--defender response round the
cumulative score is zero.  XORing the two event charges gives precisely the
three cases stated.  The unmatched initial event is an OPEN, and terminal
victory makes the unmatched final CLOSE charge zero.

Contracting an OPEN--pass--CLOSE tail would not preserve the matching: its
OPEN and CLOSE belong to the same player.  Deleting all three moves instead
leaves a pass-free complete prefix.  If the defender moves second, both
deleted touch events belonged to the attacker and the prefix matchings are
perfect.  If the defender moves first, both belonged to the defender; the
preceding attacker CLOSE becomes the path endpoint, and its charge is zero
because the following defender OPEN had to leave the cumulative score
normalized.  The deleted macro itself has zero charge, proving the pass case.
\end{proof}

The matching reformulation does not turn the causal factors into the perfect
matchings of a static graph.  This failure already occurs against a
close-first attacker on four labels.  Consider the two legal leaves
\begin{align*}
 h_1&=O_0O_1C_0O_2C_1O_3C_2C_3,\\
 h_2&=O_0O_2C_0O_1C_2O_3C_1C_3.
\end{align*}
Their response matchings are
\begin{align*}
 M_1&=\{O_0O_1,C_0O_2,C_1O_3,C_2C_3\},\\
 M_2&=\{O_0O_2,C_0O_1,C_2O_3,C_1C_3\}.
\end{align*}
The symmetric difference is two independent alternating four-cycles.  A
perfect-matching pivot may flip either cycle separately, but neither mixed
factor is causal.  Following the first mixed factor gives
$O_0O_2C_0O_1C_2$ and then demands the response $C_3$ before vertex $3$ has
opened; the other mixed factor fails symmetrically after
$O_0O_1C_0O_2C_1$.  Thus compatible response factors are not exchange-closed,
even inside this close-first policy.

Nor does the directed response factor determine the payoff after chronology
is forgotten.  The histories
\[
 O_0O_1O_2O_3C_0C_1C_2C_3,
 \qquad
 O_0O_1C_0C_1O_2O_3C_2C_3
\]
have the same directed factor
$\{O_0\to O_1,O_2\to O_3,C_0\to C_1,C_2\to C_3\}$.
Taking $3$ as the dummy, their real disjointness moments are respectively
$0$ and $02+12$.  Consequently a factor-only edge weight cannot carry $D$;
retaining the attacker policy and chronology restores the label but destroys
alternating-cycle exchange.  Ordinary Pfaffian or complementary-pivoting
parity therefore cannot supply Proposition~\ref{prop:response-factor}'s
missing causal extension without additional history-decorated cells.

\emph{Computational evidence.} Exact minimax finds such zero-normalized strategies after adjoining
the isolated dummy for every graph isomorphism class through eight real
vertices, for either defender seat.  In the complete order-eight census the
following sharper root selectors also survive: a first-seat defender may open
$d$; after a second-seat defender observes $O_x$ on a real vertex, some real
same-degree-parity $O_y$ is safe; and after $O_d$ some real OPEN is safe.
These are finite certificates, not a factor-extension proof.  Static routing
to the dummy is already false on small reachable checkpoints: a phase pivot
may first terminate at an unmatched untouched vertex.

The local choice at a normalized ordered cell makes the missing extension
especially explicit.  Suppose $Q=(x,y,Q_0)$, $|U|$ is even, and
$\deg_U(x)=\deg_U(y)=a$.  Thus both $\eta_{xy}$ fibres are even.  After an
attacker OPEN $O_z$, the front charges are
\[
 \bigl(a+B(x,z),\ a+B(y,z)\bigr).
\]
If the first bit is zero, $C_x$ is a legal normalized phase pivot to queue
$(y,Q_0,z)$; if the bits are $11$, that pivot is forbidden, while a same-$\eta$
OPEN reply remains in the balanced-front corridor; and a $10$ or $01$ pair
is a transported defect endpoint.  An OPEN--OPEN reply $O_w$ preserves
equality of the eventual consecutive $C_x,C_y$ charges exactly when
$B(x+y,z+w)=0$; further same-$\eta$ pairs preserve it again.  The
same-curvature wall supplies an odd fan of such $w$,
while Proposition~\ref{prop:response-factor} says what its one-vertex causal
pivots must ultimately assemble.  The unresolved theorem is precisely that
every attacker-pruned partial factor admits this balanced extension; neither
the cell handshake nor the static dummy endpoint proves it.

At the isolated-dummy root this can be sharpened to an odd corridor whose only
uncontrolled endpoint is the dummy branch.  The statement is for the stronger
zero-normalized target and even real order, the case used by the exact root
selectors.

\begin{proposition}[least-root odd corridor]
\label{prop:root-corridor}
Let $G$ have an even real vertex set $R$ and isolated $d$, and
assume every smaller even-real isolated-dummy board is zero-normalized safe.
Fix a real opener $x$ and put
\[
 Y_x=\{y\in R-\{x\}:\deg_G(y)=\deg_G(x)\pmod2\}.
\]
Then $|Y_x|$ is odd.  If every checkpoint $Q=(x,y)$ with $y\in Y_x$ is
unsafe, each has an OPEN as its first spoiler.  Moreover every real spoiler
extends to a finite odd-augmentation corridor of unsafe same-$\eta_{xy}$
OPEN pairs.  The only corridor exits are
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item consecutive CLOSEs $C_x,C_y$ with equal charge;
\item a zero-charge phase pivot $O_z,C_x$; or
\item the attacker opening $d$.
\end{enumerate}
Summing the corridors over the odd set $Y_x$ gives an odd aggregate exit
chain.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both degree-parity classes of an even graph have even size, by handshaking,
so $|Y_x|$ is odd.  At $Q=(x,y)$ the two front charges agree.  If the attacker
closes $x$, the defender closes $y$ with the same charge and reaches exactly
the smaller root on $G-\{x,y\}$ with $d$ still isolated.  Minimality makes
that child safe, so the first spoiler is an OPEN.

Initially $U$ has odd size and contains $d$.  Equal front charges make the
$\eta=1$ fibre even; the $\eta=0$ fibre is odd and contains $d$.  If a real
spoiler opens $z$, the number of remaining \emph{real} vertices in its own
$\eta$ fibre is odd: in the one-fibre remove $z$ from an even real set, while
in the zero-fibre first remove $d$ from an odd set and then remove $z$.
Every corresponding real OPEN reply is normalized, hence unsafe under the
chosen spoiler, and removes two vertices of one fibre.  It preserves equal
front charges, odd $|U|$, and the untouched dummy, so the construction
iterates.  Each round removes two real untouched vertices.  It must therefore
terminate at a CLOSE--CLOSE exit, at an OPEN after which $C_x$ has charge zero,
or when the attacker selects $d$.  Every internal fan and the outer $Y_x$
fan have odd augmentation, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The proposition isolates a precise parity leak rather than closing the root
induction.  After $O_d$ there are $|R|-2$ real OPEN replies, an even fan, and
$C_x$ is an additional normalized reply exactly when the common initial front
charge is zero.  Exact minimax through the complete eight-real census finds
that $O_d$ is never a spoiler at an unsafe same-parity root, but no fan-level
proof is known.  Pairwise dummy diamonds are insufficient: the checker has a
root for which a real-spoiler child crossed with $d$ is itself unsafe, although
other real replies make the actual $O_d$ branch safe.

One unbounded attacker class can nevertheless be contracted completely.  It
is the class used below to generate the causal simplex caps, so the result is
not a bounded-support statement in disguise.  Call an attacker
\emph{close-first} if it closes the front whenever that is legal and opens
only when ko or an empty queue forbids a close.  Write $J_R$ for the all-edge
chain on the real vertices, and for an ordering $P$ of $R\sqcup\{d\}$ write
$P_R$ and $P_R^2$ for the real-edge traces of the path and its square.

\begin{theorem}[complete close-first contraction]
\label{thm:close-first}
For every number of real vertices, every close-first attacker admits
an odd compatible response chain with zero terminal disjointness moment.  This
holds whether the attacker occupies the first or the second seat.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that the attacker moves first and opens a real vertex $p$.
The defender opens $v_2$ and thereafter, following every attacker close,
opens an arbitrary untouched vertex; after the last opening the remaining
fronts close.  Every Hamiltonian ordering
\[
 P=(p,v_2,\ldots,v_N)
\]
is therefore compatible.  Its overlap graph is the path $P_R$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:close-first-path}
 D_R=J_R+P_R.
\end{equation}
The affine hull of these path traces is the whole $E(K_R)$.  Indeed, if a
real edge weighting $A$ has constant path sum, compare two orders which are
identical except for the local segments
\[
 a,d,x,b,\qquad a,x,d,b.
\]
Their difference gives $A_{xb}=A_{ax}$.  Varying the three real vertices
makes every real edge carry one common value $t$.  Putting the dummy at an
endpoint or in the interior changes the number of real path edges by one, so
$t=0$.  The cases with at most three real vertices follow directly; for
three, the three traces from
\[
 (p,x,y,d),\quad(p,y,x,d),\quad(p,d,x,y)
\]
XOR to $J_R$.  Hence $J_R$ is an odd affine sum of the $P_R$, and
\eqref{eq:close-first-path} gives a zero sum of the corresponding $D_R$.

If $|R|\le1$, a dummy-root game is trivial.  Otherwise, if the first attacker
opener is the dummy, use two compatible response
families.  One-block play realizes every Hamiltonian path on $R$ as the real
overlap graph.  Alternatively, for each real $x$ the defender plays
\[
 O_d,O_x,C_d,C_x.
\]
The queue is empty, so the attacker must open some history-dependent
$y_x\in R-\{x\}$; resuming one-block play realizes an isolated $x$ together
with every Hamiltonian path on $R-\{x\}$ beginning at $y_x$.  Any edge
weighting constant on all Hamiltonian paths of $K_R$ is uniform: comparing
the paths $x,a,y,r,L$ and $y,a,x,r,L$ gives $A_{yr}=A_{xr}$, and varying the
vertices makes $A_e=t$ for every real edge when $|R|\ge4$; equality of the
three two-edge path sums gives the same conclusion when $|R|=3$.  A full
path and an isolate-first path have respectively $|R|-1$ and $|R|-2$ edges,
so constancy on their union forces $t=0$.  Thus this union has full affine
hull and again contains $J_R$.  For $|R|=2$ the two families have traces
$J_R$ and $0$.  This proves the first-seat assertion,
including the dummy-root wall which one-block paths alone would not cover.

Now let the close-first attacker move second.  The case $|R|\le1$ is trivial,
so assume at least two real vertices.  The defender first opens a
vertex $p$; ko forces the attacker to open a vertex $q(p)$; the defender then
always opens while an untouched vertex remains.  Every remaining opening
order is under defender control, and the real overlap graph is $P_R^2$ for a
Hamiltonian order with prefix $(p,q(p))$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:close-first-square}
 D_R=J_R+P_R^2.
\end{equation}
It remains to show that $J_R$ lies in the affine hull of the available square
traces.

First suppose some real $p$ has real $q=q(p)$.  Restrict to that root.  If
$F_A(P)=\langle A,P_R^2\rangle$ is constant on all orders with prefix
$(p,q)$, extend the real-edge weighting $A$ by zero on dummy edges.  Swapping
adjacent tail vertices in the local words
\[
 a,b,x,y,c,e,\qquad a,b,y,x,c,e
\]
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-square-swap}
 A_{ax}+A_{ye}+A_{ay}+A_{xe}=0.
\end{equation}
For $|R|\ge7$, putting $e=d$ and varying the tail shows that all $p$-tail edges have one
value $\alpha$, all $q$-tail edges one value $\beta$, and all tail-tail edges
one value $t$; write $A_{pq}=\gamma$.  Moving $d$ to tail positions
$3,4,N-1,N$ changes the nonconstant part of $F_A$ through
\[
 \gamma+\beta+t,\quad
 \gamma+\alpha+\beta,\quad
 \gamma+\alpha,\quad
 \gamma+\alpha+t.
\]
Constancy forces $\alpha=\beta=t=0$.  The only possible residual functional
is supported on $pq$; its constant value is $\gamma=\langle A,J_R\rangle$.
By affine separation this is exactly the condition
$J_R\in\Aff\{P_R^2:P\text{ begins }(p,q)\}$.  The finitely shorter ranks are witnessed directly by
the odd supports of sizes $1,1,3,5,7$ for $|R|=2,3,4,5,6$; these explicit
orders are checked in the companion experiment by
\path{close_first_low_rank_certificates}.

It remains that $q(p)=d$ for every real root $p$.  Include also the dummy root,
where the forced second opener is some real $q_0$.  Thus the response family
contains all square paths beginning $(p,d)$, for every real $p$, together with
all those beginning $(d,q_0)$.  If $F_A$ is constant on this union, comparing
the two allowed prefixes $(d,q_0,x,y,\ldots)$ and
$(q_0,d,x,y,\ldots)$ gives $A_{q_0y}=0$.  Further adjacent-tail comparisons
are also explicit.  For distinct $p,x,y,z\in W=R-\{q_0\}$, compare
\[
 (p,d,x,q_0,y,z,\ldots),\qquad
 (p,d,q_0,x,y,z,\ldots).
\]
The difference is $A_{px}+A_{xz}$, so every edge inside $W$ has one value
$t$.  On $(d,q_0,w_1,\ldots,w_{|W|})$ the $W$-edge contribution is
$(2|W|-3)t=t$, whereas on an order $(p,d,x,\ldots,q_0)$ with $q_0$
last and all of $W-\{p,x\}$ in the omitted positions, it is
$t+(2(|R|-2)-3)t=0$.  Both values equal the common constant, so $t=0$.
Hence $A=0$, and the
union in fact has full affine hull.  Direct odd supports of sizes $1,1,3$
cover $|R|=2,3,4$ in the same companion function; the comparison applies
from $|R|=5$ onward.  Therefore
$J_R$ is an odd affine sum in every second-seat case, and
\eqref{eq:close-first-square} finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:close-first} closes the policy responsible for the unbounded
caps, but not an arbitrary policy.  It also exposes why a simple monotonicity
argument fails: replacing one forced close by an attacker OPEN prunes the
very adjacent-event diamond which would add the correcting rooted triangle.
A responder restricted to OPEN while untouched vertices remain is already
false on $K_4-e$ plus the dummy; the winning unrestricted reply is a
phase-changing front CLOSE.  Thus neither ``more overlap is easier'' nor a
single-block reduction removes the causal phase problem.

Positional reduction makes the last attempted normalization exact.  Let a
\emph{leafmost deviation} be an attacker state
$s=(U,Q,\mathsf{ko}=0,t)$, with $Q=(f,Q_0)$, at which a positional winning policy chooses
$O_z$ although $C_f$ is legal, and is close-first at every strict descendant.
Suppose replacing that move by $C_f$, while retaining the positional tail,
is losing.

\begin{proposition}[leafmost-deviation spoiler dichotomy]
\label{prop:leafmost-spoiler}
A defender spoiler after the replacement $C_f$ has exactly one of
the following forms.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item It is $O_z$ and $Q_0\ne()$.  Then the two paths
      $O_z;C_f$ and $C_f;O_z$ reconverge publicly, and irreducibility forces
      $B(f,z)=1$.
\item It is $O_z$ and $Q_0=()$.  The two paths agree except that the latter
      has a ko-protected singleton queue (and their score sheets may differ
      by $B(f,z)$); this is the singleton wall.
\item It is $O_w$ for $w\ne z$, producing genuinely different queue orders.
\item It is the close of the new front $g$, when $Q_0=(g,Q_1)$; this is the
      phase-changing two-close spoiler.
\end{enumerate}
A pass cannot be the spoiler.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After $C_f$, the vertex $z$ is still untouched, so an OPEN is legal and a
pass is not.  The displayed OPENs and, when $Q_0$ is nonempty, the close of
its front exhaust the legal moves.  If the spoiler is $O_z$ away from the
singleton wall, Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-holonomy} says that its state
and the winning $O_z;C_f$ state differ only by the score bit $B(f,z)$.
If that bit were zero, positionality would give the same winning
continuation after the replacement, a contradiction.  The remaining cases
are the three exact ways public reconvergence fails: ko at a singleton, a
different append/delete order, or an additional front close.
\end{proof}

The untouched-dummy part of the conditioned tail does admit a complete
contraction, for an arbitrary preloaded queue rather than only a root.

\begin{theorem}[untouched-dummy close-first tail]
\label{thm:untouched-dummy-tail}
Let the defender move from a state with nonempty queue
$Q=(q_1,\ldots,q_L)$ and untouched set $U$ containing the isolated dummy
$d$.  If the attacker is close-first from this state onward, the defender can
force future score zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The defender may choose an arbitrary ordering
$P=(p_1,\ldots,p_m)$ of $U$, always OPENing the next $p_i$.  While untouched
vertices remain, the close-first attacker closes one FIFO front after each
such OPEN.  Once $U$ is empty all later charges vanish.  Directly reading the
front at each round gives the future score
\begin{equation}\label{eq:close-first-tail-permutation}
 F(P)=
 \sum_{\substack{i<j\\i\le L}}B(q_i,p_j)
 +\sum_{a+L<b}B(p_a,p_b).
\end{equation}
It suffices to show that $F(P)=0$ for some permutation.

Suppose instead that $F$ is constant.  Swap adjacent entries $x,y$ in
positions $j,j+1$.  The only changed contributions are those at distance one
across the queue lag, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:close-first-tail-swap}
 F(\cdots xy\cdots)+F(\cdots yx\cdots)
 =B(x+y,a_j+b_j),
\end{equation}
where
\[
 a_j=\begin{cases}q_j,&j\le L,\\p_{j-L},&j>L,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 b_j=\begin{cases}p_{j+L+1},&j+L+1\le m,\\0,&j+L+1>m.
 \end{cases}
\]
Put $y=d$ in \eqref{eq:close-first-tail-swap}; every pairing with $d$
vanishes.

If $m\le L+1$, every $b_j$ vanishes and the equations give
$B(q_j,x)=0$ for every queue row which occurs in the first sum of
\eqref{eq:close-first-tail-permutation}; the second sum is empty.  Hence
$F=0$.  If $m=L+2$, use the final swap $j=L+1$: here
$a_j=p_1$ and $b_j=0$, and arbitrary placement of the other vertices proves
$B|_U=0$.  The swaps $j\le L$ then kill every scoring $q_j$ row.  If
$m\ge L+3$, the final swap again has $a_j\in U,b_j=0$ and proves
$B|_U=0$; the remaining swaps kill the scoring queue rows in the same way.
Thus a constant $F$ is the constant zero.  If $F$ is nonconstant, its
$\F_2$-valued image already contains zero.  In either case choose a
zero-valued ordering $P$ and follow the displayed OPEN strategy.
\end{proof}

The scalar conclusion hides a stronger affine fact.  For disjoint vertex
sets write
\[
 \mathcal E(S)=\Span_{\F_2}\{st:s,t\in S,\ s\ne t\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal E(S,T)=\Span_{\F_2}\{st:s\in S,\ t\in T\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[untouched-dummy close-first full live face]
\label{thm:untouched-dummy-full-face}
In the state of Theorem~\ref{thm:untouched-dummy-tail}, write
$U=\{d\}\sqcup R$ and suppose ko is clear.  For the fixed ordinary
close-first attacker, let $W$ be the even continuation-moment space, projected
to the coordinates which are still live at this state.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:untouched-dummy-full-face}
 W=\mathcal E(R)\oplus\mathcal E(Q,R).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the affine hull of the future terminal moments is the whole
live face.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $X=\mathcal E(R)\oplus\mathcal E(Q,R)$.  It suffices to show that every
linear functional $\ell$ on $X$ which annihilates $W$ is zero.  Such an
$\ell$ has one constant value $c$ on all odd terminal continuation moments,
since the difference of two such moments lies in $W$.  We induct on $|R|$,
uniformly in the nonempty queue $Q=(f,Q_0)$.

Assume first that $|R|\ge2$.  For each $x\in R$, take the defender move
$O_x$; the close-first attacker answers $C_f$.  The child is again a clear
defender state with untouched dummy and real untouched set $R-\{x\}$.
An even chain inside this child is also an even chain at the parent, because
its common prefix has even total coefficient.  By induction the child even
space is its whole live coordinate space.  Therefore $\ell$ vanishes on
\[
 \mathcal E(R-\{x\})
 \oplus\mathcal E(Q_0,R-\{x\})
 \oplus\mathcal E(\{x\},R-\{x\}).
\]
As $x$ varies, this kills every coefficient in $\mathcal E(R)$ and in
$\mathcal E(Q_0,R)$.  The only coefficients which may remain are those of
$fR$.  The charge of the forced $C_f$ in the $O_x$ branch then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:untouched-dummy-full-face-open}
 c=\sum_{u\in R-\{x\}}\ell(fu)
 \qquad(x\in R).
\end{equation}
If the defender instead begins with $C_f$, every later live coordinate has
already been killed, while this close contributes all of $fR$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:untouched-dummy-full-face-close}
 c=\sum_{u\in R}\ell(fu).
\end{equation}
Comparing the two displays gives $\ell(fx)=0$ for every $x$, and then $c=0$.

If $R=\varnothing$, then $X=0$.  Finally let $R=\{u\}$ and write
$Q=(v_0,\ldots,v_{L-1})$.  The defender continuation beginning with $O_u$
has zero live moment, so $c=0$.  For each $0\le k<L$, there is also a legal
continuation in which precisely $v_0,\ldots,v_k$ close before $O_u$: if $k$
is even, begin
\[
O_d,C_{v_0},C_{v_1},\ldots,C_{v_k},O_u,
\]
and if $k$ is odd, omit the initial $O_d$.  In the first word the attacker
takes the even-indexed closes and the defender the odd-indexed ones; in the
second word these roles are reversed.  Thus every attacker move displayed is
its forced close-first reply, every displayed defender close is legal, and
the last close is followed by the defender's $O_u$.  After this prefix
complete the play arbitrarily.  Constancy of $\ell$ now gives
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^k\ell(v_i u)=0
 \qquad(0\le k<L).
\]
Successive differences kill every $\ell(v_i u)$.  Thus the annihilator of
$W$ in $X^*$ is zero in every case, proving \eqref{eq:untouched-dummy-full-face}.
Since the odd continuation set is a nonempty $W$-coset contained in $X$, it
is then all of $X$ as well.
\end{proof}

The word ``live'' is essential.  Coordinates frozen by the prefix are not in
$X$ and need not be absorbable by the tail.  With sole real edge $ab$ and
$c,d$ isolated, the legal prefix
\[
 O_a,O_c,C_a,O_b
\]
reaches $Q=(c,b)$ and $U=\{d\}$ with accumulated moment $ab$.  Every future
charge is zero, so Theorem~\ref{thm:untouched-dummy-full-face} correctly gives
$W=X=0$ but cannot remove the frozen prefix bit.  This is precisely why the
conditioned tail theorem does not by itself close the root ancestry.

Consequently, while the dummy is untouched a leafmost deviation can only
enter a close-first tail on residual target zero.  In the minimum-bad descent,
residual one must first cross to zero through the preceding odd CLOSE; if the
dummy has already been spent, Theorem~\ref{thm:untouched-dummy-tail} no longer
applies.  These are exactly the odd-CLOSE spike and post-dummy domination
boundaries in Proposition~\ref{prop:minimum-bad-predecessor}.

The dummy hypothesis can in fact be removed completely once the tail begins
at a clear defender checkpoint.  This is strictly stronger than the root
close-first contraction: the queue may be arbitrary, and the conclusion is a
single scalar response rather than an affine combination of root histories.

\begin{theorem}[conditioned close-first contraction]
\label{thm:conditioned-close-first}
Let the defender move from a coherent state with nonempty queue,
$\mathsf{ko}=0$, and arbitrary untouched set $U$.  Against an attacker which
CLOSEs whenever CLOSE is legal, the defender has a continuation of future
score zero.  No dummy or parity hypothesis on the live graph is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a close-first attacker and suppose that it forces future score one from
some such defender state.  Among coherent ko-clear defender states with
nonempty queue carrying such a target-one close-first strategy tree, choose a
counterexample of minimum remaining move rank.  Legal moves preserve coherence
and strictly decrease this rank.  Write
\[
 Q=(f,Q_0).
\]
If $U$ is empty, every remaining charge is zero.  If $U=\{x\}$, the defender
opens $x$; the forced $C_f$ and every later close then see empty untouched
set.  Hence $|U|\ge2$.

We first dispose of the only small fibre which is not covered by the uniform
degree comparison below.  Let $U=\{x,y\}$ and write $Q=(f,Q_0)$.  If $f$
misses, say, $y$, the defender opens $x$.  The forced $C_f$ sees only $y$ and
has charge zero; the defender then opens $y$, after which all charges vanish.
We may therefore assume that $f$ sees both $x$ and $y$.

After $O_x,C_f$ the score has changed by one and the defender faces the
singleton untouched set $\{y\}$ with queue $(Q_0,x)$.  It may simply drain
that queue.  The close-first attacker also closes whenever it moves, and
after the queue empties the sole remaining vertex opens, passes if necessary,
and closes at charge zero.  Since the attacker tree forces the score already
present after $C_f$, this defender line gives
\[
 \sum_{v\in Q_0}B(v,y)+B(x,y)=0.
\]
Reversing $x,y$ gives
\[
 \sum_{v\in Q_0}B(v,x)+B(x,y)=0.
\]
Now instead drain the original queue before either untouched vertex opens.
Its total charge is
\[
 B(f,x)+B(f,y)
 +\sum_{v\in Q_0}\bigl(B(v,x)+B(v,y)\bigr)=0:
\]
the front contribution is $1+1$, and the two displayed equations leave two
copies of $B(x,y)$.  Once the queue is empty, the first remaining OPEN creates
ko and forces the other vertex to OPEN before either can close, so all later
charges vanish.  This zero-charge defender continuation contradicts the
target-one tree.  Thus $|U|=2$ cannot be a counterexample.

Assume now $|U|\ge3$.  For every $x\in U$, let the defender open $x$.  The
attacker must close $f$, reaching a smaller clear defender state with
nonempty queue and prefix charge
\[
 c_x=\deg_{U-\{x\}}(f).
\]
If some $c_x$ were zero, the restricted attacker tree would be a smaller
counterexample.  Hence $c_x=1$ for every $x$.  With
$p=\deg_U(f)$ this says
\[
 p+B(f,x)=1\qquad(x\in U).
\]
The alternative $p=1$ would make every $B(f,x)$ zero and hence $p=0$.
Therefore $f$ is universal on $U$, $|U|$ is even, and $p=0$; in particular
$|U|\ge4$.

Suppose first that $Q=(f)$.  The defender closes $f$ at charge zero.  At the
now empty, ko-clear queue the attacker opens some $z$, creating the
ko-protected singleton queue $(z)$; the defender opens an arbitrary $y\ne z$,
and the attacker is forced to close $z$.  Minimality
would require
\[
 \deg_{U-\{z,y\}}(z)=1\qquad(y\ne z),
\]
since a zero charge would expose a smaller counterexample.  Write
\[
 a_y=B(z,y),\qquad \delta=\sum_{u\ne z}B(z,u).
\]
The displayed equations are
$\delta+a_y=1$ for every $y$, so all $a_y$ have one value $b$.  But
$|U|-1$ is odd, whence $\delta=b$ and every displayed charge is actually
zero, a contradiction.

Finally write $Q=(f,h,Q_1)$.  For distinct $x,y\in U$, use the four-move
prefix
\[
 O_x,C_f,O_y,C_h.
\]
The first close has charge one.  If
$e_{xy}=\deg_{U-\{x,y\}}(h)$ were also one, the two charges would cancel and
leave a smaller counterexample.  Thus $e_{xy}=0$ for every pair.  Comparing
two pairs with one common vertex shows that $B(h,-)$ is constant on $U$;
since $|U|$ is even, $\deg_U(h)=0$.  The defender can now play $C_f$ and the
attacker must answer $C_h$; both charges are zero.  If $Q_1$ is nonempty this
is immediately a smaller counterexample.  If $Q_1$ is empty, the defender
opens some $x$ onto the empty queue, creating ko; the attacker is forced to
open some $y\ne x$.  The resulting clear state has queue $(x,y)$, the defender
to move, and smaller rank, so it is again a counterexample.  This final
contradiction proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

The preceding theorem has a useful stopped extension at an empty root.  The
notation below is deliberately different from the universal-full-fan
predicate $P(H)$ used earlier in the repair-forest discussion.

Call an attacker \emph{stopped close-first} if its rules are as follows.  At a
clear node---that is, $Q\ne()$ and $\mathsf{ko}=0$---it must CLOSE when the
current score is zero, while at score one it may STOP and win immediately; if
it declines to STOP, it must still CLOSE.  When ko or an empty queue forbids
CLOSE, it may choose any legal OPEN; at a nonterminal state with no legal move,
the usual forced pass occurs.  The defender retains every ordinary legal move.
A completed play is won by the attacker exactly when its terminal score is
one.  Write $\mathsf P_{\rm stop}(H)$ when the defender wins this stopped game
from score zero, empty queue, and all vertices of $H$ untouched, with the
defender to move.  No dummy is assumed.

For distinct $x,y\in V(H)$, call the ordered pair $x\to y$ \emph{bad} when
the stopped attacker wins from the defender state
\[
 U=V(H)-\{x,y\},\qquad Q=(x,y),\qquad
 \mathsf{ko}=0,\qquad g=0.
\]
All quantities in the next lemma lie in $\F_2$.  Put
\[
 X_z=B(x,z),\quad Y_z=B(y,z),\quad a=B(x,y),
\]
\[
 \alpha=\sum_{z\in U}X_z,\qquad
 \beta=\sum_{z\in U}Y_z,
\]
and, for the full graph $H$,
\[
 p_v=\deg_H(v),\qquad
 b_v=\sum_{w\sim v}p_w,\qquad
 r=\alpha+\beta=p_x+p_y,\qquad
 \epsilon=|V(H)|.
\]

\begin{lemma}[stopped bad-pair fan]
\label{lem:stopped-bad-pair}
Assume $\mathsf P_{\rm stop}(K)$ for every proper induced subgraph $K$ of
$H$, and assume the present lemma for every graph of smaller order.  If
$x\to y$ is bad in $H$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stopped-bad-bits}
 \beta=1,\qquad b_x=1,
 \qquad \alpha=1\Longrightarrow\epsilon=1.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $\alpha=\beta=0$, the defender plays $C_x$, the score-zero attacker is
forced to play $C_y$, and the game returns at score zero to the smaller empty
root on $U$.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stopped-alpha-zero}
 \alpha=0\Longrightarrow\beta=1.
\end{equation}

There is a second elementary absorber.  If some $z\in U$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stopped-three-fibre}
 p_z=r,\qquad X_z+Y_z=r,
\end{equation}
then the defender uses
\[
 O_z,C_x,C_y,C_z,
\]
where the first and third moves are its own.  The first two close charges
agree because
\[
 \alpha+X_z=\beta+Y_z,
\]
and the last charge is $p_z+X_z+Y_z=0$.  The line therefore returns at score
zero to the smaller empty root on $U-\{z\}$.  Hence the fibre
\eqref{eq:stopped-three-fibre} is empty for a bad pair.

The parity of that fibre is the sum over $U$ of
\[
 (p_z+1+r)(X_z+Y_z+1+r).
\]
Now
\[
 \sum_U p_z=\sum_U(X_z+Y_z)=r
\]
and
\[
 \sum_U p_z(X_z+Y_z)=b_x+b_y+ar.
\]
Since $|U|=\epsilon$ modulo two, emptiness gives the exact moment identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stopped-bad-moment}
 b_x+b_y=(1+r)\epsilon+ar.
\end{equation}

It remains to use the complete zero-charge OPEN fan.  Set
\[
 Z=\{z\in U:X_z=\alpha\},
 \qquad c=\sum_{w\in U}X_wY_w.
\]
For every $z\in Z$, the defender move $O_z$ followed by the forced $C_x$
has charge zero and reaches the smaller bad pair $y\to z$ in $H-x$.
Applying the inductive lemma to this child gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stopped-child-beta}
 p_z+\alpha+Y_z=1
 \qquad(z\in Z),
\end{equation}
because the left side is its second-front charge.  The child's source bit is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stopped-child-b}
 b_y^{H-x}=b_y+ap_x+c=1.
\end{equation}
Indeed, deleting $x$ changes the degree bit of every remaining $w$ by
$B(x,w)$.  Finally the child front charge is
$\alpha'_z=\beta+Y_z$.  If $\epsilon=1$, then $H-x$ has even order, so the
inductive implication in \eqref{eq:stopped-bad-bits} forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stopped-child-alpha}
 \epsilon=1\Longrightarrow Y_z=\beta
 \qquad(z\in Z).
\end{equation}

First suppose $Z=\varnothing$.  Then $\alpha=0$: if $\alpha=1$, its odd set
of neighbours in $U$ would be nonempty.  Since no $X_z$ is zero, $x$ is
universal on $U$, and $0=\alpha=|U|=\epsilon$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:stopped-alpha-zero} gives $\beta=1$.  Hence
\[
 p_x=a,\qquad p_y=a+1,
 \qquad \sum_U p_z=p_x+p_y=1.
\]
Because $x$ is universal on $U$,
\[
 b_x=ap_y+\sum_U p_z=a(a+1)+1=1.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:stopped-bad-bits} holds at every terminal domination wall.

Assume now that $Z\ne\varnothing$ and $\alpha=1$.  The set
$Z=N_H(x)\cap U$ has odd cardinality, and
\eqref{eq:stopped-child-beta} says $p_z=Y_z$ on $Z$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stopped-alpha-one-bx}
 b_x=ap_y+\sum_Zp_z=a(1+\beta)+c=ar+c.
\end{equation}
Also $p_x=a+1$, so $ap_x=0$, and
\eqref{eq:stopped-child-b} gives $b_y=1+c$.  Substitution in
\eqref{eq:stopped-bad-moment} yields
\[
 1+ar=(1+r)\epsilon+ar,
\]
or $\beta\epsilon=1$, since $r=1+\beta$.  Thus
$\beta=\epsilon=1$.  Equation \eqref{eq:stopped-child-alpha} now gives
$Y_z=1$ for every $z\in Z$.  Hence $c=1$, while $r=0$, and
\eqref{eq:stopped-alpha-one-bx} gives $b_x=1$.

Finally suppose $Z\ne\varnothing$ and $\alpha=0$.  By
\eqref{eq:stopped-alpha-zero}, $\beta=r=1$.  Here $Z$ is the set of
nonneighbours of $x$ in $U$, and $|Z|\equiv\epsilon\pmod2$.  Summing
\eqref{eq:stopped-child-beta}, now $p_z+Y_z=1$, over $Z$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stopped-alpha-zero-sum}
 b_x+c=\epsilon.
\end{equation}
Explicitly,
\[
 \sum_Zp_z=\sum_U(1+X_z)p_z=r+b_x+ap_y=1+b_x
\]
because $p_y=a+1$, while $\sum_ZY_z=\beta+c=1+c$.
Since $p_x=a$, equation \eqref{eq:stopped-child-b} gives
$b_y=1+a+c$.  Equation \eqref{eq:stopped-bad-moment}, with $r=1$, gives
$b_x+b_y=a$, hence
\[
 b_x+c=1.
\]
Comparison with \eqref{eq:stopped-alpha-zero-sum} yields $\epsilon=1$.
Equation \eqref{eq:stopped-child-alpha} forces $Y_z=1$ on $Z$.  Thus
$\sum_ZY_z=1$; since $\sum_UY_z=\beta=1$, we have $c=0$, and therefore
$b_x=1$.  This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[stopped close-first empty-root contraction]
\label{thm:stopped-close-first-root}
For every finite graph $H$, $\mathsf P_{\rm stop}(H)$ holds.
Equivalently, from a defender-first empty no-dummy root of score zero, no
stopped close-first attacker can force either a score-one STOP or terminal
score one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We prove the theorem and Lemma~\ref{lem:stopped-bad-pair} simultaneously by
strong induction on $|V(H)|$: the lemma at order $n$ uses the theorem and the
lemma only on smaller induced graphs, and then proves the theorem at order
$n$.

The empty graph is terminal at score zero.  On a one-vertex graph the moves
are an OPEN, a forced pass clearing ko, and a zero-charge CLOSE.  Now suppose
$|V(H)|\ge2$ and that $\mathsf P_{\rm stop}(H)$ fails.  After any first
defender OPEN $O_x$, ko forbids CLOSE and the attacker must have some winning
OPEN $O_y$.  Thus every vertex of $H$ has an outgoing bad arc $x\to y$.

Give each vertex the rank
\[
 R(v)=
 \begin{cases}
 2,&(p_v,b_v)=(0,1),\\
 1,&(p_v,b_v)=(1,1),\\
 0,&b_v=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Every bad arc strictly decreases this rank.  If $\alpha=0$, then
$\beta=1$, $a=p_x$, and $p_y=1+p_x$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:stopped-bad-moment}, with $r=1$ and $b_x=1$, gives
$b_y=1+p_x$.  Hence
\[
 (0,1)\longrightarrow(1,1)
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 (1,1)\longrightarrow b=0.
\]
If $\alpha=1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:stopped-bad-pair} gives
$\beta=\epsilon=1$.  Thus $r=0$, $p_y=p_x$, and
\eqref{eq:stopped-bad-moment} gives $b_y=0$.

A finite directed graph in which every vertex has positive outdegree contains
a directed cycle, while strict descent of $R$ forbids one.  This
contradiction proves $\mathsf P_{\rm stop}(H)$ and completes the mutual
induction.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:stopped-close-first-root} closes exactly the empty-root base
left by a STOP normalization.  It does not justify iterating a branchwise
collapse through the odd-CLOSE spike (A) or the singleton ko wall (B$'$) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:minimum-bad-predecessor}.  There the closest
$C_y;O_x$ and $O_x;C_y$ histories differ by the frozen edge $xy$ and by ko
until another real OPEN occurs; descendant close-first spaces cannot in
general absorb that defect.  The unresolved bridge is therefore an odd
affine cancellation across the complete defender fan at the (A)/(B$'$)
ancestor, not another empty-root theorem.

In particular, the poststate of a leafmost attacker OPEN is a clear defender
state with nonempty queue and a close-first attacker tail.  Such a deviation
can force only residual target zero, whether or not the dummy has already
been spent.  The remaining root problem is therefore not the conditioned
tail conjecture stated in the previous draft: it is the transport of those
target-zero deviations across the preceding odd CLOSE.

Neither ``the dummy has already been touched'' nor ``$z$ is the unique
untouched neighbour of $f$'' follows locally.  For the latter, take
$K_{2,2}$ on parts $\{0,1\},\{2,3\}$ after the reachable spent-dummy prefix
$O_dO_1O_0C_d$: at $U=\{2,3\},Q=(1,0)$ the irreducible $O_2$ has a
close-first zero tail although front $1$ has two untouched neighbours.  If
the defender opens $3$, the untouched set is empty; if it closes $1$, the
forced close of $0$ has the same unit charge, and all later charges vanish.
Replacing $O_2$ by $C_1$ is spoiled by $O_2,O_3$, leaving the forced $C_0$
as the sole unit charge.  For the former claim, with sole real edge $01$, the
reachable prefix $O_2O_1C_2$ leaves $U=\{0,d\},Q=(1)$; after $O_0$, either
$O_d$ or $C_1$ leaves only zero charges, while replacing it by $C_1$ has
unit charge and the spoiler $O_d$ makes every later charge zero.  Thus the
isolated dummy remains untouched in this irreducible target-zero example.
Hence the last-deviation program
does not reduce to a local uniqueness lemma.  Theorem~\ref{thm:conditioned-close-first}
closes every post-OPEN close-first tail, but only at residual target one; the
irreducible examples above live on residual target zero after an earlier odd
CLOSE.

This distinction cannot be removed by requiring close-first play only on the
residual-one sheet.  On the graph
\[
 E=\{01,02,12,03\}
\]
consider the clear defender state
\[
 U=\{0,2\},\qquad Q=(1,3),
\]
with residual target one.  There is an odd-forcing attacker policy which
always CLOSEs on residual one and makes exactly one voluntary target-zero
OPEN.  After defender $O_0$ it plays the unit $C_1$; the branches $O_2$ and
$C_3$ are answered respectively by the zero $C_3$ and by $O_2$ in place of
the now-odd $C_0$.  The $O_2$ branch is symmetric, except that defender
$C_3$ has charge one and is followed by the unit $C_2$.  If the defender
initially plays the zero $C_1$, the attacker plays the unit $C_3$; the two
remaining vertices then open across the empty-queue ko wall and all later
charges vanish.  Every defender line has future score one.  The sole
deviation is the target-zero singleton state
$U=\{2\},Q=(0)$, where replacing $O_2$ by the unit $C_0$ is spoiled by the
ko-wall $O_2$.  An isolated dummy may already have been spent along the legal
zero prefix $O_d,O_1,O_3,C_d$.  Thus induction on the last residual switch is
false at a reachable conditioned state; the root ancestry must still couple
different earlier defender branches.

This boundary also explains why the nearest imported techniques do not close
the problem.  A standard strategy-stealing pass must be optional and harmless;
the dummy has two mandatory events which change FIFO legality.  A static
copycat argument requires an involution or a parity-cell partition, absent on
the $64$ finite witnesses above.  Local complementation changes the required
pairing data but is not a legal FIFO move.  Queue layouts supply terminology
for nonnesting but no adversarial parity strategy.

\emph{Computational evidence.} A useful stronger conjecture is false: without the dummy, the
initial mover does not always force even score on even-order graphs.  The first
failures occur at order eight, in exactly seven isomorphism classes:
\verb|GCZN^{|, \verb|GCrU~{|, \verb|GEhvn{|, \verb|GCx}~{|,
\verb|GEjt~{|, \verb|GEl~v{|, and \verb|GUZv^{|.  They are
anti-mover-controlled: the second player can force the target parity, whether
that target is even or odd.  In particular the weaker finite conjecture that a
second-seat even player wins every even-order no-dummy board survives this
screen, but it is not proved.  Bare alternating rank or Witt class cannot
classify these outcomes: $P_3$ and $K_2\sqcup K_1$ have congruent rank-two
alternating adjacency forms over $\F_2$ but different seat outcomes.

The smallest genuine affine cancellation is already a three-history
fan, not a dummy cone.  With real vertices $1,2,3$, let a first-seat attacker open the
dummy and thereafter close whenever possible, otherwise opening the least
untouched vertex.  After the shared prefix $O_d,O_1,C_d$, the defender can

\begin{proposition}[causal simplex caps]
\label{prop:causal-cap}
For every $r\ge4$ there is a reachable defender node below a fixed
first-seat attacker at which the terminal cut-quotient labels project onto
\[
 \mathcal C_r=\{\mathbf1\}\cup
 \{\mathbf1+e_i:i=1,\ldots,r\}\subseteq\F_2^r.
\]
The set $\mathcal C_r$ contains neither zero nor three distinct elements
whose sum is zero.  Moreover any odd terminal response chain with cut-valued
moment has support at least $r$ when $r$ is odd and at least $r+1$ when $r$
is even.  These bounds are unbounded in $r$.  At $r=4$ the bound five is
attained by an explicit compatible response chain.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take real vertices $0,v$ and an $r$-set $U$, together with the isolated dummy
$d$.  Let the first-seat attacker and defender follow the prefix
\[
 A:O_0,\quad D:O_d,\quad A:C_0,\quad D:O_v,\quad A:C_d.
\]
The accumulated disjointness chain is
$\sum_{x\in U\cup\{v\}}0x=\delta\{0\}$, hence vanishes in the cut quotient,
and the resulting state is $Q=(v)$, with untouched set $U$ and the defender
to move.  From then on let the attacker close whenever possible, otherwise
open the least untouched vertex.

Project $T_0D$ onto the star coordinates $vi$, $i\in U$.  If the defender
first closes $v$, its interval is disjoint from every vertex of $U$, giving
$\mathbf1$.  If the defender first opens $i$, the attacker immediately
closes $v$, so $v$ overlaps only $i$ and the label is
$\mathbf1+e_i$.  Later play cannot change these coordinates.  Every listed
first move is legal, so the projected terminal label set is exactly
$\mathcal C_r$.

Put $a=\mathbf1$ and $b_i=\mathbf1+e_i$.  A relation
\[
 \alpha a+\sum_i\beta_i b_i=0
\]
has, on coordinate $j$, the equation
$\alpha+\sum_i\beta_i+\beta_j=0$.  Hence all $\beta_j$ equal one bit $t$.
For $t=0$ the relation is trivial; for $t=1$ it is the unique nonempty
relation, with
\[
 \beta_i=1\ \hbox{for every }i,
 \qquad \alpha=1+r\pmod2.
\]
Its support is therefore $r$ for odd $r$ and $r+1$ for even $r$, in either
case odd.  Since any zero quotient chain projects to a relation in
$\mathcal C_r$, this proves the support lower bound.  For $r\ge4$, sums of
two distinct elements of $\mathcal C_r$ have weight one or two, while its
elements have weight $r-1$ or $r$; this proves the cap assertion.

For attainment at $r=4$, label the untouched vertices $1,2,3,4$, take $v=5$
and $d=6$, and retain the displayed prefix.  The following five defender
continuations are compatible with the close-first attacker:
{\small
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
766&O_3C_5C_3O_1O_2C_1C_2O_4PC_4\\
797&O_2C_5O_4C_2O_3C_4O_1C_3C_1\\
853&O_1C_5O_3C_1O_4C_3O_2C_4C_2\\
861&C_5O_1O_3C_1O_4C_3O_2C_4C_2\\
491&O_4C_5O_1C_4C_1O_2O_3C_2C_3.
\end{array}
\]
}
Here the left column is the binary integer for $T_0D$ in lexicographic edge
order
\[
 12,13,14,15,23,24,25,34,35,45.
\]
Directly,
\[
 766\mathbin\oplus797\mathbin\oplus853\mathbin\oplus861
 \mathbin\oplus491=0.
\]
The schedules and labels are independently checked by
\path{experiments/linking_game.py::close_first_schedule_quotient}.  The lower
bound already proved makes five the exact minimum.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:causal-cap} rules out more than a universal
three-history fan.  No induction can bound the support of a certificate inside
each defender child independently.  A root contraction must couple these
growing caps to a wider collection of earlier sibling branches.

Nor is one arbitrary sibling enough.  \emph{Computational evidence.} At the immediately preceding
checkpoint
\[
 U=\{1,2,3,4,5\},\qquad Q=(6),
\]
with $6$ the dummy and the defender to move, restrict attention to the two
defender children $O_1$ and $O_5$.  Exact backward induction constructs a
history-dependent attacker whose combined terminal image in the $T_0$
quotient is a $52$-point cap: zero is absent and no two distinct labels have
their sum in the image.  The cap and the independent target-safety recursion
are pinned by
\path{experiments/linking_game.py::quotient_target_response_labels}.  Thus the
proposed ancestor rule ``every cap child is broken by each additional
sibling'' is false already at $r=4$.  This is not a root counterexample: the
full defender fan also contains $C_6,O_2,O_3,O_4$, and those branches remain
available to a global odd flow.  It is not an affine counterexample even on
the partial fan, since the cap itself contains the five-circuit
$7\mathbin\oplus11\mathbin\oplus13\mathbin\oplus14\mathbin\oplus15=0$.

The exact ancestor-cap escape rule is false as well, not merely its pairwise
version.  \emph{Computational evidence.} Use five real vertices $0,\ldots,4$, dummy $5$, and the
$T_0$ quotient with bit order $12,13,14,23,24,34$.  One first-seat attacker
has the spine
\[
 A:O_0,\quad D:O_2,\quad A:C_0,\quad
 D:O_1,\quad A:O_5,\quad D:C_2.
\]
The selected child has $U=\{3,4\}$, $Q=(1,5)$, quotient score $1$, and an
attacker continuation with terminal image
\[
 X=\{1,3\},\qquad \{0\}\cup(X+X)=\{0,2\}.
\]
For example, the two compatible continuations
\[

 O_4C_1O_3C_5C_4C_3
\]
have labels $1$ and $3$.  At each of the three preceding defender nodes,
every sibling subtree admits an independently chosen deterministic attacker
continuation whose entire image avoids $\{0,2\}$.  These branchwise
continuations combine into one strategy because the first divergent defender
history separates their subtrees.  The target recursion and all ten sibling
checks are executable in
\path{experiments/linking_game.py::quotient_target_response_labels}.
Consequently there need not be any single ancestor sibling label in
$\{0\}\cup(X+X)$.  This still is not a counterexample to affine contraction:
several sibling images may have a larger odd dependence even though none
contains the proposed one-step escape label.

Proposition~\ref{prop:virtual-open-pruning} proves that even the natural fan
over \emph{all} second openings is too rigid.  The global affine target
survives inside either singleton real branch, so a proof must choose its odd
subset of defender branches using continuation data; neither dummy-edge
contraction nor the full root fan is a valid fixed recipe.

\section{Relation to Gold--Arf normal play}

The reductions in \path{writeups/goldarf.tex} identify the terminal
$\sigma$-charge with the overlap parity of $G$-edges.  Since overlap and
disjointness partition $E(G)$,
\[
  \sigma(h)=|E(G)|+F_G(h)\pmod2.
\]
Therefore an affirmative answer to Question~\ref{q:contraction} would force
$\sigma=|E(G)|$ for every support graph and both seats.

This arbitrary-graph theorem is not load-bearing for the
Gold problem. The deterministic Witt re-framing reduces every loaded
support graph to a matching, where the FIFO theorem is proved, and the
phase-aware claim compiler turns its forced charge into normal play.  Question
\ref{q:contraction} is a strict combinatorial generalization of the FIFO
mechanism rather than a gap in the Gold theorem.

\section{Conclusion and evidence boundary}

The exact reductions in this paper replace the FIFO game by a causal affine
response problem over the binary edge space.  They prove the interval and
live-star identities, the affine separation criterion, the homogeneous-cell
recursion, the cut-space and two-graph decompositions, the correlated cut and
continuation moment, the initial-cut contraction, the stopped close-first
theorem, and the obstruction results for the stronger local induction
templates considered above.  The stopped close-first argument and its
finite-fan absorbers are also kernel-checked in
\path{formal/Ogdoad/Fifo.lean}.

The remaining statement is Conjecture~\ref{q:contraction}: a causal affine
contraction for an arbitrary conditioned tail.  Its difficulty is genuinely
multibranch.  The response factor must transport four-cycle defects across a
complete defender fan while allowing odd wall fans, front-cell deletion,
matching-phase pivots, and frozen ko-wall edges.  The dual descent reduces a
minimal counterexample to an even-$|U|$ singleton phase or an imbalanced
multicell front, but neither ancestry type is yet contracted.

every graph isomorphism class through eight real vertices with an isolated

      winning line uses a phase-changing front CLOSE, so the close-first
      theorem cannot be extended by a one-block monotonicity argument.
\item \TESTED{} The displayed pair of order-eight Euler graphs proves that a
      primary repair forest need not have a cut-valued affine
      response moment uniformly for a secondary Euler functional: the pinned
      attack has $88$ normalized histories, all of secondary moment one.  This
      refutes a graph-independent carrier strengthening, not scalar zero
      normalization.
\item \TESTED{} Ordered same-colour safety and the weaker existence of a safe
      same-colour mate both fail at order eight.  The open-completion lift also
      fails at the displayed safe checkpoint, where a zero close is the unique
      safe response.  These are failures of structural proof templates, not
      counterexamples to the root theorem.
\item \OPEN{} The global FIFO affine-contraction statement
      \eqref{eq:affine-target} is not proved.  Neither the complete finite
      census nor the graph-free affine searches are evidence of a general
      chain contraction.  Theorem~\ref{thm:stopped-close-first-root} closes
      the defender-first stopped empty-root base, but it does not contract an
      arbitrary conditioned stopped tail and therefore does not iterate
      through the (A)/(B$'$) ancestry pattern.  Equivalently at the normalized
      frontier, the missing assertion is a strategy-relative causal extension
      theorem for the charge-balanced response factor, allowing odd wall
      fans, front-cell deletion, matching-phase pivots, and frozen ko-wall
      edges to share their transported four-cycle defects across the complete
      defender fan.  Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-descent} reduces any
      counterexample to an even-$|U|$ singleton phase or an imbalanced
      multicell front, but neither multibranch ancestry phase has yet been
      contracted.
\end{itemize}
