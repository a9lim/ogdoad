\large Exact Order Reductions and the Open \(0/1/4\) Rule}

\begin{abstract}
The finite part of a Kummer carry in Conway's field of ordinal nimbers is
Lenstra's excess \(m_p\), indexed by an odd prime \(p\).  Every presently
certified value belongs to \(\{0,1,4\}\), with the value predicted by the
component structure of \(\operatorname{ord}_p(2)\).  We give an exact
reduction of this rule to four universal multiplicative-order assertions.
They concern, respectively, a synchronized structural norm, a selected
projective class on every ordinary odd Kummer spine, a Gaussian period in a
cubic norm tower, and a corrected norm on the exceptional
\(2\cdot 3^k\) spine.  The reduction retains the full primary valuation in
each finite cyclic group; testing only divisibility of an order by \(p\) is
not sufficient in general.

For each arm we record a concise selected-coordinate normal form.  The
singleton-even part becomes a Conway--Fermat quotient-order problem and a
zero-at-multiples Fibonacci recurrence.  The ordinary arm becomes a
power-residue symbol of one marked cyclotomic unit.  The cubic arm becomes
generation by one recursively selected element of a norm-one torus.  The
exceptional arm becomes irreducibility of an explicit Capelli composition
after a corrected quadratic norm.  In that exceptional column we also obtain
the unconditional level-dependent bound
\(m_\ell<16(3^k)^4\) when \(f(\ell)=2\cdot3^k\) and \(k\geq2\).
We further identify the literal Conway--Fermat points on a fixed
supersingular elliptic curve, prove that the ordinary marked primes form a
unique inert cyclotomic ray, and show that the cubic recursion and the
exceptional auxiliary ancestry are affine forms of one toric Dickson system.
These structures
sharpen the selected phase which remains to be evaluated; they do not force
its nonvanishing.
These are reductions, not proofs of the four universal assertions.  Formal
verification establishes the general
group, norm, and polynomial identities and several finite arithmetic
certificates; its universal exceptional target is a definition rather than
a theorem.  Finite computations are reported separately and are not used as
evidence for a universal conclusion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Conway's nim addition and nim multiplication make the ordinals into a proper
class field of characteristic two.  Its finite subfields are canonically
embedded, and its initial transfinite part is assembled by a sequence of
Kummer extensions \cite{conway1976,lenstra1977,lenstra1978}.  Lenstra's
description isolates, for each odd prime \(p\), a carry
\[
 \alpha_p=\kappa_{f(p)}+m_p,
 \qquad f(p)=\operatorname{ord}_p(2),
\]
where \(\kappa_{f(p)}\) is a structural tower element and the natural number
\(m_p\) is the \emph{finite excess}.  The same construction is part of the
general structural description of ordinal fields \(\mathrm{On}_p\)
\cite{dimuro2011}.

Let \(\mathcal Q(h)\) denote Lenstra's component set for \(\kappa_h\).
The available data suggest the following rigid rule.

\begin{conjecture}[the \(0/1/4\) rule]\label{conj:rule}
For every odd prime \(p\),
\[
 m_p=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&\mathcal Q(f(p))\text{ is not a singleton odd prime power},\\
  4,&f(p)=2\cdot3^k\text{ for some }k\geq1,\\
  1,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
\end{conjecture}

The purpose of this article is to state the exact mathematical frontier of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:four-arms} shows that it is
equivalent to four selected order assertions, denoted \(Z,O,C,D\).  None of
them is known in full.  The word \emph{selected} is essential: each field
contains many elements with the same degree, trace, norm, or generic
cyclotomic shape, while the nim construction chooses one recursively marked
Frobenius orbit.  The open content is the multiplicative order of that orbit.

The main results are organized as follows.  Section~\ref{sec:prelim} gives
the exact power and norm criteria.  Section~\ref{sec:four} states and proves
the four-arm equivalence.  Sections~\ref{sec:z}--\ref{sec:d} give one
lossless normal form for each arm.  Section~\ref{sec:boundedness} separates
the stronger \(0/1/4\) rule from Lenstra's boundedness question.
Section~\ref{sec:verification} states the formal and computational boundary.

\section{Finite-field and tower preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

For a finite algebraic nimber \(x\), write
\[
 d(x)=[\mathbb F_2(x):\mathbb F_2].
\]
The element \(\kappa_h\) and its component set \(\mathcal Q(h)\) are those
of Lenstra's tower decomposition.  In particular, \(\kappa_h\) is the sum of
the selected components \(\kappa_q\), \(q\in\mathcal Q(h)\), and
\(h\mid d(\kappa_h)\).  The excess is equivalently
\[
 m_p=\min\left\{m\geq0:
 \kappa_{f(p)}+m\text{ is not a }p\text{-th power in its defining finite
 field}\right\}.
\]
Finite integers in this formula are identified with their canonical finite
nimbers.  We use standard finite-field facts, including cyclicity of the
multiplicative group, the norm formula, and Capelli's irreducibility lemma,
as collected in \cite{lidlniederreiter1997}.

\begin{proposition}[exact power criterion]\label{prop:power}
Let \(p\) be a prime, let \(p\mid 2^E-1\), and let
\(x\in\mathbb F_{2^E}^{\times}\).  Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(x\) is a \(p\)-th power in \(\mathbb F_{2^E}^{\times}\);
\item \(x^{(2^E-1)/p}=1\);
\item \(\operatorname{ord}(x)\mid(2^E-1)/p\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, \(x\) is not a \(p\)-th power if and only if
\[
 v_p(\operatorname{ord}(x))=v_p(2^E-1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose a generator \(g\) of the cyclic group of order \(2^E-1\) and write
\(x=g^a\).  The congruence \(py\equiv a\pmod{2^E-1}\) is soluble exactly
when \(p\mid a\), which is equivalent to
\(x^{(2^E-1)/p}=1\).  The order formulation follows from
\(x^n=1\) if and only if \(\operatorname{ord}(x)\mid n\).  The final
statement retains the full \(p\)-primary valuation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the valuation cannot be discarded]\label{rem:valuation}
If \(h=f(p)\) and \(h\mid E\), lifting the exponent gives
\[
 v_p(2^E-1)=v_p(2^h-1)+v_p(E/h).
\]
Thus the shortcut
\(p\mid\operatorname{ord}(x)\) is equivalent to nonexistence of a
\(p\)-th root only when the ambient \(p\)-part has valuation one.  Every
statement below uses Proposition~\ref{prop:power}, not the shortcut.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[norm reduction]\label{prop:norm}
Let \(h\mid E\), let \(p\mid2^h-1\), and let
\(x\in\mathbb F_{2^E}^{\times}\).  Then
\[
 x^{(2^E-1)/p}
 =\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb F_{2^E}/\mathbb F_{2^h}}(x)^{(2^h-1)/p}.
\]
In particular, \(x\) is a \(p\)-th power in \(\mathbb F_{2^E}\) if and
only if its norm is a \(p\)-th power in \(\mathbb F_{2^h}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The norm is
\(x^{(2^E-1)/(2^h-1)}\).  Raising it to \((2^h-1)/p\) gives the displayed
identity.  Proposition~\ref{prop:power} gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The last equivalence is also visible on Kummer quotients: extension and norm
multiply the class by the relative degree.  In the applications below, the
relevant relative degrees are prime to the current prime, so no selected
primary coordinate is lost.

\section{The four exact arms}\label{sec:four}

We first define the four order assertions.  For \(h>1\), put
\[
 \Pi_h=
 \prod_{\substack{\ell\text{ odd prime}\\
                   \operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(2)=h}}
 \ell^{v_{\ell}(2^h-1)},
 \qquad
 T_h=\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb F_{2^{d(\kappa_h)}}/
                            \mathbb F_{2^h}}(\kappa_h).
\]
The product \(\Pi_h\) is the complete primary part of
\(\mathbb F_{2^h}^{\times}\) supported at primes for which \(h\) is the
first residue degree.

For an odd prime \(r\), let \(b_r=d(\alpha_r)\), and for \(a\geq1\) put
\[
 x_{r,a}=\kappa_{r^a},\qquad
 F_{r,a}=\mathbb F_{2^{r^ab_r}},\qquad
 y_{r,a}=x_{r,a}+1,
\]
with \(F_{r,0}=\mathbb F_{2^{b_r}}\).  Define the cyclic projective
quotient
\[
 G_{r,a}=F_{r,a}^{\times}/F_{r,a-1}^{\times},
 \qquad
 L_{r,a}=|G_{r,a}|=\Phi_{r^a}(2^{b_r}).
\]

For \(k\geq1\), set \(h=3^k\), let
\(\zeta_k=\kappa_{3^k}\), and define
\[
 \gamma_k=\zeta_k+\zeta_k^{-1}
 \in\mathbb F_{2^h}^{\times}.
\]
Finally, put \(q=2^h\), \(\omega=\zeta_k^h\in\mathbb F_4\), and
\[
 A_k=\zeta_k^2+\zeta_k+\omega,
 \qquad
 M_k=A_k^{q-1},
 \qquad
 \Psi_k=\frac{\Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)}{3}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[four-arm reduction]\label{thm:four-arms}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule} is equivalent to the conjunction of the following
four universal assertions.
\begin{description}
\item[\(Z\).] For every \(h>1\) such that \(\mathcal Q(h)\) is not a
singleton odd prime power,
\[
 \Pi_h\mid\operatorname{ord}(T_h).
\]
\item[\(O\).] For every odd prime \(r\ne3\), every \(a\geq1\), and every
prime \(p\ne r\) dividing \(L_{r,a}\),
\[
 v_p\!\left(\operatorname{ord}
   (y_{r,a}F_{r,a-1}^{\times})\right)=v_p(L_{r,a}).
\]
\item[\(C\).] The element \(\gamma_k\) is primitive in
\(\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}^{\times}\) for every \(k\geq1\).
\item[\(D\).] One has
\[
 \Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k)
 \qquad(k\geq1).
\]
\end{description}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix an odd prime \(p\) and write \(h=f(p)\).  If
\(\mathcal Q(h)\) is not a singleton odd prime power, the proposed value is
zero.  Proposition~\ref{prop:z-support} below identifies the simultaneous
zero claim at residue degree \(h\) with \(Z\).

Suppose next that \(\mathcal Q(h)=\{r^a\}\), where \(r\) is odd.
Lenstra's degree and component formulas
\cite{lenstra1977,lenstra1978} give
\[
 h=r^as,\qquad s\mid b_r,\qquad \kappa_h=x_{r,a}.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-projective} shows that the row primes are exactly the
prime divisors \(p\ne r\) of \(L_{r,a}\), and that \(m_p=1\) is equivalent
to the full primary-order equality in \(O\).

It remains to separate \(r=3\).  Here \(b_3=2\), so
\(h=3^a\) or \(h=2\cdot3^a\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:c-half-angle}
identifies the first case with primitivity of \(\gamma_a\), which is \(C\).
Propositions~\ref{prop:d-lower} and \ref{prop:d-exact} identify the second
case with \(D\).  The cases are disjoint and exhaustive.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[status of the theorem]
Theorem~\ref{thm:four-arms} is an equivalence.  It does not establish any of
the four universal assertions.  The known finite cases and the exact
formalization boundary are stated in Section~\ref{sec:verification}.
\end{remark}

\section{The zero arm}\label{sec:z}

\subsection{Structural norm and synchronized phase}

\begin{proposition}[exact support criterion]\label{prop:z-support}
Let \(h>1\), let \(E=d(\kappa_h)\), and let
\(T_h=\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb F_{2^E}/\mathbb F_{2^h}}(\kappa_h)\).
For an odd prime \(p\) with \(f(p)=h\),
\[
 m_p=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_p(\operatorname{ord}(T_h))=v_p(2^h-1).
\]
Consequently, \(m_p=0\) for every such \(p\) if and only if
\(\Pi_h\mid\operatorname{ord}(T_h)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By definition, \(m_p=0\) exactly when \(\kappa_h\) is not a \(p\)-th
power in \(\mathbb F_{2^E}\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:norm} moves the test
to \(T_h\in\mathbb F_{2^h}\), and Proposition~\ref{prop:power} gives the
valuation equality.  Multiplying the independent primary conditions gives
the final assertion.
\end{proof}

When \(\kappa_h\) has several components, the structural norm is not the
product of separate component norms.  The missing datum is a synchronized
Frobenius phase, made explicit by the following general construction.

\begin{proposition}[synchronized additive resultant]\label{prop:z-phase}
Let \(K=\mathbb F_Q\), let \(p\mid Q-1\), and choose
\(u_i\in L_i=\mathbb F_{Q^{r_i}}\) of degree \(r_i\) over \(K\).
Put
\[
 R=\operatorname{lcm}(r_1,\ldots,r_t),
 \qquad M=\mathbb F_{Q^R},
 \qquad u=\sum_{i=1}^t u_i,
\]
and assume \(u\ne0\) and \(p\nmid R\).  Let
\[
 \Omega=\left(\prod_i\mathbb Z/r_i\mathbb Z\right)
          \big/\langle(1,\ldots,1)\rangle.
\]
For a phase \(\mathbf d=(d_i)\), define
\[
 F_{\mathbf d}(Z)=
 \prod_{j=0}^{R-1}
 \left(Z+\sum_i u_i^{Q^{j+d_i}}\right).
\]
Then \(F_{\mathbf d}\in K[Z]\), it depends only on the class of
\(\mathbf d\), and
\[
 \prod_{0\leq j_i<r_i}
 \left(Z+\sum_i u_i^{Q^{j_i}}\right)
 =\prod_{\mathbf d\in\Omega}F_{\mathbf d}(Z).
\]
The selected common embedding is the zero phase, for which
\[
 F_{\mathbf 0}(0)=\operatorname{N}_{M/K}(u),
 \qquad
 [F_{\mathbf 0}(0)]=R[u]
 \quad\text{in }M^{\times}/M^{\times p}.
\]
Hence \(u\) is a non-\(p\)-th-power if and only if the selected constant
term \(F_{\mathbf 0}(0)\) is a non-\(p\)-th-power.  Separate component
minimal polynomials determine the product over all phases, but not the
selected phase when \(|\Omega|>1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Diagonal translation has free orbits of size \(R\) on the tuples
\((j_i)\).  Frobenius cycles the factors in each orbit, so each orbit product
lies in \(K[Z]\), and partitioning the tuples proves the factorization.  The
zero-phase factors are exactly the \(M/K\)-conjugates of \(u\), giving the
norm.  On the \(p\)-Kummer quotient the norm exponent is
\[
 1+Q+\cdots+Q^{R-1}\equiv R\pmod p.
\]
Since \(p\nmid R\), multiplication by \(R\) is invertible.  Independent
Frobenius shifts of the components preserve every separate minimal polynomial
while translating the phase, which proves the final limitation.
\end{proof}

In the multicomponent part of \(Z\), the relative degrees supplied by
Lenstra's component theorem satisfy the coprimality hypothesis in
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-phase}.  Thus \(Z\) is an order assertion about one
distinguished factor, not about the unmarked additive composed product.

\subsection{The singleton-even Conway--Fermat problem}

Put
\[
 h_n=2^{n+1},\qquad c_n=\kappa_{h_n}=2^{2^n},
 \qquad E_n=\mathbb F_{2^{h_n}},
 \qquad F_n=2^{2^n}+1,
\]
and take \(E_{-1}=\mathbb F_2\).  The Conway tower relation is
\[
 c_n^2+c_n=a_{n-1},
 \qquad a_{n-1}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}c_j.
\]
Let
\[
 \delta_n=\operatorname{ord}
 \bigl(c_nE_{n-1}^{\times}\bigr)
 \quad\text{in }E_n^{\times}/E_{n-1}^{\times}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Conway--Fermat form]\label{prop:z-fermat}
One has \(1<\delta_n\mid F_n\).  The zero arm at every prime divisor of
\(F_n\) is equivalent to
\[
 \delta_n=F_n.
\]
Equivalently, \(c_0c_n\) is primitive in \(E_n\) for every \(n\) if and
only if these equalities hold at every preceding level.  This is the
selected-order problem studied for Conway towers by Popovych
\cite{popovych2018}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The quotient group has order
\[
 \frac{2^{2^{n+1}}-1}{2^{2^n}-1}=F_n,
\]
and \(c_n\notin E_{n-1}\), so its quotient order is a nontrivial divisor.
Every odd prime \(p\mid F_n\) has \(f(p)=2^{n+1}\), and its entire primary
part in \(E_n^{\times}\) lies in the quotient.  Proposition~\ref{prop:power}
therefore gives \(m_p=0\) exactly when the quotient order contains the full
\(p\)-part.  Imposing this at every prime divisor of \(F_n\) is equivalent to
\(\delta_n=F_n\).  The primitive-product equivalence follows because the
successive quotient orders are supported on pairwise coprime Fermat numbers
and \(c_0\) has order three.
\end{proof}

The precise zero-at-multiples coordinate is especially small.  Define
Fibonacci polynomials over \(\mathbb F_2\) by
\[
 S_0(X)=0,\qquad S_1(X)=1,
 \qquad S_{j+2}(X)=S_{j+1}(X)+XS_j(X).
\]

\begin{proposition}[zero-at-multiples normal form]\label{prop:z-fibonacci}
For every \(n\geq0\),
\[
 \delta_n=\min\{j>0:S_j(a_{n-1})=0\}.
\]
In fact,
\[
 S_j(a_{n-1})=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\delta_n\mid j.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \delta_n=F_n
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 S_{F_n/\ell}(a_{n-1})\ne0
 \quad\text{for every prime }\ell\mid F_n.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(c=c_n\), \(a=a_{n-1}=c(c+1)\), and
\(u=(c+1)/c\).  Induction from the recurrence gives the Binet identity
\[
 S_j(a)=c^j+(c+1)^j=c^j(1+u^j).
\]
The quotient-to-norm-one isomorphism
\[
 E_n^{\times}/E_{n-1}^{\times}\longrightarrow
 \ker\operatorname{N}_{E_n/E_{n-1}},
 \qquad zE_{n-1}^{\times}\longmapsto z^{|E_{n-1}^{\times}|},
\]
sends the class of \(c\) to \(u\).  Thus
\(\operatorname{ord}(u)=\delta_n\), and the displayed equivalences follow.
\end{proof}

The recurrence is a lossless encoding of the unknown order, not an order
bound.  Its least zero period is already \(\delta_n\).  In particular,
factorizations, trace identities, or automata derived only from this word
reconstruct the selected order rather than force it to be \(F_n\).  The
classical iterated quadratic tower of Wiedemann has a related norm-one form
but a different recursion, so its order results do not transfer by a
Frobenius identification \cite{wiedemann1988}.  The recent general
Artin--Schreier tower theorem of Cagliero, Herman, and Szechtman proves the
exact product of the relative orders, but its maximality condition remains
precisely that every relative factor \(\delta_j\) be full; it does not prove
\(\delta_j=F_j\) \cite{caglierohermanszechtman2025}.

There is nevertheless an exact elliptic realization of this arm.  It is
useful because it isolates, rather than removes, the remaining Kummer
coordinate.

\begin{proposition}[supersingular realization and pairing boundary]
\label{prop:z-elliptic}
On
\[
 \mathcal E:\quad y^2+y=x^3+x^2
\]
put \(P_n=(c_n,c_{n+1})\).  Then \(P_n\in\mathcal E\), and for \(n\geq2\)
\[
 [F_n]P_n=O.
\]
More precisely, if \(\pi\) is absolute Frobenius and
\(\iota=\pi+[1]\), then
\[
 \iota(x,y)=(x+1,y+x+1),\qquad
 \iota^2=[-1],\qquad
 \pi^4=[-4].
\]
For
\[
 g=\frac{y+1}{y}
\]
one has
\[
 g(P_n)=\frac{c_{n+1}+1}{c_{n+1}},
 \qquad \operatorname{ord}(g(P_n))=\delta_{n+1}.
\]
If \(T=(0,0)\), then \(T\) has order five and
\[
 \operatorname{div}(g)
 =2[-T]+[2T]-2[T]-[-2T].
\]
Thus \(g\) is the quotient of the two fixed doubling Miller functions at
\(-T\) and \(T\), but it is not a Weil or reduced-Tate pairing function
attached to the \(F_n\)-torsion point \(P_n\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Conway recursion gives
\[
 c_{n+1}^2+c_{n+1}=a_n
 =c_n(c_n^2+c_n)=c_n^3+c_n^2,
\]
so \(P_n\) lies on \(\mathcal E\).  The displayed coordinate formula for
\(\iota(P)=\pi(P)+P\) follows from the chord law; applying it twice gives
the inverse \((x,y)\mapsto(x,y+1)\).  Hence
\(\pi^2+2\pi+2=0\) in \(\operatorname{End}(\mathcal E)\), and squaring this
relation gives \(\pi^4=[-4]\).

Put \(H=2^{n+1}\).  The \(2^H\)-Frobenius fixes \(c_n\) and sends
\(c_{n+1}\) to \(c_{n+1}+1\), so it sends \(P_n\) to \(-P_n\).
For \(n\geq2\),
\[
 \pi^H=(\pi^4)^{2^{n-1}}=[2^{2^n}],
\]
and therefore \([2^{2^n}+1]P_n=O\).

The formula for \(g(P_n)\) and Proposition~\ref{prop:z-fibonacci} give its
order.  The five rational points of \(\mathcal E/\mathbb F_2\) show that
\(T\) has order five.  Direct intersection with the two horizontal lines
gives
\[
 \operatorname{div}(y)=2[T]+[-2T]-3[O],\qquad
 \operatorname{div}(y+1)=2[-T]+[2T]-3[O],
\]
and subtraction gives the divisor of \(g\).  Equivalently,
\(g=f_{2,-T}/f_{2,T}\) for the \(\mathbb F_2\)-rational functions normalized
at \(O\).  Its divisor has coefficients \(2,1,-2,-1\) at fixed rational
five-torsion, so it cannot be an \(F_n\)-Miller function attached to \(P_n\),
even modulo an \(F_n\)-th power.  Any Weil or reduced Tate pairing on
\(\mathcal E[F_n]\) takes values in \(\mu_{F_n}\), whereas \(g(P_n)\) has
order dividing \(F_{n+1}\).
The Fermat numbers are pairwise coprime and \(\delta_{n+1}>1\), so these
values cannot agree.
\end{proof}

The proposition supplies exact CM annihilator control of the literal Conway
point, but generator status still does not follow from annihilation by
\(F_n\).  For \(n\geq3\), no elliptic endomorphism can carry \(P_n\) to
\(P_{n-1}\): the order of such an image would divide both coprime numbers
\(F_n\) and \(F_{n-1}\), although \(P_{n-1}\ne O\).  For \(n=2\), the same
conclusion follows from \(\operatorname{ord}(P_1)=3\) and
\(\operatorname{ord}(P_2)\mid17\).  The actual ancestry relation
\(x(P_n)=y(P_{n-1})\) is therefore not an isogeny division tower.  The
unresolved statement remains exactly the next-level value
\(\operatorname{ord}(g(P_n))=F_{n+1}\).

\begin{proposition}[two complete multicomponent levels]\label{prop:z-small}
Assertion \(Z\) holds at \(h=12\) and \(h=24\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At \(h=12\), the selected element is the sum of components of degrees six
and four; take
\[
 (A,B,J)=(\mathbb F_{2^6},\mathbb F_{2^4},\mathbb F_{2^2}).
\]
At \(h=24\), the components have degrees six and eight, but the relevant
maximal proper support fields are
\[
 (A,B,J)=(\mathbb F_{2^{12}},\mathbb F_{2^8},\mathbb F_{2^4}).
\]
The degree-six component lies in \(A\setminus J\), while the degree-eight
component lies in \(B\setminus J\).  In both cases \([A:J]=3\) and
\([B:J]=2\), so multiplication identifies \(A\otimes_JB\) with their
compositum.  The selected sum corresponds to
\(x\otimes1+1\otimes y\), a tensor of rank two, whereas every element of
\(A^{\times}B^{\times}\) has tensor rank one.  Hence the selected class is
nontrivial in
\[
 \mathbb F_{2^h}^{\times}/(A^{\times}B^{\times}).
\]
The quotient orders are, respectively,
\[
 \frac{(2^{12}-1)(2^2-1)}{(2^6-1)(2^4-1)}
   =\Phi_{12}(2)=13,
 \qquad
 \frac{(2^{24}-1)(2^4-1)}{(2^{12}-1)(2^8-1)}
   =\Phi_{24}(2)=241.
\]
Both are prime, so a nontrivial class generates the complete primitive-support
quotient.  Proposition~\ref{prop:z-support} gives \(Z\) at the two levels.
\end{proof}

The universal arm \(Z\) therefore remains the simultaneous generation
problem for the distinguished synchronized norm, including the all-level
Conway--Fermat equalities \(\delta_n=F_n\).

\section{The ordinary odd Kummer arm}\label{sec:o}

Fix an odd prime \(r\), let \(b=b_r=d(\alpha_r)\), and set
\[
 x_0=\alpha_r,
 \qquad x_a=\kappa_{r^a}\ (a\geq1),
 \qquad y_a=x_a+1,
 \qquad F_a=\mathbb F_{2^{r^ab}}.
\]
The Kummer equations are \(x_1^r=\alpha_r\) and
\(x_a^r=x_{a-1}\) for \(a>1\).

\begin{proposition}[projective order criterion]\label{prop:o-projective}
For every \(a\geq1\),
\[
 \operatorname{N}_{F_a/F_{a-1}}(y_a)=y_{a-1}.
\]
The cyclic quotient \(G_{r,a}=F_a^{\times}/F_{a-1}^{\times}\) has order
\[
 L_{r,a}=\frac{2^{r^ab}-1}{2^{r^{a-1}b}-1}
        =\Phi_{r^a}(2^b).
\]
If \(p\ne r\) is a prime divisor of \(L_{r,a}\), then
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_p(2^b)=r^a,
 \qquad f(p)=r^as\text{ for some }s\mid b,
 \qquad \mathcal Q(f(p))=\{r^a\}.
\]
Conversely, every singleton row with component \(r^a\) arises from such a
prime.  For each corresponding row,
\[
 m_p=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_p\!\left(\operatorname{ord}
  (y_aF_{a-1}^{\times})\right)=v_p(L_{r,a}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The relative conjugates of \(x_a\) are \(\xi x_a\),
\(\xi\in\mu_r\).  Evaluating their binomial at one gives the norm identity.
The quotient order follows from the field degrees.  For \(p\ne r\), the
cyclotomic divisor criterion applied to
\(p\mid\Phi_{r^a}(2^b)\) gives
\(\operatorname{ord}_p(2^b)=r^a\).  Writing \(h=f(p)\) then gives
\(h=r^as\) with \(s\mid b\), and Lenstra's minimality and component theorem
give \(\mathcal Q(h)=\{r^a\}\).  The converse is the same argument in
reverse.

The prime \(p\) does not divide \(|F_{a-1}^{\times}|\), so its full primary
contribution lies in the quotient.  The selected element \(x_a\) has order
prime to \(p\), hence is a \(p\)-th power; therefore \(m_p\geq1\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:power} applied to \(y_a\) proves the final equivalence.
\end{proof}

For a particular row \(h=r^as\), \(s\mid b\), the large ambient test may be
reduced to the transverse field \(K=\mathbb F_{2^{r^as}}\):
\[
 \Theta_{r,a,s}
 =\operatorname{N}_{F_a/K}(x_a+1).
\]
Then \(m_p=1\) exactly when the order of \(\Theta_{r,a,s}\) contains the
full \(p\)-part of \(2^{r^as}-1\).  By contrast,
\[
 \operatorname{N}_{F_a/F_{r,0}}(x_a+1)=1+\alpha_r
\]
lies in a group whose order is prime to \(p\).  The lower Kummer norm is
therefore blind to the current primary coordinate; the transverse norm is
not.

\subsection{The marked cyclotomic unit}

Let \(N_a=\operatorname{ord}(x_a)\), choose a primitive complex
\(N_a\)-th root \(\xi_{N_a}\), and choose a prime \(\mathfrak P\) above two
whose residue map sends \(\xi_{N_a}\) to the selected element \(x_a\).
Put \(\varepsilon_a=1-\xi_{N_a}\).  Its reduction is \(1+x_a=y_a\).

\begin{proposition}[selected residue-symbol form]\label{prop:o-symbol}
Let \(p\ne r\) be a prime divisor of \(L_{r,a}\).  In a cyclotomic field
containing \(\mu_{N_a}\) and \(\mu_p\), the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the projective class of \(y_a\) has full \(p\)-primary order;
\item \(y_a\) is not a \(p\)-th power in \(F_a\);
\item the power-residue symbol satisfies
\[
 \left(\frac{\varepsilon_a}{\mathfrak P}\right)_p\ne1;
\]
\item \(\mathfrak P\) does not split in the Kummer extension obtained by
adjoining a \(p\)-th root of \(\varepsilon_a\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first two conditions are Proposition~\ref{prop:o-projective}.  The usual
unramified power-residue formula is
\[
 \left(\frac{\varepsilon_a}{\mathfrak P}\right)_p
 =\overline{\varepsilon_a}^{(|F_a|-1)/p}.
\]
It is nontrivial exactly when the residue is not a \(p\)-th power.  Kummer
theory identifies this symbol with the Frobenius action on a chosen
\(p\)-th root; see \cite{washington1997}.
\end{proof}

This form explains the real difficulty of \(O\).  The conductor and the
unmarked conjugate set are determined by the tower, but the conjecture asks
for one residue symbol at the prime over two selected by Conway ancestry.

\begin{proposition}[the selected prime is a unique inert ray]
\label{prop:o-inert-ray}
Put \(M=\operatorname{ord}(\alpha_r)\).  Choose compatible primitive roots
\(\xi_{N_a}\) with \(\xi_{N_0}\mapsto\alpha_r\) and
\(\xi_{N_a}^r=\xi_{N_{a-1}}\).  Then
\[
 N_a=\operatorname{ord}(x_a)=r^aM,
 \qquad \operatorname{ord}_{N_a}(2)=br^a.
\]
Let \(K_a=\mathbb Q(\xi_{N_a})\), and let \(\mathfrak P_a\mid2\) be the
prime selected by \(\xi_{N_a}\mapsto x_a\).  For \(a\geq1\),
\(\mathfrak P_a\) is the unique prime of \(K_a\) above
\(\mathfrak P_{a-1}\), with relative residue degree \(r\).  Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{N}_{K_a/K_{a-1}}(1-\xi_{N_a})
 =1-\xi_{N_{a-1}}.
\]
If \(p\ne r\) divides \(L_{r,a}\), then the lower residue field has
trivial \(p\)-Kummer quotient.  Consequently the residue-field norm
\[
 F_a^\times/(F_a^\times)^p\longrightarrow
 F_{a-1}^\times/(F_{a-1}^\times)^p,
\]
or equivalently the corresponding finite-field corestriction, sends the
selected current class to zero and imposes no restriction on it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The irreducible Kummer steps \(X^r-x_{a-1}\) increase the \(r\)-primary
part of the root order by one and preserve every other primary part.  This
gives \(N_a=r^aM\).  The defining-field equality
\(\mathbb F_2(x_a)=\mathbb F_{2^{br^a}}\) then gives
\(\operatorname{ord}_{N_a}(2)=br^a\).

The first irreducible binomial also forces \(r\mid M\).  Hence
\([K_a:K_{a-1}]=r\), while the displayed orders show that the residue-degree
ratio at the selected primes is also \(r\).  Since two is unramified in
these odd-conductor cyclotomic fields, the fundamental identity forces one
prime above \(\mathfrak P_{a-1}\), of residue degree \(r\).  Taking the
product over the \(r\)-th roots of unity gives the norm identity.

Finally,
\(p\mid\Phi_{r^a}(2^b)\) but
\(p\nmid2^{br^{a-1}}-1\).  Raising to the \(p\)-th power is therefore an
automorphism of the lower residue-field multiplicative group, so its
\(p\)-Kummer quotient is zero.  The last assertion follows after reduction
at \(\mathfrak P_{a-1}\); no claim is made that the global cyclotomic-unit
class has trivial number-field norm.
\end{proof}

Thus full ancestry leaves no hidden choice of a prime after the birth level:
it determines one compatible inert ray.  What it does not determine is the
new augmentation coordinate of the cyclotomic unit on that ray.  Global
reciprocity only relates the product of the conjugate symbols, which is one,
and ramified conductor formulas at primes above \(p\) determine a different
local character.  A nontrivial ray character may still vanish on the fixed
class of \(\mathfrak P_a\).  The missing theorem is therefore a
selected-prime Artin-symbol nonvanishing statement, not a decomposition,
norm, or conductor calculation.

\begin{proposition}[phase conservation above the birth level]
\label{prop:o-tail}
Fix \(a\), a prime \(p\ne r\) dividing \(L_{r,a}\), and for \(j\geq a\)
put
\[
 \Theta_j=(1+x_j)^{(2^{r^jb}-1)/p}\in\mu_p.
\]
Then
\[
 \Theta_j=\Theta_a\qquad(j\geq a).
\]
Thus passage to later levels of the same Kummer spine creates no new current
\(p\)-coordinate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 S_j=\frac{2^{r^jb}-1}{2^{r^{j-1}b}-1}.
\]
The norm identity gives \((1+x_j)^{S_j}=1+x_{j-1}\), while
\[
 \frac{2^{r^jb}-1}{p}
 =S_j\frac{2^{r^{j-1}b}-1}{p}.
\]
Therefore \(\Theta_j=\Theta_{j-1}\), and induction proves the claim.
\end{proof}

For \(r\ne3\), assertion \(O\) is exactly the universal nontriviality of
these marked symbols at their birth levels.  General lower bounds for orders
in finite fields and cyclotomic constructions do not by themselves evaluate
the selected residue symbol.

\section{The cubic-power arm}\label{sec:c}

Let \(h=3^k\), \(\zeta_k=\kappa_{3^k}\), and
\(\gamma_k=\zeta_k+\zeta_k^{-1}\).

\begin{proposition}[cyclotomic and half-angle structure]
\label{prop:c-half-angle}
The element \(\zeta_k\) is a primitive \(3^{k+1}\)-st root of unity,
\[
 \mathbb F_2(\zeta_k)=\mathbb F_{2^{2h}},
 \qquad
 \mathbb F_2(\gamma_k)=\mathbb F_{2^h}.
\]
If \(s\) is the inverse of two modulo \(3^{k+1}\), then
\[
 \zeta_k+1=\zeta_k^s(\zeta_k^s+\zeta_k^{-s}),
 \qquad
 (\zeta_k^s+\zeta_k^{-s})^2=\gamma_k,
\]
and hence
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\zeta_k+1)
 =3^{k+1}\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k).
\]
The translates \(\zeta_k+2\) and \(\zeta_k+3\) have the same order.
Consequently, for a prime \(p\) with \(f(p)=3^k\), one has
\[
 m_p=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_p(\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k))=v_p(2^h-1).
\]
Assuming \(\gamma_{k-1}\) is primitive, all such rows have excess one exactly
when \(\gamma_k\) is primitive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The defining Kummer equation gives \(\zeta_k^{3^k}=\kappa_2\), a primitive
cube root.  Since two has order \(2\cdot3^k\) modulo \(3^{k+1}\), the
cyclotomic polynomial \(X^{2h}+X^h+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_2\).
The least exponent \(j>0\) with \(2^j\equiv\pm1\pmod{3^{k+1}}\) is \(h\),
which gives the degree of \(\gamma_k\).

The half-angle identities are direct.  Their two factors lie in the coprime
circle and real parts of \(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}^{\times}\), of orders dividing
\(2^h+1\) and \(2^h-1\), respectively.  Squaring preserves odd order, so
the order formula follows.  The other two translates are Frobenius conjugate
to the same decomposition.  Finally, a prime with \(f(p)=h\) cannot divide
the \(3\)-power factor, so Proposition~\ref{prop:power} gives the stated
criterion.
\end{proof}

The selected Gaussian period obeys
\[
 \gamma_k^3+\gamma_k=\gamma_{k-1},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{N}_{\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}/
                        \mathbb F_{2^{3^{k-1}}}}(\gamma_k)=\gamma_{k-1}.
\]
This propagates every old primary factor.  It does not control the new
factor \(\Phi_{3^k}(2)\).

\begin{proposition}[selected norm-one generator]\label{prop:c-eta}
Put
\[
 q_k=2^{3^{k-1}},
 \qquad
 U_k=\ker\!\left(
 \operatorname{N}_{\mathbb F_{q_k^3}/\mathbb F_{q_k}}
 \right),
 \qquad
 \eta_k=\gamma_k^{q_k-1},
\]
and set \(\eta_0=0\).  Then
\[
 |U_k|=q_k^2+q_k+1=\Phi_{3^k}(2),
\qquad
\mathbb F_2(\eta_k)=\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}},
\]
and \(\eta_k\) is the selected root of the irreducible cubic
\[
 X^3+\eta_{k-1}X^2+(\eta_{k-1}+1)X+1.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \operatorname{N}(\eta_k)=1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}(\eta_k)=\eta_{k-1},
 \qquad
 e_2(\eta_k)=\eta_{k-1}+1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 C_k
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C_{k-1}\text{ and }
 \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)=q_k^2+q_k+1,
\]
where \(C_j\) denotes primitivity of \(\gamma_j\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The element \(\eta_k\) has norm one by its definition.  A direct Frobenius
calculation from \(\zeta_k^{q_k}\) gives its displayed cubic; its symmetric
coefficients give the norm, trace, and second elementary symmetric function.
It cannot lie in the base field, since a base-field element of \(U_k\) has
order dividing \(\gcd(q_k-1,3)=1\).  Hence the cubic is irreducible.
Since its quadratic coefficient recovers \(\eta_{k-1}\), induction from
\(\eta_0=0\) gives
\(\mathbb F_2(\eta_k)=\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}\).

The cyclic group \(\mathbb F_{q_k^3}^{\times}\) splits as the product of
\(\mathbb F_{q_k}^{\times}\) and \(U_k\), whose orders are coprime because
\(q_k\equiv2\pmod3\).  The norm determines the old factor of \(\gamma_k\),
while exponentiation by \(q_k-1\) detects the new \(U_k\)-factor without
changing its order.  This proves the last equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[exact Capelli dichotomy]\label{prop:c-capelli-dichotomy}
Let \(Q_k\in\mathbb F_2[X]\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\eta_k\),
and let \(\ell\) be a prime divisor of
\(N_k=\Phi_{3^k}(2)\).  Exactly one of the following occurs:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\eta_k\notin(\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}^\times)^\ell\), and
\(Q_k(X^\ell)\) is irreducible of degree \(3^k\ell\);
\item \(\eta_k\in(\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}^\times)^\ell\), and
\(Q_k(X^\ell)\) is the product of exactly \(\ell\) distinct irreducible
polynomials, each of degree \(3^k\).
\end{enumerate}
In particular, both the successful and failed cases have an odd number of
irreducible factors.  Factor-count parity, and hence any criterion which sees
only that parity, cannot decide the current \(C\)-coordinate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(h=3^k\).  The preceding proposition gives
\(\mathbb F_2(\eta_k)=\mathbb F_{2^h}\), while
\(\ell\mid2^h-1\) gives
\(\mu_\ell\subset\mathbb F_{2^h}\).  Capelli's lemma and the prime-degree
Kummer criterion therefore say that \(Q_k(X^\ell)\) is irreducible exactly
when \(\eta_k\) is not an \(\ell\)-th power.

Suppose instead that \(b^\ell=\eta_k\) in \(\mathbb F_{2^h}\).  Every root
of \(Q_k(X^\ell)\) is an \(\ell\)-th root of a Frobenius conjugate of
\(\eta_k\), hence belongs to \(\mathbb F_{2^h}\), because that field also
contains \(\mu_\ell\).  Such a root has degree at least \(h\) over
\(\mathbb F_2\), since its \(\ell\)-th power has degree \(h\), and therefore
has degree exactly \(h\).  The composition is separable and has degree
\(h\ell\), so it has exactly \(\ell\) distinct irreducible factors, all of
degree \(h\).  Finally \(\ell\) is odd because it divides \(2^h-1\).
\end{proof}

For a prime \(\ell\mid |U_k|\), put
\[
 \Omega_{k,\ell}=\eta_k^{|U_k|/\ell}.
\]
The exact terminal test is
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)=|U_k|
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Omega_{k,\ell}\ne1
 \quad\text{for every prime }\ell\mid |U_k|.
\]
This formulation again retains the full \(\ell\)-primary valuation.  The
minimal polynomial of \(\gamma_k\) is also explicit.  If
\(D_n(X,1)\) is the Dickson polynomial defined by
\(D_n(t+t^{-1},1)=t^n+t^{-n}\), then
\[
 P_k(X)=D_{3^k}(X,1)+1=(X^3+X)^{\circ k}+1
\]
is irreducible of degree \(3^k\), and \(C_k\) says precisely that it is a
primitive polynomial.  Irreducibility is therefore already built into the
tower; primitivity is the open statement.  General lower bounds for
multiplicative orders of Gaussian periods, such as
\cite{ahmadishparlinskivoloch2010}, do not give maximal order for this
recursively selected period.

In fact degree is maximally insensitive here.  Every prime
\(\ell\mid\Phi_{3^k}(2)\) satisfies
\(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(2)=3^k\).  If \(z\ne1\) lies in \(U_k\), choose
such a prime \(\ell\mid\operatorname{ord}(z)\).  Then
\([\mathbb F_2(z):\mathbb F_2]\) is both divisible by
\(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(2)=3^k\) and at most \(3^k\), hence equals
\(3^k\).  Thus even an element of proper order in \(U_k\) has the same full
field degree as a generator.

The recursion in Proposition~\ref{prop:c-eta} is lossless.  It reconstructs
every finite selected truncation up to Frobenius, and Frobenius preserves
multiplicative order.  Trace, norm, and the sparse cubic therefore identify
the correct selected coordinate but do not evaluate \(\Omega_{k,\ell}\).
This is the exact remaining content of arm \(C\).

\section{The exceptional arm}\label{sec:d}

Let \(h=3^k\), \(q=2^h\), \(\zeta=\zeta_k\), and
\(\omega=\zeta^h\in\mathbb F_4\).

\begin{proposition}[unconditional lower bound]\label{prop:d-lower}
If \(p\) is a prime with \(f(p)=2\cdot3^k\), then \(m_p\geq4\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Such a prime divides \(2^h+1\) and divides neither \(3^{k+1}\) nor
\(2^h-1\).  The element \(\zeta\) has order \(3^{k+1}\), while
Proposition~\ref{prop:c-half-angle} gives
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\zeta+m)
 =3^{k+1}\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k)
 \quad(m=1,2,3),
\]
with \(\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k)\mid2^h-1\).  Hence all four translates
\(m=0,1,2,3\) have order prime to \(p\), and each is a \(p\)-th power in its
defining field.  The definition of the excess gives the claim.
\end{proof}

The translate by four lies in the compositum with \(\mathbb F_{16}\), so
its first useful invariant is a quadratic norm rather than an order
calculation in \(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\).

\begin{proposition}[corrected norm]\label{prop:d-norm}
In \(\mathbb F_{2^{4h}}/\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\), the nontrivial relative
Frobenius sends the finite nimber \(4\) to \(5\), and
\[
 \operatorname{N}_{\mathbb F_{2^{4h}}/
                        \mathbb F_{2^{2h}}}(\zeta+4)
 =(\zeta+4)(\zeta+5)
 =\zeta^2+\zeta+\omega=A_k.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The intersection of \(\mathbb F_{16}\) and \(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\) is
\(\mathbb F_4\), so the relative Frobenius is the nontrivial automorphism of
\(\mathbb F_{16}/\mathbb F_4\) and exchanges \(4\) and \(5\).  These are
the two roots of \(Y^2+Y+\omega\); hence \(4\cdot5=4^2+4=\omega\).
Expanding the product proves the formula.
\end{proof}

Define
\[
 R_k(Y)=\operatorname{Res}_X
 \left(X^{2h}+X^h+1,\;Y+X^h+X^2+X\right)
 \in\mathbb F_2[Y].
\]

\begin{theorem}[exact exceptional target]\label{prop:d-exact}
The polynomial \(R_k\) is the irreducible degree-\(2h\) minimal polynomial
of \(A_k\).  The prime divisors of
\[
 \Psi_k=\frac{\Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)}3
\]
are exactly the primes \(\ell\ne3\) with
\(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(2)=2h\), with their full valuations in
\(2^{2h}-1\).  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(m_{\ell}=4\) for every prime \(\ell\) with
\(f(\ell)=2\cdot3^k\);
\item \(\Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k)\), where
\(M_k=A_k^{q-1}\);
\item \(A_k\) is not an \(\ell\)-th power in
\(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\) for every prime \(\ell\mid\Psi_k\);
\item \(R_k(X^{\ell})\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_2\) for every
prime \(\ell\mid\Psi_k\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The element \(\zeta\) has minimal polynomial
\(X^{2h}+X^h+1\).  Suppose \(A_k^{2^j}=A_k\), and reduce
\(a=2^j\) modulo \(3h\).  The resulting relation on \(\zeta\) has support
the symmetric difference of
\[
 \{ah,a,2a\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{h,1,2\}.
\]
Every multiple of \(X^{2h}+X^h+1\) of degree below \(3h\) has support a
disjoint union of triples \(\{r,r+h,r+2h\}\).  If
\(a\equiv2\pmod3\), the support contains \(h,2h\) but not zero.  If
\(a\equiv1\pmod3\), cancellation of the \(h\)-terms leaves at most two
members in each residue class modulo \(h\).  Both are impossible for a
nonempty union of triples.  The support is therefore empty, forcing
\(a=1\).  Thus \(A_k\) has Frobenius orbit length \(2h\), and the displayed
resultant is its irreducible minimal polynomial.

This also verifies the defining field used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:d-norm}.  Put \(\beta=\zeta+4\).  The identity
\(A_k=\operatorname{N}(\beta)\) puts \(A_k\) in
\(\mathbb F_2(\beta)\), so the degree just proved shows that
\(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\subseteq\mathbb F_2(\beta)\).  On the other hand
\(\beta\notin\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\): otherwise
\(4=\beta+\zeta\) would belong to that field, contrary to
\(\mathbb F_{16}\cap\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}=\mathbb F_4\).  Hence
\(\mathbb F_2(\beta)=\mathbb F_{2^{4h}}\).

Writing \(a_0=2^{3^{k-1}}\), one has
\[
 \Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)=a_0^2-a_0+1\equiv3\pmod9.
\]
After removing its single factor of three, the cyclotomic divisor criterion
gives the asserted prime support and valuations.

Fix \(\ell\mid\Psi_k\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:d-lower} gives
\(m_{\ell}\geq4\).  Propositions~\ref{prop:norm} and
\ref{prop:d-norm} give
\[
 m_{\ell}=4
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A_k\notin(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}^{\times})^{\ell}.
\]
Raising to \(q-1\) is invertible on the current \(\ell\)-Kummer quotient,
because \(q-1\equiv-2\pmod\ell\).  Hence this is equivalent to the full
\(\ell\)-primary part appearing in \(\operatorname{ord}(M_k)\).  Imposing
it for every current prime gives assertion 2.  Finally, \(\mu_{\ell}\) lies
in \(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\), so Kummer theory and Capelli's lemma identify
non-\(\ell\)-power of \(A_k\) with irreducibility of
\(R_k(X^{\ell})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[effective bound on the exceptional column]
\label{prop:d-bound}
Let \(k\geq2\), let \(h=3^k\), and let \(\ell\mid\Psi_k\) be prime.
Then
\[
 m_\ell<16h^4.
\]
More precisely, let \(t\) be the least power of two satisfying
\[
 t\geq\left\lceil\log_2(h-1)\right\rceil.
\]
Then \(m_\ell\leq4^t-1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 K=\mathbb F_{4^h}=\mathbb F_4(\zeta),
 \qquad C_a=\mathbb F_{4^t},
 \qquad L_a=KC_a=\mathbb F_{4^{ht}},
\]
where \(t=2^{a-1}\).  Choose a character
\(\chi:K^\times\to\mu_\ell\) of exact order \(\ell\), and define
\[
 \chi_a=\chi\circ\operatorname{N}_{L_a/K},
 \qquad
 S_{a,k,\ell}=\sum_{c\in C_a}\chi_a(\zeta+c).
\]
Because \(h\) is odd and \(t\) is a power of two, the fields \(K\) and
\(C_a\) are linearly disjoint over \(\mathbb F_4\), and
\([L_a:K]=t\).  This degree is prime to \(\ell\), so norm induces an
isomorphism on \(\ell\)-Kummer quotients.  In particular, \(\chi_a\) has
kernel \((L_a^\times)^\ell\).  No term \(\zeta+c\) vanishes, and equality
in the complex triangle inequality gives
\[
 \zeta+C_a\subseteq(L_a^\times)^\ell
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 S_{a,k,\ell}=4^t.
\]

The character \(\chi\) is the primitive even Dirichlet character modulo
\(X^h+\omega\) over \(\mathbb F_4[X]\).  Write its \(L\)-polynomial as
\[
 L_\chi(T)=(1-T)\prod_{j=1}^{h-2}(1-\alpha_jT).
\]
The function-field Riemann hypothesis gives
\(\lvert\alpha_j\rvert=2\), and constant-field extension raises each
reciprocal root to its \(t\)-th power \cite[Chapters 4, 8, and
9]{rosen2002}.  Comparing the degree-one coefficient after extension gives
\[
 S_{a,k,\ell}
 =-\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{h-2}\alpha_j^t\right),
 \qquad
 \lvert S_{a,k,\ell}\rvert\leq1+(h-2)2^t.
\]
If \(2^t\geq h-1\), then
\[
 1+(h-2)2^t<4^t.
\]
Thus some \(c\in C_a\) makes \(\zeta+c\) a non-\(\ell\)-th power.
Kummer-quotient injectivity from its defining field to \(L_a\) shows that
the same is true in the defining field.  The elements of \(C_a\) are exactly
the finite nimbers below \(4^t\), so \(m_\ell\leq4^t-1\).

Finally, the least power of two \(t\) above
\(\lceil\log_2(h-1)\rceil\) satisfies
\[
 t<2(\log_2h+1),
\]
and hence \(4^t<16h^4\).  This proves the stated bound.
\end{proof}

The open statement \(D\) is therefore the family
\[
 D'_k:\qquad \Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k).
\]
It is a current-level assertion, not a cumulative divisibility statement.
The factor \(A_k\) in Proposition~\ref{prop:d-norm} is also essential:
replacing it by a scalar product of separate translates gives the wrong norm.

\begin{proposition}[prime current factor]\label{prop:d-prime}
Let \(k\geq2\), put
\[
 s=3^{k-1},\qquad a=2^s,\qquad
 \Lambda_k=a^2-a+1=3\Psi_k,
\]
and define \(P_k=M_k^{a+1}\).  Then
\[
 P_k^{\Lambda_k}=1,\qquad
 [\mathbb F_2(P_k):\mathbb F_2]=2\cdot3^k,
\]
and exponentiation by \(a+1\) preserves every primary part supported on
\(\Psi_k\).  Consequently \(D'_k\) holds whenever \(\Psi_k\) is prime.
In particular, \(\Psi_2=19\) and \(\Psi_3=87211\) are prime, so
\(D'_2\) and \(D'_3\) hold.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(h=3^k\) and \(q=a^3=2^h\).  Since
\[
 q+1=(a+1)(a^2-a+1),
\]
the norm-one relation \(M_k^{q+1}=1\) gives
\(P_k^{\Lambda_k}=1\).  Moreover,
\(\gcd(\Lambda_k,a+1)=3\) and \(v_3(\Lambda_k)=1\), hence
\(\gcd(\Psi_k,a+1)=1\).  This proves the assertion about primary parts.

The congruences
\[
 a\equiv h-1,\qquad a^2\equiv h+1,\qquad
 a^3\equiv-1,\qquad a^4\equiv2h+1
 \pmod{3h}
\]
give, by direct Frobenius expansion,
\[
 P_k=
 \frac{\zeta^4+\zeta+\omega}
      {\omega^2\zeta^4+\zeta^3+1}.
\]
Also \(P_k^q=P_k^{-1}\).  If \(P_k\in\mathbb F_q\), then \(P_k=1\);
cross-multiplication would give
\[
 \zeta^{h+4}+\zeta^h+\zeta^3+\zeta+1=0.
\]
If \(P_k\in\mathbb F_{a^2}\), its order divides
\(\gcd(\Lambda_k,a^2-1)=3\), so \(P_k\in\{1,\omega,\omega^2\}\).
The two remaining values similarly give
\[
 \zeta^{h+3}+\zeta=0
\]
or
\[
 \zeta^{h+4}+\zeta^{h+3}+\zeta^4+\zeta^3+\zeta+1=0.
\]
Each displayed polynomial is nonzero and has degree below \(2h\), contradicting
the minimal polynomial \(X^{2h}+X^h+1\) of \(\zeta\).

Every proper subfield of \(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\) lies in
\(\mathbb F_q\) or \(\mathbb F_{a^2}\).  Thus \(P_k\) has degree \(2h\).
If \(\Psi_k\) is prime, its order, a divisor of \(3\Psi_k\), cannot divide
three and must therefore contain \(\Psi_k\).  The primary-part assertion
transfers this divisibility to \(\operatorname{ord}(M_k)\).
\end{proof}

The cubic and exceptional current factors are coprime:
\[
 \gcd\bigl(\Phi_{3^k}(2),\Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)\bigr)=1.
\]
Thus arm \(C\) and arm \(D\) occupy distinct primary coordinates.  Norm
recursion in the cubic tower cannot by itself transfer the missing order from
one arm to the other.  Their common Conway ancestry is relevant only through
the explicitly selected additive coefficients.

\begin{proposition}[common toric cubic dynamics]
\label{prop:cd-toric}
Over a field of characteristic two, for \(z\ne0\) put
\[
 \varpi(z)=z+z^{-1},\qquad D(X)=X^3+X,
 \qquad F_c(U)=U^3+cU^2+cU,
\]
where \(c^2+c=1\).  Then
\[
 D(\varpi(z))=\varpi(z^3),
 \qquad
 F_c(U)=D(U+c)+c^2,
\]
and
\[
 F_{c^2}(F_c(U))=D(D(U+c))+c.
\]
In the literal cubic tower choose alternating \(c_j\in\mathbb F_4\) with
\(c_j^2+c_j=1\) and \(c_{j-1}=c_j^2\), and put
\(u_j=\gamma_j+c_j\).  Then
\[
 F_{c_j}(u_j)=u_{j-1}.
\]
Consequently the selected \(C\) ancestry is the inversion quotient of the
\(3\)-division tree on \(\mathbb G_m\), while this auxiliary recursion used
in the analysis of \(D\) is a two-periodic affine coordinate on the same
toric tree.  This does not identify \(u_k\) with the exceptional target
\(A_k\), whose Kummer class remains the one in
Theorem~\ref{prop:d-exact}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expanding \((z+z^{-1})^3+(z+z^{-1})\) in characteristic two leaves
\(z^3+z^{-3}\).  The second identity is another direct expansion using
\(c^2+c=1\), which also gives \(c^3=1\).  Applying it first with \(c\) and
then with \(c^2\), and using \(c^4=c\), gives the third identity.
Finally, \(\gamma_j^3+\gamma_j=\gamma_{j-1}\), so the second identity gives
\[
 F_{c_j}(u_j)=D(\gamma_j)+c_j^2
 =\gamma_{j-1}+c_{j-1}=u_{j-1}.
\]
\end{proof}

This exact identification rules out direct identification with several
tempting division systems.  The map
\(D\) is not additive:
\[
 D(X+Y)-D(X)-D(Y)=XY(X+Y),
\]
and its degree is three, whereas a nonconstant Drinfeld or Carlitz
endomorphism in characteristic two is an additive polynomial of
power-of-two degree.  Nor is this a smooth elliptic Latt\`es system: infinity
is a totally invariant exceptional point of the polynomial \(D\), while a
finite quotient of a separable elliptic isogeny of degree greater than one has no
exceptional point, since the inverse images upstairs grow with every
iterate and quotient fibres stay bounded.  The relevant algebraic group is
\(\mathbb G_m\), the smooth group of a nodal cubic, not a hidden smooth
elliptic division tower.

The natural characteristic-zero Dickson lift \(X^3-3X\) has the same
semiconjugacy with cubing.  The selected \(C\)-preimages come from roots of
unity and therefore are preperiodic and have canonical height zero.  The
forward-orbit primitive-divisor theorem of Ingram and Silverman assumes an
infinite orbit and therefore does not apply \cite{ingramsilverman2009}.
More fundamentally, these viewpoints still end at the same selected values
\(\Omega_{k,\ell}\); none evaluates them, and the exceptional target remains
separate as stated above.

\section{Boundedness and the remaining theorem}\label{sec:boundedness}

Lenstra asked whether the finite excesses are absolutely bounded
\cite{lenstra1977}.  Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule} would give the bound four,
but boundedness itself is weaker than any maximal-order assertion above.

For \(a\geq0\), let
\[
 C_a=\mathbb F_{2^{2^a}}
\]
be the complete finite Conway subfield at that level.  For an odd prime
\(p\), put \(x_p=\kappa_{f(p)}\) and
\(L_{p,a}=\mathbb F_2(x_p)C_a\).

\begin{proposition}[finite-line criterion for absolute boundedness]
\label{prop:bounded}
For every \(p\) and \(a\),
\[
 m_p<2^{2^a}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{there exists }c\in C_a\text{ such that }
 x_p+c\notin L_{p,a}^{p},
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sup_p m_p<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{for some fixed }a,
 \text{ every }p\text{ has such a }c\in C_a.
\]
\end{proposition}

Here \(L^p=\{y^p:y\in L\}\) for a field \(L\).

\begin{proof}
For \(c\in C_a\), let \(F_c=\mathbb F_2(x_p+c)\).  Since
\(x_p=(x_p+c)+c\), one has \(F_cC_a=L_{p,a}\).  The degree
\([L_{p,a}:F_c]\) divides \([C_a:\mathbb F_2]=2^a\), so it is prime to the
odd prime \(p\).  Extension to \(L_{p,a}\) is therefore injective on
\(p\)-Kummer quotients: restriction followed by norm is multiplication by
this invertible degree.  Thus \(x_p+c\) is a \(p\)-th power in its defining
field exactly when it is a \(p\)-th power in \(L_{p,a}\).

The finite nimbers below \(2^{2^a}\) are exactly \(C_a\), which proves the
first equivalence from the definition of \(m_p\).  A fixed \(a\) gives the
uniform bound \(2^{2^a}-1\).  Conversely, choose \(a\) with
\(2^{2^a}\) larger than a given uniform bound.
\end{proof}

The proposition says that boundedness is uniform nonsaturation of one fixed
finite affine line.  It does not require any of the particular translates
\(0,1,4\) to have maximal primary order.  No fixed line is presently known to
work for all primes.

The universal frontier can now be stated without auxiliary machinery:
\begin{enumerate}
\item prove generation by the synchronized structural norm \(T_h\), including
the Conway--Fermat equalities \(\delta_n=F_n\);
\item prove full primary order for the marked projective unit \(y_{r,a}\) on
every ordinary odd spine;
\item prove that every selected Gaussian period \(\gamma_k\), equivalently
every selected norm-one increment \(\eta_k\), is primitive;
\item prove \(\Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k)\) for every exceptional
level.
\end{enumerate}
Each item is a one-dimensional selected Kummer nonvanishing problem after
the reductions above.  Generic degree, trace, norm, or unmarked reciprocity
data either determine a different coordinate or reconstruct the same
selected coordinate.  They do not supply the missing nonvanishing.

\section{Conclusion}

The finite excess problem is not presently a problem of discovering another
candidate sequence.  Its exact candidate is Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:four-arms} reduces it to four explicit selected-order
assertions.  The reductions explain both the strength of the numerical
pattern and the persistence of the proof gap: all coarse finite-field
invariants are compatible with more than one value of the selected Kummer
coordinate.

The most economical remaining target is therefore direct.  One must prove
nonvanishing of the recursively selected Euler quotient in each of the four
arms, with full primary valuation.  Until those four statements are proved,
the universal \(0/1/4\) rule and even absolute boundedness remain open.
