\begin{abstract}
Lenstra's construction of ordinal nim multiplication assigns a finite excess
$m_p$ to every odd prime $p$.  The observed values obey a simple $0/1/4$
rule determined by the component support of
$f(p)=\operatorname{ord}_p(2)$, but no universal proof is known.

We give an exact status theorem.  The rule is equivalent to four
full-primary-order assertions for elements selected by the literal Conway
tower: a synchronized structural norm, a marked cyclotomic value, a Gaussian
period in a norm-one torus, and a corrected conductor-five norm.  These are
lossless reductions, not hypotheses, and all four universal assertions remain
open.  The paper then isolates the arithmetic content of each coordinate.  It
constructs the primitive-support quotient and the Conway--Fermat packet
recursion, identifies the ordinary Artin symbol, proves the cubic Singer
recursion, a partition selected-order bound, and a sharp first-relation
threshold, proves an ordinary section sieve and a Kummer-ratio packing sieve,
and gives the exceptional Capelli
criterion, a primitive-only palindromicity test, a one-third Frobenius
compression, an intermediate-field packing sieve, and a level-dependent
translate bound.  The compression proves the exceptional arm whenever its
current cyclotomic quotient is prime, and the packing sieve proves a selected
composite large-factor range, while the remaining composite levels stay open.
A final reciprocity theorem
shows that Frobenius, character, and affine-holonomy transforms of one marked
Kummer phase can only erase or re-encode that phase; they cannot evaluate it
without an independent ancestry input.

The proof record distinguishes paper theorems, ordinary kernel-checked
algebra, native-decided named finite certificates, source-backed data, and the
still-open universal rule.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Conway's nim addition and multiplication make the ordinals a proper class
field of characteristic two.  Its finite subfields are canonically embedded,
and its first transfinite layers are assembled by explicit Kummer extensions
\cite{conway1976,lenstra1977,lenstra1978}.  Lenstra's description associates
to each odd prime $p$ an element
\[
  \alpha_p=\kappa_{f(p)}+m_p,
  \qquad f(p)=\operatorname{ord}_p(2),
\]
where $\kappa_{f(p)}$ is determined by the tower and $m_p\in\mathbb N$ is
the \emph{finite excess}.  The same structure appears in the description of
the ordinal fields $\mathrm{On}_p$ \cite{dimuro2011}.

Let $\mathcal Q(h)$ be Lenstra's component set for $\kappa_h$.  The
available data suggest the following rule.

\begin{conjecture}[the $0/1/4$ rule]\label{conj:rule}
For every odd prime $p$,
\[
 m_p=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&\mathcal Q(f(p))\text{ is not a singleton odd prime power},\\
  4,&f(p)=2\cdot3^k\text{ for some }k\geq1,\\
  1,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
\end{conjecture}

The term \emph{selected} is essential.  A finite field contains many
elements having the same degree, trace, norm, or cyclotomic support, whereas
the Conway construction recursively marks one Frobenius orbit.  The open
content is the primary multiplicative order of that orbit.  Computations are
valuable for individual rows and as countermodels to overstrong lemmas, but
they do not prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule}.

\begin{thmA}
The $0/1/4$ rule is equivalent to the conjunction of four selected-order
assertions $Z,O,C,D$ stated in Theorem~\ref{thm:arms}.  Each assertion is an
exact Euler nonpower test for one Conway-selected coordinate and retains the
full primary valuation.  The reductions, the coordinate formulas, the
packet-universal Fermat residual of Proposition~\ref{prop:z-packet-universal},
selected Singer and partition bounds of Propositions~\ref{prop:sieve}
and~\ref{prop:c-partition}, the cubic first-relation theorem
and marked-projective obstruction of
Propositions~\ref{prop:c-first-relation} and~\ref{prop:c-projective}, and the exceptional compression
and packing and translate bounds of
Propositions~\ref{prop:d-one-third}, \ref{prop:d-packing},
and~\ref{prop:d-bound} are proved here.
Corollary~\ref{cor:o-large-factor} settles the ordinary large-factor case,
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-ratio-packing} enlarges that range polynomially in
$r^a$; the compression settles the exceptional prime-complement case, and
the intermediate-field sieve settles an exponential-in-$\sqrt{3^k}$
composite large-factor range.
None of
$Z,O,C,D$ is proved universally; consequently the $0/1/4$ rule and absolute
boundedness of the finite excess remain open.
\end{thmA}

This theorem is the organizing spine of the paper.  The point is not to
replace one conjecture by four opaque ones, but to remove every ambient-field
ambiguity while preserving the one datum that coarse invariants lose: the
Frobenius orbit selected recursively by the Conway tower.

After finite-field preliminaries, Section~\ref{sec:arms} gives the four-arm
equivalence.  Sections~\ref{sec:z}--\ref{sec:d} develop one lossless selected
coordinate for each arm.  Section~\ref{sec:nim-reciprocity} proves the
saturation boundary for reciprocity arguments built from one marked phase.
Section~\ref{sec:bounded} separates the stronger $0/1/4$ rule from absolute
boundedness, and Section~\ref{sec:verification} records the authority of every
claim class used in the paper.

\section{Finite-field preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

For a finite algebraic nimber $x$, write
\[
  d(x)=[\mathbb F_2(x):\mathbb F_2].
\]
The element $\kappa_h$ is the sum of the selected components
$\kappa_q$, $q\in\mathcal Q(h)$, and $h\mid d(\kappa_h)$.  The excess has
the equivalent form
\[
  m_p=\min\{m\geq0:\kappa_{f(p)}+m
      \text{ is not a $p$-th power in its defining finite field}\}.
\]
Finite integers are identified with their canonical finite nimbers.  We use
standard facts about finite fields and Capelli composition
\cite{lidlniederreiter1997}.

\begin{proposition}[exact power criterion]\label{prop:power}
Let $p$ be prime, let $p\mid2^E-1$, and let
$x\in\mathbb F_{2^E}^{\times}$.  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $x$ is a $p$-th power;
\item $x^{(2^E-1)/p}=1$;
\item $\operatorname{ord}(x)\mid(2^E-1)/p$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, $x$ is not a $p$-th power if and only if
\[
  v_p(\operatorname{ord}(x))=v_p(2^E-1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose a generator $g$ of the cyclic multiplicative group and write
$x=g^a$.  Solvability of $py\equiv a\pmod{2^E-1}$, the displayed power
test, and the order divisibility are equivalent.  The final statement is
their $p$-primary form.
\end{proof}

The valuation cannot be replaced by the weaker assertion
$p\mid\operatorname{ord}(x)$.  If $h=f(p)$ and $h\mid E$, lifting the
exponent gives
\[
  v_p(2^E-1)=v_p(2^h-1)+v_p(E/h).
\]
Every order assertion below retains the full right-hand valuation.

\begin{proposition}[norm reduction]\label{prop:norm}
Let $h\mid E$, let $p\mid2^h-1$, and let
$x\in\mathbb F_{2^E}^{\times}$.  Then
\[
 x^{(2^E-1)/p}
 =\operatorname N_{\mathbb F_{2^E}/\mathbb F_{2^h}}(x)^{(2^h-1)/p}.
\]
Thus $x$ is a $p$-th power in $\mathbb F_{2^E}$ if and only if its norm is
a $p$-th power in $\mathbb F_{2^h}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The norm exponent is $(2^E-1)/(2^h-1)$.  Multiply it by
$(2^h-1)/p$ and apply Proposition~\ref{prop:power}.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact four-arm reduction}\label{sec:arms}

For $h>1$, put
\[
 \Pi_h=
 \prod_{\substack{\ell\text{ odd prime}\\
                    \operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(2)=h}}
 \ell^{v_{\ell}(2^h-1)},
 \qquad
 T_h=\operatorname N_{\mathbb F_{2^{d(\kappa_h)}}/
                            \mathbb F_{2^h}}(\kappa_h).
\]
Thus $\Pi_h$ is the full primary part supported at primes first occurring at
residue degree $h$.

For an odd prime $r$, let $b_r=d(\alpha_r)$.  Put
$x_{r,0}=\alpha_r$, and for $a\geq1$ put
\[
 x_{r,a}=\kappa_{r^a}.
\]
For every $a\geq0$, define
\[
 F_{r,a}=\mathbb F_{2^{r^ab_r}},\qquad
 y_{r,a}=x_{r,a}+1,
\]
and for $a\geq1$ define
\[
 G_{r,a}=F_{r,a}^{\times}/F_{r,a-1}^{\times},
 \qquad
 L_{r,a}=|G_{r,a}|=\Phi_{r^a}(2^{b_r}).
\]

For $k\geq1$, set $h=3^k$, let $\zeta_k=\kappa_{3^k}$, and put
\[
 \gamma_k=\zeta_k+\zeta_k^{-1}\in\mathbb F_{2^h}^{\times}.
\]
Finally, let $q=2^h$, let $\omega=\zeta_k^h\in\mathbb F_4$, and define
\[
 A_k=\zeta_k^2+\zeta_k+\omega,
 \qquad M_k=A_k^{q-1},
 \qquad \Psi_k=\frac{\Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)}3.
\]

\begin{theorem}[four-arm reduction]\label{thm:arms}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule} is equivalent to the conjunction of the following
four universal assertions.
\begin{description}
\item[$Z$.] If $\mathcal Q(h)$ is not a singleton odd prime power, then
      $\Pi_h\mid\operatorname{ord}(T_h)$.
\item[$O$.] If $r\ne3$ is odd, $a\geq1$, and $p\ne r$ is a prime
      divisor of $L_{r,a}$, then
      \[
      v_p\!\left(\operatorname{ord}
       (y_{r,a}F_{r,a-1}^{\times})\right)=v_p(L_{r,a}).
      \]
\item[$C$.] The element $\gamma_k$ is primitive in
      $\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}^{\times}$ for every $k\geq1$.
\item[$D$.] One has
      $\Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k)$ for every $k\geq1$.
\end{description}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $p$ and put $h=f(p)$.  If $\mathcal Q(h)$ is not a singleton odd prime
power, Proposition~\ref{prop:z-support} below identifies $m_p=0$ with the
corresponding primary factor of $Z$.

If $\mathcal Q(h)=\{r^a\}$ with $r$ odd, Lenstra's component formulas give
$h=r^as$ with $s\mid b_r$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:o-projective} identifies
the row primes with $p\ne r$ dividing $L_{r,a}$ and identifies $m_p=1$
with assertion $O$.

When $r=3$, one has $b_3=2$.  The cases are $h=3^a$ and
$h=2\cdot3^a$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:c-half} identifies the former with
$C$, while Theorem~\ref{thm:d-target} identifies the latter with $D$.
These cases are disjoint and exhaustive.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:arms} is an equivalence, not a proof of any of its four
universal assertions.  None of $Z,O,C,D$ is presently known in full.
\end{remark}

\section{The zero arm: structural norms and Fermat packets}\label{sec:z}

\subsection{Synchronized structural norm}

\begin{proposition}[exact support criterion]\label{prop:z-support}
Let $E=d(\kappa_h)$.  For an odd prime $p$ with $f(p)=h$,
\[
 m_p=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_p(\operatorname{ord}(T_h))=v_p(2^h-1).
\]
Consequently every such row has excess zero if and only if
$\Pi_h\mid\operatorname{ord}(T_h)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the defining excess formula, $m_p=0$ exactly when $\kappa_h$ is not a $p$-th
power.  Proposition~\ref{prop:norm} moves the test to $T_h$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:power} gives the primary valuation.
\end{proof}

When $\kappa_h$ has several components, $T_h$ is not determined by separate
component norms.  Their common embedding carries a synchronized Frobenius
phase.  The following quotient removes every proper-subfield ambiguity while
retaining that phase.

\begin{proposition}[cubical primitive-support coordinate]
\label{prop:cube}
Let $S$ be the set of prime divisors of $h$, let
$M=\mathbb F_{2^h}$, and write $\varphi(x)=x^2$ and
$\sigma_r=\varphi^{h/r}$.  For $A\subseteq S$, put
$\sigma_A=\prod_{r\in A}\sigma_r$, and define
\[
 H_h=\prod_{r\in S}\mathbb F_{2^{h/r}}^{\times},
 \qquad
 \Delta_h(x)=
 \frac{\prod_{|A|\text{ even}}\sigma_A(x)}
      {\prod_{|A|\text{ odd}}\sigma_A(x)}.
\]
Then
\[
 \ker\Delta_h=H_h,
 \qquad |\operatorname{im}\Delta_h|=\Phi_h(2),
\]
and $\Delta_h$ induces an isomorphism
$M^{\times}/H_h\simeq\operatorname{im}\Delta_h$.  Arm $Z$ at degree $h$
is equivalent to
\[
  \Pi_h\mid\operatorname{ord}(\Delta_h(T_h)).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The operator is the power map
\[
  x\longmapsto x^{\prod_{r\mid h,\ r\text{ prime}}(1-2^{h/r})}.
\]
In the cyclic group of order $2^h-1$, lifting the exponent prime by prime
shows that its kernel has order
$\operatorname{lcm}_{r\in S}(2^{h/r}-1)$, exactly the order of $H_h$.
The cyclotomic product formula gives quotient order $\Phi_h(2)$.  A prime
with residue degree $h$ divides no proper-subfield order, so the quotient
preserves its full primary valuation.  Proposition~\ref{prop:z-support}
gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The cubical coordinate is canonical, but nonidentity is weaker than full
primary order.  Moreover, an additive cube made from independently shifted
components is not the synchronized norm of their selected sum.  Thus neither
linear disjointness nor a nonzero raw cross-ratio proves $Z$.

\subsection{The singleton-even Conway--Fermat chain}

The zero arm also contains the singleton-even chain.  Put
\[
 h_n=2^{n+1},\qquad c_n=\kappa_{h_n}=2^{2^n},\qquad
 E_n=\mathbb F_{2^{h_n}},\qquad F_n=2^{2^n}+1,
\]
and $E_{-1}=\mathbb F_2$.  The tower relation is
\[
 c_n^2+c_n=\prod_{j<n}c_j.
\]
Let
\[
 \delta_n=\operatorname{ord}
   (c_nE_{n-1}^{\times})
 \quad\text{in }E_n^{\times}/E_{n-1}^{\times}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Conway--Fermat selected order]\label{thm:fermat}
One has $1<\delta_n\mid F_n$, and the zero arm at every prime divisor of
$F_n$ is equivalent to
\[
  \delta_n=F_n\qquad(n\geq0).
\]
All compatible binary root choices form one absolute-Frobenius orbit, so
they have the same order vector $(\delta_0,\ldots,\delta_n)$.  The remaining
assertion is therefore intrinsic to the selected tower rather than to an
extra choice of root.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The quotient group has order
$(2^{2^{n+1}}-1)/(2^{2^n}-1)=F_n$, and $c_n\notin E_{n-1}$.  Every prime
$p\mid F_n$ has $f(p)=2^{n+1}$, with its full primary part in this quotient.
Proposition~\ref{prop:power} gives the displayed equality for $\delta_n$.

A compatible prefix has two extensions at each level, so there are
$2^{n+1}$ compatible tuples.  Frobenius preserves the defining equations,
and the $2^{n+1}$ Frobenius conjugates are distinct because $c_n$ has that
degree.  They exhaust the tuples and preserve all relative orders.
\end{proof}

The recurrence has a deterministic packet form.  If
$u_n=(c_n+1)/c_n$, then $\operatorname{ord}(u_n)=\delta_n$ and
$a_{n-1}=\prod_{j<n}c_j=u_n/(u_n+1)^2$.  For each divisor $e>1$ of $F_n$,
the inverse pairs of primitive $e$-th roots give a squarefree packet
polynomial
\[
 C_e(X)=
 \prod_{[z]\in\mu_e^{\mathrm{prim}}/(z\sim z^{-1})}
 \left(X-\frac{z}{(z+1)^2}\right)\in\mathbb F_2[X].
\]
Every irreducible factor has degree $2^n$, and
\[
 \delta_n=e
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{the selected minimal polynomial of $a_{n-1}$ divides $C_e$}.
\]
If $P$ is such a factor, its unique irreducible child is
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_X(P(X),Y^2+XY+X^3).
\]
Thus the literal chain is one deterministic orbit beginning at $X+1$.
Full parent conductor does not imply full child conductor: exact degree-$16$
packet examples have identical full parent order but deterministic children
of different conductor.  Consequently any induction must use the complete
marked orbit, not only the integer conductor.

The literal multiplication operator retains that selector in a compact
two-block form.  At the edge $c_n^2=c_n+a_{n-1}$, let $A$ denote
multiplication by $a_{n-1}$ on $E_{n-1}$.  Multiplication by
$a_{n-1}c_n$ on $E_{n-1}\oplus c_nE_{n-1}$ has matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&A^2\\ A&A\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This identity is kernel-checked.  The Lean declaration is
\begin{center}
\texttt{fermat\_selected\_product\_}\\
\texttt{mul\_pair}.
\end{center}
If $S_r$ denotes the characteristic-two Fibonacci polynomial, the genuine
level-$n$ target is
\[
 S_{F_n/\ell}(a_{n-1})\ne0\qquad(\ell\mid F_n\text{ prime}).
\]
Equivalently, one applies the same polynomial to multiplication by
$a_{n-1}$ on $E_{n-1}$.  Write $M_n$ for the displayed two-block operator.
It represents multiplication by $a_n=a_{n-1}c_n$, so it is the recursive
interface to the \emph{next}-level target
\[
 S_{F_{n+1}/\ell}(M_n)\ne0
 \qquad(\ell\mid F_{n+1}\text{ prime}).
\]
At the lower index one instead has the automatic exclusion
\[
 S_{F_n/\ell}(a_n)\ne0,
 \quad\text{equivalently}\quad
 S_{F_n/\ell}(M_n)\ne0
 \qquad(\ell\mid F_n),
\]
because a zero would put $a_n$ in $E_{n-1}$: zero-block propagation reaches
$S_{F_n}$, and the telescoping partial-trace identity then forces
$a_n^{|E_{n-1}|}=a_n$, whereas its Frobenius displacement is
$a_{n-1}\ne0$.  This wrong-index exclusion is kernel-checked as
\begin{center}
\texttt{fibPolyValue\_quadratic\_birth\_}\par
\texttt{lower\_period\_ne\_zero}.
\end{center}
It is not the selected-order conjecture.  The block formula remains an exact
interface to the displayed next-level nonvanishing problem; it does not prove
that target.

At the genuine index there is nevertheless a sharper exact residual.  Let
$d=F_{n+1}/\ell=2m+1>1$ and put $x=a_n$.  Characteristic-two doubling and
Cassini give
\begin{align*}
 S_m(x)^4+x^{d-2}&=S_d(x)S_{d-2}(x),\\
 S_{m+1}(x)^4+x^d&=S_d(x)S_{d+2}(x).
\end{align*}
Consequently, over a field,
\[
 S_d(x)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 S_m(x)^4=x^{d-2}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 S_{m+1}(x)^4=x^d.
\]
The converse is literal: squaring
$S_d=S_{m+1}^2+xS_m^2$ makes the two displayed powers cancel.  The pair is
strictly sharper than either equation separately.  Indeed, in
$\mathbb F_2[X]$ its two left-hand residual polynomials have monic gcd exactly
$S_d$.  To see the only needed coprimality, any common divisor of
$S_{d-2}$ and $S_{d+2}$ divides $X^{d-2}S_4=X^{d-2}$ by the continuant
determinant identity, while the positive odd-index polynomial $S_{d-2}$ has
constant coefficient one.  Thus $S_{d-2}$ and $S_{d+2}$ are coprime, and
the two factorizations give the stated gcd.

This exact branch selector still does not close the Conway--Fermat target.
If $d-1=2^s g$ with $g$ odd, its lower equation is
\[
 S_g(x)^{2^{s+1}}=x^{d-2}.
\]
Writing $q=|E_n|$, choose $K$ by the exact congruence
$2^{s+1}K\equiv d-2\pmod{q-1}$.  Since $x\ne0$, $x^{q-1}=1$, and the
$2^{s+1}$-power Frobenius is injective, the equation becomes
$S_g(x)=x^K$.  This is precisely the existing AE4 inverse-exponent seam.
The upper equation removes the adjacent Fibonacci branch but, after
$S_d(x)=0$ is imposed, follows by squaring the same endpoint relation twice
and using the lower equation; no present ancestry identity contradicts the
resulting monomial equality.
The two ordinary identities and the field equivalence are kernel-checked as
\begin{center}
\texttt{fibPolyValue\_odd\_lower\_fourth\_factor},\par
\texttt{fibPolyValue\_odd\_upper\_fourth\_factor},\quad\text{and}\quad
\texttt{fibPolyValue\_odd\_eq\_zero\_iff\_fourth\_residuals}.
\end{center}

In fact the inverse-exponent seam is not merely compatible with deficient
packets: it is a universal identity on every such packet.

\begin{proposition}[packet-universal Fermat residual]
\label{prop:z-packet-universal}
Let $s,g\geq1$, let $d=Rg+1$ with $R=2^s$, and let $u\ne0,1$ satisfy
$u^d=1$ in a field of characteristic two.  Put
\[
 \phi(u)=\frac{u}{(u+1)^2},\qquad x=\phi(u),\qquad
 f=S_g(x),\qquad h=S_{g+1}(x),\qquad
 B=\frac{x^g}{f^2}.
\]
Then $f\ne0$ and
\[
 B=\phi(u^g),\qquad B^R=x,
 \qquad \frac hf=T_s(B),
\]
where $T_s(z)=z+z^2+\cdots+z^{2^{s-1}}$.
Consequently both fourth-power residual coordinates produced by a zero of
$S_d$ are identities for every nontrivial $d$-th root---equivalently, on
every packet $C_e$ with $1<e\mid d$---before the Conway parent is selected.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The characteristic-two Binet identity is
\[
 S_j(\phi(u))=\frac{u^j+1}{(u+1)^j}.
\]
It first gives $S_d(x)=0$.  Since $\gcd(g,d)=1$ and $u\ne1$, it also gives
$f\ne0$, and direct substitution gives
\[
 \frac{x^g}{f^2}=\frac{u^g}{(u^g+1)^2}=\phi(u^g).
\]
The map $\phi$ commutes with every binary Frobenius power and satisfies
$\phi(v^{-1})=\phi(v)$.  Since $u^{Rg}=u^{d-1}=u^{-1}$,
this proves $B^R=x$.

Put $t=(u^g+1)^{-1}$.  Then $B=t+t^2$, so the partial Frobenius trace
telescopes:
\[
 T_s(B)=t+t^R
 =\frac1{u^g+1}+\frac{u}{u+1}.
\]
The last equality uses
$(u^g+1)^R=u^{Rg}+1=u^{-1}+1$.  A second application of the Binet identity
gives
\[
 \frac hf
 =\frac{u^{g+1}+1}{(u+1)(u^g+1)}
 =\frac1{u^g+1}+\frac{u}{u+1},
\]
proving the assertion.  Thus these residual coordinates characterize the
ambient torsion packet but do not distinguish its recursively selected
member.
\end{proof}

The Binet identity in this proof is kernel-checked as
\texttt{fibPolyValue\_torus\_binet}.  The proposition sharpens the failure
boundary: any successful induction must use the deterministic parent label
in
\[
 u_{n+1}(u_n+1)^3=u_n(u_{n+1}+1)^2,
\]
not only the fourth-power residual, inverse Frobenius, trace, or norm of the
child packet.

This isolates the part not settled by the existing Conway-tower literature.
Popovych proves lower bounds and verifies the required relative full orders
through level eleven using the known Fermat factorizations
\cite{popovych2018}.  Cagliero, Herman, and Szechtman prove an exact product
formula for global order in terms of the relative orders, as well as a normal
basis theorem for the associated tower elements
\cite{caglierohermanszechtman2025}.  Neither result forces every relative
factor $\delta_n$ to equal $F_n$; that all-level equality is precisely the
remaining selected assertion here.

There is an exact supersingular realization of the selected ancestry.

\begin{proposition}[supersingular realization and pairing boundary]
\label{prop:z-elliptic}
On
\[
 \mathcal E:\quad y^2+y=x^3+x^2
\]
put $P_n=(c_n,c_{n+1})$.  Then $P_n\in\mathcal E$, and for $n\geq2$,
\[
 [F_n]P_n=O.
\]
More precisely, if $\pi$ is absolute Frobenius and $\iota=\pi+[1]$, then
\[
 \iota(x,y)=(x+1,y+x+1),\qquad
 \iota^2=[-1],\qquad \pi^4=[-4].
\]
For $g=(y+1)/y$, one has
\[
 g(P_n)=\frac{c_{n+1}+1}{c_{n+1}},
 \qquad \operatorname{ord}(g(P_n))=\delta_{n+1}.
\]
If $T=(0,0)$, then $T$ has order five and
\[
 \operatorname{div}(g)
 =2[-T]+[2T]-2[T]-[-2T].
\]
Thus $g$ is the quotient of the two fixed doubling Miller functions at
$-T$ and $T$, but it is not a Weil or reduced-Tate pairing function attached
to the $F_n$-torsion point $P_n$ when $n\geq2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Conway recursion gives
\[
 c_{n+1}^2+c_{n+1}
 =c_n(c_n^2+c_n)=c_n^3+c_n^2,
\]
so $P_n$ lies on $\mathcal E$.  The coordinate formula for
$\iota(P)=\pi(P)+P$ follows from the chord law.  Applying it twice gives the
inverse $(x,y)\mapsto(x,y+1)$, so
$\pi^2+2\pi+2=0$ in $\operatorname{End}(\mathcal E)$ and hence
$\pi^4=[-4]$.

Put $H=2^{n+1}$.  The $2^H$-Frobenius fixes $c_n$ and sends $c_{n+1}$ to
$c_{n+1}+1$, hence sends $P_n$ to $-P_n$.  For $n\geq2$,
\[
 \pi^H=(\pi^4)^{2^{n-1}}=[2^{2^n}],
\]
which gives $[2^{2^n}+1]P_n=O$.

The formula for $g(P_n)$ is immediate from the coordinates, and its order is
$\delta_{n+1}$ by the definition of $u_{n+1}$.  The five rational points of
$\mathcal E/\mathbb F_2$ show that $T$ has order five.  Direct intersection
with the two horizontal lines gives
\[
 \operatorname{div}(y)=2[T]+[-2T]-3[O],\qquad
 \operatorname{div}(y+1)=2[-T]+[2T]-3[O],
\]
and subtraction gives the divisor of $g$.  Equivalently,
$g=f_{2,-T}/f_{2,T}$ for functions normalized at $O$.  Its divisor is
supported on fixed rational five-torsion, with coefficients $2,1,-2,-1$.
For $n\geq2$, these valuations are not divisible by $F_n\geq17$, whereas the
divisor of an $F_n$-Miller function and every $F_n$-th-power correction have
all coefficients divisible by $F_n$.  Thus $g$ cannot be such a function
attached to $P_n$, even modulo an $F_n$-th power.
A Weil or reduced Tate pairing on $\mathcal E[F_n]$ takes values in
$\mu_{F_n}$, whereas $g(P_n)$ has order dividing $F_{n+1}$.  Pairwise
coprimality of the Fermat numbers and $\delta_{n+1}>1$ exclude equality.
\end{proof}

The proposition supplies exact CM annihilator control but not generator
status.  For $n\geq3$, no elliptic endomorphism can carry $P_n$ to
$P_{n-1}$: the image order would divide both coprime numbers $F_n$ and
$F_{n-1}$, although $P_{n-1}\ne O$.  For $n=2$, the same conclusion follows
from $\operatorname{ord}(P_1)=3$ and $\operatorname{ord}(P_2)\mid17$.
Thus the relation
$x(P_n)=y(P_{n-1})$ is not an isogeny division tower, and pairing functoriality
along this proposed tower cannot propagate primitivity.  A perfect alternating
pairing likewise guarantees some detecting partner, not nonvanishing on a
prescribed line.

The tempting cubic curve $Y^2+Y=X^3$ is not a coordinate rewrite of
$\mathcal E$: the two curves have respectively three and five rational points
over $\mathbb F_2$.  For $u=(Y+1)/Y$ on that auxiliary curve,
$u/(u+1)^2=X^3$; every positive-level Fermat-torsion value is already a cube
because $\gcd(F_n,3)=1$.  Its natural cubic Kummer class is therefore trivial
and cannot replace the selected five-torsion function above.  The remaining
statement is still exclusion of every proper-conductor packet along the
complete resultant chain.  Trace, relative norm, irreducibility, and the
etale Jacobian hold for proper packets as well as the full packet.

\section{The ordinary arm: a marked cyclotomic value}\label{sec:o}

Fix an odd prime $r$, write $b=b_r$, and abbreviate
$x_a=x_{r,a}$, $y_a=x_a+1$, and $F_a=F_{r,a}$.

\begin{proposition}[projective order criterion]\label{prop:o-projective}
For $a\geq1$,
\[
 \operatorname N_{F_a/F_{a-1}}(y_a)=y_{a-1},
 \qquad
 |F_a^{\times}/F_{a-1}^{\times}|=L_{r,a}.
\]
If $p\ne r$ divides $L_{r,a}$, then the corresponding singleton row has
\[
 m_p=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_p\!\left(\operatorname{ord}
 (y_aF_{a-1}^{\times})\right)=v_p(L_{r,a}).
\]
Every singleton row with component $r^a$ arises this way.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The relative conjugates of $x_a$ are $\xi x_a$ for $\xi\in\mu_r$;
evaluating their binomial at one gives the norm identity.  The quotient order
is the quotient of the two finite-field unit orders.  The cyclotomic divisor
criterion gives $\operatorname{ord}_p(2^b)=r^a$ for $p\ne r$ dividing
$L_{r,a}$, and Lenstra's component theorem identifies precisely the
singleton rows.  The lower unit group has no $p$-part, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:power} gives the last equivalence.
\end{proof}

The full recursive ancestry can also be retained in one binary polynomial.
Let $P_r\in\mathbb F_2[T]$ be the minimal polynomial of
$x_0=\alpha_r$.

\begin{proposition}[bottom-ancestor divisibility]
\label{prop:o-bottom-divisibility}
Put $q=|F_{a-1}|$, $N=L_{r,a}$, and let $p\ne r$ divide $N$.
Then $\gcd(p,q-1)=1$.  Write $d=N/p$, and choose $e,t\geq0$ such that
\[
 pe=1+t(q-1).
\]
Then the current $p$-coordinate of $y_aF_{a-1}^{\times}$ is deficient if
and only if
\[
 P_r(T^{r^a})\mid
 (1+T)^d+(1+T^r)^e
 \qquad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[T].
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, $N\equiv r\pmod {q-1}$, so
$\gcd(N,q-1)=r$.
The polynomial $P_r(T^{r^a})$ is the minimal polynomial of $x_a$.
The coordinate is deficient exactly when $c=(1+x_a)^d$ lies in
$F_{a-1}^{\times}$.  In that case the norm identity gives
\[
 c^p=(1+x_a)^N=1+x_a^r.
\]
Since $c^{q-1}=1$ and $pe=1+t(q-1)$,
$c=(c^p)^e=(1+x_a^r)^e$.  Conversely this equality puts $c$ in
$F_{a-1}$.  In characteristic two the equality is precisely the vanishing
at $x_a$ of the displayed binary polynomial, which is equivalent to
divisibility by its minimal polynomial.
\end{proof}

This is a lossless selector-retaining reduction, not a proof of arm~$O$:
the remaining assertion is that the displayed divisibility never occurs for
the literal polynomial $P_r$ and every current factor $p$.

For a polynomial $B(T)=\sum_m b_mT^m$ and $0\leq j<n$, define its
$j$-th $n$-section by
\[
 \operatorname{Sec}_{n,j}(B)(U)=\sum_{k\geq0}b_{j+kn}U^k.
\]

\begin{lemma}[polynomial section criterion]
\label{lem:o-section}
Let $k$ be a field, let $0\ne P\in k[U]$, and let $n\geq1$.  Then
\[
 P(T^n)\mid B(T)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 P(U)\mid\operatorname{Sec}_{n,j}(B)(U)
 \quad(0\leq j<n).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every polynomial has the unique decomposition
$B(T)=\sum_{j<n}T^j\operatorname{Sec}_{n,j}(B)(T^n)$.
Multiplication by $P(T^n)$ preserves each residue class of exponents modulo
$n$.  Applying the decomposition to the quotient proves the forward
implication, and summing the sectionwise quotients proves the converse.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Lucas-section ordinary case]
\label{cor:o-large-factor}
Put $n=r^a$, let $p\ne r$ divide $N=L_{r,a}$, and put $d=N/p$.
If some $0\leq j<n$ satisfies
\[
 r\nmid j,\qquad \binom dj\equiv1\pmod2,
 \qquad d-j<bn,
\]
then the current $p$-coordinate of $y_aF_{a-1}^{\times}$ has full primary
order.  In particular this holds if $d\leq bn$, and hence when $N/r$ is a
prime distinct from $r$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the polynomial of Proposition~\ref{prop:o-bottom-divisibility}, the
$j$-th section receives no term from $(1+T^r)^e$, since $r\mid n$ but
$r\nmid j$.  Its other summand has constant coefficient one and degree at
most $\lfloor(d-j)/n\rfloor<b$.  It is therefore a nonzero polynomial of degree
less than $\deg P_r=b$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:o-section} rules out the required
divisibility.  Finally, $d$ is odd, so when $d\leq bn$ one may take $j=1$.
If $p=N/r$, then $d=r\leq br^a$.
\end{proof}

The full Kummer orbit gives a second sieve which is substantially stronger
when the tower level is large.

\begin{proposition}[Kummer-ratio packing sieve]
\label{prop:o-ratio-packing}
Assume $r\ne3$ and $a\geq1$, put $n=r^a$, and let the prime $p\ne r$ divide
$N=L_{r,a}$.  Set
\[
 d=N/p,\qquad
 D=r^{a-1}(r-1),\qquad s=(r-1)/2.
\]
If the current $p$-coordinate is deficient, then
\[
 d\geq
 \mathcal R(D,s)+
 \left\lceil
 \frac{\mathcal R(D,s+1)-\mathcal R(D,s)}r
 \right\rceil,
 \qquad
 \mathcal R(D,t):=
 \sum_{j=0}^{t}2^j\binom Dj\binom tj.
\]
Consequently arm~$O$ holds at every such factor satisfying
\[
 \frac{L_{r,a}}p<
 \mathcal R\left(r^{a-1}(r-1),\frac{r-1}{2}\right)
 +
 \left\lceil\frac1r\left[
 \mathcal R\left(r^{a-1}(r-1),\frac{r+1}{2}\right)
 -\mathcal R\left(r^{a-1}(r-1),\frac{r-1}{2}\right)
 \right]\right\rceil.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $K=F_{a-1}$ and $x=x_a$.  The nonpower radicand gives
$r\mid2^b-1$, so LTE gives $r^a\mid2^{br^{a-1}}-1$; hence $K$ contains
$\mu_n$.  Since $F_a=K(x)$, the element $x$ has minimal-polynomial degree
$r$ over $K$.  Choose representatives
$\mathcal C$ for $\mu_n/\mu_r$.  Deficiency and
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-bottom-divisibility} give, at every root $cx$ of
$P_r(T^n)$ and every $\zeta\in\mu_r$,
\[
 (1+\zeta cx)^d
 =(1+(\zeta cx)^r)^e
 =(1+(cx)^r)^e
 =(1+cx)^d.
\]
Every numerator and denominator below is nonzero: an equality
$1+\xi x=0$ with $\xi\in\mu_n$ would put $x$ in $K$, contradicting its
relative degree $r>1$.
Thus each of the
\[
 u_{c,\zeta}=\frac{1+\zeta cx}{1+cx},
 \qquad c\in\mathcal C,\quad
 \zeta\in\mu_r\setminus\{1\},
\]
is a $d$-th root of unity.  There are
$|\mathcal C|(r-1)=D$ such ratios.

Index these ratios by a set $I$ of size $D$.  For every vector
$v=(v_i)_{i\in I}\in\mathbb Z^I$ with $\sum_i|v_i|\leq s$, put
$U_v=\prod_i u_i^{v_i}$.  We claim that the $U_v$ are distinct.  If
$U_v=U_w$, set $g_i=v_i-w_i$.  After separating positive and negative
exponents, the resulting rational-function equality at $x$ has the form
\[
 A(x)=B(x),
 \qquad \deg A,\deg B\leq\sum_i|g_i|\leq2s=r-1<r,
\]
where $A,B\in K[T]$ have constant coefficient one.  Since the minimal
polynomial of $x$ over $K$ has degree $r$, one has $A=B$ as polynomials.
This low-degree step is the ordinary Lean theorem
\texttt{polynomial\_eq\_of\_aeval\_eq\_of\_natDegree\_lt\_minpoly}.

For a fixed $c\in\mathcal C$, the ratios form the star
\[
 \frac{1+\zeta cT}{1+cT},\qquad
 \zeta\in\mu_r\setminus\{1\}.
\]
Stars belonging to different cosets have disjoint linear factors.  Within a
star, the valuation at the distinct factor $1+\zeta cT$ recovers the
corresponding exponent.  Unique factorization therefore forces every
$g_i=0$, and hence $v=w$.

To count these integer vectors, choose the $j$ nonzero coordinates, their
signs, and their positive absolute values.  There are $\binom Dj$, $2^j$,
and $\binom sj$ choices respectively, the last count being the number of
positive $j$-tuples with sum at most $s$.  Hence there are exactly
$\mathcal R(D,s)$ vectors.  Their values are distinct roots of $X^d-1$,
which has at most $d$ roots in a field.  This proves the inner-ball
contribution.

Part of the next signed shell can be retained after separating the ratios by
their leading phase.  For $i=(c,\zeta)$ put $\chi_i=\zeta$ and, for
$v\in\mathbb Z^I$, put
\[
 \chi(v)=\prod_i\chi_i^{v_i}\in\mu_r.
\]
The outer shell of vectors of signed $\ell^1$-weight exactly $s+1$ has
$\mathcal R(D,s+1)-\mathcal R(D,s)$ elements.  Some fibre $H$ of $\chi$ in
that shell therefore has at least
\[
 \left\lceil
 \frac{\mathcal R(D,s+1)-\mathcal R(D,s)}r
 \right\rceil
\]
elements.  We claim that the $U_v$ for the entire inner ball together with
$H$ are distinct.

Indeed, suppose that $U_v=U_w$ for two vectors in this union and set
$g=v-w$.  Form the same cross-products $A,B\in K[T]$, without cancelling
common factors.  Both have constant coefficient one and exact degree
\[
 t=\sum_i|g_i|.
\]
If at least one vector is in the inner ball, then $t\leq s+(s+1)=r$.
If both lie in $H$, then $t\leq2(s+1)=r+1$, while the quotient of the
leading coefficients is
$\prod_i\chi_i^{g_i}=\chi(v)/\chi(w)=1$; their leading terms cancel.  Thus
in either case $A-B=0$ or $\deg(A-B)\leq r$.  The minimal polynomial $m_x$
divides $A-B$.  If the difference were nonzero, this degree bound and
$\deg m_x=r$ would give $A-B=\lambda m_x$ for some $\lambda\in K^\times$.
But $A-B$ has constant coefficient zero, whereas the relative Kummer
minimal polynomial $m_x$ has nonzero constant coefficient, a contradiction.
This boundary step is the ordinary Lean theorem
\texttt{polynomial\_eq\_of\_aeval\_eq\_of\_natDegree\_le\_minpoly\_of\_coeff\_zero}.
Thus $A=B$, and the same disjoint-star valuations force $g=0$.

The union therefore injects into the roots of $X^d-1$.  Its cardinality is
at least the displayed inner-ball-plus-shell expression, proving the bound
and its contrapositive.
\end{proof}

Let $N_a=\operatorname{ord}(x_a)$.  The Kummer relation
$x_a^r=x_{a-1}$ and the relative degree $r$ give
$N_a=rN_{a-1}$.  Choose the roots and primes recursively:
$\xi_{N_a}$ is a primitive complex $N_a$-th root satisfying
\[
 \xi_{N_a}^r=\xi_{N_{a-1}},
\]
and $\mathfrak P_a\mid2$ is the prime whose residue sends
$\xi_{N_a}$ to $x_a$.  Since $x_a^r=x_{a-1}$, this makes
$\mathfrak P_a$ restrict to $\mathfrak P_{a-1}$.  Put
$\varepsilon_a=1-\xi_{N_a}$.
For a row prime $p$, use the intrinsic residue-field notation
\[
 \left(\frac{\varepsilon_a}{\mathfrak P_a}\right)_p
 :=\overline{\varepsilon_a}^{\,(|F_a|-1)/p}\in\mu_p(F_a).
\]
This is an Euler symbol in the selected residue field.  It agrees with the
usual local power-residue/Artin symbol after adjoining $\mu_p$ and choosing
the prime above $\mathfrak P_a$.

\begin{theorem}[selected cyclotomic-value form]\label{thm:o-symbol}
For $p\ne r$ dividing $L_{r,a}$, arm $O$ at $(r,a,p)$ is equivalent to
\[
  \left(\frac{\varepsilon_a}{\mathfrak P_a}\right)_p\ne1.
\]
The compatible primes $\mathfrak P_a$ form a unique inert ray above two:
$\mathfrak P_a$ is the unique prime above $\mathfrak P_{a-1}$, with
relative residue degree $r$, and
\[
 \operatorname N_{\mathbb Q(\xi_{N_a})/
                     \mathbb Q(\xi_{N_{a-1}})}(1-\xi_{N_a})
 =1-\xi_{N_{a-1}}.
\]
Nevertheless, residue-field corestriction kills the new $p$-coordinate and
does not determine the displayed residue symbol.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The reduction of $\varepsilon_a$ is $y_a$.  The unramified power-residue
formula gives
\[
 \left(\frac{\varepsilon_a}{\mathfrak P_a}\right)_p
 =y_a^{(|F_a|-1)/p},
\]
so Proposition~\ref{prop:power} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-projective} give the equivalence.

The Kummer steps increase the $r$-primary part of $N_a$ by one and give
$\operatorname{ord}_{N_a}(2)=br^a$.  The relative cyclotomic degree and
the residue-degree ratio are therefore both $r$, forcing one prime above the
preceding selected prime.  The global norm identity is the product over the
$r$-th roots of unity.  Finally,
$p\mid\Phi_{r^a}(2^b)$ but $p\nmid2^{br^{a-1}}-1$, so the lower residue
field has trivial $p$-Kummer quotient.  Its norm receives no information
about the new selected class.
\end{proof}

The word \emph{value} in the theorem is deliberate.  The element
$1-\xi_{N_a}$ is a cyclotomic number and is an $S$-unit for the primes
dividing $N_a$; it is a global unit exactly when $N_a$ is not a prime power.
For example, in the $r=7$ chain one has $N_a=7^{a+1}$ and its absolute norm
is $7$.  This distinction does not alter reduction modulo the selected prime
over two or the relative norm identity, but any global Kummer formulation
must retain the conductor primes in $S$ (or in the ray modulus).

The selected symbol has an exact Jacobi--Fourier expansion.  Let
$Q=|F_a|$, let $H=\langle x_a\rangle\subset F_a^\times$ have order $N_a$,
put $M=(Q-1)/N_a$, and let $\chi$ be a complex-valued order-$p$ power
character.  Extend complex-valued multiplicative characters by zero at zero
and define
\[
 f(h)=\chi(1-h),\qquad
 \widehat f(\theta)=\sum_{h\in H}f(h)\theta(h)^{-1}
 \quad(\theta\in\widehat H).
\]
For an extension $\psi_\theta$ of $\theta$ to $F_a^\times$, and with
$H^\perp$ the character group of $F_a^\times/H$, one has
\[
 \widehat f(\theta)
 =\frac1M\sum_{\lambda\in H^\perp}
 J(\psi_\theta^{-1}\lambda,\chi),
 \qquad
 f(x_a)=\frac1{N_a}\sum_{\theta\in\widehat H}
 \theta(x_a)\widehat f(\theta).
\]
Indeed, the character average
$M^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in H^\perp}\lambda$ is the indicator of $H$;
inserting it in the first Fourier sum gives the Jacobi sums, and Fourier
inversion gives the second identity.  This is a fully explicit reciprocity
formula, but the selected label survives in $\theta(x_a)$ and the Jacobi
unit phases.  Ideal factorization or valuation formulas for the Jacobi sums
do not by themselves determine this inverse-DFT entry.  This separation of
the ideal and $p$-adic unit conditions is also visible in Uehara's
cyclotomic-unit criterion \cite{uehara1985}; it supplies an exact test, not a
uniform nonvanishing theorem for the Conway-selected entry.

After adjoining $\mu_p$ and retaining the conductor primes in the $S$-ray
modulus, this residue-field condition becomes a marked Artin-symbol
assertion, not merely a statement about decomposition or conductor.  Its
ray character splits into a ramified local logarithm and an unramified
ideal-class character evaluated at $[\mathfrak P_a]$.  Stickelberger
annihilation reduces the latter to an explicit decomposition-invariant
quotient, but does not make it vanish uniformly.  This is a genuine boundary:
at the actual Conway conductor
$r=11$, $a=1$, $p=23$, one contributing Stickelberger scalar vanishes with
exact $23$-adic valuation one.  The corresponding unramified degree-$23$
class line is nontrivial, and every prime over two has nontrivial Artin symbol
on it, although the literal row still has $m_{23}=1$.  Thus successful rows
need not have a zero unramified term; the open assertion is noncancellation in
the actual selected sum.

\section{The cubic arm: a selected Singer generator}\label{sec:c}

Let $h=3^k$, $\zeta_k=\kappa_{3^k}$, and
$\gamma_k=\zeta_k+\zeta_k^{-1}$.

\begin{proposition}[half-angle reduction]\label{prop:c-half}
The element $\zeta_k$ is a primitive $3^{k+1}$-st root of unity,
\[
 \mathbb F_2(\zeta_k)=\mathbb F_{2^{2h}},
 \qquad
 \mathbb F_2(\gamma_k)=\mathbb F_{2^h}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\zeta_k+1)
 =3^{k+1}\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k),
\]
and the translates by $2$ and $3$ have the same order.  Hence the rows with
$f(p)=3^k$ have excess one precisely when $\gamma_k$ has the full relevant
primary order; over all levels this is equivalent to primitivity of every
$\gamma_k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The defining Kummer equation gives $\zeta_k^{3^k}=\kappa_2$, a primitive
cube root.  The order of two modulo $3^{k+1}$ is $2h$, and the least exponent
giving $\pm1$ is $h$, proving the field degrees.  If $s$ is the inverse of
two modulo $3^{k+1}$, then
\[
 \zeta_k+1=\zeta_k^s(\zeta_k^s+\zeta_k^{-s}),
 \qquad
 (\zeta_k^s+\zeta_k^{-s})^2=\gamma_k.
\]
The factors lie in the coprime circle and real parts of the unit group,
giving the order identity.  Frobenius gives the other translates, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:power} finishes the reduction.
\end{proof}

The half-angle test also has a lossless group-algebra form that removes all
denominators.  Put
\[
 J_h(X)=X^{2h}+X^h+1,
 \qquad B_d(X)=(X^2+1)^d+X^d\in\mathbb F_2[X].
\]
Since $J_h$ is the minimal polynomial of $\zeta_k$ and
$\gamma_k=\zeta_k^{-1}(\zeta_k^2+1)$, one has
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_k^d=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 J_h(X)\mid B_d(X).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, in
$\mathbb F_2[X,X^{-1}]/(X^{3h}-1)$ the parity vector of
$X^d(1+X^{-2})^d$ belongs to the affine coset $1+(J_h)$, not to the ideal
$(J_h)$ itself.  Thus arm $C$ is exactly the universal assertion that, for
every prime $\ell\mid 2^h-1$,
\[
 J_h(X)\nmid B_{(2^h-1)/\ell}(X).
\]
Lucas's theorem makes the support of $B_d$ explicit from the binary digits of
$d$, but it does not by itself prove these selected nondivisibilities.  The
denominator-free equivalence above is also kernel-checked as
\texttt{halfAngle\_pow\_eq\_one\_iff\_minpoly\_dvd}; it is a reduction of
$C$, not a proof of $C$.

There is also an exact stable-polynomial formulation.  Put $P_0(X)=X$ and
\[
 P_{j+1}(X)=P_j(X)^3+P_j(X)^2+1,
\]
so that $P_j=f^{\circ j}$ for $f(X)=X^3+X^2+1$.  In characteristic two,
$t(X)=X+1$ conjugates $g(X)=X^3+X$ to $f$: $f=t\circ g\circ t$.
The triple-angle identity gives $g(\gamma_j)=\gamma_{j-1}$ with
$\gamma_0=1$, hence $b_j=\gamma_j+1$ is a root of $P_j$.  Since $b_j$ has
degree $3^j$, $P_j$ is its minimal polynomial.  Lin and Wang prove that $f$
is stable, equivalently that all these iterates are irreducible
\cite{linwang2024}; their theorem does not assert multiplicative primitivity.

Indeed, this missing strengthening is exactly arm $C$.  With
$q=2^{3^{j-1}}$, both $\gamma_j$ and $b_j$ have relative norm
$\gamma_{j-1}$ to $\mathbb F_q$, and
\[
 \gamma_j b_j^2=\gamma_{j-1}.
\]
Because $\gcd(q-1,q^2+q+1)=1$, the norm propagates every old primary part.
For a new prime $\ell\mid q^2+q+1$, the base-field factor
$\gamma_{j-1}$ is an $\ell$-th power, while the displayed identity gives
$[\gamma_j]+2[b_j]=0$ in the $\ell$-Kummer quotient.  Since $\ell$ is odd,
$\gamma_j$ is an $\ell$-th power exactly when $b_j$ is.  Induction therefore
shows that all $P_j$ with $j\geq1$ are primitive polynomials exactly when
all $\gamma_j$ with $j\geq1$ are primitive.  Stability supplies the degrees;
primitive preservation is the original selected-order problem, not a known
solution of it.

Put $q_k=2^{3^{k-1}}$ and
\[
 U_k=\ker\!\left(
 \operatorname N_{\mathbb F_{q_k^3}/\mathbb F_{q_k}}
 \right),
 \qquad
 \eta_k=\gamma_k^{q_k-1},
 \qquad \eta_0=0.
\]

\begin{theorem}[selected norm-one coordinate]\label{thm:c-eta}
One has $|U_k|=q_k^2+q_k+1=\Phi_{3^k}(2)$.  The element $\eta_k$ generates
$\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}$ and is the selected root of
\[
 X^3+\eta_{k-1}X^2+(\eta_{k-1}+1)X+1.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 C_k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C_{k-1}\text{ and }\operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)=|U_k|,
\]
where $C_j$ denotes primitivity of $\gamma_j$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The definition puts $\eta_k$ in the norm-one group.  Frobenius calculation
from $\zeta_k$ gives the displayed cubic; its coefficients are respectively
the trace, second symmetric function, and norm.  A base-field element of
$U_k$ has order dividing $\gcd(q_k-1,3)=1$, so the nontrivial cubic is
irreducible.  Its quadratic coefficient recursively recovers
$\eta_{k-1}$, proving the field degree.  Finally, the finite-field unit group
splits into its base-field and norm-one factors, whose orders are coprime.
The norm propagates the old part of $\gamma_k$ and $\eta_k$ detects exactly
the new factor.
\end{proof}

The recursion selects one Frobenius orbit inside a Singer difference set.
Indeed, for
\[
 R(x)=\frac{x^3+x+1}{x^2+x},
 \qquad
 W(x)=\frac{x+\omega}{x+\omega^2},
\]
one has
\[
  R(\eta_k)=\eta_{k-1},
  \qquad W(R(x))=W(x)^3,
  \qquad W(\eta_k)=\zeta_k^2.
\]
Thus the complete lower ancestry, not merely degree or trace, marks the
orbit.  The displayed conjugacy exposes its cyclotomic phase but
does not evaluate the multiplicative order of $\eta_k$.

\begin{proposition}[selected Singer sieve]\label{prop:sieve}
Let $N=|U_k|$ and $h=3^k$.  Then
\[
  \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)\geq h(h-1)+1.
\]
If a prime $\ell\mid N$ is missing from the selected order at its full
valuation, then
\[
  \frac N\ell\geq h(h-1)+1.
\]
Hence every prime $\ell>N/(h(h-1)+1)$ occurs with full valuation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The selected Frobenius orbit has $h$ points in the Singer
$(N,q_k+1,1)$ difference set.  Its $h(h-1)$ ordered off-diagonal quotients
are distinct nonidentity elements of $\langle\eta_k\rangle$, giving the
first bound.  If the full $\ell$-part is missing, the orbit lies in the
subgroup of order $N/\ell$, which must contain all these quotients.
\end{proof}

The sieve is polynomial in $h$, while $N$ is exponential.  It proves a
genuine selected large-factor range but not arm $C$.

The cyclotomic selector also permits a complementary partition bound.  This
is the usual low-degree collision argument for Gaussian periods, specialized
to the composite conductor $3^{k+1}$ and therefore retaining the literal
selected element rather than replacing it by an arbitrary normal generator.

\begin{proposition}[selected norm-one partition bound]
\label{prop:c-partition}
Let $n=3^{k+1}=3h$.  For an integer $m\geq1$ put
\[
 \mathcal A_m=\{1\leq j\leq m:3\nmid j\},\qquad
 t_m=|\mathcal A_m|,\qquad s_m=\sum_{j\in\mathcal A_m}j.
\]
Let
\[
 \mathcal U_h=\{1\leq j<n:3\nmid j\},
\]
choose $0\leq z_j<2h$ with $2^{z_j}\equiv j\pmod n$, and, for
$E\subseteq\mathcal U_h$, put
\[
 Q_E=\sum_{j\in E}2^{z_j},\qquad s_E=\sum_{j\in E}j.
\]
For distinct $E,F\subseteq\mathcal U_h$, set
\[
 A=E\setminus F,\quad B=F\setminus E,\quad
 W=s_A+s_B,\quad j_0=\min(A\cup B).
\]
Then $\eta_k^{Q_E}\ne\eta_k^{Q_F}$ if either
\[
 s_A\not\equiv s_B\pmod3\ \text{ and }\ W<h,
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 s_A\equiv s_B\pmod3\ \text{ and }\ W-2j_0<h.
\]
In particular, weighted symmetric-difference sum below $h$ rules out every
collision.

Put
\[
 R_h=\frac{h-1}{2},\qquad
 \mathcal P_h=
 \sum_{r=0}^{R_h}[X^r]
 \prod_{\substack{j\geq1\\3\nmid j}}(1+X^j).
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)\geq\mathcal P_h,
 \qquad
 \log_2\mathcal P_h=
 \left(\frac{\pi}{3\log 2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt h.
\]
If $s_m<h$, then also
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)\geq2^{t_m}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \log_2\operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)
 \geq \left(\frac{\pi}{3\log 2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt h,
\]
and the elementary initial-segment subfamily alone gives
\[
 \log_2\operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)
 \geq \sqrt{\frac{4h}{3}}-O(1).
\]
If a prime $\ell\mid N=|U_k|$ is absent from the order of $\eta_k$ at its
full valuation, then $N/\ell\geq\mathcal P_h$ and, whenever $s_m<h$, also
$N/\ell\geq2^{t_m}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $w=\zeta_k^2$.  The identity $W(\eta_k)=w$ gives
\[
 \eta_k=\frac{\omega+\omega^2w}{1+w}.
\]
The order of two modulo $n$ is $2h$, so the exponents $z_j$ in the statement
exist.  They satisfy
\[
 \eta_k^{2^{z_j}}
 =\omega^j\frac{1+(\omega w)^j}{1+w^j}.
\]
A collision $\eta_k^{Q_E}=\eta_k^{Q_F}$ first permits cancellation of all indices in
$E\cap F$: the relevant numerator and denominator factors are nonzero at
$w$, because both $w$ and $\omega w$ are primitive $n$-th roots.  Put
$A=E\setminus F$, $B=F\setminus E$, and $W=s_A+s_B$ as in the statement.
Clearing the remaining
denominators makes $w$ a root of the difference between
\[
 \omega^{s_A}
 \prod_{j\in A}(1+(\omega X)^j)\prod_{j\in B}(1+X^j)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \omega^{s_B}
 \prod_{j\in B}(1+(\omega X)^j)\prod_{j\in A}(1+X^j).
\]
Each polynomial has degree $W$.  The element $w$ is a
primitive $n$-th root and the order of four modulo $n$ is $h$, so its minimal
polynomial over $\mathbb F_4$ is $X^h+\omega^2$.  Thus a collision with
$W<h$ would make the two polynomials identical.  Their constant coefficients
first force $s_A\equiv s_B\pmod3$.  Cancel that common scalar and let $j_0$
be the least index in $A\cup B$.  If, say, $j_0\in A$, then the coefficients
of $X^{j_0}$ on the two
sides are respectively $\omega^{j_0}$ and $1$.  They differ because
$3\nmid j_0$; the other case is symmetric.  More sharply, if
$s_A\not\equiv s_B\pmod3$, the
difference has span $W$, while if $s_A\equiv s_B\pmod3$, its constant and
leading terms cancel but its coefficients at degrees $j_0$ and $W-j_0$ are
nonzero, so its span is $W-2j_0$.  A nonzero multiple of $X^h+\omega^2$ has
span at least $h$.  This proves both pairwise criteria in the statement.

When $s_m<h$, necessarily $m<n$, so $\mathcal A_m\subseteq\mathcal U_h$.
For $E,F\subseteq\mathcal A_m$, their symmetric difference has weighted sum
at most $s_m$.  Hence all $2^{t_m}$ displayed powers are distinct.

Every subset $E$ with $s_E\leq R_h$ belongs to a family whose pairwise
weighted symmetric differences have sum at most $2R_h<h$.  The number of
such subsets is exactly $\mathcal P_h$, proving the first finite bound.  For
its asymptotic, put
\[
 G(t)=\prod_{j\geq1}(1+e^{-tj}),\qquad
 F(e^{-t})=\prod_{3\nmid j}(1+e^{-tj})=\frac{G(t)}{G(3t)}.
\]
The standard $q$-Pochhammer asymptotic gives
\[
 G(t)\sim2^{-1/2}\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi^2}{12t}\right),
 \qquad
 \frac{F(e^{-t})}{1-e^{-t}}
 \sim t^{-1}\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi^2}{18t}\right).
\]
The coefficients of $F(X)/(1-X)$ are the nonnegative, nondecreasing
cumulative counts in $\mathcal P_h$.  Ingham's exponential Tauberian theorem
\cite{ingham1941} therefore gives
\[
 \log\mathcal P_h
 =2\sqrt{\frac{\pi^2R_h}{18}}+o(\sqrt h)
 =\frac\pi3\sqrt h+o(\sqrt h).
\]
Finally, $t_m=2m/3+O(1)$ and $s_m=m^2/3+O(m)$; taking the largest $m$ with
$s_m<h$ gives the elementary initial-segment estimate.  The final assertion
follows because a missing full $\ell$-part would force
$\operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)\mid N/\ell$.
\end{proof}

There is a second, sharp consequence of the same selected formula.  It
classifies the first possible one-sided relation rather than merely packing
many pairwise-distinct powers.

\begin{proposition}[sharp selected first-relation threshold]
\label{prop:c-first-relation}
Retain the notation above.  For every nonempty $E\subseteq\mathcal U_h$,
put $S=s_E$ and decompose
\[
 B_E(X)=\prod_{j\in E}(1+X^j)
 =B_0(X^3)+XB_1(X^3)+X^2B_2(X^3),
 \qquad B_a\in\mathbb F_2[Y].
\]
If $a_0\equiv-S\pmod3$, then the exact all-weight criterion is
\[
 \eta_k^{Q_E}=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Y^{2H}+Y^H+1\mid B_a(Y)
 \quad\text{for both }a\ne a_0,
 \qquad H=h/3.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \eta_k^{Q_E}=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s_E\geq2h+1.
\]
The threshold is sharp: with $H=h/3$,
\[
 E_0=\{1,h-1,h+1\},\qquad
 Q_{E_0}=1+2^H+2^{2H}=|U_k|,\qquad s_{E_0}=2h+1.
\]
Consequently, if a prime $\ell\mid N=|U_k|$ is absent from the selected
order at its full valuation, write
\[
 \frac N\ell=\sum_{0\leq z<2H}\epsilon_z2^z,
 \qquad j_z\equiv2^z\pmod{3h},\quad1\leq j_z<3h.
\]
Then necessarily
\[
 \sum_{\epsilon_z=1}j_z\geq2h+1.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 P_E(X)=\omega^S B_E(\omega X)+B_E(X).
\]
The selected formula shows that $\eta_k^{Q_E}=1$ exactly when
$M(X)=X^h+\omega^2$, the minimal polynomial of $w$ over $\mathbb F_4$,
divides $P_E$.  If $[X^r]B_E=b_r\in\mathbb F_2$, then
\[
 [X^r]P_E=b_r(1+\omega^{S+r}).
\]
Thus the scalar multiplier vanishes precisely on the section
$r\equiv-S\pmod3$.  Write
the three sections as in the statement.
Since $h=3H$, division by
$M(X)=(X^3)^H+\omega^2$ respects these three sections.  Divisibility by $M$
therefore forces each of the two surviving $B_a(Y)$ to be divisible by
$Y^H+\omega^2$.  Because its coefficients lie in $\mathbb F_2$, conjugation
also forces divisibility by $Y^H+\omega$, and hence by
\[
 (Y^H+\omega^2)(Y^H+\omega)=Y^{2H}+Y^H+1.
\]
The converse is immediate because the displayed polynomial contains
$Y^H+\omega^2$ as a factor.  This proves the exact all-weight criterion.

If $S<2h$, every surviving section has degree below $2H$.  If $S=2h$, the
top term lies in the vanished residue-zero section, so the same strict bound
holds for the two surviving sections.  Hence both are zero.  The constant
term of $B_E$ then forces the sole supported section to be residue zero, but
the least $j\in E$ contributes coefficient one at $X^j$, in a nonzero residue
class.  This contradiction proves $S\geq2h+1$.

The congruence $2^H\equiv h-1\pmod{3h}$ follows by induction from
$2\equiv2\pmod9$ after cubing; squaring gives
$2^{2H}\equiv h+1\pmod{3h}$.  Hence $E_0$ has the asserted exponent, and
$\eta_k^N=1$ proves sharpness.  Finally, $N$ is odd and prime to three, so
$\ell\geq5$ and $N/\ell<2^{2H}$.  Its binary indices lie below $2H$ and
have distinct residues modulo $3h$.  A missing full $\ell$-part gives
$\eta_k^{N/\ell}=1$, so the first assertion gives the displayed residue-weight
bound.
\end{proof}

The all-weight criterion has an equivalent projective-torsion form which
pinpoints why Hilbert--90 does not close it.  This retains the marked root,
rather than replacing it by a generic norm-one element.

\begin{proposition}[marked projective obstruction and cohomological blindness]
\label{prop:c-projective}
Put $H=h/3$, $q=2^H$, $N=q^2+q+1$, and let
$\ell\mid N$ be prime, with $d=N/\ell$.  In
\[
 L=\mathbb F_{2^{2h}},\qquad K=\mathbb F_{q^2},
\]
write $w=\zeta_k^2$ and
\[
 Z=\frac{(1+w)^d}{w^2}.
\]
Then
\[
 \eta_k^d=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Z\in K.
\]
Moreover, with $\alpha=w^3$,
\[
 Z^\ell
 =\omega^2(1+\alpha)\alpha^{-(2\ell+1)/3}\in K.
\]
Thus $[Z]\in L^\times/K^\times$ is an explicitly marked
$\ell$-torsion class, and arm~$C$ asks that this class be nontrivial for
every current $\ell$.

Let $\sigma(x)=x^{q^2}$ generate $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$.  On
$\mu_\ell$ one has
\[
 1+\sigma+\sigma^2=0,
 \qquad \sigma-1\text{ is bijective},
 \qquad H^1(\langle\sigma\rangle,\mu_\ell)=0.
\]
Consequently the norm and Hilbert--90 conditions on the displayed torsion
class are automatic; they do not distinguish $[Z]=1$ from a nontrivial
marked class.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The congruences
\[
 2^H\equiv h-1,\qquad 2^{2H}\equiv h+1\pmod{3h}
\]
give
\[
 w^q=\omega^2w^{-1},\qquad w^{q^2}=\omega^2w.
\]
The selected formula in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:c-partition} gives
\[
 \frac{1+\omega^2w}{1+w}=\omega\eta_k^{q+1}.
\]
Since every prime divisor $\ell$ of $N$ satisfies
$\ell\equiv1\pmod3$, also $d\equiv1\pmod3$.  Therefore
\[
 Z^{q^2-1}
 =\omega^2\left(\frac{1+\omega^2w}{1+w}\right)^d
 =\eta_k^{d(q+1)}.
\]
As $\gcd(q+1,N)=1$, this is one exactly when $\eta_k^d=1$,
which proves the fixed-field equivalence.

The three conjugates under $q$-powering satisfy
\[
 (1+w)^N
 =(1+w)(1+\omega^2w^{-1})(1+\omega^2w)
 =\frac{\omega^2(1+w^3)}w.
\]
Hence
\[
 Z^\ell=\frac{(1+w)^N}{w^{2\ell}}
 =\omega^2(1+\alpha)\alpha^{-(2\ell+1)/3};
\]
the exponent is integral because $\ell\equiv1\pmod3$.  The left side is
fixed by $q^2$-powering (equivalently, this also follows by direct use of
$\alpha^{q^2}=\alpha$), so it lies in $K$.

Finally put $Q=q^2$.  Modulo $\ell$,
\[
 1+Q+Q^2=1+q^2+q^4\equiv1+q^2+q=0,
\]
because $q^3\equiv1$.  On the other hand
$\gcd(Q-1,\ell)=1$: any common divisor of $q^2-1$ and
$q^2+q+1$ divides three, whereas $\ell\ne3$.  Thus the norm operator is
zero on $\mu_\ell$ and the coboundary operator $\sigma-1$ is an
automorphism.  The standard cyclic formula
\[
 H^1=\ker(1+\sigma+\sigma^2)/(\sigma-1)\mu_\ell
\]
is therefore zero.  This proves the claimed blindness, not the desired
pointwise nontriviality of $[Z]$.

More explicitly, $c=\sigma(Z)/Z$ lies in $\mu_\ell$ because $Z^\ell\in K$.
There is a unique $\rho\in\mu_\ell$ with
$c=\sigma(\rho)/\rho$, and then $Z/\rho\in K$, so $[Z]=[\rho]$ in
$L^\times/K^\times$.  Every possible marked projective class is therefore
represented by a coboundary; the zero cohomology class does not say whether
its representing root $\rho$ equals one.
\end{proof}

Together, the partition and first-relation bounds strengthen the polynomial
Singer sieve to an exponential-in-$\sqrt h$ exclusion and a sharp
weight-$2h$ exclusion for the actual norm-one coordinate.  They remain
subexponential and do not classify relations above the norm triple, so they
do not force primitivity.

There is an exact number-field factorization of the remaining index.  Put
$L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{3^{k+1}})^+$ and
$K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{3^k})^+$, reduce global units $E_F$ and circular units
$C_F$ modulo the inert prime over two, and write
\[
 R_F=(\mathcal O_F/2)^{\times}/\overline E_F,
 \qquad T_F=\overline E_F/\overline C_F.
\]
Then
\[
 [U_k:\langle\eta_k\rangle]
 =|\ker(\operatorname N:R_L\to R_K)|
  \frac{[\overline E_L:\overline C_L]}
       {[\overline E_K:\overline C_K]}.
\]
The first factor is a relative principal $2$-ray kernel; the second divides
the relative real class-number quotient.  Even class number one would leave
the principal-ray factor.  This is not a formal possibility only: analogous
prime-conductor periods can have inert two and class number one while their
finite circular-unit reduction has nontrivial defect
\cite{hoechsmann1995}.  Arm $C$ is exactly the assertion that both factors
in the displayed index formula vanish at every level.

\section{The exceptional arm: corrected norm and Capelli}\label{sec:d}

Let $h=3^k$, $q=2^h$, $\zeta=\zeta_k$, and
$\omega=\zeta^h\in\mathbb F_4$.

\begin{proposition}[first possible translate]\label{prop:d-lower}
If $f(p)=2\cdot3^k$, then $m_p\geq4$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Such a prime divides $2^h+1$ but neither $3^{k+1}$ nor $2^h-1$.  The
half-angle decomposition in Proposition~\ref{prop:c-half} shows that each of
$\zeta,\zeta+1,\zeta+2,\zeta+3$ has order prime to $p$.  All four are
$p$-th powers in their defining fields, so the excess definition gives the
claim.
\end{proof}

The translate by four lies in a compositum with $\mathbb F_{16}$.  The
nontrivial relative Frobenius over $\mathbb F_4$ exchanges the nimbers $4$
and $5$, which are the roots of $Y^2+Y+\omega$.

\begin{proposition}[corrected norm]\label{prop:d-norm}
In $\mathbb F_{2^{4h}}/\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}$,
\[
 \operatorname N(\zeta+4)
 = (\zeta+4)(\zeta+5)
 = \zeta^2+\zeta+\omega=A_k.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \mathbb F_2(\zeta+4)=\mathbb F_{2^{4h}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One has $4+5=1$ and $4\cdot5=\omega$.  Expanding the product gives the
formula.

Put $v=\zeta+4$ and suppose $v^{2^j}=v$.  Then
\[
 c:=\zeta^{2^j}+\zeta=4^{2^j}+4
\]
belongs both to $\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}$ and to $\mathbb F_{16}$.  Since $h$ is
odd, their intersection is $\mathbb F_4$, so
$c\in\{0,1,\omega,\omega^2\}$.  If $c\ne0$, the Frobenius conjugate
$\zeta^{2^j}=\zeta+c$ would have order
$3^{k+1}\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k)>3^{k+1}$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:c-half}, whereas every Frobenius conjugate of $\zeta$
has order exactly $3^{k+1}$.  Hence $c=0$.  It follows simultaneously that
$2h\mid j$ and $4\mid j$.  Their least common multiple is $4h$, so the
Frobenius orbit of $v$ has size $4h$, proving the generation assertion.
\end{proof}

Define
\[
 R_k(Y)=\operatorname{Res}_X
 \left(X^{2h}+X^h+1,\;Y+X^h+X^2+X\right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[exact exceptional target]\label{thm:d-target}
The polynomial $R_k$ is the irreducible degree-$2h$ minimal polynomial of
$A_k$.  The prime divisors of
$\Psi_k=\Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)/3$ are exactly the primes
$\ell\ne3$ with $\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)=2h$, with their full
valuations.  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every row with $f(\ell)=2\cdot3^k$ has $m_\ell=4$;
\item $\Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k)$;
\item $A_k$ is not an $\ell$-th power in $\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}$ for every
      prime $\ell\mid\Psi_k$;
\item $R_k(X^\ell)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb F_2$ for every prime
      $\ell\mid\Psi_k$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The element $\zeta$ has minimal polynomial $X^{2h}+X^h+1$.  To justify the
degree of $A_k$, suppose $A_k^{2^j}=A_k$, put
$t\equiv2^j\pmod{3h}$, and write $u=\zeta^t$.  If $j$ is even, then
$\omega^t=\omega$ and
\[
 (u+\zeta)(u+\zeta+1)=0,
\]
so $u$ is either $\zeta$ or $\zeta+1$.  If $j$ is odd, then
$\omega^t=\omega^2$ and
\[
 (u+\zeta+\omega)(u+\zeta+\omega^2)=0,
\]
so $u$ is either $\zeta+\omega$ or $\zeta+\omega^2$.  The three translated
possibilities are impossible: Proposition~\ref{prop:c-half} gives each the
same order as $\zeta+1$, namely
$3^{k+1}\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k)>3^{k+1}$; here
$\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k)>1$ because
$\mathbb F_2(\gamma_k)=\mathbb F_{2^h}$ and $h>1$.  On the other hand, $u$ is a
$3^{k+1}$-st root of unity.  Hence $u=\zeta$, so
$2^j\equiv1\pmod{3h}$.  Since the order of two modulo $3h=3^{k+1}$ is
$2h$, the Frobenius orbit of $A_k$ has size $2h$.  Thus $A_k$ has degree
$2h$ and the displayed resultant is its minimal polynomial.

The cyclotomic divisor criterion applied to
$\Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)$ gives the stated prime support after removing its
single factor of three.  Proposition~\ref{prop:d-lower} and the corrected
norm, now taken from the actual defining field of $\zeta+4$, give
\[
 m_\ell=4
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A_k\notin(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}^{\times})^\ell.
\]
Raising to $q-1$ is invertible on the current $\ell$-Kummer quotient, so
this is equivalent to the full $\ell$-primary part occurring in
$\operatorname{ord}(M_k)$.  Finally, $\mu_\ell$ lies in the defining field;
Kummer theory and Capelli's lemma identify nonpower with irreducibility of
$R_k(X^\ell)$.
\end{proof}

The exceptional Euler test also has a lossless sparse form parallel to the
three-block criterion in the cubic arm.

\begin{proposition}[sparse exceptional block criterion]
\label{prop:d-block}
Let $n=3h$, let $\ell\mid\Psi_k$ be prime, put
$d=(q+1)/\ell$, and work in
$\mathcal R_h=\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^n-1)$.  With $x$ the class of $X$, set
\[
 \mathcal A=x^2+x+x^h,\qquad
 \iota(x)=x^{-1},\qquad C=\mathcal A^d.
\]
Write the unique cyclic representative of $V=C+\iota(C)$ as
\[
 V=V_0+X^hV_1+X^{2h}V_2,
 \qquad \deg V_i<h.
\]
Then
\[
 M_k^{(q+1)/\ell}=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 V_0=V_1=V_2.
\]
If $d=\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}\epsilon_i2^i$, then, losslessly in
$\mathcal R_h$,
\[
 C=\prod_{\epsilon_i=1}
 \left(x^{2^{i+1}}+x^{2^i}+x^{h2^i}\right),
\]
where exponents are read modulo $n$.  Thus arm $D$ is equivalent to failure
of this three-block equality for every current prime $\ell$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The order of two modulo $3^{k+1}=n$ is $2h$, so
$q=2^h\equiv-1\pmod n$.  Hence $q$-powering on $\mathcal R_h$ is
$\iota$.  In the selected field
$\mathbb F_2[X]/(J_h)$, where $J_h=X^{2h}+X^h+1$, the image of
$\mathcal A$ is $A_k$, and
\[
 M_k^d=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (A_k^d)^q=A_k^d
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 J_h\mid C+\iota(C).
\]
The first equivalence is kernel-checked abstractly as
\texttt{exceptional\_euler\_fixed\_iff}.  Modulo $J_h$, characteristic two
gives $X^{2h}=X^h+1$, and therefore
\[
 V\equiv(V_0+V_2)+X^h(V_1+V_2).
\]
The right side has degree less than $2h=\deg J_h$, so it vanishes exactly
when $V_0=V_1=V_2$.  The product formula is the binary-power expansion of
$\mathcal A^d$ in characteristic two.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[primitive-only palindromicity]
\label{cor:d-palindromic}
In the notation of Proposition~\ref{prop:d-block}, one always has
\[
 V_0+V_1+V_2=0.
\]
Consequently the following conditions are equivalent:
\[
 M_k^d=1,
 \qquad V_0=V_1=V_2,
 \qquad V_0=V_1=V_2=0,
 \qquad C=\iota(C)\text{ in }\mathcal R_h.
\]
In particular, every proper cyclic fold through
$\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^h-1)$ vanishes identically on $V$; no Fourier test on a
proper $3$-power quotient can detect arm~$D$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By lifting the exponent of three,
\[
 v_3(2^h+1)=k+1,
\]
so $n=3^{k+1}$ divides $q+1$.  Since $\ell\ne3$, it follows from
$q+1=\ell d$ that $n\mid d$.  Modulo $X^h-1$ one has
\[
 \mathcal A=X^2+X+1,
 \qquad \iota(\mathcal A)=X^{-2}\mathcal A.
\]
Hence
\[
 \iota(C)=X^{-2d}C=C
       \pmod {X^h-1}.
\]
The image of $V_0+X^hV_1+X^{2h}V_2$ in this quotient is
$V_0+V_1+V_2$, proving the first assertion.  If the three blocks are equal
to $W$, their sum is $3W=W$ in characteristic two, so $W=0$.  The converse
is immediate, and every quotient $X^m-1$ with $m\mid h$ factors through
$X^h-1$.
\end{proof}

There is a second, complementary compression which shortens the binary
product by one third before testing this palindromicity.

\begin{proposition}[one-third Frobenius compression]
\label{prop:d-one-third}
Put
\[
 H=3^{k-1},\qquad h=3H,\qquad Q=2^H,
 \qquad S=Q^2-Q+1=3\Psi_k,
\]
and, for a prime $\ell\mid\Psi_k$, put $e=S/\ell$.  Then
\[
 Q\equiv h-1\pmod {3h},\qquad
 S\equiv3\pmod {3h},\qquad
 e\equiv3\pmod {3h},
\]
and
\[
 d=(Q+1)e.
\]
In $\mathcal R_h$, define
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal B}=\mathcal A^{Q+1}.
\]
It has the lossless seven-term representative
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal B}
 =1+x^{-2}+x^h+x^{h+1}+x^{2h}+x^{2h+1}+x^{2h+2},
\]
and $C=\widetilde{\mathcal B}^{e}$.  In the primitive component
$\mathbb F_2[X]/(J_h)$ this reduces further to the trinomial
\[
 \mathcal B=x^{-2}+x+x^{2h+2}.
\]
Thus
\[
 M_k^d=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal B^e=\iota(\mathcal B)^e
       \text{ in }\mathbb F_2[X]/(J_h),
\]
while arm~$D$ asks for the opposite inequality.  The exponent now has at
most $2H+1=2h/3+1$ binary places, rather than $h+1$.

If $e=3$---equivalently, if $\Psi_k=\ell$ is prime---then the opposite
inequality always holds.  Hence every prime-complement level satisfies
arm~$D$; in particular this gives proof-level, non-enumerative certificates
for $k=2$ and $k=3$.

More generally, put $r=\Psi_k/\ell$ and
\[
 \varepsilon_k=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&k\text{ even},\\
  \omega^2,&k\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]
For the reciprocal ratio
\[
 P=\frac{\iota(\mathcal B)}{\mathcal B}
   =M_k^{Q+1}
\]
one has the exact identities
\[
 P^{\Psi_k}=\varepsilon_k,
 \qquad
 M_k^d=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P^r=\varepsilon_k.
\]
Thus, after removing its explicitly known three-primary phase, the unresolved
composite target has exponent $r=\Psi_k/\ell\equiv1\pmod {2h}$ rather than
$e=3\Psi_k/\ell$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The congruence for $Q$ follows by induction.  The base case is
$2\equiv3-1\pmod9$.  If
$Q+1=h(1+3t)$, write $h=3s$ and cube.  Then
\[
 Q^3+1
 =3h\left(1+3\left[
 t-s(1+3t)^2+s^2(1+3t)^3
 \right]\right),
\]
which is the required congruence at the next level.  Substitution gives
\[
 S\equiv(h-1)^2-(h-1)+1\equiv3\pmod {3h}.
\]
Since $S=3\Psi_k$ and $\ell\mid\Psi_k$, the integer $e$ is divisible by
three.  Moreover $\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)=2h$ implies
$h\mid\ell-1$.  Therefore $3h\mid(\ell-1)e$, and from $\ell e=S$ one gets
$e\equiv S\equiv3\pmod {3h}$.

The factorization $Q^3+1=(Q+1)S$ gives $d=(Q+1)e$.  Frobenius and the first
congruence give
\[
 \mathcal A^Q
 =x^{2h-2}+x^{h-1}+x^{2h}.
\]
Multiplication by $\mathcal A=x^2+x+x^h$ cancels the two copies of
$x^{2h-1}$ and gives the displayed seven terms.  Modulo $J_h$, the three
terms $1+x^h+x^{2h}$ vanish and
$x^{h+1}+x^{2h+1}=x$, leaving $\mathcal B$.  The fixed-field equivalence is
then Proposition~\ref{prop:d-block} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:d-palindromic} applied to
$C=\widetilde{\mathcal B}^{e}$.

We next determine the three-primary phase.  Since
$(Q+1)\Psi_k=(q+1)/3$,
\[
 P^{\Psi_k}
 =M_k^{(q+1)/3}
 =A_k^{(q^2-1)/3}
 =\operatorname N_{\mathbb F_{4^h}/\mathbb F_4}(A_k).
\]
The minimal polynomial of $\zeta$ over $\mathbb F_4$ is $X^h+\omega$.
If $y^2+y+\omega=0$, its other conjugate is $y+1$, so the resultant formula
for the norm gives
\[
 \operatorname N(A_k)
 =(y^h+\omega)((y+1)^h+\omega).
\]
Reduction by $y^2+y+\omega=0$ gives $y^{15}=1$ and
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cccc}
  h\bmod15&3&9&12&6\\ \hline
  y^h&\omega+\omega^2y&\omega+\omega y&1+\omega^2y&\omega y.
 \end{array}
\]
The residues of $3^k$ modulo $15$ run through $3,9,12,6$.  Using
$y(y+1)=\omega$, the displayed product is therefore $\omega^2$ in the
first and third cases and $1$ in the second and fourth.  This proves
$P^{\Psi_k}=\varepsilon_k$.

Now $e=3r$.  If $P^e=1$, then $P^r$ is a cube root of unity.  Moreover
\[
 (P^r)^\ell=P^{\Psi_k}=\varepsilon_k.
\]
Every current prime satisfies $\ell\equiv1\pmod3$, so raising a cube root
of unity to the $\ell$-th power fixes it.  Hence $P^r=\varepsilon_k$.
The converse follows from $\varepsilon_k^3=1$.  Finally, since every prime
factor of $r$ is current and hence congruent to one modulo $2h$, one has
$r\equiv1\pmod {2h}$.

It remains to prove the prime-complement assertion, where $r=1$.  Put
$z=\zeta$ and multiply numerator and denominator of $P$ by $z^2$.  Then
\[
 P=\frac{N}{D},\qquad
 N=z^4+z+\omega,\qquad
 D=1+z^3+\omega^2z^4,
\]
and direct factorization over $\mathbb F_4$ gives
\[
 N+D=\omega(z+\omega^2)(z^3+\omega^2),
 \qquad
 N+\omega^2D=\omega^2(z+1)(z^3+\omega).
\]
If $k$ is even, the first identity shows $P\ne1$; if $k$ is odd, the second
shows $P\ne\omega^2$.  Indeed $z$ has order $3h\ge27$, so neither $z$ nor
$z^3$ can equal the displayed cube root of unity.  Thus
$P\ne\varepsilon_k$, as required.  The only earlier level has
$\Psi_1=1$ and no current prime.
\end{proof}

The parity-twisted exponent reduction does not by itself settle composite
$\Psi_k$.  After putting $U=\varepsilon_k^{-1}P$, it says exactly that
$U^{\Psi_k}=1$ and that arm~$D$ asks for
$U^{\Psi_k/\ell}\ne1$ for every current prime $\ell$.  Thus it isolates the
prime-to-three current coordinate but does not prove that every one of its
prime factors occurs in $\operatorname{ord}(U)$.  Here the normalization uses
$\Psi_k\equiv1\pmod3$ and $r\equiv1\pmod3$, the latter already following from
$r\equiv1\pmod {2h}$.

The ordinary-arm ratio packing has an exceptional analogue, provided the
multiplier roots of unity and the degree of the marked root are balanced by
an intermediate constant field.

\begin{proposition}[intermediate-field exceptional packing sieve]
\label{prop:d-packing}
Assume $k\geq2$, put $h=3^k$, and let the prime $\ell$ divide $\Psi_k$.
For $0\leq j<k$, set
\[
 m_j=3^j,\qquad R_j=3^{k-j},\qquad
 s_j=\left\lfloor\frac{R_j-1}{8}\right\rfloor
\]
and define
\[
 \mathcal S_k=
 \max_{0\leq j<k}
 \sum_{t=0}^{\min(2m_j,s_j)}
 2^t\binom{2m_j}{t}\binom{s_j}{t}.
\]
If the current $\ell$-coordinate is deficient, then
\[
 \frac{\Psi_k}{\ell}\geq\mathcal S_k.
\]
Consequently arm~$D$ holds at every current factor satisfying
\[
 \frac{\Psi_k}{\ell}<\mathcal S_k.
\]
Moreover, if $H_2(x)=-x\log_2x-(1-x)\log_2(1-x)$ is binary entropy, then
\[
 \log_2\mathcal S_k
 \geq c_0\sqrt h-O(\log h),
 \qquad
 c_0=\frac14+\frac23H_2\!\left(\frac38\right)
              +\frac38H_2\!\left(\frac23\right)
 >1.2306.
\]
Thus this sieve proves an exponential-in-$\sqrt h$ large-factor range at
every exceptional level, while remaining subexponential in $h$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $z=\zeta$, $d=\Psi_k/\ell$, and regard the normalized phase as the
rational function
\[
 U(T)=\varepsilon_k^{-1}\frac{\mathcal N(T)}{\mathcal D(T)},
 \qquad
 \mathcal N(T)=T^4+T+\omega,
 \qquad
 \mathcal D(T)=1+T^3+\omega^2T^4.
\]
The polynomial $T^h+\omega$ is the minimal polynomial of $z$ over
$\mathbb F_4$.  The order of four modulo $3h$ is $h$, and the map
\[
 t\longmapsto z^{4^t-1},\qquad 0\leq t<h,
\]
is a bijection from the Frobenius orbit indices to $\mu_h$: every value is
in $\mu_h$, and injectivity follows from the orbit size.  Hence the roots of
$T^h+\omega$ are exactly $cz$, $c\in\mu_h$.  Since $U$ is defined over
$\mathbb F_4$, deficiency $U(z)^d=1$ implies
\[
 U(cz)^d=1\qquad(c\in\mu_h).
\]

Fix $j$, abbreviate $m=m_j$, $R=R_j$, $s=s_j$, and put
$E=\mathbb F_{4^m}$.  LTE gives
\[
 v_3(4^m-1)=1+v_3(m)=j+1,
\]
so $\mu_{3m}\subset E$.  Since $E\subset\mathbb F_{4^h}$,
$[E(z):E]=h/m=R$.  Also $z^R$ has order $3m$ and lies in $E$, so the
minimal polynomial of $z$ over $E$ is the binomial $T^R+z^R$.

Over $\mathbb F_4$ introduce the six monic quadratics
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
 f_1=T^2+\omega T+1,
 &f_2=T^2+\omega T+\omega,
 &f_3=T^2+\omega^2T+1,\\
 f_4=T^2+T+\omega,
 &f_5=T^2+T+\omega^2,
 &f_6=T^2+\omega^2T+\omega^2.
\end{array}
\]
They are distinct and irreducible by direct inspection in $\mathbb F_4$.
Up to nonzero scalar factors,
\[
 \mathcal N(T)=f_1(T)f_2(T),\qquad
 \mathcal D(T)\doteq f_3(T)f_4(T),
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal N(\omega T)\doteq f_4(T)f_5(T),\qquad
 \mathcal D(\omega T)=f_2(T)f_6(T),
\]
where $\doteq$ denotes equality up to a nonzero scalar.  Since $m$ is odd,
$E\cap\mathbb F_{16}=\mathbb F_4$, so all six quadratics remain irreducible
over $E$.

Choose representatives $\mathcal C$ for $\mu_{3m}/\mu_3$.  Scaled factors
belonging to different representatives are disjoint.  Indeed, a common root
of $f_a(cT)$ and $f_b(c'T)$ would give
$c'/c\in\mathbb F_{16}^{\times}\cap\mu_{3m}=\mu_3$, hence $c=c'$ by the
choice of representatives, and then $a=b$ because the six factors are
distinct.  In particular, for each $c\in\mathcal C$ the two elements
\[
 u_{c,0}=U(cz),\qquad u_{c,1}=U(\omega cz)
\]
have, up to scalars, the factor patterns
\[
 u_{c,0}\doteq\frac{f_1(cz)f_2(cz)}{f_3(cz)f_4(cz)},
 \qquad
 u_{c,1}\doteq\frac{f_4(cz)f_5(cz)}{f_2(cz)f_6(cz)}.
\]
Their denominators are nonzero: otherwise a conjugate of $z$ would be a
root over $\mathbb F_4$ of the degree-four polynomial $\mathcal D$, whereas
$z$ and all its conjugates have degree $h\geq9$.  All $2m$ displayed
elements are $d$-th roots of unity.

Index them by a set $I$ of size $2m$.  For
$v\in\mathbb Z^I$ with $\sum_i|v_i|\leq s$, put
$V_v=\prod_i u_i^{v_i}$.  Suppose $V_v=V_w$ and set $g=v-w$.  Separating
positive and negative exponents and cross-multiplying gives an equality at
$z$ between two polynomials in $E[T]$, each of degree at most
\[
 4\sum_i|g_i|\leq8s<R.
\]
The degree-$R$ minimal polynomial therefore forces equality of the two
polynomials.  Unique factorization now recovers every exponent: for a fixed
$c$, the valuation at $f_1(cT)$ gives $g_{c,0}=0$, and the valuation at
$f_5(cT)$ gives $g_{c,1}=0$.  Cross-star disjointness handles all
$c\in\mathcal C$.  Thus the $V_v$ are distinct.

The signed $\ell^1$ ball in $\mathbb Z^{2m}$ has exactly
\[
 \sum_{t=0}^{\min(2m,s)}
 2^t\binom{2m}{t}\binom{s}{t}
\]
points.  Since their images are distinct roots of $X^d-1$, this number is
at most $d$.  Taking the maximum over $j$ proves the exact bound.

For the asymptotic statement, first let $k$ be even and take
$j=k/2-1$.  Then, with $x=\sqrt h$,
\[
 2m=\frac23x,\qquad s=\frac38x+O(1).
\]
The summand with $t=\lfloor x/4\rfloor$ and Stirling's formula give
\[
 \log_2\mathcal S_k\geq
 \left[\frac14+\frac23H_2\!\left(\frac38\right)
                  +\frac38H_2\!\left(\frac23\right)\right]x
 -O(\log h).
\]
If $k$ is odd, take $j=(k-3)/2$.  Then
\[
 2m=\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}x,
 \qquad s=\frac{3\sqrt3}{8}x+O(1),
\]
and the same choice $t=\lfloor x/4\rfloor$ gives coefficient
\[
 \frac14+\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}H_2\!\left(\frac{3\sqrt3}{8}\right)
 +\frac{3\sqrt3}{8}H_2\!\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}\right)
 >1.2341>c_0.
\]
The $O(\log h)$ errors are the standard logarithmic errors in the two
binomial-coefficient estimates.  This proves the uniform claim for all
sufficiently large $k$; enlarging the implicit constant absorbs the finitely
many smaller levels.
\end{proof}

This packing theorem is a genuine composite-$\Psi_k$ range, not a universal
proof: its lower bound is exponential in $\sqrt h$, whereas $\Psi_k$ is
exponential in $h$.  Logarithmic or Hasse
derivatives do not add an intrinsic constraint in this finite etale quotient:
$\mathbb F_2[X]/(J_h)$ has no nonzero $\mathbb F_2$-derivations, while the
formal polynomial derivative does not descend because
$J_h'=X^{h-1}$ is a unit modulo $J_h$.  A derivative argument would therefore
need genuinely new lift or multiplicity information.  Proposition~
\ref{prop:d-one-third} and Proposition~\ref{prop:d-packing} therefore prove
the prime-complement case and a large-factor range, not universal arm~$D$.

\begin{proposition}[effective exceptional bound]\label{prop:d-bound}
Let $k\geq2$, $h=3^k$, and let $\ell\mid\Psi_k$ be prime.  If $t$ is the
least power of two satisfying
$t\geq\lceil\log_2(h-1)\rceil$, then
\[
  m_\ell\leq4^t-1<16h^4.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $K=\mathbb F_{4^h}$ and extend constants to
$L=K\mathbb F_{4^t}$.  For a character $\chi$ of exact order $\ell$, the
sum
\[
  S=\sum_{c\in\mathbb F_{4^t}}
      (\chi\circ\operatorname N_{L/K})(\zeta+c)
\]
equals $4^t$ precisely when every translate is an $\ell$-th power.  The
associated primitive even function-field character has $L$-polynomial
$(1-T)\prod_{j=1}^{h-2}(1-\alpha_jT)$ with $|\alpha_j|=2$.  Constant-field
extension therefore gives
\[
  |S|\leq1+(h-2)2^t<4^t
\]
when $2^t\geq h-1$; see \cite{rosen2002}.  Some translate is a nonpower,
and norm injectivity on the $\ell$-Kummer quotient returns the statement to
its defining field.  The elements of $\mathbb F_{4^t}$ are the finite
nimbers below $4^t$.  The displayed polynomial estimate follows from the
minimal choice of $t$.
\end{proof}

This bound is level-dependent and does not prove absolute boundedness or the
value four.

The exceptional coordinate also has an exact circular-ray factorization.
In the real field generated by roots of orders $3^{k+1}$ and $5$, let
$\mathfrak P$ be the prime selected by $\zeta\mapsto\zeta_k$ and the
conductor-five coordinate $\mapsto\omega$.  Let $E_L$ and $C_L$ be the
global and circular units and let a subscript $\ell$ denote the primary
projection after reduction.  Then
\[
 \overline C_{L,\ell}
 =\langle A_{k,\ell}\rangle
 =\langle M_{k,\ell}\rangle,
\]
and
\[
 [k_{\mathfrak P,\ell}:\langle M_{k,\ell}\rangle]
 =\left|\ker\bigl(
   \operatorname{Cl}_L(\mathfrak P)_\ell\to
   \operatorname{Cl}_L{}_{\ell}\bigr)\right|
  |(\overline E_L/\overline C_L)_\ell|.
\]
The second factor is controlled by the real class number, but the first is a
principal ray kernel.  The marked conductor-five cyclotomic unit is globally
not an $\ell$-th power; the height theorem of Amoroso--Dvornicich gives this
directly \cite{amorosodvornicich2000}.  Global nonpower does not determine
its Frobenius at the selected prime.  Arm $D$ is exactly the assertion that
both factors in the displayed ray-class index vanish.

The current $C$ and $D$ coordinates are orthogonal.  If
$q_0=2^{h/3}$ and $q=q_0^3$, their norm-one tori have orders
\[
 q_0^2+q_0+1
 \quad\text{and}\quad q+1,
\]
which are coprime.  The exceptional cubical operator kills the cubic
subfield, while the local norm kills the exceptional torus.  Hence the two
principal-ray factors cannot force one another through the shared prime over
two.  Each requires its own marked unit-image calculation.

Even a perfect one-dimensional Kummer--Artin pairing does not show that a
prescribed prime has nonzero image in the Artin quotient.  The obstruction
already occurs arithmetically.

\begin{proposition}[perfect-pairing no-go]\label{prop:pairing-no-go}
There exist a number field $K$ containing $\mu_3$, a global unit $u$ which is
not a cube in $K$, and exactly one prime $\mathfrak q$ of $K$ above two; this
prime splits completely in the nontrivial cyclic Kummer extension
$K(\sqrt[3]{u})/K$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take
\[
 K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3,\sqrt3),
 \qquad u=2+\sqrt3.
\]
The Pell equation gives
$\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)}^\times=\{\mathord\pm u^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}$,
so $u$ is not a cube in the real quadratic subfield.  If $X^3-u$ acquired a
root in its quadratic extension $K$, its cubic factorization over the real
subfield would also have a linear factor.  Hence $u$ is not a cube in $K$,
and adjoining a cube root gives a nontrivial cyclic cubic extension.

Locally at two, $\mathbb Q_2(\zeta_3)/\mathbb Q_2$ is unramified quadratic
and adjoining $i$ is ramified quadratic.  The two local quadratics are
linearly disjoint, so $K\otimes\mathbb Q_2$ is a degree-four field; hence two
has a unique prime $\mathfrak q$ in $K$.  In its residue field
$\sqrt3\equiv1$, so $u\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak q}$.  The polynomial $X^3-u$
has the simple root $1$ modulo $\mathfrak q$, because its derivative at one
is the unit $3$.  Hensel's lemma makes $u$ a cube in $K_{\mathfrak q}$; since
$\mu_3\subset K_{\mathfrak q}$, the polynomial splits completely there and
$\mathfrak q$ splits completely in $K(\sqrt[3]{u})$.
\end{proof}

Thus nontriviality of the Kummer line, one-dimensionality of its dual, and
even uniqueness of the prime above two are compatible with zero marked
pairing.  In the four arms, perfect duality reduces the target to
nonvanishing of the Conway-selected prime class; it does not prove that
nonvanishing.

\section{Nim reciprocity saturation and marked holonomy}
\label{sec:nim-reciprocity}

The residue-symbol reductions suggest using reciprocity to evaluate the
remaining phase.  There is a precise saturation boundary for that program.
Let $\ell$ be an odd prime, let $s\in\mu_\ell$, and put
$E=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)$.  The two-axis Frobenius--cyclotomic family
is $s_{j,c}=s^{2^j c}$ for $0\leq j<E$ and
$c\in\mathbb F_\ell^\times$.

\begin{proposition}[reciprocity saturation]\label{prop:reciprocity-saturation}
The following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every multiplicative word in the $s_{j,c}$ is $s^\lambda$, where
\[
 \lambda=\sum_{j,c}w_{j,c}2^jc\pmod\ell.
\]
It is blind when $\lambda=0$ and, when $\lambda\ne0$, equals one exactly when
the original marked phase equals one.
\item For multiplicative characters $\chi,\psi$ of a finite field and
$a\ne0$, the Jacobi convolution
\[
 J_{\chi,\psi}(a)=\sum_x\chi(x)\psi(a-x)
\]
satisfies
\[
 J_{\chi,\psi}(a)=\chi(a)\psi(a)J_{\chi,\psi}(1).
\]
In particular, if both characters are powers of one order-$\ell$ character,
the marked dependence is again one power of the same phase.
\item In the affine group on $\mathbb F_\ell$, write $(t,q)$, with
$q\in\mathbb F_\ell^\times$, for the map $x\mapsto qx+t$.  If $q\ne1$, all
$(t,q)$ are conjugate.  Consequently every class function is independent of
$t$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first statement follows by collecting exponents.  For the second,
substitute $x=ay$ and factor $\chi(a)\psi(a)$ from every summand.  For the
third, conjugation by the translation $x\mapsto x+a$ changes $t$ to
$t+(1-q)a$; taking $a=t/(q-1)$ makes this zero.
\end{proof}

Thus norm products, weighted Stickelberger words, and Gauss or Jacobi
transforms either erase or re-encode one phase.  A class function on the
single fixed-multiplier affine coset is blinder still: it forgets the
translation coordinate in part~3.  None of these operations independently
evaluates the selected phase.

Ordinary cross-arm multiplicative words face the same boundary.  If a strict
ancestor lies in a field $\mathbb F_{2^d}$ with $f(\ell)\nmid d$, exactness
of the residue degree gives
$\ell\nmid2^d-1$; the $\ell$-power map is therefore an automorphism on that
ancestor group and its $\ell$-Kummer image is trivial.  Thus every cross-arm
word built from strict lower-level coordinates in such degrees separates its
coprime-primary torsion.  Nor does the next $\ell$-component give an induction:
its defining radicand is
$\alpha_\ell=\kappa_{f(\ell)}+m_\ell$, so irreducibility proves only that the
actual winning translate is a non-$\ell$-th power.  Using that fact to identify
$m_\ell$ with the conjectural $0$, $1$, or $4$ would already assume the target.

The intrinsic coordinate is a full Frobenius-orbit holonomy, not a one-step
affine translation.  Let $\beta\in\mathbb F_{2^E}^\times$ be the selected
element, put $s=\beta^{(2^E-1)/\ell}$, choose $\alpha^\ell=\beta$ in an
algebraic closure, fix a primitive $\zeta\in\mu_\ell$, and let
$\varphi(z)=z^2$.  Then
\[
 \frac{\varphi^E(\alpha)}{\alpha}
 =\alpha^{2^E-1}
 =\beta^{(2^E-1)/\ell}=s.
\]
This ratio is unchanged when $\alpha$ is replaced by $\zeta^a\alpha$,
because $\varphi^E$ fixes $\mu_\ell$.  If $s=\zeta^T$, the $E$-step action
on the labeled roots is the pure translation $e_x\mapsto e_{x+T}$.  Hence on
the Fourier lines
\[
 v_r=\sum_x\zeta^{-rx}e_x,
 \qquad \varphi^E(v_r)=\zeta^{rT}v_r.
\]
The diagonal eigenvalue at $r=1$ is exactly the selected Kummer--Artin
symbol.

Equivalently, choose a root frame over each conjugate
$\beta_j=\beta^{2^j}$ and write the one-step maps as
$e_{j,x}\mapsto e_{j+1,2x+t_j}$.  Their cyclic holonomy is
\[
 T=\sum_{j=0}^{E-1}2^{E-1-j}t_j\pmod\ell.
\]
Changing frames by $\alpha_j\mapsto\zeta^{a_j}\alpha_j$ sends
$t_j$ to $t_j+2a_j-a_{j+1}$; with $a_E=a_0$, these changes telescope because
$2^E=1$ modulo $\ell$.  Thus $T$ is intrinsic, while the individual
off-diagonal coefficients
\[
 \rho(t_j,2)v_r=\zeta^{r2^{-1}t_j}v_{r2^{-1}}
\]
are gauge-dependent.  Every proper edge can be normalized away recursively,
but a closed gauge cannot erase the cyclic holonomy: all but one edge may be
made trivial, with the remaining edge carrying $T$.

This also explains the cocycle no-go.  A homomorphic splitting on a
commutative multiplier subgroup obeys
$t(qr)=t(q)+q t(r)$.  Fixing $q_0\ne1$ and comparing
$t(qq_0)$ with $t(q_0q)$ gives
\[
 t(q)=(1-q)a,
 \qquad a=\frac{t(q_0)}{1-q_0}.
\]
Every such cocycle is a coboundary and has trivial full-period transfer.
Powers of one affine lift give this law on the subgroup generated by its
multiplier.  A general labeled section has a factor set, but changing root
frames changes that factor set by a coboundary; only its cyclic holonomy is
gauge-invariant.

Affine ancestry between two such torsor paths cannot create a missing phase.
If the vertex maps are $x\mapsto\lambda x+b_j$, then the target translations
have the form
\[
 u_j=\lambda t_j+b_{j+1}-2b_j,
\]
and the path holonomies satisfy
\[
 U_n=\lambda T_n+b_n-2^n b_0.
\]
On a closed $E$-cycle the endpoint term telescopes, so $U=\lambda T$.
Consequently an affine recursion from a strict ancestor with trivial
$\ell$-holonomy cannot prove a nonzero current phase; a successful relation
must contain a genuinely non-affine or independently nonzero closing source.

A successful nim-reciprocity proof must therefore retain and independently
evaluate the marked holonomy $T$, equivalently $s$, along the literal Conway
ancestry.  Constructing the holonomy from the selected phase is an exact
reformulation, not yet its evaluation.

\section{Boundedness and the remaining theorem}\label{sec:bounded}

Lenstra asked whether the finite excesses are absolutely bounded
\cite{lenstra1977}.  Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule} would give the bound four,
but boundedness is strictly weaker than the maximal-order assertions above.

For $a\geq0$, let $C_a=\mathbb F_{2^{2^a}}$.  For an odd prime $p$, put
$x_p=\kappa_{f(p)}$ and $L_{p,a}=\mathbb F_2(x_p)C_a$.

\begin{proposition}[finite-line criterion]\label{prop:bounded}
For every $p$ and $a$,
\[
 m_p<2^{2^a}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{some }c\in C_a\text{ has }x_p+c\notin L_{p,a}^p.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sup_p m_p<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{one fixed $a$ works for every $p$}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $F_c=\mathbb F_2(x_p+c)$, one has $F_cC_a=L_{p,a}$ and
$[L_{p,a}:F_c]\mid2^a$, which is prime to $p$.  Extension is therefore
injective on $p$-Kummer quotients.  The finite nimbers below $2^{2^a}$ are
exactly $C_a$, proving the first equivalence from the excess definition.  A
fixed $a$ gives a uniform bound; conversely choose $a$ above any given
uniform bound.
\end{proof}

No fixed finite affine line is known to work for every prime.  The complete
universal frontier remains the four selected order assertions in
Theorem~\ref{thm:arms}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item full primary order for the synchronized structural norm, including
      every Conway--Fermat equality $\delta_n=F_n$;
\item full primary projective order for the marked ordinary cyclotomic value;
\item primitivity of every selected Gaussian period $\gamma_k$, equivalently
      generation by every $\eta_k$;
\item $\Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k)$ at every exceptional level.
\end{enumerate}
Each is now one-dimensional and ancestry-sensitive.  Degree, trace, norm,
conductor, factor parity, or a statement about generic ambient points does
not decide it.

\section{Conclusion}

The finite-excess problem is now four exact selected-order problems.  The
zero arm needs a synchronized primitive-support coordinate, the ordinary arm
one Artin symbol on the Conway-selected inert ray, and the cubic and
exceptional arms the faithful finite reductions of two marked units.  Every
reduction retains the full primary valuation; none replaces the selected
coordinate by a generic element of the same field.

This is also the obstruction to the presently available proof routes.
Trace, norm, conductor, class group, and reciprocity packages can reconstruct
or transport the marked phase, but the saturation theorem shows that
homogeneous transforms of that same phase do not evaluate it.  A proof of the
$0/1/4$ rule must add an ancestry identity with independent arithmetic
content.  Until that identity closes all four arms, both the rule and
absolute boundedness remain open.
