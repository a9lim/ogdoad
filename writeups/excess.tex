\begin{abstract}
The finite part of a Kummer carry in Conway's field of ordinal nimbers is
Lenstra's excess \(m_p\), indexed by an odd prime \(p\).  Every presently
certified value belongs to \(\{0,1,4\}\), with the value predicted by the
component structure of \(\operatorname{ord}_p(2)\).  We give an exact
reduction of this rule to four universal multiplicative-order assertions.
They concern, respectively, a synchronized structural norm, a selected
projective class on every ordinary odd Kummer spine, a Gaussian period in a
cubic norm tower, and a corrected norm on the exceptional
\(2\cdot 3^k\) spine.  The reduction retains the full primary valuation in
each finite cyclic group; testing only divisibility of an order by \(p\) is
not sufficient in general.

For each arm we record a concise selected-coordinate normal form.  The
singleton-even part becomes a Conway--Fermat quotient-order problem and a
zero-at-multiples Fibonacci recurrence.  The ordinary arm becomes a
power-residue symbol of one marked cyclotomic unit.  The cubic arm becomes
generation by one recursively selected element of a norm-one torus.  The
exceptional arm becomes irreducibility of an explicit Capelli composition
after a corrected quadratic norm.  In that exceptional column we also obtain
the unconditional level-dependent bound
\(m_\ell<16(3^k)^4\) when \(f(\ell)=2\cdot3^k\) and \(k\geq2\).
We further identify the literal Conway--Fermat points on a fixed
supersingular elliptic curve, prove that the ordinary marked primes form a
unique inert cyclotomic ray, and show that the cubic recursion and the
exceptional auxiliary ancestry are affine forms of one toric Dickson system.
We further identify exact proper-conductor packets and marked prime chains in
the Conway--Fermat arm, and factor the exceptional selected index into a
principal ray kernel and a class-number-controlled circular-unit quotient.
An exact full-parent/proper-child packet witness rules out conductor-only
induction, while an actual \(r=11,p=23\) Conway conductor has a vanishing
Stickelberger character scalar of exact \(23\)-adic valuation one, hence an
actual cyclic order-\(23\) irregular class component.  An exact local/global
Kummer certificate further proves that every prime over two, including the
Conway-selected prime, generates this irregular line, despite the successful
ordinary row.
These structures sharpen the selected phase which remains to be evaluated;
they do not force its nonvanishing.
These are reductions, not proofs of the four universal assertions.  Formal
verification establishes the general
group, norm, and polynomial identities and several finite arithmetic
certificates; its universal exceptional target is a definition rather than
a theorem.  Finite computations are reported separately and are not used as
evidence for a universal conclusion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Conway's nim addition and nim multiplication make the ordinals into a proper
class field of characteristic two.  Its finite subfields are canonically
embedded, and its initial transfinite part is assembled by a sequence of
Kummer extensions \cite{conway1976,lenstra1977,lenstra1978}.  Lenstra's
description isolates, for each odd prime \(p\), a carry
\[
 \alpha_p=\kappa_{f(p)}+m_p,
 \qquad f(p)=\operatorname{ord}_p(2),
\]
where \(\kappa_{f(p)}\) is a structural tower element and the natural number
\(m_p\) is the \emph{finite excess}.  The same construction is part of the
general structural description of ordinal fields \(\mathrm{On}_p\)
\cite{dimuro2011}.

Let \(\mathcal Q(h)\) denote Lenstra's component set for \(\kappa_h\).
The available data suggest the following rigid rule.

\begin{conjecture}[the \(0/1/4\) rule]\label{conj:rule}
For every odd prime \(p\),
\[
 m_p=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&\mathcal Q(f(p))\text{ is not a singleton odd prime power},\\
  4,&f(p)=2\cdot3^k\text{ for some }k\geq1,\\
  1,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
\end{conjecture}

The purpose of this article is to state the exact mathematical frontier of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:four-arms} shows that it is
equivalent to four selected order assertions, denoted \(Z,O,C,D\).  None of
them is known in full.  The word \emph{selected} is essential: each field
contains many elements with the same degree, trace, norm, or generic
cyclotomic shape, while the nim construction chooses one recursively marked
Frobenius orbit.  The open content is the multiplicative order of that orbit.

The main results are organized as follows.  Section~\ref{sec:prelim} gives
the exact power and norm criteria.  Section~\ref{sec:four} states and proves
the four-arm equivalence.  Sections~\ref{sec:z}--\ref{sec:d} give one
lossless normal form for each arm.  Section~\ref{sec:boundedness} separates
the stronger \(0/1/4\) rule from Lenstra's boundedness question.
Section~\ref{sec:verification} states the formal and computational boundary.

\section{Finite-field and tower preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

For a finite algebraic nimber \(x\), write
\[
 d(x)=[\mathbb F_2(x):\mathbb F_2].
\]
The element \(\kappa_h\) and its component set \(\mathcal Q(h)\) are those
of Lenstra's tower decomposition.  In particular, \(\kappa_h\) is the sum of
the selected components \(\kappa_q\), \(q\in\mathcal Q(h)\), and
\(h\mid d(\kappa_h)\).  The excess is equivalently
\[
 m_p=\min\left\{m\geq0:
 \kappa_{f(p)}+m\text{ is not a }p\text{-th power in its defining finite
 field}\right\}.
\]
Finite integers in this formula are identified with their canonical finite
nimbers.  We use standard finite-field facts, including cyclicity of the
multiplicative group, the norm formula, and Capelli's irreducibility lemma,
as collected in \cite{lidlniederreiter1997}.

\begin{proposition}[exact power criterion]\label{prop:power}
Let \(p\) be a prime, let \(p\mid 2^E-1\), and let
\(x\in\mathbb F_{2^E}^{\times}\).  Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(x\) is a \(p\)-th power in \(\mathbb F_{2^E}^{\times}\);
\item \(x^{(2^E-1)/p}=1\);
\item \(\operatorname{ord}(x)\mid(2^E-1)/p\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, \(x\) is not a \(p\)-th power if and only if
\[
 v_p(\operatorname{ord}(x))=v_p(2^E-1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose a generator \(g\) of the cyclic group of order \(2^E-1\) and write
\(x=g^a\).  The congruence \(py\equiv a\pmod{2^E-1}\) is soluble exactly
when \(p\mid a\), which is equivalent to
\(x^{(2^E-1)/p}=1\).  The order formulation follows from
\(x^n=1\) if and only if \(\operatorname{ord}(x)\mid n\).  The final
statement retains the full \(p\)-primary valuation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the valuation cannot be discarded]\label{rem:valuation}
If \(h=f(p)\) and \(h\mid E\), lifting the exponent gives
\[
 v_p(2^E-1)=v_p(2^h-1)+v_p(E/h).
\]
Thus the shortcut
\(p\mid\operatorname{ord}(x)\) is equivalent to nonexistence of a
\(p\)-th root only when the ambient \(p\)-part has valuation one.  Every
statement below uses Proposition~\ref{prop:power}, not the shortcut.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[norm reduction]\label{prop:norm}
Let \(h\mid E\), let \(p\mid2^h-1\), and let
\(x\in\mathbb F_{2^E}^{\times}\).  Then
\[
 x^{(2^E-1)/p}
 =\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb F_{2^E}/\mathbb F_{2^h}}(x)^{(2^h-1)/p}.
\]
In particular, \(x\) is a \(p\)-th power in \(\mathbb F_{2^E}\) if and
only if its norm is a \(p\)-th power in \(\mathbb F_{2^h}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The norm is
\(x^{(2^E-1)/(2^h-1)}\).  Raising it to \((2^h-1)/p\) gives the displayed
identity.  Proposition~\ref{prop:power} gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The last equivalence is also visible on Kummer quotients: extension and norm
multiply the class by the relative degree.  In the applications below, the
relevant relative degrees are prime to the current prime, so no selected
primary coordinate is lost.

\section{The four exact arms}\label{sec:four}

We first define the four order assertions.  For \(h>1\), put
\[
 \Pi_h=
 \prod_{\substack{\ell\text{ odd prime}\\
                   \operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(2)=h}}
 \ell^{v_{\ell}(2^h-1)},
 \qquad
 T_h=\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb F_{2^{d(\kappa_h)}}/
                            \mathbb F_{2^h}}(\kappa_h).
\]
The product \(\Pi_h\) is the complete primary part of
\(\mathbb F_{2^h}^{\times}\) supported at primes for which \(h\) is the
first residue degree.

For an odd prime \(r\), let \(b_r=d(\alpha_r)\), and for \(a\geq1\) put
\[
 x_{r,a}=\kappa_{r^a},\qquad
 F_{r,a}=\mathbb F_{2^{r^ab_r}},\qquad
 y_{r,a}=x_{r,a}+1,
\]
with \(F_{r,0}=\mathbb F_{2^{b_r}}\).  Define the cyclic projective
quotient
\[
 G_{r,a}=F_{r,a}^{\times}/F_{r,a-1}^{\times},
 \qquad
 L_{r,a}=|G_{r,a}|=\Phi_{r^a}(2^{b_r}).
\]

For \(k\geq1\), set \(h=3^k\), let
\(\zeta_k=\kappa_{3^k}\), and define
\[
 \gamma_k=\zeta_k+\zeta_k^{-1}
 \in\mathbb F_{2^h}^{\times}.
\]
Finally, put \(q=2^h\), \(\omega=\zeta_k^h\in\mathbb F_4\), and
\[
 A_k=\zeta_k^2+\zeta_k+\omega,
 \qquad
 M_k=A_k^{q-1},
 \qquad
 \Psi_k=\frac{\Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)}{3}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[four-arm reduction]\label{thm:four-arms}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule} is equivalent to the conjunction of the following
four universal assertions.
\begin{description}
\item[\(Z\).] For every \(h>1\) such that \(\mathcal Q(h)\) is not a
singleton odd prime power,
\[
 \Pi_h\mid\operatorname{ord}(T_h).
\]
\item[\(O\).] For every odd prime \(r\ne3\), every \(a\geq1\), and every
prime \(p\ne r\) dividing \(L_{r,a}\),
\[
 v_p\!\left(\operatorname{ord}
   (y_{r,a}F_{r,a-1}^{\times})\right)=v_p(L_{r,a}).
\]
\item[\(C\).] The element \(\gamma_k\) is primitive in
\(\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}^{\times}\) for every \(k\geq1\).
\item[\(D\).] One has
\[
 \Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k)
 \qquad(k\geq1).
\]
\end{description}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix an odd prime \(p\) and write \(h=f(p)\).  If
\(\mathcal Q(h)\) is not a singleton odd prime power, the proposed value is
zero.  Proposition~\ref{prop:z-support} below identifies the simultaneous
zero claim at residue degree \(h\) with \(Z\).

Suppose next that \(\mathcal Q(h)=\{r^a\}\), where \(r\) is odd.
Lenstra's degree and component formulas
\cite{lenstra1977,lenstra1978} give
\[
 h=r^as,\qquad s\mid b_r,\qquad \kappa_h=x_{r,a}.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-projective} shows that the row primes are exactly the
prime divisors \(p\ne r\) of \(L_{r,a}\), and that \(m_p=1\) is equivalent
to the full primary-order equality in \(O\).

It remains to separate \(r=3\).  Here \(b_3=2\), so
\(h=3^a\) or \(h=2\cdot3^a\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:c-half-angle}
identifies the first case with primitivity of \(\gamma_a\), which is \(C\).
Propositions~\ref{prop:d-lower} and \ref{prop:d-exact} identify the second
case with \(D\).  The cases are disjoint and exhaustive.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[status of the theorem]
Theorem~\ref{thm:four-arms} is an equivalence.  It does not establish any of
the four universal assertions.  The known finite cases and the exact
formalization boundary are stated in Section~\ref{sec:verification}.
\end{remark}

\section{The zero arm}\label{sec:z}

\subsection{Structural norm and synchronized phase}

\begin{proposition}[exact support criterion]\label{prop:z-support}
Let \(h>1\), let \(E=d(\kappa_h)\), and let
\(T_h=\operatorname{N}_{\mathbb F_{2^E}/\mathbb F_{2^h}}(\kappa_h)\).
For an odd prime \(p\) with \(f(p)=h\),
\[
 m_p=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_p(\operatorname{ord}(T_h))=v_p(2^h-1).
\]
Consequently, \(m_p=0\) for every such \(p\) if and only if
\(\Pi_h\mid\operatorname{ord}(T_h)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By definition, \(m_p=0\) exactly when \(\kappa_h\) is not a \(p\)-th
power in \(\mathbb F_{2^E}\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:norm} moves the test
to \(T_h\in\mathbb F_{2^h}\), and Proposition~\ref{prop:power} gives the
valuation equality.  Multiplying the independent primary conditions gives
the final assertion.
\end{proof}

When \(\kappa_h\) has several components, the structural norm is not the
product of separate component norms.  The missing datum is a synchronized
Frobenius phase, made explicit by the following general construction.

\begin{proposition}[synchronized additive resultant]\label{prop:z-phase}
Let \(K=\mathbb F_Q\), let \(p\mid Q-1\), and choose
\(u_i\in L_i=\mathbb F_{Q^{r_i}}\) of degree \(r_i\) over \(K\).
Put
\[
 R=\operatorname{lcm}(r_1,\ldots,r_t),
 \qquad M=\mathbb F_{Q^R},
 \qquad u=\sum_{i=1}^t u_i,
\]
and assume \(u\ne0\) and \(p\nmid R\).  Let
\[
 \Omega=\left(\prod_i\mathbb Z/r_i\mathbb Z\right)
          \big/\langle(1,\ldots,1)\rangle.
\]
For a phase \(\mathbf d=(d_i)\), define
\[
 F_{\mathbf d}(Z)=
 \prod_{j=0}^{R-1}
 \left(Z+\sum_i u_i^{Q^{j+d_i}}\right).
\]
Then \(F_{\mathbf d}\in K[Z]\), it depends only on the class of
\(\mathbf d\), and
\[
 \prod_{0\leq j_i<r_i}
 \left(Z+\sum_i u_i^{Q^{j_i}}\right)
 =\prod_{\mathbf d\in\Omega}F_{\mathbf d}(Z).
\]
The selected common embedding is the zero phase, for which
\[
 F_{\mathbf 0}(0)=\operatorname{N}_{M/K}(u),
 \qquad
 [F_{\mathbf 0}(0)]=R[u]
 \quad\text{in }M^{\times}/M^{\times p}.
\]
Hence \(u\) is a non-\(p\)-th-power if and only if the selected constant
term \(F_{\mathbf 0}(0)\) is a non-\(p\)-th-power.  Separate component
minimal polynomials determine the product over all phases, but not the
selected phase when \(|\Omega|>1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Diagonal translation has free orbits of size \(R\) on the tuples
\((j_i)\).  Frobenius cycles the factors in each orbit, so each orbit product
lies in \(K[Z]\), and partitioning the tuples proves the factorization.  The
zero-phase factors are exactly the \(M/K\)-conjugates of \(u\), giving the
norm.  On the \(p\)-Kummer quotient the norm exponent is
\[
 1+Q+\cdots+Q^{R-1}\equiv R\pmod p.
\]
Since \(p\nmid R\), multiplication by \(R\) is invertible.  Independent
Frobenius shifts of the components preserve every separate minimal polynomial
while translating the phase, which proves the final limitation.
\end{proof}

In the multicomponent part of \(Z\), the relative degrees supplied by
Lenstra's component theorem satisfy the coprimality hypothesis in
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-phase}.  Thus \(Z\) is an order assertion about one
distinguished factor, not about the unmarked additive composed product.

There is nevertheless a canonical multiplicative coordinate on the whole
primitive-support quotient.  It packages all lower-field ambiguity into one
cubical cross-ratio without discarding the selected phase.

\begin{proposition}[cubical primitive-support coordinate]
\label{prop:z-cubical-coordinate}
Let \(h>1\), let \(S\) be the set of prime divisors of \(h\), and put
\(M=\mathbb F_{2^h}\).  Write \(\varphi(x)=x^2\) and
\(\sigma_r=\varphi^{h/r}\) for \(r\in S\).  For
\(A\subseteq S\), put \(\sigma_A=\prod_{r\in A}\sigma_r\), and define
\[
 H_h=\prod_{r\in S}\mathbb F_{2^{h/r}}^\times,
 \qquad
 \Delta_h(x)=
 \frac{\prod_{|A|\text{ even}}\sigma_A(x)}
      {\prod_{|A|\text{ odd}}\sigma_A(x)}.
\]
Then
\[
 \ker\Delta_h=H_h,
 \qquad |\operatorname{im}\Delta_h|=\Phi_h(2),
\]
so \(\Delta_h\) induces an isomorphism
\[
 M^\times/H_h\ \simeq\ \operatorname{im}\Delta_h.
\]
In particular, for the selected structural norm \(T_h\), arm \(Z\) at
degree \(h\) is equivalent to
\[
 \Pi_h\mid\operatorname{ord}(\Delta_h(T_h)).
\]
Thus the universal multicomponent problem is the full primary order of one
explicit marked cubical cross-ratio.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cyclic group \(M^\times\) has order \(m=2^h-1\).  The operator above is
the power map
\[
 \Delta_h(x)=x^e,
 \qquad e=\prod_{r\mid h,\ r\text{ prime}}(1-2^{h/r}).
\]
Let \(d_r=2^{h/r}-1\) and \(L=\operatorname{lcm}_{r\in S}d_r\).
The subgroup generated by the lower fields has order \(L\), and pairing
subsets \(A\) and \(A\mathbin\triangle\{r\}\) shows that it lies in
\(\ker\Delta_h\).  It remains to prove \(\gcd(m,e)=L\).

Fix an odd prime \(\ell\mid m\), put
\(f=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)\), \(t=h/f\), and
\(c=v_\ell(2^f-1)\).  Lifting the exponent gives
\[
 v_\ell(m)=c+v_\ell(t),
 \qquad
 v_\ell(d_r)=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&r\nmid t,\\
  c+v_\ell(t/r),&r\mid t.
 \end{cases}
\]
If \(t=1\), both the \(\ell\)-adic valuations of \(\gcd(m,e)\) and of
\(L\) are zero.  If \(t\) is a positive power of \(\ell\), only
\(r=\ell\) contributes, and both valuations are
\(c+v_\ell(t)-1\).  Otherwise some prime \(r\ne\ell\) divides \(t\), and
both valuations equal the full value \(c+v_\ell(t)\).  Hence
\(\gcd(m,e)=L\).  The standard cyclotomic product identity now gives
\(m/L=\Phi_h(2)\), proving the kernel and image claims.

For every prime \(\ell\) occurring in \(\Pi_h\), one has
\(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)=h\), so the \(\ell\)-primary part of every
proper lower-field group is trivial.  The quotient map, and therefore
\(\Delta_h\), preserves the full \(\ell\)-primary order of \(T_h\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-support} gives the last equivalence.
\end{proof}

The raw additive cube behind this proposition does yield a universal
nonidentity test.  In characteristic two, for edge differences
\(d_1,\ldots,d_s\),
\[
 \prod_{|A|\text{ even}}
   \left(b+\sum_{i\in A}d_i\right)
 +
 \prod_{|A|\text{ odd}}
   \left(b+\sum_{i\in A}d_i\right)
 =
 \prod_{|A|\text{ odd}}\sum_{i\in A}d_i.
\]
Indeed, the even subset sums form a hyperplane \(W\) over \(\mathbb F_2\),
and its subspace polynomial
\(L_W(X)=\prod_{w\in W}(X+w)\) is additive; the left side is
\(L_W(b)+L_W(b+d_1)=L_W(d_1)\), the odd-coset product.  Hence linearly
disjoint separated components make the raw cubical coordinate nontrivial.
This does not finish \(Z\): \(T_h\) is a synchronized norm of the selected
sum rather than a separated cube, the norm may kill a nontrivial raw class,
and nonidentity is weaker than the full primary order required when
\(\Pi_h\) is composite.

\subsection{The singleton-even Conway--Fermat problem}

Put
\[
 h_n=2^{n+1},\qquad c_n=\kappa_{h_n}=2^{2^n},
 \qquad E_n=\mathbb F_{2^{h_n}},
 \qquad F_n=2^{2^n}+1,
\]
and take \(E_{-1}=\mathbb F_2\).  The Conway tower relation is
\[
 c_n^2+c_n=a_{n-1},
 \qquad a_{n-1}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}c_j.
\]
Let
\[
 \delta_n=\operatorname{ord}
 \bigl(c_nE_{n-1}^{\times}\bigr)
 \quad\text{in }E_n^{\times}/E_{n-1}^{\times}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Conway--Fermat form]\label{prop:z-fermat}
One has \(1<\delta_n\mid F_n\).  The zero arm at every prime divisor of
\(F_n\) is equivalent to
\[
 \delta_n=F_n.
\]
Equivalently, \(c_0c_n\) is primitive in \(E_n\) for every \(n\) if and
only if these equalities hold at every preceding level.  This is the
selected-order problem studied for Conway towers by Popovych
\cite{popovych2018}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The quotient group has order
\[
 \frac{2^{2^{n+1}}-1}{2^{2^n}-1}=F_n,
\]
and \(c_n\notin E_{n-1}\), so its quotient order is a nontrivial divisor.
Every odd prime \(p\mid F_n\) has \(f(p)=2^{n+1}\), and its entire primary
part in \(E_n^{\times}\) lies in the quotient.  Proposition~\ref{prop:power}
therefore gives \(m_p=0\) exactly when the quotient order contains the full
\(p\)-part.  Imposing this at every prime divisor of \(F_n\) is equivalent to
\(\delta_n=F_n\).  The primitive-product equivalence follows because the
successive quotient orders are supported on pairwise coprime Fermat numbers
and \(c_0\) has order three.
\end{proof}

The ordinal choice of the least compatible root is not an additional
multiplicative phase.  It is exactly a choice of Frobenius gauge.

\begin{proposition}[all compatible branches form one Frobenius orbit]
\label{prop:z-frobenius-gauge}
Let \(\mathcal T_n\) be the set of tuples \((x_0,\ldots,x_n)\) in
\(\overline{\mathbb F}_2\) satisfying
\[
 x_0^2+x_0=1,
 \qquad
 x_i^2+x_i=\prod_{j<i}x_j\quad(1\leq i\leq n).
\]
If \((c_0,\ldots,c_n)\) is the literal Conway tuple, then
\[
 \mathbb Z/2^{n+1}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathcal T_n,
 \qquad
 j\longmapsto(c_0^{2^j},\ldots,c_n^{2^j})
\]
is a bijection.  Consequently every compatible binary root-choice string
has the same relative order vector \((\delta_0,\ldots,\delta_n)\).  In
particular, changing the top root sends
\[
 u_n=\frac{c_n+1}{c_n}\longmapsto u_n^{-1}
\]
and does not change \(\delta_n\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every prefix has exactly two extensions, because the roots of each displayed
quadratic differ by one.  Hence \(|\mathcal T_n|=2^{n+1}\).  Frobenius
preserves all the equations, and the displayed tuples are distinct because
\(c_n\) has exact degree \(2^{n+1}\).  They therefore exhaust
\(\mathcal T_n\).  If \(q=|E_{i-1}|=2^{2^i}\), then
\[
 E_i^\times/E_{i-1}^\times\longrightarrow
 \ker\operatorname N_{E_i/E_{i-1}},
 \qquad [z]\longmapsto z^{q-1}
\]
is an isomorphism and sends \([c_i]\) to
\(c_i^{q-1}=(c_i+1)/c_i=u_i\).  Thus \(\operatorname{ord}(u_i)=\delta_i\),
and Frobenius preserves the whole relative-order vector.  Finally, the
nontrivial element over \(E_{n-1}\) is
\(\operatorname{Fr}^{2^n}:x\mapsto x^{2^{2^n}}\); it sends \(c_n\) to
\(c_n+1\), giving the inverse formula.
\end{proof}

Proper quotient orders admit an exact irreducible-packet description.  For
odd \(e>1\), let \(\mu_e^{\mathrm{prim}}\) be the primitive \(e\)-th roots
in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_2\), put
\[
 \phi(z)=\frac{z}{(z+1)^2},
\]
and define
\[
 C_e(X)=
 \prod_{[z]\in\mu_e^{\mathrm{prim}}/(z\sim z^{-1})}
 (X-\phi(z))\in\mathbb F_2[X].
\]
Continue the empty-level convention with \(a_{-1}=1\) and
\(Q_{-1}=X+1\).

\begin{proposition}[primitive-order packet]
\label{prop:z-order-packet}
The polynomial \(C_e\) is monic and squarefree of degree
\(\varphi(e)/2\).  If \(e\mid F_n\), then every irreducible factor of
\(C_e\) has degree \(2^n\), and, for the degree-\(2^n\) minimal
polynomial \(Q_{n-1}\) of \(a_{n-1}\),
\[
 \delta_n=e
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Q_{n-1}\mid C_e.
\]
For every odd \(m\), one also has the exact factorization
\[
 S_m(X)=\prod_{\substack{e\mid m\\e>1}}C_e(X).
\]
Thus the Fibonacci test is the disjoint union of the exact proper-order
packets; it does not mix their phases.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Cross-multiplication gives
\[
 \phi(z)=\phi(w)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (z+w)(1+zw)=0,
\]
so the fibres are exactly inverse pairs and the definition is unambiguous.
Frobenius preserves the root set, proving \(C_e\in\mathbb F_2[X]\).
If \(e\mid F_n\), then \(2^{2^n}\equiv-1\pmod e\).  The order
\(d=\operatorname{ord}_e(2)\) divides \(2^{n+1}\) but not \(2^n\), so
\(d=2^{n+1}\).  The unique involution of \(\langle2\rangle\) is therefore
\(-1=2^{d/2}\), and the least \(j\) with
\(2^j\equiv\pm1\pmod e\) is \(d/2=2^n\).  Equivalently every Frobenius
orbit on inverse pairs has length \(2^n\).  This proves the factor-degree
claim.  Since \(a_{n-1}=\phi(u_n)\) and
\(\operatorname{ord}(u_n)=\delta_n\) by
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-frobenius-gauge}, the fibre identity gives the
selected order equivalence.  Finally, the two sides of the last display are monic of
degree \((m-1)/2\), and their roots are exactly \(\phi(z)\) for
\(z^m=1\), \(z\ne1\).
\end{proof}

The packet recursion itself is deterministic and preserves all of the usual
unramified Artin--Schreier invariants, even for a proper order packet.

\begin{proposition}[unique irreducible packet child]
\label{prop:z-packet-child}
Put \(d=2^n\), \(q=2^d\), and let \(e>1\) divide \(F_n=q+1\).  Every
root \(t\) of \(C_e\) has
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_2}(t)=1.
\]
The polynomial
\[
 G_t(Y)=Y^2+tY+t^3
\]
is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_q\).  If \(y\) is either root, then
\[
 y^q=y+t,\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_{q^2}/\mathbb F_q}(y)=t,\qquad
 \operatorname N_{\mathbb F_{q^2}/\mathbb F_q}(y)=t^3,\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_{q^2}/\mathbb F_2}(y)=1.
\]
Consequently \(y\) belongs to a unique packet \(C_{e'}\), for a unique
\(e'>1\) dividing \(F_{n+1}\).

More intrinsically, if \(P\) is any irreducible factor of \(C_e\), then
\[
 R_P(Y)=\operatorname{Res}_X
   \bigl(P(X),Y^2+XY+X^3\bigr)
\]
is irreducible of degree \(2^{n+1}\).  The map \(P\mapsto R_P\) is
injective.  Thus the literal Conway packet is the orbit of \(X+1\) under a
deterministic irreducible-child map; the remaining assertion is that this
orbit always chooses the full packet \(C_{F_n}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(t=\phi(z)\) with \(z\) primitive of order \(e\), and put
\(c=(z+1)^{-1}\).  Since \(z^q=z^{-1}\), direct calculation gives
\[
 c^2+c=t,\qquad c^q=c+1.
\]
Thus \(X^2+X+t\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_q\), which is equivalent
to the asserted absolute trace of \(t\).  Substitution \(Y=tc\) gives
\[
 G_t(tc)=t^2(c^2+c+t)=0.
\]
Its other root is \(t(c+1)\), so the displayed conjugacy, trace, and norm
formulas follow; transitivity of trace gives the last one.

The element \(y\) has degree \(2^{n+1}\): it lies in
\(\mathbb F_{q^2}\), is not in \(\mathbb F_q\), and
\(t=y+y^q\in\mathbb F_2(y)\) has degree \(2^n\).  Since its absolute
trace is one, \(X^2+X+y\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\).
For either root \(b\), the quotient \((b+1)/b\) is a nonidentity element
of the norm-one torus of order \(q^2+1=F_{n+1}\), and
\(\phi((b+1)/b)=y\).  The inverse-pair fibre calculation in
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-order-packet} gives the unique \(e'\).

Finally, \(R_P\) is monic of degree \(2\deg P=2^{n+1}\), and a root \(y\)
of it has exactly that degree by the preceding paragraph.  Hence \(R_P\)
is the minimal polynomial of \(y\) and is irreducible.  The parent factor is
recovered from \(t=y+y^{2^{2^n}}\), proving injectivity.
\end{proof}

The exact conductor transition can also be written without resultants.  It
shows why the child conductor is not a function of the unmarked parent
integer alone.

\begin{proposition}[marked packet-conductor transition]
\label{prop:z-packet-conductor-transition}
Retain Proposition~\ref{prop:z-packet-child}, and write
\(t=\phi(z)\), where \(z\) has order \(e\mid q+1\).  Choose the child
\[
 y=\frac{t}{z+1}=\frac{z}{(z+1)^3},
\]
choose \(b\) with \(b^2+b=y\), and put \(w=(b+1)/b\).  Then
\[
 y^q=zy,\qquad
 w+w^{-1}=\frac1y=\frac{(z+1)^3}{z},\qquad
 z=\frac{w+w^{-1}}{w^q+w^{-q}}.
\]
The child conductor is \(e'=\operatorname{ord}(w)\mid q^2+1\).  If
\(D_0=0\), \(D_1=X\), and
\(D_{m+2}=XD_{m+1}+D_m\), then
\[
 e'=\min\left\{m>0:
  D_m\left(\frac{(z+1)^3}{z}\right)=0\right\}.
\]

Conversely, let \(w\in\mu_{q^2+1}\), put
\(A=w+w^{-1}\) and \(A_q=A^q\), and assume \(A A_q\ne0\).  The packet
of \(w\) is a child under the deterministic Conway resultant precisely when
\[
 (AA_q)^2=(A+A_q)^3.                                      \tag{\(\dagger\)}
\]
When \((\dagger)\) holds, the marked parent coordinate is
\[
 z=A/A_q,\qquad t=\phi(z)=A^{-1}+A_q^{-1},
\]
so its conductor is \(\operatorname{ord}(z)\).  Thus a proper-order point in
the ambient child packet is not a counterexample unless it also satisfies
\((\dagger)\) along the complete preceding ancestry.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With \(c=(z+1)^{-1}\), one has \(t=c(c+1)\),
\(c^q=c+1\), and \(c+1=zc\).  Hence
\(y=tc\) and \(y^q=t(c+1)=zy\).  Since
\(\phi(w)=y\), the inverse-pair identity gives
\(w+w^{-1}=1/y\); applying the \(q\)-th power and taking the quotient
gives the formula for \(z\).  The recurrence for \(D_m\) gives
\[
 D_m(w+w^{-1})=w^m+w^{-m}.
\]
Because \(w\) has odd order, the right side vanishes exactly when
\(w^m=1\), proving the minimum formula.

For the converse, put \(y=A^{-1}\) and \(y^q=A_q^{-1}\).  The child
equation is
\[
 yy^q=(y+y^q)^3,
\]
which becomes \((\dagger)\) after clearing the nonzero denominator.
Under this relation, direct simplification gives
\(\phi(A/A_q)=A^{-1}+A_q^{-1}\), completing the proof.
\end{proof}

There is a concrete marked witness to the distinction between conductor and
ancestry.  Encode a binary polynomial \(\sum a_iX^i\) by the integer
\(\sum a_i2^i\), and put
\[
 P^{(0)}=\mathtt{0x18dcf},\qquad
 P^{(1)}=\mathtt{0x18753}.
\]
Both polynomials are irreducible of degree sixteen and have trace
coefficient one.  Since \(F_4=65537\) is prime, both therefore belong to
the full packet \(C_{65537}\): trace one makes the next
Artin--Schreier polynomial irreducible, its quotient coordinate is a
nonidentity element of \(\mu_{65537}\), and primality forces full order.
The factor \(P^{(0)}\) is the literal selected factor at that level.
Nevertheless their deterministic children
are
\[
 R_{P^{(0)}}=\mathtt{0x1d05a9a3b},\qquad
 R_{P^{(1)}}=\mathtt{0x1dc43dbcf},
\]
and exact Fibonacci reduction gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &S_{641}&S_{6700417}\\ \hline
 R_{P^{(0)}}&\mathtt{0x88f2ef2c}&\mathtt{0x1f3c2614}\\
 R_{P^{(1)}}&\mathtt{0xcad49535}&0.
\end{array}
\]
Both have \(S_{F_5}=0\), and \(641\) and \(6700417\) are prime.  Thus the
first child has full conductor
\(F_5=641\cdot6700417\), while the second has the proper conductor
\(6700417\).  Since the selected degree-sixteen parent is \(P^{(0)}\ne
P^{(1)}\), the second parent is not on the literal preceding ancestry.  Hence
full parent conductor does not imply full child conductor, even for the
marked deterministic child map.  What remains special is the entire iterated
orbit from \(X+1\), not the conductor at one level.  The exact bit-polynomial
resultants, Rabin irreducibility tests, and Fibonacci residues are replayed by
the self-test in \texttt{experiments/fermat\_selected\_screen.py}.

The corresponding failure of low-order descent is uniform.

\begin{proposition}[Dickson tangency boundary]
\label{prop:z-dickson-tangency}
Let \(m>1\) be odd, put \(s=v_2(m-1)\), and assume \(s\geq2\).  For the
characteristic-two Dickson word in
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-packet-conductor-transition},
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_X\bigl(D_m(X)+X\bigr)=2^{s-1}+1.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 D_m(X)=X+X^{2^{s-1}+1}U_m(X),\qquad U_m(0)=1.
\]
If \(\ell\mid F_{n+1}\) is prime, then
\[
 D_\ell(X)\equiv X\pmod {X^{2^{n+1}+1}}.
\]
The first coefficient which distinguishes extraction of an \(\ell\)-th
root from the identity therefore lies beyond the unreduced basis of the
degree-\(2^{n+1}\) marked child coordinate.  It becomes visible only after
reduction by that exact marked child polynomial.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Embed \(\mathbb F_2[X]\) into \(\mathbb F_2((T))\) by
\[
 u=1+T,\qquad X=u+u^{-1}=\frac{T^2}{1+T}.
\]
The Binet identity gives
\[
 D_m(X)+X=(u^{m-1}+1)(u+u^{-m}).
\]
The two factors have exact \(T\)-adic valuations \(2^s\) and \(2\),
respectively, because \(v_2(m+1)=1\).  Since
\(\operatorname{ord}_T(X)=2\), the asserted \(X\)-order follows.  Finally,
\(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)=2^{n+2}\) implies
\(v_2(\ell-1)\geq n+2\), giving the congruence.
\end{proof}

Iterating Proposition~\ref{prop:z-packet-conductor-transition} gives a
top-only description, but not a new order bound.  Put \(Q_i=2^{2^i}\), set
\(W_n=w_n\), and recursively define
\[
 W_{i-1}=
 \frac{W_i+W_i^{-1}}
      {W_i^{Q_{i-1}}+W_i^{-Q_{i-1}}}.
\]
Complete literal ancestry is equivalent to nonvanishing of all denominators,
\(W_i\in\mu_{F_i}\setminus\{1\}\), the primitive-cubic condition on
\(W_0\), and the image equation \((\dagger)\) at every step.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-frobenius-gauge}, this top-only torsion system cuts
out exactly one Frobenius/inverse packet.  Consequently its intersection
with a proper subgroup \(\mu_d\), \(d\mid F_n\), is nonempty exactly when
\(\delta_n\mid d\).  General torsion-intersection finiteness or Bezout
counting therefore restates, rather than proves, the selected primitivity
claim.

The packets have a canonical integral lift.  Let
\[
 K_e=\mathbb Q(\zeta_e+\zeta_e^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \vartheta_e=(2+\zeta_e+\zeta_e^{-1})^{-1}.
\]
For odd \(e>1\), the element \(1+\zeta_e\) is a unit because
\(\Phi_e(-1)=\Phi_{2e}(1)=1\); hence \(\vartheta_e\) is an integral unit.
Indeed,
\[
 (1+\zeta_e)(1+\zeta_e^{-1})
 =2+\zeta_e+\zeta_e^{-1}.
\]
It generates \(K_e\), since
\(\zeta_e+\zeta_e^{-1}=\vartheta_e^{-1}-2\).  The minimal polynomial of
\(\vartheta_e\) reduces coefficientwise modulo two to \(C_e\): its
conjugates become
\[
 \frac1{z+z^{-1}}=\frac z{(z+1)^2}.
\]
Since \(C_e\) is squarefree, two does not divide
\([\mathcal O_{K_e}:\mathbb Z[\vartheta_e]]\).  Dedekind's factorization
theorem therefore identifies its irreducible factors precisely with the
residue polynomials of \(\vartheta_e\) at primes above two.  Thus a proper
value of \(\delta_n\) is exactly selection of one marked prime factor for a
proper conductor \(e\mid F_n\); unmarked abelian Frobenius data cannot decide
which factor is selected.

The complete downward ancestry of these marked factors also has an exact
number-field formulation.

\begin{proposition}[proper-order prime-chain criterion]
\label{prop:z-prime-chain}
Choose divisors \(e_i>1\) of \(F_i\) for \(0\leq i\leq n\), with
\(e_0=3\), and put \(L=K_{e_0}\cdots K_{e_n}\).  Then
\[
 \delta_i=e_i\quad(0\leq i\leq n)
\]
if and only if there is a prime \(\mathfrak P\mid2\) of \(L\) such that,
with \(t_i=\vartheta_{e_i}\bmod\mathfrak P\),
\[
 t_0=1,
 \qquad
 t_i^2+t_{i-1}t_i+t_{i-1}^3=0
 \quad(1\leq i\leq n).
\]
Hence the all-level Conway--Fermat assertion is precisely the following
finite-level statement: for every \(n\), no choice
\((e_0,\ldots,e_n)\) with at least one \(e_i<F_i\) admits such a marked
prime chain.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Assume first that \(\delta_i=e_i\).  Then \(u_i\) is primitive of order
\(e_i\) and \(a_{i-1}=\phi(u_i)\).  Since the \(e_i\) divide pairwise
coprime Fermat numbers, the tuple \((u_i)\) determines, under the Chinese
remainder isomorphism of the root groups, one primitive root of order
\(\prod_i e_i\).  Evaluation of the corresponding cyclotomic integer ring
in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_2\) defines a prime above two; restricting it to
\(L\) gives \(\mathfrak P\) and
\(t_i=\vartheta_{e_i}\bmod\mathfrak P=a_{i-1}\).  The displayed congruence
is then the selected resultant identity
\[
 a_{i-1}^2+a_{i-2}a_{i-1}+a_{i-2}^3=0.
\]
This proves necessity.

Conversely, every \(t_i\) is nonzero and is a root of \(C_{e_i}\).  Each
has exact degree \(2^i\) by Proposition~\ref{prop:z-order-packet}, so their
finite residue fields are nested.  Put
\[
 c_{i-1}=t_i/t_{i-1}\qquad(1\leq i\leq n).
\]
The congruence gives
\(c_{i-1}^2+c_{i-1}=t_{i-1}\), and telescoping gives
\(\prod_{j<i}c_j=t_i\).  Choose \(c_n\) as either root of
\(X^2+X+t_n\).  Then
\[
 \phi\left(\frac{c_i+1}{c_i}\right)=t_i
 \qquad(0\leq i\leq n).
\]
Indeed, \(\phi((c+1)/c)=c^2+c\) in characteristic two.  Each fibre over a
root of \(C_{e_i}\) consists of a primitive \(e_i\)-th root and its inverse,
so the corresponding quotient order is \(e_i\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-frobenius-gauge} transfers this compatible
branch to the literal Conway branch, proving sufficiency.
\end{proof}

The requirement that the packet factors arise at one prime over two does not
itself couple their phases.  The coupling begins only with the displayed
Conway congruences.

\begin{proposition}[independence of the marked primes before ancestry]
\label{prop:z-prime-independence}
Retain divisors \(e_i>1\) of \(F_i\) for \(0\leq i\leq n\), and write

\[
 K_i=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{e_i}+\zeta_{e_i}^{-1}),\qquad
 G_i=\operatorname{Gal}(K_i/\mathbb Q),\qquad
 D_i=\langle[2]\rangle\subseteq G_i.
\]

Then the \(K_i\) are linearly disjoint, \(|D_i|=2^i\), and for
\(L=K_0\cdots K_n\) the decomposition group of two is the diagonal subgroup

\[
 D=\langle([2],\ldots,[2])\rangle\subseteq\prod_{i=0}^nG_i,
 \qquad |D|=2^n.
\]

The restriction map on primes over two,

\[
 \left(\prod_{i=0}^nG_i\right)/D
 \longrightarrow
 \prod_{i=0}^nG_i/D_i,
\]

is surjective, and every fibre has cardinality
\(2^{n(n-1)/2}\).  Thus every tuple of individually marked packet factors is
realized by a common prime of \(L\); Proposition~\ref{prop:z-prime-chain}
adds exactly the ancestry congruences and no hidden decomposition condition.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The \(e_i\) divide pairwise coprime Fermat numbers.  Chinese remaindering in
the full cyclotomic fields, followed by passage to the real subfields, gives
linear disjointness and

\[
 \operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)=\prod_iG_i.
\]

For \(e_i\mid F_i\), the least \(j>0\) with
\(2^j\equiv\pm1\pmod {e_i}\) is \(2^i\), so the real decomposition group has
order \(2^i\).  Frobenius in the product is the diagonal tuple and has order
\(\operatorname{lcm}_i2^i=2^n\).  Any tuple of cosets \(g_iD_i\) has the
representative \((g_i)_i\), proving surjectivity.  Its fibre is
\((\prod_iD_i)/D\), of order

\[
 \frac{\prod_{i=0}^n2^i}{2^n}=2^{n(n-1)/2}.
\]
\end{proof}

There is likewise no concealed multiplicity or singularity in the ancestry
equations themselves.

\begin{proposition}[the ancestry scheme is one finite-field orbit]
\label{prop:z-ancestry-scheme}
Over \(k=\mathbb F_2\), put

\[
 A_n=
 k[T_0,\ldots,T_n]/
 \bigl(T_0+1,\,
   T_i^2+T_{i-1}T_i+T_{i-1}^3:1\leq i\leq n\bigr),
\]

and write \(t_i\) for the image of \(T_i\).  Then

\[
 A_n\simeq\mathbb F_{2^{2^n}}.
\]

More precisely, each \(A_i/A_{i-1}\) is quadratic,

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{A_i/A_{i-1}}(t_i)=t_{i-1},\qquad
 \operatorname{N}_{A_i/A_{i-1}}(t_i)=t_{i-1}^3,
 \qquad \operatorname{Tr}_{A_i/\mathbb F_2}(t_i)=1,
\]

and

\[
 t_i^{\,2^{2^{i-1}}}=t_i+t_{i-1}.
\]

The Jacobian determinant of the defining equations is
\(\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}t_i\), hence is a unit.  Consequently the scheme is finite
\'etale, with \(2^n\) geometric points forming one Frobenius orbit and all
local intersection multiplicities equal to one.  If \(P_i\) is obtained by
the successive resultants of Proposition~\ref{prop:z-packet-child}, then
\(P_i\) is exactly the irreducible minimal polynomial of \(t_i\), with no
extra factor or multiplicity.

Finally, adjoining packet equations \(C_{e_i}(T_i)=0\) produces either the
same field \(A_n\), when the literal orbit lies in every chosen packet, or
the zero ring.  Thus Jacobians, discriminants, resultants, and intersection
multiplicities cannot exclude a proper packet chain without already proving
the conjectural divisibility \(P_i\mid C_{e_i}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Assume inductively that \(A_{i-1}=\mathbb F_{2^{2^{i-1}}}\) and that
\(\operatorname{Tr}(t_{i-1})=1\).  Since \(t_{i-1}\ne0\), division by
\(t_{i-1}^2\) and the substitution \(c=t_i/t_{i-1}\) turn the next equation
into

\[
 c^2+c=t_{i-1}.
\]

The absolute trace criterion makes this Artin--Schreier polynomial
irreducible.  Its two roots differ by one, which gives the displayed relative
Frobenius formula.  Relative trace and norm are respectively \(t_{i-1}\) and
\(t_{i-1}^3\), while trace transitivity gives absolute trace one.  Moreover
\(t_{i-1}=t_i+t_i^{2^{2^{i-1}}}\), so the top coordinate recovers every
ancestor and generates the whole field.

The Jacobian matrix is lower bidiagonal: its diagonal entries are
\(1,t_0,\ldots,t_{n-1}\).  These are nonzero in the field, proving the
\'etale
claim.  Sequential elimination therefore gives the stated irreducible
minimal polynomials without powers or extraneous components.  Finally,
\(A_n\) is a field, so quotienting by the values \(C_{e_i}(t_i)\) either adds
only zero relations or kills the ring.  This proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The precise zero-at-multiples coordinate is especially small.  Define
Fibonacci polynomials over \(\mathbb F_2\) by
\[
 S_0(X)=0,\qquad S_1(X)=1,
 \qquad S_{j+2}(X)=S_{j+1}(X)+XS_j(X).
\]

\begin{proposition}[zero-at-multiples normal form]\label{prop:z-fibonacci}
For every \(n\geq0\),
\[
 \delta_n=\min\{j>0:S_j(a_{n-1})=0\}.
\]
In fact,
\[
 S_j(a_{n-1})=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\delta_n\mid j.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \delta_n=F_n
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 S_{F_n/\ell}(a_{n-1})\ne0
 \quad\text{for every prime }\ell\mid F_n.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(c=c_n\), \(a=a_{n-1}=c(c+1)\), and
\(u=(c+1)/c\).  Induction from the recurrence gives the Binet identity
\[
 S_j(a)=c^j+(c+1)^j=c^j(1+u^j).
\]
The quotient-to-norm-one isomorphism
\[
 E_n^{\times}/E_{n-1}^{\times}\longrightarrow
 \ker\operatorname{N}_{E_n/E_{n-1}},
 \qquad zE_{n-1}^{\times}\longmapsto z^{|E_{n-1}^{\times}|},
\]
sends the class of \(c\) to \(u\).  Thus
\(\operatorname{ord}(u)=\delta_n\), and the displayed equivalences follow.
\end{proof}

The recurrence is a lossless encoding of the unknown order, not an order
bound.  Its least zero period is already \(\delta_n\).  In particular,
factorizations, trace identities, or automata derived only from this word
reconstruct the selected order rather than force it to be \(F_n\).

The matrix distinction can be made exact.  For a symmetric matrix \(B\), the
Seyfarth--Ranade block
\[
 \Omega(B)=\begin{pmatrix}B&I\\I&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
implements the polynomial \(Q\)-transform and the Wiedemann recursion
\cite{wiedemann1988,seyfarthranade2012}.  In the Conway tower, if \(B_i\)
represents multiplication by
\(s_i=a_{i-1}^{-1}=u_i+u_i^{-1}\), the literal recursion is instead
\[
 B_{i+1}=\Psi(B_i):=
 \Omega(B_i)^2+\operatorname{diag}(B_i,B_i)+I
 =\begin{pmatrix}B_i^2+B_i&B_i\\B_i&B_i\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Writing \(C=\Omega(B)\), one has the exact identities
\[
 C^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\I&B\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Psi(B)=C^{-1}(C+I)^3,
\]
and
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}I&I\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
 \Psi(B)
 \begin{pmatrix}I&0\\I&I\end{pmatrix}
 =\operatorname{diag}(B^2,B).
\]
Thus the Conway word inserts the cubic Laurent map
\(z\mapsto(z+1)^3/z\), whereas the \(Q\)-transform word uses
\(z\mapsto z+z^{-1}=(z+1)^2/z\).  Their rational degrees are different,
so no fractional-linear conjugacy identifies the two recursions.  The
congruence to \(\operatorname{diag}(B^2,B)\) records only the bilinear-form
class; every nonsingular symmetric trace-one packet realization over
\(\mathbb F_2\) has the same nonalternating class.

For an eigenvalue \(s\) of \(B_i\), the new eigenvalue satisfies
\(X^2+s^2X+s^3=0\), exactly the reciprocal Conway child equation.  The two
block recursions already differ in degree eight: their characteristic
polynomial bitmasks are respectively \(\mathtt{0x1d7}\) and
\(\mathtt{0x18b}\).  Moreover
\[
 \Omega(B)^m=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  S_{m+1}(B)&S_m(B)\\
  S_m(B)&S_{m-1}(B)
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
so for the literal Conway matrices
\[
 \delta_n
 =\operatorname{ord}\!\left(\Omega(\Psi^n([1]))\right)
 =\operatorname{fibind}\!\left(\chi_{\Psi^n([1])}\right).
\]
The analogous maximal-index assertion for the literal iterated
\(\Omega\)-word is precisely Wiedemann's conjecture, not a proved theorem of
Seyfarth--Ranade \cite{wiedemann1988,seyfarthranade2012}.  The stabilizer
\(\Omega(B_i)\) is a regular irreducible
symplectic element in a cyclic nonsplit torus of order \(2^d+1\), but this
forces only \(\operatorname{ord}_e(2)=2d\), not \(e=2^d+1\).  Choose a
symmetric realization \(B\) of the reciprocal of \(P^{(1)}\).
Then \(\Psi(B)\) is symmetric, and \(\Omega(\Psi(B))\) is an irreducible
64-dimensional symplectic stabilizer of the proper order \(6700417\).  Thus
matrix symmetry, torus type, and centralizer size cannot replace the complete
nested \(\Psi\)-ancestry.

The recent general
Artin--Schreier tower theorem of Cagliero, Herman, and Szechtman proves the
exact product of the relative orders, but its maximality condition remains
precisely that every relative factor \(\delta_j\) be full; it does not prove
\(\delta_j=F_j\) \cite{caglierohermanszechtman2025}.

There is nevertheless an exact elliptic realization of this arm.  It is
useful because it isolates, rather than removes, the remaining Kummer
coordinate.

\begin{proposition}[supersingular realization and pairing boundary]
\label{prop:z-elliptic}
On
\[
 \mathcal E:\quad y^2+y=x^3+x^2
\]
put \(P_n=(c_n,c_{n+1})\).  Then \(P_n\in\mathcal E\), and for \(n\geq2\)
\[
 [F_n]P_n=O.
\]
More precisely, if \(\pi\) is absolute Frobenius and
\(\iota=\pi+[1]\), then
\[
 \iota(x,y)=(x+1,y+x+1),\qquad
 \iota^2=[-1],\qquad
 \pi^4=[-4].
\]
For
\[
 g=\frac{y+1}{y}
\]
one has
\[
 g(P_n)=\frac{c_{n+1}+1}{c_{n+1}},
 \qquad \operatorname{ord}(g(P_n))=\delta_{n+1}.
\]
If \(T=(0,0)\), then \(T\) has order five and
\[
 \operatorname{div}(g)
 =2[-T]+[2T]-2[T]-[-2T].
\]
Thus \(g\) is the quotient of the two fixed doubling Miller functions at
\(-T\) and \(T\), but it is not a Weil or reduced-Tate pairing function
attached to the \(F_n\)-torsion point \(P_n\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Conway recursion gives
\[
 c_{n+1}^2+c_{n+1}=a_n
 =c_n(c_n^2+c_n)=c_n^3+c_n^2,
\]
so \(P_n\) lies on \(\mathcal E\).  The displayed coordinate formula for
\(\iota(P)=\pi(P)+P\) follows from the chord law; applying it twice gives
the inverse \((x,y)\mapsto(x,y+1)\).  Hence
\(\pi^2+2\pi+2=0\) in \(\operatorname{End}(\mathcal E)\), and squaring this
relation gives \(\pi^4=[-4]\).

Put \(H=2^{n+1}\).  The \(2^H\)-Frobenius fixes \(c_n\) and sends
\(c_{n+1}\) to \(c_{n+1}+1\), so it sends \(P_n\) to \(-P_n\).
For \(n\geq2\),
\[
 \pi^H=(\pi^4)^{2^{n-1}}=[2^{2^n}],
\]
and therefore \([2^{2^n}+1]P_n=O\).

The formula for \(g(P_n)\) and Proposition~\ref{prop:z-fibonacci} give its
order.  The five rational points of \(\mathcal E/\mathbb F_2\) show that
\(T\) has order five.  Direct intersection with the two horizontal lines
gives
\[
 \operatorname{div}(y)=2[T]+[-2T]-3[O],\qquad
 \operatorname{div}(y+1)=2[-T]+[2T]-3[O],
\]
and subtraction gives the divisor of \(g\).  Equivalently,
\(g=f_{2,-T}/f_{2,T}\) for the \(\mathbb F_2\)-rational functions normalized
at \(O\).  Its divisor has coefficients \(2,1,-2,-1\) at fixed rational
five-torsion, so it cannot be an \(F_n\)-Miller function attached to \(P_n\),
even modulo an \(F_n\)-th power.  Any Weil or reduced Tate pairing on
\(\mathcal E[F_n]\) takes values in \(\mu_{F_n}\), whereas \(g(P_n)\) has
order dividing \(F_{n+1}\).
The Fermat numbers are pairwise coprime and \(\delta_{n+1}>1\), so these
values cannot agree.
\end{proof}

The proposition supplies exact CM annihilator control of the literal Conway
point, but generator status still does not follow from annihilation by
\(F_n\).  For \(n\geq3\), no elliptic endomorphism can carry \(P_n\) to
\(P_{n-1}\): the order of such an image would divide both coprime numbers
\(F_n\) and \(F_{n-1}\), although \(P_{n-1}\ne O\).  For \(n=2\), the same
conclusion follows from \(\operatorname{ord}(P_1)=3\) and
\(\operatorname{ord}(P_2)\mid17\).  The actual ancestry relation
\(x(P_n)=y(P_{n-1})\) is therefore not an isogeny division tower.  The
unresolved statement remains exactly the next-level value
\(\operatorname{ord}(g(P_n))=F_{n+1}\).

\begin{proposition}[two-block tensor obstruction]\label{prop:z-two-block}
Let \(h=r^a s^b\), where \(r\ne s\) are primes and \(a,b\geq1\), and put
\[
 M=\mathbb F_{2^h},\quad
 A=\mathbb F_{2^{h/r}},\quad
 B=\mathbb F_{2^{h/s}},\quad
 J=\mathbb F_{2^{h/(rs)}}.
\]
If \(u\in A\setminus J\) and \(v\in B\setminus J\), then
\[
 u+v\notin A^\times B^\times.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \left|M^\times/(A^\times B^\times)\right|=\Phi_h(2).
\]
Consequently, if \(\Phi_h(2)\) is prime, the class of \(u+v\) generates
this quotient.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One has \(A\cap B=J\), \([A:J]=s\), and \([B:J]=r\).  The relative
degrees are coprime, so multiplication identifies
\(A\otimes_J B\) with \(M\).  Choose \(J\)-bases of \(A\) and \(B\)
beginning with \((1,u)\) and \((1,v)\).  The coefficient matrix of
\(u\otimes1+1\otimes v\) has the anti-diagonal minor
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
of nonzero determinant, whereas every element of \(A^\times B^\times\)
is a pure tensor and has coefficient-matrix rank one.  This proves the
first assertion.  Cyclotomic cancellation gives
\[
 \frac{(2^h-1)(2^{h/(rs)}-1)}
      {(2^{h/r}-1)(2^{h/s}-1)}=\Phi_h(2),
\]
and the final assertion follows because a nonidentity element of a group of
prime order generates it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[five complete multicomponent levels]\label{prop:z-small}
Assertion \(Z\) holds at
\[
 h=12,\ 15,\ 24,\ 36,\ 40.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At \(h=12\), the selected element is the sum of components of degrees six
and four; take
\[
 (A,B,J)=(\mathbb F_{2^6},\mathbb F_{2^4},\mathbb F_{2^2}).
\]
At \(h=24\), the components have degrees six and eight, but the relevant
maximal proper support fields are
\[
 (A,B,J)=(\mathbb F_{2^{12}},\mathbb F_{2^8},\mathbb F_{2^4}).
\]
The two selected components lie in \(A\setminus J\) and \(B\setminus J\),
respectively.  Proposition~\ref{prop:z-two-block} makes the selected class
nontrivial, and the prime quotient orders
\[
 \Phi_{12}(2)=13,
 \qquad \Phi_{24}(2)=241
\]
make it a generator.

The same proposition applies at \(h=40\).  Here the two selected components
are \(u=\kappa_5\in\mathbb F_{2^{20}}\setminus\mathbb F_{2^4}\) and
\(v=\kappa_8\in\mathbb F_{2^8}\setminus\mathbb F_{2^4}\), while
\(\Phi_{40}(2)=61681\) is prime.  Hence the selected class again generates.

At \(h=36\), put \(c=\kappa_3\), \(x=\kappa_4\),
\(y=\kappa_9\), and \(z=x+y\).  Over
\(J=\mathbb F_{2^6}\), the relations
\[
 c^6+c^3+1=0,\qquad x^2+x=c^3,\qquad y^3=c
\]
identify \(\mathbb F_{2^{36}}\) with
\(J(y)\otimes_J J(x)\).  In the row basis \((1,y,y^2)\) and column basis
\((1,x)\), direct reduction gives
\[
 [z^{37}]=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1+c^2+c^3&c^2\\
  c^3+c^4&c+c^3+c^4\\
  0&c
 \end{pmatrix}
\]
and
\[
 [z^{109}]=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1+c+c^3&1+c+c^2+c^4\\
  c+c^3+c^4+c^5&c^2+c^3+c^4\\
  1+c^2+c^3+c^5&c^2+c^3+c^4+c^5
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The upper-left minors are respectively
\(1+c^3+c^4\) and \(c^2+c^3+c^4+c^5\), both nonzero because
\([\mathbb F_2(c):\mathbb F_2]=6\).  Thus neither power lies in
\(J(y)^\times J(x)^\times\).  Since
\(\Phi_{36}(2)=4033=37\cdot109\), the nonidentity of \([z^{37}]\) forces
\(109\mid\operatorname{ord}([z])\), while that of \([z^{109}]\) forces
\(37\mid\operatorname{ord}([z])\).  Thus \([z]\) has full order.

Finally, the level \(h=15\) has a nontrivial synchronized norm.  Put
\(a=\kappa_2\), \(c=\kappa_3\), \(b=\kappa_4\), and
\(d=\kappa_5\), so that
\[
 a^2+a+1=0,\qquad c^3=a,\qquad b^2+b=a,\qquad d^5=b.
\]
The selected element is \(\kappa_{15}=c+d\) in
\(\mathbb F_{2^{60}}\).  If \(\sigma\) is the \(2^{15}\)-Frobenius, put
\[
 u=c+\sigma(c)\in\mathbb F_{2^3},\qquad
 v=d+\sigma(d)+\sigma^2(d)+\sigma^3(d)\in\mathbb F_{2^5}.
\]
Reduction with the four displayed tower relations gives
\[
 T_{15}=\operatorname N_{\mathbb F_{2^{60}}/\mathbb F_{2^{15}}}(c+d)
 =1+v+v^2+v^3+u+u^2v+u^2v^3+u^2v^4.
\]
The same reduction gives
\[
 u=c+c^2+ac^2,
 \quad
 v=d+abd^2+(1+ab)d^3+a(1+b)d^4.
\]
Thus \(u^2+u=ac(c+1)\ne0\).  Also the coefficient of \(d^4\) in
\(v^2+v\) is \(a+b\ne0\); since \(d\) has degree five over
\(\mathbb F_{2^4}\), this proves \(v^2+v\ne0\).  Hence \(u\) and \(v\)
have degrees three and five.  In the tensor bases \((1,u,u^2)\) and
\((1,v,v^2,v^3,v^4)\), the coefficient matrix of \(T_{15}\) is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&1&1&1&0\\
 1&0&0&0&0\\
 0&1&0&1&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its first two rows and columns have nonzero determinant, so
\(T_{15}\notin\mathbb F_{2^3}^\times\mathbb F_{2^5}^\times\).  The
quotient has prime order \(\Phi_{15}(2)=151\), hence the selected norm
generates it.  Proposition~\ref{prop:z-support} now gives \(Z\) at all five
levels.
\end{proof}

The universal arm \(Z\) therefore remains the simultaneous generation
problem for the distinguished synchronized norm, including the all-level
Conway--Fermat equalities \(\delta_n=F_n\).

\section{The ordinary odd Kummer arm}\label{sec:o}

Fix an odd prime \(r\), let \(b=b_r=d(\alpha_r)\), and set
\[
 x_0=\alpha_r,
 \qquad x_a=\kappa_{r^a}\ (a\geq1),
 \qquad y_a=x_a+1,
 \qquad F_a=\mathbb F_{2^{r^ab}}.
\]
The Kummer equations are \(x_1^r=\alpha_r\) and
\(x_a^r=x_{a-1}\) for \(a>1\).

\begin{proposition}[projective order criterion]\label{prop:o-projective}
For every \(a\geq1\),
\[
 \operatorname{N}_{F_a/F_{a-1}}(y_a)=y_{a-1}.
\]
The cyclic quotient \(G_{r,a}=F_a^{\times}/F_{a-1}^{\times}\) has order
\[
 L_{r,a}=\frac{2^{r^ab}-1}{2^{r^{a-1}b}-1}
        =\Phi_{r^a}(2^b).
\]
If \(p\ne r\) is a prime divisor of \(L_{r,a}\), then
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_p(2^b)=r^a,
 \qquad f(p)=r^as\text{ for some }s\mid b,
 \qquad \mathcal Q(f(p))=\{r^a\}.
\]
Conversely, every singleton row with component \(r^a\) arises from such a
prime.  For each corresponding row,
\[
 m_p=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_p\!\left(\operatorname{ord}
  (y_aF_{a-1}^{\times})\right)=v_p(L_{r,a}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The relative conjugates of \(x_a\) are \(\xi x_a\),
\(\xi\in\mu_r\).  Evaluating their binomial at one gives the norm identity.
The quotient order follows from the field degrees.  For \(p\ne r\), the
cyclotomic divisor criterion applied to
\(p\mid\Phi_{r^a}(2^b)\) gives
\(\operatorname{ord}_p(2^b)=r^a\).  Writing \(h=f(p)\) then gives
\(h=r^as\) with \(s\mid b\), and Lenstra's minimality and component theorem
give \(\mathcal Q(h)=\{r^a\}\).  The converse is the same argument in
reverse.

The prime \(p\) does not divide \(|F_{a-1}^{\times}|\), so its full primary
contribution lies in the quotient.  The selected element \(x_a\) has order
prime to \(p\), hence is a \(p\)-th power; therefore \(m_p\geq1\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:power} applied to \(y_a\) proves the final equivalence.
\end{proof}

For a particular row \(h=r^as\), \(s\mid b\), the large ambient test may be
reduced to the transverse field \(K=\mathbb F_{2^{r^as}}\):
\[
 \Theta_{r,a,s}
 =\operatorname{N}_{F_a/K}(x_a+1).
\]
Then \(m_p=1\) exactly when the order of \(\Theta_{r,a,s}\) contains the
full \(p\)-part of \(2^{r^as}-1\).  By contrast,
\[
 \operatorname{N}_{F_a/F_{r,0}}(x_a+1)=1+\alpha_r
\]
lies in a group whose order is prime to \(p\).  The lower Kummer norm is
therefore blind to the current primary coordinate; the transverse norm is
not.

\subsection{The marked cyclotomic unit}

Let \(N_a=\operatorname{ord}(x_a)\), choose a primitive complex
\(N_a\)-th root \(\xi_{N_a}\), and choose a prime \(\mathfrak P\) above two
whose residue map sends \(\xi_{N_a}\) to the selected element \(x_a\).
Put \(\varepsilon_a=1-\xi_{N_a}\).  Its reduction is \(1+x_a=y_a\).

\begin{proposition}[selected residue-symbol form]\label{prop:o-symbol}
Let \(p\ne r\) be a prime divisor of \(L_{r,a}\).  In a cyclotomic field
containing \(\mu_{N_a}\) and \(\mu_p\), the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the projective class of \(y_a\) has full \(p\)-primary order;
\item \(y_a\) is not a \(p\)-th power in \(F_a\);
\item the power-residue symbol satisfies
\[
 \left(\frac{\varepsilon_a}{\mathfrak P}\right)_p\ne1;
\]
\item \(\mathfrak P\) does not split in the Kummer extension obtained by
adjoining a \(p\)-th root of \(\varepsilon_a\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first two conditions are Proposition~\ref{prop:o-projective}.  The usual
unramified power-residue formula is
\[
 \left(\frac{\varepsilon_a}{\mathfrak P}\right)_p
 =\overline{\varepsilon_a}^{(|F_a|-1)/p}.
\]
It is nontrivial exactly when the residue is not a \(p\)-th power.  Kummer
theory identifies this symbol with the Frobenius action on a chosen
\(p\)-th root; see \cite{washington1997}.
\end{proof}

This form explains the real difficulty of \(O\).  The conductor and the
unmarked conjugate set are determined by the tower, but the conjecture asks
for one residue symbol at the prime over two selected by Conway ancestry.

\begin{proposition}[the selected prime is a unique inert ray]
\label{prop:o-inert-ray}
Put \(M=\operatorname{ord}(\alpha_r)\).  Choose compatible primitive roots
\(\xi_{N_a}\) with \(\xi_{N_0}\mapsto\alpha_r\) and
\(\xi_{N_a}^r=\xi_{N_{a-1}}\).  Then
\[
 N_a=\operatorname{ord}(x_a)=r^aM,
 \qquad \operatorname{ord}_{N_a}(2)=br^a.
\]
Let \(K_a=\mathbb Q(\xi_{N_a})\), and let \(\mathfrak P_a\mid2\) be the
prime selected by \(\xi_{N_a}\mapsto x_a\).  For \(a\geq1\),
\(\mathfrak P_a\) is the unique prime of \(K_a\) above
\(\mathfrak P_{a-1}\), with relative residue degree \(r\).  Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{N}_{K_a/K_{a-1}}(1-\xi_{N_a})
 =1-\xi_{N_{a-1}}.
\]
If \(p\ne r\) divides \(L_{r,a}\), then the lower residue field has
trivial \(p\)-Kummer quotient.  Consequently the residue-field norm
\[
 F_a^\times/(F_a^\times)^p\longrightarrow
 F_{a-1}^\times/(F_{a-1}^\times)^p,
\]
or equivalently the corresponding finite-field corestriction, sends the
selected current class to zero and imposes no restriction on it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The irreducible Kummer steps \(X^r-x_{a-1}\) increase the \(r\)-primary
part of the root order by one and preserve every other primary part.  This
gives \(N_a=r^aM\).  The defining-field equality
\(\mathbb F_2(x_a)=\mathbb F_{2^{br^a}}\) then gives
\(\operatorname{ord}_{N_a}(2)=br^a\).

The first irreducible binomial also forces \(r\mid M\).  Hence
\([K_a:K_{a-1}]=r\), while the displayed orders show that the residue-degree
ratio at the selected primes is also \(r\).  Since two is unramified in
these odd-conductor cyclotomic fields, the fundamental identity forces one
prime above \(\mathfrak P_{a-1}\), of residue degree \(r\).  Taking the
product over the \(r\)-th roots of unity gives the norm identity.

Finally,
\(p\mid\Phi_{r^a}(2^b)\) but
\(p\nmid2^{br^{a-1}}-1\).  Raising to the \(p\)-th power is therefore an
automorphism of the lower residue-field multiplicative group, so its
\(p\)-Kummer quotient is zero.  The last assertion follows after reduction
at \(\mathfrak P_{a-1}\); no claim is made that the global cyclotomic-unit
class has trivial number-field norm.
\end{proof}

Thus full ancestry leaves no hidden choice of a prime after the birth level:
it determines one compatible inert ray.  What it does not determine is the
new augmentation coordinate of the cyclotomic unit on that ray.  Global
reciprocity only relates the product of the conjugate symbols, which is one,
and ramified conductor formulas at primes above \(p\) determine a different
local character.  A nontrivial ray character may still vanish on the fixed
class of \(\mathfrak P_a\).  The missing theorem is therefore a
selected-prime Artin-symbol nonvanishing statement, not a decomposition,
norm, or conductor calculation.

The separation between the ramified local calculation and the selected
unramified ideal class can be made exact.  Put
\[
 F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{N_a}),\qquad E=F(\zeta_p),\qquad
 q_0=2^{br^{a-1}},\qquad
 \rho=\frac{1-\zeta_{N_a}^{q_0}}{1-\zeta_{N_a}}.
\]
Choose a prime \(\mathfrak P_0\mid2\) with the Conway reduction and write
\[
 \left(\frac{\rho}{\mathfrak P_0}\right)_p
 =\zeta_p^{\ell_{\mathfrak P_0}}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[ramified-local/unramified-class split]
\label{prop:o-ray-split}
The assertion \(O(r,a,p)\) is equivalent to
\(\ell_{\mathfrak P_0}\ne0\).  For a prime \(\lambda\mid p\), let
\(f=\operatorname{ord}_{N_a}(p)\), let \(\tau\) be the Teichm\"uller lift of
the selected \(N_a\)-th root in the residue field, put
\(u=\tau^{p^{f-1}}\), and define
\[
 \mathcal L_p(T)=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\frac{T^i}{i},\qquad
 A_\lambda=
 \frac{\mathcal L_p(u)}{(1-u)^p}
 -\frac{\mathcal L_p(u^{q_0})}{(1-u^{q_0})^p}.
\]
Then \(A_\lambda=0\) exactly when
\(\rho\in E_\lambda^{\times p}\).  The collection
\((A_\lambda)_{\lambda\mid p}\) determines the Kummer ray character
\(\chi_\rho\) only modulo an unramified character.  More precisely, after
choosing any ray-character lift \(\chi_A\) of these local data,
\[
 \ell_{\mathfrak P_0}
 =\ell^A_{\mathfrak P_0}+\eta([\mathfrak P_0]),
 \qquad
 \eta=\chi_\rho\chi_A^{-1}
 \in\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}(E),\mu_p).
\]
The local data alone determine the selected value if and only if
\([\mathfrak P_0]\in p\operatorname{Cl}(E)\).  In general the open target
is noncancellation of the displayed ramified local term with the marked
unramified class term.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Modulo the selected prime,
\(\overline\rho=(1+x_a)^{q_0-1}\).  Since \(q_0\) has exact order \(r\)
modulo \(p\), its exponent is nonzero modulo \(p\); hence its Kummer class
vanishes exactly when that of \(1+x_a\) does.  This proves the first
assertion.  Expanding the local Kummer regulator at \(\lambda\) gives the
displayed truncated-log coordinate, whose vanishing is the local
\(p\)-power criterion.  Explicitly, in the maximal unramified subfield
\(K_0\) write \(\rho=[\overline\rho]t\) with
\(t\in1+p\mathcal O_{K_0}\).  The Teichm\"uller factor is a \(p\)-th
power, while
\[
 A_\lambda=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 t\in1+p^2\mathcal O_{K_0}
 =(1+p\mathcal O_{K_0})^p,
\]
the last equality following from the \(p\)-adic logarithm.  Adjoining
\(\mu_p\) creates no new \(p\)-th roots of elements of \(K_0\): if
\(\rho=z^p\) after that extension, the norm of \(z\), whose relative
degree is \(p-1\), gives a \(p\)-th root already in \(K_0\).

The kernel of restriction from \(p\)-ray characters to their ramified
local characters (in the admissible image modulo global units) is precisely
\(\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}(E),\mu_p)\).  Thus any two global
lifts of the same \(A_\lambda\)'s differ by the displayed unramified
character, and evaluation at \(\mathfrak P_0\) gives the additive formula.
For a finite abelian group \(H\), the intersection of the kernels of all
maps \(H\to\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) is \(pH\).  Applying this to
\(H=\operatorname{Cl}(E)\) proves the final equivalence.
\end{proof}

The unramified summand in Proposition~\ref{prop:o-ray-split} has a precise
Stickelberger quotient.  It is not removed merely by the fact that the
selected prime is decomposition-invariant.

\begin{proposition}[Stickelberger quotient for the marked prime class]
\label{prop:o-stickelberger-class}
Let
\[
 G=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q),\qquad
 D=\langle\operatorname{Frob}_2\rangle,\qquad
 A=\operatorname{Cl}(E)/p\operatorname{Cl}(E),\qquad
 c=[\mathfrak P_0]\bmod p.
\]
Let \(I_{\mathrm{St}}\subset\mathbb F_p[G]\) be the reduction of the
integral Stickelberger ideal.  Then the cyclic module generated by \(c\) is
a quotient of
\[
 \mathcal S_{E,D}=
 \mathbb F_p[G]/
 \bigl(I_{\mathrm{St}}+(d-1:d\in D)\bigr).
\]
In particular, \(\mathcal S_{E,D}=0\) implies
\([\mathfrak P_0]\in p\operatorname{Cl}(E)\), so the unramified term in
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-ray-split} vanishes.  If
\(\mathcal S_{E,D}\ne0\), the module \(\mathcal S_{E,D}\) with its class
of one is an exact countermodel to any deduction using only decomposition
invariance and Stickelberger annihilation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every element of \(D\) fixes the prime \(\mathfrak P_0\), so
\((d-1)c=0\).  Stickelberger's theorem gives
\(I_{\mathrm{St}}A=0\).  Hence the map
\[
 \mathbb F_p[G]\longrightarrow A,
 \qquad r\longmapsto rc
\]
factors through \(\mathcal S_{E,D}\), with image
\(\mathbb F_p[G]c\).  This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}

On a semisimple contributing character, write
\(\chi=\omega^{-1}\theta\), where \(\omega\) is the Teichmuller character,
\(\theta\) is even, and decomposition invariance gives
\(\theta(2)=\omega(2)\).  If \(m\) is the conductor of \(\theta\), direct
pairing of residues under multiplication by two gives
\[
 B_{1,\chi}
 \equiv
 \sum_{\substack{pm/2<a<pm\\(a,pm)=1}}
   \frac{\overline\theta(a)}a
 \pmod p,
\]
up to the Euler factors
\(1-\ell^{-1}\overline\theta(\ell)\) for primes added to the conductor.
This is a genuine irregular half-sum: the condition at two forces neither
its vanishing nor its nonvanishing.  A nonzero value kills that character
block; a zero value is the exact irregular obstruction.  If
\(p\mid\varphi(N_a)\), the augmentation value is only the first test in the
corresponding \(p\)-group algebra, and the full obstruction is the quotient
by all projected Stickelberger elements.  Thus the class term is reduced to
an explicit irregular eigenspace question, not eliminated by ambiguous-class
or decomposition formulas; see \cite{washington1997} for the Stickelberger
annihilator and character evaluation used here.

The Conway conductor restricts, but does not remove, this irregularity.  For
every contributing \(\theta\), its restriction to
\[
 H=\ker\bigl((\mathbb Z/N_a\mathbb Z)^\times
       \longrightarrow(\mathbb Z/(N_a/r)\mathbb Z)^\times\bigr)
 =\langle q_0\rangle
\]
is nontrivial, since \(\theta(q_0)=q_0\) has exact order \(r\) modulo
\(p\).  Hence the conductor \(m\) of \(\theta\) contains the full
\(r\)-part of \(N_a\), and omitted Euler primes can only be primes other
than \(r\) inherited from \(M\).  Writing
\[
 J(\theta)=
 \prod_{\substack{\ell\mid N_a\\\ell\nmid m}}
   \bigl(1-\ell^{-1}\overline\theta(\ell)\bigr)
 \sum_{\substack{pm/2<u<pm\\(u,pm)=1}}
   \frac{\overline\theta(u)}u,
\]
the semisimple marked class can survive only in reflected character blocks
with \(J(\theta)=0\).  An omitted Euler factor vanishes exactly when
\(\overline\theta(\ell)=\ell\); it is automatically nonzero if
\(-1\in\langle2,\ell\rangle\) modulo \(pm\).  Neither this subgroup
condition nor nonvanishing of the half-sum follows uniformly from ancestry.

The half-sum can in fact vanish at an actual Conway conductor.

\begin{proposition}[actual irregular marked-conductor block]
\label{prop:o-actual-irregular}
At the literal birth level \(r=11\), \(a=1\), \(p=23\), the selected
ancestor has minimal polynomial
\[
 P_{\alpha_{11}}(X)=
 P_{\alpha_5}\bigl((X+1)^5\bigr)=X^{20}+X^{16}+X^5+X+1,
 \qquad P_{\alpha_5}(X)=X^4+X+1,
\]
and order \(M=2^{20}-1\).  Hence
\[
 N=11M=3\cdot5^2\cdot11^2\cdot31\cdot41=11534325,
 \qquad \operatorname{ord}_N(2)=220,
\]
while \(23\mid\Phi_{11}(2^{20})\) and
\(23\nmid\varphi(N)=5280000\).

There is an even primitive character \(\theta\) of conductor
\(363=3\cdot11^2\), inflated to modulus \(N\), with
\(\overline\theta(2)=2\), for which the full projected Stickelberger scalar
in \(J(\theta)\) is zero modulo \(23\).  None of its omitted-conductor Euler
factors vanishes.  Thus uniform nonvanishing of the actual-conductor
Bernoulli--Stickelberger scalar is false.

More precisely, put \(\Theta=\omega\theta^{-1}\), and let \(L\) be the cyclic
imaginary degree-\(22\) field cut out by \(\Theta\).  Then
\[
 v_{23}\bigl(B_{1,\Theta^{-1}}\bigr)=1,
\]
and the \(\Theta\)-part of \(\operatorname{Cl}(L)\) has order exactly \(23\).
Thus this is an actual nonzero irregular class component, not merely a
vanishing first annihilator.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take \(z=5\in\mathbb F_{23}^{\times}\), and on the conductor-\(3\) and
conductor-\(121\) components define, using discrete logarithms to the base
two,
\[
 \overline\theta(a)=
 z^{11\log_{2,3}(a)+13\log_{2,121}(a)}.
\]
Then \(\overline\theta(2)=z^{11+13}=z^2=2\).  Its conductor-\(3\)
component is nontrivial because \(z^{11}=-1\ne1\).  The character is even
because \(11+13\cdot55\equiv0\pmod{22}\), and it is primitive at \(121\) because
\(\overline\theta(2^{10})=z^{20}\ne1\).  For
\(\chi=\omega^{-1}\theta\), grouping the \(2420\) terms of the half-sum by
\(\overline\theta(u)=5^e\) gives the coefficient vector
\[
 (22,2,12,12,21,13,18,20,5,8,1,10,18,8,6,6,20,21,3,15,0,11).
\]
Its integer dot product with the least nonnegative residues of
\((5^e)_{e=0}^{21}\) modulo \(23\) is
\(2737=23\cdot119\), so the \(\mathbb F_{23}\)-dot product is zero.  The
omitted primes \(5,31,41\) contribute Euler
factors \(20,6,6\), whose product is \(7\ne0\pmod{23}\).  The displayed
minimal polynomial is the previously certified literal ancestor polynomial.
In the polynomial basis of
\(\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^{20}+X^{16}+X^5+X+1)\), with \(\alpha_{11}=X\), the five
nonidentity bit encodings
\[
 \alpha_{11}^{M/q}=
 \mathtt{0x536},\ \mathtt{0xfac8},\ \mathtt{0x6da4},\
 \mathtt{0xc6a9e},\ \mathtt{0x19bc8}
 \quad(q=3,5,11,31,41)
\]
certify \(\operatorname{ord}(\alpha_{11})=M\); the remaining conductor
arithmetic is immediate.

For the sharper valuation, lift \(5\) to its Teichmuller representative
\(28\pmod {23^2}\).  Direct grouping of the same half-sum modulo \(23^2\)
gives
\[
 B_{1,\Theta^{-1}}\equiv414=23\cdot18\pmod {23^2}.
\]
Hence its \(23\)-adic valuation is one.  Solomon's class-component theorem
\cite[Theorem~II.1]{solomon1990}, applied to the odd semisimple character
\(\Theta\ne\omega\), identifies this valuation with the
\(\mathbb Z_{23}[\Theta]\)-length of the \(\Theta\)-class component.  The
value field here is \(\mathbb Q_{23}\), so that component has order \(23\).
\end{proof}

This is not an \(O\)-counterexample: the literal row has \(m_{23}=1\).
The class line and its marked prime can in fact both be determined exactly.

\begin{proposition}[marked Artin certificate in the irregular block]
\label{prop:o-actual-artin}
Retain the notation of Proposition~\ref{prop:o-actual-irregular}, put
\(K_0=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{363})\),
\(E=K_0(\mu_{23})=\mathbb Q(\mu_{8349})\), and let
\(e_a\in\{1,\ldots,22\}\) satisfy
\[
 e_a\overline\theta(a)\equiv1\pmod {23}
 \qquad(a\in(\mathbb Z/363\mathbb Z)^\times).
\]
Then the global unit
\[
 \varepsilon=\prod_{a\in(\mathbb Z/363\mathbb Z)^\times}
                 (1-\zeta_{363}^a)^{e_a}
\]
defines a nontrivial everywhere-unramified degree-\(23\) Kummer extension
of \(E\).  It descends to an unramified cyclic degree-\(23\) extension
\(H/L\) in the \(\Theta\)-component.  Every prime
\(\mathfrak q\mid2\) of \(L\), and in particular the prime obtained from
the Conway-selected ray, has nontrivial Artin symbol in \(H/L\).
Consequently its class generates the order-\(23\) component of
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-actual-irregular}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(\Phi_{363}(1)=1\), the displayed element is a global unit.
Reindexing the product gives
\[
 \sigma_b(\varepsilon)\equiv
 \varepsilon^{\theta(b)}\pmod {E^{\times23}}.
\]
If \(\alpha^{23}=\varepsilon\) and
\(\tau(\alpha)=\zeta_{23}\alpha\), conjugation on
\(\langle\tau\rangle\) is therefore through
\(\omega\theta^{-1}=\Theta\).

It remains to certify primarity at \(23\) and nontriviality at two.  Over
\(\mathbb F_{23}\),
\[
 h_1(X)=X^{22}+2X^{11}+4
\]
is irreducible by Rabin's criterion.  The polynomial
\[
 h_2(X)=X^{22}+255X^{11}+487\pmod {23^2}
\]
is a Hensel lift dividing \(\Phi_{363}\).  In
\(R=(\mathbb Z/23^2\mathbb Z)[T]/(h_2)\), direct evaluation of the displayed
product gives, in increasing coefficient order,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \bar\varepsilon={}&(344,186,101,283,348,493,134,197,425,138,469,424,\\
                    &133,435,387,355,122,446,153,13,122,226),
 \end{split}
\]
and exact exponentiation gives
\[
 \bar\varepsilon^{23^{22}-1}=1.
\]
For an unramified extension \(F/\mathbb Q_{23}\) of residue size \(Q\),
write \(x=[\bar x]\langle x\rangle\) with
\(\langle x\rangle\in1+23\mathcal O_F\).  The Teichmuller factor is a
\(23\)-rd power, and the \(23\)-adic logarithm gives
\[
 x\in F^{\times23}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 x^{Q-1}\equiv1\pmod {23^2}.
\]
Thus \(\varepsilon\) is a local \(23\)-rd power at every prime above
\(23\).  Away from \(23\), adjoining a \(23\)-rd root of a unit is

For nontriviality, work in the exact binary field
\[
 \mathbb F_2[X]/(X^{110}+X^{33}+1).
\]
The modulus is Rabin-certified irreducible.  If
\[
 z=(1+X+X^2)^{(2^{110}-1)/8349},
\]
then exact prime-quotient screens give \(\operatorname{ord}(z)=8349\).
Reducing \(\zeta_{363}\) to \(z^{23}\) gives
\[
 \bar\varepsilon=\mathtt{0x533a83d1770ba6987acd705100a},
\]
and
\[
 \bar\varepsilon^{(2^{110}-1)/23}
 =\mathtt{0x230624ca45da272928c1ab2181d1}
 =(z^{363})^4\ne1.
\]
Hence \(M/E\) has degree \(23\), and the displayed prime
\(\mathfrak Q\mid2\) has Artin symbol \(\zeta_{23}^4\).

Let \(U=\ker\Theta\); then \(|U|=220\) is prime to \(23\), and \(U\)
acts trivially on \(\operatorname{Gal}(M/E)\).  The preimage of \(U\) in

\(f(\mathfrak Q/\mathfrak q)=110\), since
\(\Theta(2)=1\).  Base change of Frobenius gives

 \operatorname{Art}_{\mathfrak Q}(M/E)
 =\operatorname{Art}_{\mathfrak q}(H/L)^{110}.

As \(23\nmid110\), the right-hand Artin symbol is nontrivial (its exponent

This is still not an \(O\)-counterexample.  Rather, it proves something
sharper about the obstruction: the unramified term in
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-ray-split} is genuinely nonzero at the selected
prime, while the literal row succeeds.  Hence in any fixed ray-character
split the ramified lift does not cancel this unramified value; it need not
itself be nonzero.  Decomposition invariance,
Stickelberger annihilation, or an assertion that ancestry makes the selected

\begin{proposition}[phase conservation above the birth level]
\label{prop:o-tail}
Fix \(a\), a prime \(p\ne r\) dividing \(L_{r,a}\), and for \(j\geq a\)
put
\[
 \Theta_j=(1+x_j)^{(2^{r^jb}-1)/p}\in\mu_p.
\]
Then
\[
 \Theta_j=\Theta_a\qquad(j\geq a).
\]
Thus passage to later levels of the same Kummer spine creates no new current
\(p\)-coordinate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 S_j=\frac{2^{r^jb}-1}{2^{r^{j-1}b}-1}.
\]
The norm identity gives \((1+x_j)^{S_j}=1+x_{j-1}\), while
\[
 \frac{2^{r^jb}-1}{p}
 =S_j\frac{2^{r^{j-1}b}-1}{p}.
\]
Therefore \(\Theta_j=\Theta_{j-1}\), and induction proves the claim.
\end{proof}

For \(r\ne3\), assertion \(O\) is exactly the universal nontriviality of
these marked symbols at their birth levels.  General lower bounds for orders
in finite fields and cyclotomic constructions do not by themselves evaluate
the selected residue symbol.

\section{The cubic-power arm}\label{sec:c}

Let \(h=3^k\), \(\zeta_k=\kappa_{3^k}\), and
\(\gamma_k=\zeta_k+\zeta_k^{-1}\).

\begin{proposition}[cyclotomic and half-angle structure]
\label{prop:c-half-angle}
The element \(\zeta_k\) is a primitive \(3^{k+1}\)-st root of unity,
\[
 \mathbb F_2(\zeta_k)=\mathbb F_{2^{2h}},
 \qquad
 \mathbb F_2(\gamma_k)=\mathbb F_{2^h}.
\]
If \(s\) is the inverse of two modulo \(3^{k+1}\), then
\[
 \zeta_k+1=\zeta_k^s(\zeta_k^s+\zeta_k^{-s}),
 \qquad
 (\zeta_k^s+\zeta_k^{-s})^2=\gamma_k,
\]
and hence
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\zeta_k+1)
 =3^{k+1}\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k).
\]
The translates \(\zeta_k+2\) and \(\zeta_k+3\) have the same order.
Consequently, for a prime \(p\) with \(f(p)=3^k\), one has
\[
 m_p=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_p(\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k))=v_p(2^h-1).
\]
Assuming \(\gamma_{k-1}\) is primitive, all such rows have excess one exactly
when \(\gamma_k\) is primitive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The defining Kummer equation gives \(\zeta_k^{3^k}=\kappa_2\), a primitive
cube root.  Since two has order \(2\cdot3^k\) modulo \(3^{k+1}\), the
cyclotomic polynomial \(X^{2h}+X^h+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_2\).
The least exponent \(j>0\) with \(2^j\equiv\pm1\pmod{3^{k+1}}\) is \(h\),
which gives the degree of \(\gamma_k\).

The half-angle identities are direct.  Their two factors lie in the coprime
circle and real parts of \(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}^{\times}\), of orders dividing
\(2^h+1\) and \(2^h-1\), respectively.  Squaring preserves odd order, so
the order formula follows.  The other two translates are Frobenius conjugate
to the same decomposition.  Finally, a prime with \(f(p)=h\) cannot divide
the \(3\)-power factor, so Proposition~\ref{prop:power} gives the stated
criterion.
\end{proof}

The selected Gaussian period obeys
\[
 \gamma_k^3+\gamma_k=\gamma_{k-1},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{N}_{\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}/
                        \mathbb F_{2^{3^{k-1}}}}(\gamma_k)=\gamma_{k-1}.
\]
This propagates every old primary factor.  It does not control the new
factor \(\Phi_{3^k}(2)\).

\begin{proposition}[selected norm-one generator]\label{prop:c-eta}
Put
\[
 q_k=2^{3^{k-1}},
 \qquad
 U_k=\ker\!\left(
 \operatorname{N}_{\mathbb F_{q_k^3}/\mathbb F_{q_k}}
 \right),
 \qquad
 \eta_k=\gamma_k^{q_k-1},
\]
and set \(\eta_0=0\).  Then
\[
 |U_k|=q_k^2+q_k+1=\Phi_{3^k}(2),
\qquad
\mathbb F_2(\eta_k)=\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}},
\]
and \(\eta_k\) is the selected root of the irreducible cubic
\[
 X^3+\eta_{k-1}X^2+(\eta_{k-1}+1)X+1.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \operatorname{N}(\eta_k)=1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}(\eta_k)=\eta_{k-1},
 \qquad
 e_2(\eta_k)=\eta_{k-1}+1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 C_k
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C_{k-1}\text{ and }
 \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)=q_k^2+q_k+1,
\]
where \(C_j\) denotes primitivity of \(\gamma_j\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The element \(\eta_k\) has norm one by its definition.  A direct Frobenius
calculation from \(\zeta_k^{q_k}\) gives its displayed cubic; its symmetric
coefficients give the norm, trace, and second elementary symmetric function.
It cannot lie in the base field, since a base-field element of \(U_k\) has
order dividing \(\gcd(q_k-1,3)=1\).  Hence the cubic is irreducible.
Since its quadratic coefficient recovers \(\eta_{k-1}\), induction from
\(\eta_0=0\) gives
\(\mathbb F_2(\eta_k)=\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}\).

The cyclic group \(\mathbb F_{q_k^3}^{\times}\) splits as the product of
\(\mathbb F_{q_k}^{\times}\) and \(U_k\), whose orders are coprime because
\(q_k\equiv2\pmod3\).  The norm determines the old factor of \(\gamma_k\),
while exponentiation by \(q_k-1\) detects the new \(U_k\)-factor without
changing its order.  This proves the last equivalence.
\end{proof}

The cubic ancestry admits a closed rational-dynamical form.  This makes the
word ``selected'' in Proposition~\ref{prop:c-eta} completely explicit.

\begin{proposition}[Singer trace ancestry and M\"obius conjugacy]
\label{prop:c-singer-ancestry}
Put \(q=2^{3^{k-1}}\), \(N=q^2+q+1\), and
\[
 \mathcal D_k=\{x\in U_k:x^{q+1}+x+1=0\}.
\]
For \(x\in\mathcal D_k\), define
\[
 R(x)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_{q^3}/\mathbb F_q}(x)
     =\frac{x^3+x+1}{x^2+x}.
\]
If
\(
 \mathcal A_j=\{\eta_j^{2^i}:0\leq i<3^j\}
\)
and \(\mathcal A_0=\{0\}\), then
\[
 \mathcal D_k\cap R^{-1}(\mathcal A_{k-1})=\mathcal A_k.
\]
Thus the complete lower ancestry selects one unique absolute-Frobenius orbit
inside the Singer set \(\mathcal D_k\).

If \(Q_j\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\eta_j\), then, with
\(A=X^2+X\) and \(B=X^3+X+1\),
\[
 Q_k(X)=A^{3^{k-1}}Q_{k-1}(B/A),\qquad Q_0(X)=X.
\]
Over \(\mathbb F_4\), choose \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\) and put
\[
 W(x)=\frac{x+\omega}{x+\omega^2}.
\]
Then
\[
 W(R(x))=W(x)^3.
\]
Consequently \(z_k=W(\eta_k)\) has exact order \(3^{k+1}\), and
in fact the orientation fixed by the Conway root is
\[
 z_k=\zeta_k^2.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \eta_k=\omega^2\frac{z_k+\omega^2}{z_k+1}.
\]
The current order assertion is equivalent to the family of literal
polynomial nondivisibilities
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)=N
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Q_k(X)\nmid X^{N/\ell}-1
 \quad\hbox{for every prime }\ell\mid N.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(x\in\mathcal D_k\), its defining equation gives
\[
 x^q=\frac{x+1}{x},\qquad x^{q^2}=\frac1{x+1}.
\]
Adding the three conjugates gives the displayed formula for \(R\).  The fibre
over \(t\) is exactly the root set of
\[
 X^3+tX^2+(t+1)X+1.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:c-eta} therefore shows that the fibre over each
\(\eta_{k-1}^{2^i}\) consists of the three conjugates
\(\eta_k^{2^{i+a\,3^{k-1}}}\), \(0\leq a<3\).  These fibres exhaust
\(\mathcal A_k\), proving the orbit identity.  Taking their product gives
the denominator-cleared recursion for \(Q_k\).

Direct expansion using \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\) gives
\[
 \frac{R(x)+\omega}{R(x)+\omega^2}
 =\frac{(x+\omega)^3}{(x+\omega^2)^3}.
\]
There are no poles along the selected iteration: \(\eta_j\notin\mathbb F_2\)
for \(j\geq1\), and
\(\mathbb F_{2^{3^j}}\cap\mathbb F_4=\mathbb F_2\), so neither denominator
\(\eta_j^2+\eta_j\) nor \(\eta_j+\omega^2\) vanishes.
Since \(R^{\circ k}(\eta_k)=0\) and \(W(0)=\omega^2\), one has
\(z_k^{3^k}=\omega^2\), hence \(z_k\) has exact order \(3^{k+1}\).
The selected orientation can be read without ambiguity.  If
\(h=3^k\), then
\[
 q=2^{3^{k-1}}\equiv h-1\pmod{3h},
\]
where the congruence follows from the case \(2\equiv2\pmod9\) by successive
cubing.  Therefore \(\zeta_k^q=\omega\zeta_k^{-1}\).  With
\(y=\zeta_k^2\), direct substitution gives
\[
 \eta_k=\frac{\gamma_k^q}{\gamma_k}
 =\frac{\omega+\omega^2y}{1+y}
 =\omega^2\frac{y+\omega^2}{y+1}.
\]
Consequently \(\eta_k+\omega=y/(1+y)\) and
\(\eta_k+\omega^2=1/(1+y)\), whence
\(W(\eta_k)=y=\zeta_k^2\).  Finally,
irreducibility of \(Q_k\), followed by the prime-divisor test in the cyclic
group of order \(N\), gives the last equivalence.
\end{proof}

This proposition identifies the literal selected orbit rather than an ambient
degree or norm class.  It does not prove primitivity: the conjugacy turns the
remaining assertion into the power-residue order of one marked M\"obius
cyclotomic unit.  In particular, the lower trace ancestry is nonlinear, while
the relative norm of every element of \(\mathcal D_k\) is one; no multiplicative
descent to the preceding level survives.

The same marked phase has an exact integral resultant and an explicit binary
support.  This turns a possible parity attack into a concrete polynomial
problem without replacing the selected root by an average.

\begin{proposition}[selected M\"obius resultant]
\label{prop:c-mobius-resultant}
Let \(h=3^k\), \(n=3^{k+1}=3h\),
\(N=\Phi_{3^k}(2)\), and let \(z=\zeta_k^2\).  For an odd divisor
\(d\mid N\), put
\[
 \mathcal R_d(X)=
 (X+X^h)^d+X^{2hd}(X+1)^d\in\mathbb Z[X].
\]
Then
\[
 \eta_k^d=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 2\mid\operatorname{Res}_X(\Phi_n(X),\mathcal R_d(X)).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)=N
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Res}(\Phi_n,\mathcal R_{N/\ell})
 \text{ is odd for every prime }\ell\mid N.
\]

Equivalently, over \(\mathbb F_4\) define
\[
 P_d(X)=(X+\omega^2)^d+\omega^d(X+1)^d.
\]
Modulo two its coefficient of \(X^j\) is zero unless the binary digits of
\(j\) form a submask of those of \(d\); among those submasks it vanishes
exactly when
\[
 j\equiv2d\pmod3.
\]
Thus the support of the selected resultant polynomial is known exactly.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The orientation in Proposition~\ref{prop:c-singer-ancestry} gives
\[
 \eta_k=\omega^2\frac{z+\omega^2}{z+1},
 \qquad z^h=\omega^2,\qquad z^{2h}=\omega.
\]
Since \(z+1\ne0\), clearing the denominator shows that
\(\eta_k^d=1\) exactly when \(P_d(z)=0\) in characteristic two, or
equivalently when \(\mathcal R_d(z)=0\).  The order of two modulo \(n\)
is \(2h=\varphi(n)\), so two is inert in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)\).
Therefore vanishing at the selected reduction is equivalent to evenness of
the field norm of \(\mathcal R_d(z)\), which is the displayed integer
resultant.  The prime-divisor test gives the second equivalence.

Finally, Lucas's theorem says that
\(\binom dj\) is odd exactly for the binary submasks \(j\) of \(d\).
For such \(j\), the coefficient in \(P_d\) is
\[
 \omega^{2(d-j)}+\omega^d,
\]
which vanishes exactly when \(2(d-j)\equiv d\pmod3\), or
\(j\equiv2d\pmod3\).
\end{proof}

This support theorem alone cannot decide the parity.  The candidate exponents
\(N/\ell\) are exponentially larger than \(\deg\Phi_n=2h\), so degree does
not exclude the cyclotomic factor; at \(d=N\) the factor necessarily occurs.
Euclidean reduction of \(\mathcal R_d\) modulo \(\Phi_n\) is exactly the
original selected-order test.  A proof through this route therefore requires
a new proper-divisor parity theorem, not merely the sparse support.

The obstruction can also be separated exactly into a circular-unit term and
a principal ray term.  This is useful because it identifies which part is not
controlled by ordinary class-number information.

\begin{proposition}[exact relative circular-ray obstruction]
\label{prop:c-circular-ray}
For \(k\geq1\), put
\[
 L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{3^{k+1}})^+,
 \qquad K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{3^k})^+,
 \qquad h=3^k,
 \qquad q=2^{h/3}.
\]
For \(F=K,L\), let \(\mathfrak p_F=2\mathcal O_F\), let
\(k_F=(\mathcal O_F/\mathfrak p_F)^\times\), and let \(E_F\) and \(C_F\)
be respectively the global units and the circular units, with \(-1\) included
in \(C_F\).  Write bars for reduction modulo \(\mathfrak p_F\), and set
\[
 U=\ker(\operatorname N:k_L\to k_K),\qquad
 T_F=\overline E_F/\overline C_F,
 \quad S_F=k_F/\overline C_F,
 \quad R_F=k_F/\overline E_F.
\]
Then \(|U|=q^2+q+1\), the selected element satisfies
\(\eta_k=\overline{\tau(\gamma_k)/\gamma_k}\) for
\(\tau(\zeta)=\zeta^{2^{h/3}}\), and there is a canonical isomorphism
\[
 U/\langle\eta_k\rangle
 \simeq \ker(\operatorname N:S_L\to S_K).
\]
All norm maps in the rows
\[
 1\longrightarrow T_F\longrightarrow S_F\longrightarrow R_F
 \longrightarrow1
\]
are surjective, and hence there is an exact sequence
\[
 1\longrightarrow\ker(\operatorname N:T_L\to T_K)
 \longrightarrow U/\langle\eta_k\rangle
 \longrightarrow\ker(\operatorname N:R_L\to R_K)
 \longrightarrow1.
\]
In particular,
\[
 [U:\langle\eta_k\rangle]
 =|\ker(\operatorname N:R_L\to R_K)|
  \frac{[\overline E_L:\overline C_L]}
       {[\overline E_K:\overline C_K]},
 \tag{\(*\)}
\]
where \(R_F\) is canonically the principal kernel
\[
 \ker\bigl(\operatorname{Cl}_F(\mathfrak p_F)
               \longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}_F\bigr).
\]
Moreover, the second factor in \((*)\) divides \(h_L^+/h_K^+\), the ratio
of the ordinary real class numbers.  Consequently arm \(C\) is equivalent
to simultaneous triviality of the relative principal \(2\)-ray kernel and
the relative reduced unit/circular-unit kernel.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^2\), put
\(\tau=\sigma^{h/3}\), and use the standard
circular units
\[
 c_a=\zeta^{(1-a)/2}\frac{1-\zeta^a}{1-\zeta}.
\]
Here the half-exponent is interpreted modulo \(3^{k+1}\).  The circular
units are generated by signs and the \(c_a\) with \(3\nmid a\), while the
powers of \(2\) traverse these classes modulo sign
\cite{washington1997}.  The
relations
\[
 \sigma^j(c_2)=\frac{c_{2^{j+1}}}{c_{2^j}},
 \qquad \sigma(c_2)=\frac{c_4}{c_2}=\gamma_k
\]
show that the conjugates of \(\gamma_k\) generate \(C_L\) modulo signs.
The circular-unit distribution relation gives
\(\operatorname N_{L/K}(\gamma_k)=\gamma_{k-1}\), so the circular-unit
norm is onto.  The reductions of \((\tau-1)C_L\) generate
\(\langle\eta_k\rangle\).  If
\(D_C=\ker(\operatorname N:C_L\to C_K)\), then, in additive module
notation,
\[
 3d=(1-\tau)(2+\tau)d\qquad(d\in D_C).
\]
Thus \(D_C/(\tau-1)C_L\) is killed by \(3\).  Since
\(3\nmid|U|\), reduction gives
\(\overline{D_C}=\langle\eta_k\rangle\).  The same cube-adjustment
\(c\mapsto c^3/\operatorname N(c)\) shows that this is the full kernel of
the norm on \(\overline C_L\).  The snake lemma applied to
\(1\to\overline C_F\to k_F\to S_F\to1\) now gives the first isomorphism.

The finite-field norm is onto.  Norm on \(\overline E_L\) is also onto:
an embedded base unit has relative norm its cube, and cubing is an
automorphism because \(3\nmid|k_K|\).  For these real \(3\)-power
cyclotomic fields, Sinnott's index formula and Iwasawa's \(3\)-indivisibility
give
\[
 |E_F/C_F|=h_F^+,
 \qquad 3\nmid h_F^+;
\]
see \cite{washington1997}.  Hence the same embedded-cube argument makes the
norm on \(T_L\to T_K\) onto.  The remaining surjectivities and the short
exact sequence follow from the snake lemma, and taking orders gives \((*)\).

Finally, the global circular norm is onto and
\[
 D_E/D_C\simeq
 \ker\bigl(\operatorname N:E_L/C_L\to E_K/C_K\bigr),
\]
whose order is \(h_L^+/h_K^+\).  Reduction onto the corresponding kernel
in \(T_L\to T_K\) follows by first correcting the residue norm with a
circular unit and then applying \(e\mapsto e^3/\operatorname N(e)\).
Therefore the reduced kernel has order dividing the class-number ratio.
The ray-class interpretation of \(R_F\) is the usual ray exact sequence.
\end{proof}

Even \(h_L^+=h_K^+=1\) would kill only the \(T\)-factor in \((*)\); the
principal \(2\)-ray factor remains exactly the marked residue-order problem.
Conversely, arm \(C\) does not imply class-number one.  Thus ordinary
circular-unit index theorems sharpen the location of the obstruction but do
not supply its missing selected ray coordinate.

\begin{proposition}[finite-field circular defect]\label{prop:c-defect}
For \(j\geq0\), put
\[
 L_j=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{3^{j+1}})^+,\qquad
 d_j=[(\mathcal O_{L_j}/2)^\times:\overline C_{L_j}].
\]
Then
\[
 d_j=\frac{2^{3^j}-1}
 {\operatorname{ord}(\zeta_j+\zeta_j^{-1})},
 \qquad
 [U_k:\langle\eta_k\rangle]=\frac{d_k}{d_{k-1}}.
\]
Consequently the all-level cubic assertion is equivalent to primitivity of
the specific composite-conductor periods
\(\zeta_j+\zeta_j^{-1}\) for every \(j\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The conjugates of \(\gamma_j=\zeta_j+\zeta_j^{-1}\) generate \(C_{L_j}\)
modulo signs, as in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:c-circular-ray}.  After reduction modulo two, signs
vanish and Galois conjugation is Frobenius, so
\(\overline C_{L_j}=\langle\gamma_j\rangle\).  This proves the first formula.
The norm on residue units and on reduced circular units is onto, with kernels
respectively \(U_k\) and \(\langle\eta_k\rangle\).  Taking the ratio of the
orders in these two exact rows gives the second formula.  Since \(d_0=1\),
all relative indices are one exactly when every \(d_j\) is one.
\end{proof}

This is the same finite-field circular-unit defect studied by Hoechsmann
\cite{hoechsmann1995} in the prime-conductor setting.  That setting also
supplies a sharp warning against a class-number-only proof: for
\(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{37})^+\), two is inert and the real class number is one,
yet Hoechsmann computes defect \(d_2=3\).  Thus inertness, circular generation
of the global units, and class number one still permit a nontrivial principal
\(2\)-ray kernel.  Proposition~\ref{prop:c-defect} shows that the present
\(3\)-power problem requires a theorem about its recursively marked periods,
not merely those coarse invariants.

\begin{proposition}[exact Capelli dichotomy]\label{prop:c-capelli-dichotomy}
Let \(Q_k\in\mathbb F_2[X]\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\eta_k\),
and let \(\ell\) be a prime divisor of
\(N_k=\Phi_{3^k}(2)\).  Exactly one of the following occurs:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\eta_k\notin(\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}^\times)^\ell\), and
\(Q_k(X^\ell)\) is irreducible of degree \(3^k\ell\);
\item \(\eta_k\in(\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}^\times)^\ell\), and
\(Q_k(X^\ell)\) is the product of exactly \(\ell\) distinct irreducible
polynomials, each of degree \(3^k\).
\end{enumerate}
In particular, both the successful and failed cases have an odd number of
irreducible factors.  Factor-count parity, and hence any criterion which sees
only that parity, cannot decide the current \(C\)-coordinate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(h=3^k\).  The preceding proposition gives
\(\mathbb F_2(\eta_k)=\mathbb F_{2^h}\), while
\(\ell\mid2^h-1\) gives
\(\mu_\ell\subset\mathbb F_{2^h}\).  Capelli's lemma and the prime-degree
Kummer criterion therefore say that \(Q_k(X^\ell)\) is irreducible exactly
when \(\eta_k\) is not an \(\ell\)-th power.

Suppose instead that \(b^\ell=\eta_k\) in \(\mathbb F_{2^h}\).  Every root
of \(Q_k(X^\ell)\) is an \(\ell\)-th root of a Frobenius conjugate of
\(\eta_k\), hence belongs to \(\mathbb F_{2^h}\), because that field also
contains \(\mu_\ell\).  Such a root has degree at least \(h\) over
\(\mathbb F_2\), since its \(\ell\)-th power has degree \(h\), and therefore
has degree exactly \(h\).  The composition is separable and has degree
\(h\ell\), so it has exactly \(\ell\) distinct irreducible factors, all of
degree \(h\).  Finally \(\ell\) is odd because it divides \(2^h-1\).
\end{proof}

For a prime \(\ell\mid |U_k|\), put
\[
 \Omega_{k,\ell}=\eta_k^{|U_k|/\ell}.
\]
The exact terminal test is
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)=|U_k|
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Omega_{k,\ell}\ne1
 \quad\text{for every prime }\ell\mid |U_k|.
\]
This formulation again retains the full \(\ell\)-primary valuation.  The
minimal polynomial of \(\gamma_k\) is also explicit.  If
\(D_n(X,1)\) is the Dickson polynomial defined by
\(D_n(t+t^{-1},1)=t^n+t^{-n}\), then
\[
 P_k(X)=D_{3^k}(X,1)+1=(X^3+X)^{\circ k}+1
\]
is irreducible of degree \(3^k\), and \(C_k\) says precisely that it is a
primitive polynomial.  Irreducibility is therefore already built into the
tower; primitivity is the open statement.  General lower bounds for
multiplicative orders of Gaussian periods, such as
\cite{ahmadishparlinskivoloch2010}, do not give maximal order for this
recursively selected period.

In fact degree is maximally insensitive here.  Every prime
\(\ell\mid\Phi_{3^k}(2)\) satisfies
\(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(2)=3^k\).  If \(z\ne1\) lies in \(U_k\), choose
such a prime \(\ell\mid\operatorname{ord}(z)\).  Then
\([\mathbb F_2(z):\mathbb F_2]\) is both divisible by
\(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(2)=3^k\) and at most \(3^k\), hence equals
\(3^k\).  Thus even an element of proper order in \(U_k\) has the same full
field degree as a generator.

The recursion in Proposition~\ref{prop:c-eta} is lossless.  It reconstructs
every finite selected truncation up to Frobenius, and Frobenius preserves
multiplicative order.  Trace, norm, and the sparse cubic therefore identify
the correct selected coordinate but do not evaluate \(\Omega_{k,\ell}\).
This is the exact remaining content of arm \(C\).

\section{The exceptional arm}\label{sec:d}

Let \(h=3^k\), \(q=2^h\), \(\zeta=\zeta_k\), and
\(\omega=\zeta^h\in\mathbb F_4\).

\begin{proposition}[unconditional lower bound]\label{prop:d-lower}
If \(p\) is a prime with \(f(p)=2\cdot3^k\), then \(m_p\geq4\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Such a prime divides \(2^h+1\) and divides neither \(3^{k+1}\) nor
\(2^h-1\).  The element \(\zeta\) has order \(3^{k+1}\), while
Proposition~\ref{prop:c-half-angle} gives
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\zeta+m)
 =3^{k+1}\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k)
 \quad(m=1,2,3),
\]
with \(\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k)\mid2^h-1\).  Hence all four translates
\(m=0,1,2,3\) have order prime to \(p\), and each is a \(p\)-th power in its
defining field.  The definition of the excess gives the claim.
\end{proof}

The translate by four lies in the compositum with \(\mathbb F_{16}\), so
its first useful invariant is a quadratic norm rather than an order
calculation in \(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\).

\begin{proposition}[corrected norm]\label{prop:d-norm}
In \(\mathbb F_{2^{4h}}/\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\), the nontrivial relative
Frobenius sends the finite nimber \(4\) to \(5\), and
\[
 \operatorname{N}_{\mathbb F_{2^{4h}}/
                        \mathbb F_{2^{2h}}}(\zeta+4)
 =(\zeta+4)(\zeta+5)
 =\zeta^2+\zeta+\omega=A_k.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The intersection of \(\mathbb F_{16}\) and \(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\) is
\(\mathbb F_4\), so the relative Frobenius is the nontrivial automorphism of
\(\mathbb F_{16}/\mathbb F_4\) and exchanges \(4\) and \(5\).  These are
the two roots of \(Y^2+Y+\omega\); hence \(4\cdot5=4^2+4=\omega\).
Expanding the product proves the formula.
\end{proof}

Define
\[
 R_k(Y)=\operatorname{Res}_X
 \left(X^{2h}+X^h+1,\;Y+X^h+X^2+X\right)
 \in\mathbb F_2[Y].
\]

\begin{theorem}[exact exceptional target]\label{prop:d-exact}
The polynomial \(R_k\) is the irreducible degree-\(2h\) minimal polynomial
of \(A_k\).  The prime divisors of
\[
 \Psi_k=\frac{\Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)}3
\]
are exactly the primes \(\ell\ne3\) with
\(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(2)=2h\), with their full valuations in
\(2^{2h}-1\).  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(m_{\ell}=4\) for every prime \(\ell\) with
\(f(\ell)=2\cdot3^k\);
\item \(\Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k)\), where
\(M_k=A_k^{q-1}\);
\item \(A_k\) is not an \(\ell\)-th power in
\(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\) for every prime \(\ell\mid\Psi_k\);
\item \(R_k(X^{\ell})\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_2\) for every
prime \(\ell\mid\Psi_k\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The element \(\zeta\) has minimal polynomial
\(X^{2h}+X^h+1\).  Suppose \(A_k^{2^j}=A_k\), and reduce
\(a=2^j\) modulo \(3h\).  The resulting relation on \(\zeta\) has support
the symmetric difference of
\[
 \{ah,a,2a\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{h,1,2\}.
\]
Every multiple of \(X^{2h}+X^h+1\) of degree below \(3h\) has support a
disjoint union of triples \(\{r,r+h,r+2h\}\).  If
\(a\equiv2\pmod3\), the support contains \(h,2h\) but not zero.  If
\(a\equiv1\pmod3\), cancellation of the \(h\)-terms leaves at most two
members in each residue class modulo \(h\).  Both are impossible for a
nonempty union of triples.  The support is therefore empty, forcing
\(a=1\).  Thus \(A_k\) has Frobenius orbit length \(2h\), and the displayed
resultant is its irreducible minimal polynomial.

This also verifies the defining field used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:d-norm}.  Put \(\beta=\zeta+4\).  The identity
\(A_k=\operatorname{N}(\beta)\) puts \(A_k\) in
\(\mathbb F_2(\beta)\), so the degree just proved shows that
\(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\subseteq\mathbb F_2(\beta)\).  On the other hand
\(\beta\notin\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\): otherwise
\(4=\beta+\zeta\) would belong to that field, contrary to
\(\mathbb F_{16}\cap\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}=\mathbb F_4\).  Hence
\(\mathbb F_2(\beta)=\mathbb F_{2^{4h}}\).

Writing \(a_0=2^{3^{k-1}}\), one has
\[
 \Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)=a_0^2-a_0+1\equiv3\pmod9.
\]
After removing its single factor of three, the cyclotomic divisor criterion
gives the asserted prime support and valuations.

Fix \(\ell\mid\Psi_k\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:d-lower} gives
\(m_{\ell}\geq4\).  Propositions~\ref{prop:norm} and
\ref{prop:d-norm} give
\[
 m_{\ell}=4
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A_k\notin(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}^{\times})^{\ell}.
\]
Raising to \(q-1\) is invertible on the current \(\ell\)-Kummer quotient,
because \(q-1\equiv-2\pmod\ell\).  Hence this is equivalent to the full
\(\ell\)-primary part appearing in \(\operatorname{ord}(M_k)\).  Imposing
it for every current prime gives assertion 2.  Finally, \(\mu_{\ell}\) lies
in \(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\), so Kummer theory and Capelli's lemma identify
non-\(\ell\)-power of \(A_k\) with irreducibility of
\(R_k(X^{\ell})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[effective bound on the exceptional column]
\label{prop:d-bound}
Let \(k\geq2\), let \(h=3^k\), and let \(\ell\mid\Psi_k\) be prime.
Then
\[
 m_\ell<16h^4.
\]
More precisely, let \(t\) be the least power of two satisfying
\[
 t\geq\left\lceil\log_2(h-1)\right\rceil.
\]
Then \(m_\ell\leq4^t-1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 K=\mathbb F_{4^h}=\mathbb F_4(\zeta),
 \qquad C_a=\mathbb F_{4^t},
 \qquad L_a=KC_a=\mathbb F_{4^{ht}},
\]
where \(t=2^{a-1}\).  Choose a character
\(\chi:K^\times\to\mu_\ell\) of exact order \(\ell\), and define
\[
 \chi_a=\chi\circ\operatorname{N}_{L_a/K},
 \qquad
 S_{a,k,\ell}=\sum_{c\in C_a}\chi_a(\zeta+c).
\]
Because \(h\) is odd and \(t\) is a power of two, the fields \(K\) and
\(C_a\) are linearly disjoint over \(\mathbb F_4\), and
\([L_a:K]=t\).  This degree is prime to \(\ell\), so norm induces an
isomorphism on \(\ell\)-Kummer quotients.  In particular, \(\chi_a\) has
kernel \((L_a^\times)^\ell\).  No term \(\zeta+c\) vanishes, and equality
in the complex triangle inequality gives
\[
 \zeta+C_a\subseteq(L_a^\times)^\ell
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 S_{a,k,\ell}=4^t.
\]

The character \(\chi\) is the primitive even Dirichlet character modulo
\(X^h+\omega\) over \(\mathbb F_4[X]\).  Write its \(L\)-polynomial as
\[
 L_\chi(T)=(1-T)\prod_{j=1}^{h-2}(1-\alpha_jT).
\]
The function-field Riemann hypothesis gives
\(\lvert\alpha_j\rvert=2\), and constant-field extension raises each
reciprocal root to its \(t\)-th power \cite[Chapters 4, 8, and
9]{rosen2002}.  Comparing the degree-one coefficient after extension gives
\[
 S_{a,k,\ell}
 =-\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{h-2}\alpha_j^t\right),
 \qquad
 \lvert S_{a,k,\ell}\rvert\leq1+(h-2)2^t.
\]
If \(2^t\geq h-1\), then
\[
 1+(h-2)2^t<4^t.
\]
Thus some \(c\in C_a\) makes \(\zeta+c\) a non-\(\ell\)-th power.
Kummer-quotient injectivity from its defining field to \(L_a\) shows that
the same is true in the defining field.  The elements of \(C_a\) are exactly
the finite nimbers below \(4^t\), so \(m_\ell\leq4^t-1\).

Finally, the least power of two \(t\) above
\(\lceil\log_2(h-1)\rceil\) satisfies
\[
 t<2(\log_2h+1),
\]
and hence \(4^t<16h^4\).  This proves the stated bound.
\end{proof}

The open statement \(D\) is therefore the family
\[
 D'_k:\qquad \Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k).
\]
It is a current-level assertion, not a cumulative divisibility statement.
The factor \(A_k\) in Proposition~\ref{prop:d-norm} is also essential:
replacing it by a scalar product of separate translates gives the wrong norm.

The exceptional target fits the same cubical primitive-support coordinate as
the multicomponent zero arm.  Put \(H=2h\), \(s=h/3\), and define on
\(\mathbb F_{2^H}^\times\)
\[
 \sigma_2(x)=x^{2^h},\qquad
 \sigma_3(x)=x^{2^{2s}},\qquad
 \Delta_H(x)=
 \frac{x\,\sigma_2\sigma_3(x)}{\sigma_2(x)\sigma_3(x)}.
\]
Since \(M_k=\sigma_2(A_k)/A_k\) and the two Frobenius maps commute,
\[
 \Delta_H(A_k)=\frac{\sigma_3(M_k)}{M_k}
 =M_k^{2^{2s}-1}.
\]
For every \(\ell\mid\Psi_k\), one has
\(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)=2h\), so
\(\ell\nmid2^{2s}-1\).  Thus
\[
 \Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(\Delta_H(A_k)).
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-cubical-coordinate} gives
\(|\operatorname{im}\Delta_H|=\Phi_H(2)=3\Psi_k\).  This places \(D\)
canonically in the primitive-support quotient, but does not prove generation:
the displayed exponent is an invertible change of the same selected Kummer
coordinate.

There is also a global cyclotomic-unit lift of the literal translate by four.
It proves that the Kummer extension itself is nontrivial, while sharply
separating that fact from the value of Frobenius at the selected prime.

\begin{proposition}[marked exceptional cyclotomic unit]
\label{prop:d-cyclotomic-unit}
Let \(k\geq2\), \(h=3^k\), and \(m=5\cdot3^{k+1}\).  In
\(F=\mathbb Q(\mu_m)\), choose a primitive \(m\)-th root \(g\), and put
\[
 \zeta=g^5,\qquad \theta=g^h,\qquad
 \theta^5=\zeta^h,
\]
and let \(\mathfrak p\mid2\) be the prime selected by the reductions
\(\zeta\mapsto\zeta_k\) and \(\theta\mapsto4\).  Put
\[
 \rho=\zeta/\theta,\qquad u=1+\rho.
\]
Then \(\rho\) has exact order \(m\), and for every
\(\ell\mid\Psi_k\), and every extension \(\mathfrak P\) of
\(\mathfrak p\) to \(F(\mu_\ell)\),
\[
 m_\ell=4
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \left(\frac{u}{\mathfrak P}\right)_\ell\ne1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 u\notin(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab},\times})^\ell.
\]
Thus the global Kummer extension
\(F(\mu_\ell,u^{1/\ell})/F(\mu_\ell)\) is nontrivial; the remaining
assertion is that its Frobenius at the Conway-selected prime is nontrivial.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The roots \(\zeta\) and \(\theta\) have orders \(3^{k+1}\) and \(15\),
respectively, and
\(\rho=g^{5-h}\) is primitive because \(5-h\) is coprime to both three and
five.  The selected reduction is compatible: \(\zeta_k^h=4^5=\omega\);
if \(5a+hb=1\), then \(t=\zeta_k^a4^b\) satisfies
\(t^5=\zeta_k\) and \(t^h=4\), so evaluation \(g\mapsto t\) defines the
required prime.  That prime
\(\mathfrak p\) has residue degree
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_m(2)
 =\operatorname{lcm}(2h,4)=4h.
\]
Since \(\varphi(m)=8h\), there are exactly two primes over two; the compatible
reductions select one of them.  Also
\(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)=2h\), so the residue field already contains
\(\mu_\ell\).

The reduction of
\[
 \zeta+\theta=\theta(1+\rho)=\theta u
\]
is the literal element \(\zeta_k+4\).  Because \(\ell\) is coprime to
\(15\), the reduction of \(\theta\) is an \(\ell\)-th power in the residue
field.  Hence \(u\) and the literal
translate have the same selected \(\ell\)-Kummer class.  The power-residue
formula and Propositions~\ref{prop:d-norm}--\ref{prop:d-exact} prove the
displayed equivalence.

The element \(u\) is a cyclotomic unit: \(-\rho\) has conductor \(2m\),
which is not a prime power, and therefore
\[
 \operatorname N_{F/\mathbb Q}(1+\rho)
 =\Phi_m(-1)=\Phi_{2m}(1)=1.
\]
Its logarithmic height satisfies \(h(u)\leq\log2\).  It is not a root of
unity, since an embedding with \(\rho=e^{2\pi i/m}\) has
\(|1+\rho|>1\).  If \(u=v^\ell\) for
\(v\in\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab},\times}\), then \(v\) is not torsion.  Since
\(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)=2h\geq18\), one has \(\ell\geq19\), and
\[
 h(v)=\frac{h(u)}\ell\leq\frac{\log2}{19}<\frac{\log5}{12}.
\]
The last strict inequality is \(2^{12}<5^{19}\), while the lower bound
\(h(v)\geq(\log5)/12\) is the theorem of Amoroso and Dvornicich
\cite{amorosodvornicich2000}.  This contradiction proves the global
nonpower assertion.
\end{proof}

The last statement is not yet the selected nonvanishing.  Indeed, let
\(G=(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times\) and let
\(J=\langle2\rangle\).  Then \(|J|=4h\), \(-1\notin J\), and
\(G=J\sqcup(-J)\).  If \(\lambda\) is the additive
\(\ell\)-Kummer logarithm after reduction at \(\mathfrak p\), then
characteristic two and
\(1+\rho^{-a}=\rho^{-a}(1+\rho^a)\) give
\[
 \lambda(1+\rho^{2a})=2\lambda(1+\rho^a),
 \qquad
 \lambda(1+\rho^{-a})=\lambda(1+\rho^a).
\]
Here the inverse identity uses that the root-of-unity factor
\(\rho^{-a}\), whose order is coprime to \(\ell\), has trivial
\(\ell\)-Kummer class.
Consequently every conjugate value is \(2^j c\) for the single scalar
\(c=\lambda(u)\).  All homogeneous circular-unit norm and orbit identities
are compatible with \(c=0\), even though the marked global unit is not an
\(\ell\)-th power.  Arm \(D\) is exactly the missing assertion \(c\ne0\).

The entire current circular-unit subgroup can nevertheless be identified.
This removes a possible third obstruction and leaves two exact ray-class
factors.

\begin{proposition}[exact exceptional circular-ray obstruction]
\label{prop:d-circular-ray}
Put \(n=3^{k+1}=3h\), \(f=5n\), and \(E=\mathbb Q(\mu_f)\).  Choose
roots \(z\), \(\lambda\) of orders \(n\), five, put \(\omega=z^h\), and
let
\[
 L=\mathbb Q(z,\lambda+\lambda^{-1}).
\]
Select \(\mathfrak P\mid2\) by
\(z\mapsto\zeta_k\) and
\(\lambda+\lambda^{-1}\mapsto\omega\).  Let
\(k_{\mathfrak P}\) be the residue-field multiplicative group, and let
\(E_L\) and \(C_L\) be the global and Sinnott circular units.  For
\(\ell\mid\Psi_k\), write a subscript \(\ell\) for the
\(\ell\)-primary projection after reduction.  Then
\[
 \overline C_{L,\ell}
 =\langle A_{k,\ell}\rangle
 =\langle M_{k,\ell}\rangle.
\]
Consequently
\[
 [k_{\mathfrak P,\ell}:\langle M_{k,\ell}\rangle]
 =
 \left|\ker\left(
  \operatorname{Cl}_L(\mathfrak P)_\ell
   \longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}_L{}_{\ell}
 \right)\right|
 \cdot |(\overline E_L/\overline C_L)_\ell|.
 \tag{\(**\)}
\]
The second factor divides the \(\ell\)-part of the class number of the
maximal real subfield \(L^+\).  Hence \(D\) at \((k,\ell)\) is equivalent
to simultaneous triviality of the principal \(\mathfrak P\)-ray
\(\ell\)-kernel and the reduced unit/circular-unit \(\ell\)-quotient.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The field \(L\) has degree \(4h\).  Frobenius at two has order \(2h\), so
there are exactly two primes over two and
\(k_{\mathfrak P}\simeq\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}^\times\).  Put \(x=\omega z\)
and
\[
 U=\omega\operatorname N_{E/L}(1-x\lambda)
  =\omega(1-x\lambda)(1-x\lambda^{-1}).
\]
The root \(x\lambda\) has exact conductor \(f\), so \(U\in C_L\), and
direct reduction gives
\[
 \overline U
 =\omega\bigl(1+\omega\zeta_k\omega
                  +\omega^2\zeta_k^2\bigr)
 =\zeta_k^2+\zeta_k+\omega=A_k.
\]

Let
\(\pi_\ell:k_{\mathfrak P}\to k_{\mathfrak P,\ell}\) be the canonical
projection to the full \(\ell\)-Sylow subgroup.  Use the standard normed
circular-unit generators from conductors \(d\mid f\).  If the \(3\)-part of
\(d\) is at most \(3^k\), the residue degree in
\(L\cap\mathbb Q(\mu_d)\) is at most \(2h/3\).  Because
\(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)=2h\), its residue group has no \(\ell\)-part,
so \(\pi_\ell\) kills every such generator.  This includes the pure
conductor-five terms.

At full \(3\)-conductor \(d=n\) without the factor five, every unit
\(e\in\mathbb Q(\mu_n)\) satisfies
\(\overline e/e\in\mu_\infty\): all conjugates of this quotient have
absolute value one, so Kronecker's theorem applies.  The \(2^h\)-Frobenius
is complex conjugation on this field.  The root-of-unity factor has order
prime to \(\ell\), and hence
\[
 \pi_\ell(e)^{2^h}=\pi_\ell(\overline e)=\pi_\ell(e).
\]
Since \(\ell\nmid2^h-1\), exponentiation by \(2^h-1\) is an automorphism
of the entire \(\ell\)-Sylow subgroup, forcing \(\pi_\ell(e)=1\).  Only
the top-conductor generators remain; they form two decomposition-group
orbits.  More precisely, the standard top-conductor normed generators are,
up to roots of unity, the \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\)-conjugates of
\(U\).

Let \(r\) fix \(z\) and induce the nontrivial automorphism on
\(\mathbb Q(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\).  The two orbits are coupled by
\[
 U\,r(U)
 =\omega^2\prod_{a=1}^4(1-x\lambda^a)
 =\omega^2\Phi_5(x)
 \in\mathbb Q(\mu_n)^\times.
\]
The right side has trivial full \(\ell\)-primary projection by the preceding
full-\(3\)-conductor argument.  Hence
\(\pi_\ell(r(U))=\pi_\ell(U)^{-1}\).  Within each decomposition orbit,
Frobenius sends \(\pi_\ell(U)\) to its successive \(2^j\)-th powers, and
the second orbit gives their inverses.  Thus every top-conductor circular
unit projects into the cyclic subgroup generated by
\(\pi_\ell(\overline U)=A_{k,\ell}\).  Since \(U\) itself is circular,
this proves the first equality at the full primary valuation.

Since \(M_k=A_k^{2^h-1}\) and \(\ell\nmid2^h-1\), the \(A_k\)- and
\(M_k\)-subgroups agree.  Factoring the two subgroup indices gives \((**)\).
The first factor is the principal ray kernel.  For odd \(\ell\), possible
Hasse-unit and circular-unit normalization factors in Sinnott's formula are
powers of two, so the \(\ell\)-part of \(E_L/C_L\) equals the
\(\ell\)-part of the class number of \(L^+\); reduction can only decrease
it.  This proves the divisibility and the final equivalence; see
\cite{washington1997}.
\end{proof}

The local coordinates in the cubic and exceptional arms are exactly
orthogonal, so their ray obstructions cannot cancel or force one another.

\begin{proposition}[current \(C/D\) orthogonality]
\label{prop:cd-orthogonal}
Let \(h=3^k\), \(q_0=2^{h/3}\), and \(q=q_0^3=2^h\).  Put
\[
 U_C=\ker\left(
  \operatorname N_{\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_{q_0}}
 \right),
 \qquad
 U_D=\ker\left(
  \operatorname N_{\mathbb F_{q^2}/\mathbb F_q}
 \right).
\]
Then
\[
 |U_C|=q_0^2+q_0+1,\qquad |U_D|=q+1,\qquad
 \gcd(|U_C|,|U_D|)=1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \mathbb F_{q^2}^{\times}
 =\mathbb F_q^{\times}\times U_D,
 \qquad
 \operatorname N_{\mathbb F_{q^2}/\mathbb F_q}(b,u)=b^2,
\]
and the cubical operator \(\Delta_{2h}\) is trivial on
\(\mathbb F_q^\times\) while its image lies in \(U_D\).  Thus every
current \(C\)-primary class lies in the factor killed by \(\Delta_{2h}\),
whereas every current \(D\)-primary class is killed by the local norm to
the cubic field.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One has
\[
 q+1=(q_0+1)(q_0^2-q_0+1).
\]
The number \(q_0^2+q_0+1\) is coprime to \(q_0+1\), by reduction at
\(q_0=-1\), and coprime to \(q_0^2-q_0+1\), because their difference is
\(2q_0\) and both are odd.  This proves the coprimality.  Since
\(\gcd(q-1,q+1)=1\), the two displayed subgroups give the direct-product
decomposition.  The norm is squaring on \(\mathbb F_q^\times\) and is one
on \(U_D\).  Finally, the definition of \(\Delta_{2h}\) gives one on the
subfield \(\mathbb F_q^\times\), while the identity
\(\Delta_{2h}(x)=M(x)^{2^{2h/3}-1}\) places its image in \(U_D\).
\end{proof}

In particular, the shared prime over two supplies no local norm implication
between the two principal-ray factors.  A proof in either arm must evaluate
the actual image of global units in its own coprime local factor.

The exceptional factors have a further faithful-character localization.
In the maximal real field
\(K=\mathbb Q(\mu_{3^{k+1}})^+\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), the prime over two is
totally inert and
\(G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)=\langle\sigma\rangle\) has order
\(2h\), with \(\sigma=\operatorname{Frob}_2\).  On the first current
\(\ell\)-layer, both factors in \((**)\) lie in the faithful character
\[
 \chi_\ell(\sigma)=2\pmod\ell.
\]
For the cubic step, \(\tau=\sigma^{2h/3}\) acts on this line by a primitive
cube root \(r\), so
\[
 V^{\langle\tau\rangle}=0,
 \qquad (1+\tau+\tau^2)V=(1+r+r^2)V=0
\]
even when \(V\ne0\).  Ambiguous-class, genus, and norm formulas therefore
annihilate precisely the faithful component they would need to control.
Fixed-prime Iwasawa stabilization does not supply a uniform substitute,
because \(\ell\) and its faithful character are born anew at each level.
Equivalently, after localization at this character the entire reduction map
is multiplication by one scalar.  Even if the source circular-unit and class
modules are both free of rank one, multiplication by a unit and multiplication
by \(\ell\) are equally equivariant and obey the same vanishing norm
operators, but only the first is surjective.  Thus a regulator determinant or
Fitting ideal which does not evaluate the marked local scalar merely refactors
the two indices in \((**)\); it cannot decide them.

The same boundary is visible in the most direct Gauss-sum formula.  If
\(K=\mathbb F_Q\) contains \(\mu_\ell\), let \(\chi\) be the order-\(\ell\)
character with kernel \(K^{\times\ell}\), put
\(\psi_a(x)=(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}(ax)}\), and define
\[
 G_\chi(a)=\sum_{x\in K^\times}\chi(x)\psi_a(x).
\]
The substitution \(x\mapsto a^{-1}x\) gives the exact selected formula
\[
 \chi(a)=\frac{G_\chi(1)}{G_\chi(a)}.
\]
For arm \(C\), \(a=\eta_k\) and \(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)=3^k\) is
odd, so no semiprimitive evaluation is available.  For arm \(D\), the
untwisted numerator is semiprimitive because \(2^{3^k}\equiv-1\pmod\ell\),
but evaluating the twisted denominator \(G_\chi(A_k)\) is exactly the
unknown value \(\chi(A_k)\).  In the full cyclotomic Jacobi expansion the
literal translate by four introduces conductor \(5\cdot3^{k+1}\); no power
of two is minus one modulo this conductor, so the mixed terms are not
semiprimitive either.  Explicit reciprocity therefore retains, rather than
eliminates, the marked scalar.

Arul's Jacobi congruence theorem makes this boundary exact
\cite{arul2021}.  At the first nonautomatic order, the Jacobi congruence is
equivalent to \(\ell\)-power residuacity of the finite oriented family
\(\eta_{0,j}\).  Propositions~\ref{prop:c-circular-ray} and
\ref{prop:d-circular-ray} collapse its faithful projection in the present
arms to the single marked line.  More precisely, Arul's
\(j\leftrightarrow f-j\) index relation is homogeneous for
\(0\leq i\leq\ell-3\); its delta terms occur only at
\(i=\ell-2,\ell-1\).  Thus the two conductor-five orientations in arm \(D\),
and the inverse cubic orientations in arm \(C\), preserve a zero first
coefficient.  Frobenius merely rescales it, while on the current character
the ancestry norm has eigenvalue \(1+r+r^2=0\) in \(C\) and
\(1+2^h=0\) in \(D\).  The Jacobi formalism therefore identifies the
missing scalar but does not force it to be nonzero.

Differentiating the norm relation does not manufacture the missing value.
Let \(R=\mathbb Z/\ell^v\mathbb Z\), let the current character send a relative
generator \(\tau\) of order \(r\) to \(a\in R^\times\), and put
\(N_r(T)=1+T+\cdots+T^{r-1}\).  At the birth level \(a\) has exact order
\(r\), so

\[
 N_r(a)=0,
 \qquad
 N_r'(a)=\frac r{a(a-1)}\in R^\times.
\]

The projected circular-unit distribution relation gives only
\(N_r(a)c=0\), because the current character is absent downstairs.  A
first-order lift \(\tau\mapsto a(1+\varepsilon)\) instead gives

\[
 aN_r'(a)c=b,
\]

where \(b\) is the Bockstein, or equivalently the marked localization class
at the current prime.  The derivative is a unit, but \(b\) is not determined
by the lower-level unit.  Indeed every \(c\in R\) extends to such a
first-order distribution system by choosing
\(b=aN_r'(a)c\).  Higher derivatives introduce the corresponding higher
extension classes.  Thus augmentation derivatives identify the selected
scalar with a Selmer connecting class; they do not evaluate it.

The full regulator determinant records exactly the same obstruction.  In a
conjugate circular-unit basis the reduction matrix is circulant.  Its
Fourier eigenvalue at the faithful character \(\sigma\mapsto2\) is the marked
discrete-log scalar.  If \(R_{\mathrm{faithful}}\) denotes the corresponding
principal-ray kernel and \(T_{\mathrm{faithful}}\) the reduced
unit/circular-unit quotient, faithful localization gives

\[
 v_\ell(\lambda)=
 \operatorname{length}(R_{\mathrm{faithful}})
 +\operatorname{length}(T_{\mathrm{faithful}}).
\]

These are respectively the principal-ray and reduced unit/circular-unit
factors in Propositions~\ref{prop:c-circular-ray} and
\ref{prop:d-circular-ray}.  Their nonnegative lengths cannot cancel, but a
real or ordinary cyclotomic regulator controls neither the finite
discrete-log eigenvalue nor its principal-ray factor.  The determinant
therefore packages the conjunction already required; it does not force the
faithful eigenvalue to be a unit.

\begin{proposition}[prime current factor]\label{prop:d-prime}
Let \(k\geq2\), put
\[
 s=3^{k-1},\qquad a=2^s,\qquad
 \Lambda_k=a^2-a+1=3\Psi_k,
\]
and define \(P_k=M_k^{a+1}\).  Then
\[
 P_k^{\Lambda_k}=1,\qquad
 [\mathbb F_2(P_k):\mathbb F_2]=2\cdot3^k,
\]
and exponentiation by \(a+1\) preserves every primary part supported on
\(\Psi_k\).  Consequently \(D'_k\) holds whenever \(\Psi_k\) is prime.
In particular, \(\Psi_2=19\) and \(\Psi_3=87211\) are prime, so
\(D'_2\) and \(D'_3\) hold.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(h=3^k\) and \(q=a^3=2^h\).  Since
\[
 q+1=(a+1)(a^2-a+1),
\]
the norm-one relation \(M_k^{q+1}=1\) gives
\(P_k^{\Lambda_k}=1\).  Moreover,
\(\gcd(\Lambda_k,a+1)=3\) and \(v_3(\Lambda_k)=1\), hence
\(\gcd(\Psi_k,a+1)=1\).  This proves the assertion about primary parts.

The congruences
\[
 a\equiv h-1,\qquad a^2\equiv h+1,\qquad
 a^3\equiv-1,\qquad a^4\equiv2h+1
 \pmod{3h}
\]
give, by direct Frobenius expansion,
\[
 P_k=
 \frac{\zeta^4+\zeta+\omega}
      {\omega^2\zeta^4+\zeta^3+1}.
\]
Also \(P_k^q=P_k^{-1}\).  If \(P_k\in\mathbb F_q\), then \(P_k=1\);
cross-multiplication would give
\[
 \zeta^{h+4}+\zeta^h+\zeta^3+\zeta+1=0.
\]
If \(P_k\in\mathbb F_{a^2}\), its order divides
\(\gcd(\Lambda_k,a^2-1)=3\), so \(P_k\in\{1,\omega,\omega^2\}\).
The two remaining values similarly give
\[
 \zeta^{h+3}+\zeta=0
\]
or
\[
 \zeta^{h+4}+\zeta^{h+3}+\zeta^4+\zeta^3+\zeta+1=0.
\]
Each displayed polynomial is nonzero and has degree below \(2h\), contradicting
the minimal polynomial \(X^{2h}+X^h+1\) of \(\zeta\).

Every proper subfield of \(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}\) lies in
\(\mathbb F_q\) or \(\mathbb F_{a^2}\).  Thus \(P_k\) has degree \(2h\).
If \(\Psi_k\) is prime, its order, a divisor of \(3\Psi_k\), cannot divide
three and must therefore contain \(\Psi_k\).  The primary-part assertion
transfers this divisibility to \(\operatorname{ord}(M_k)\).
\end{proof}

The cubic and exceptional current factors are coprime:
\[
 \gcd\bigl(\Phi_{3^k}(2),\Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)\bigr)=1.
\]
Thus arm \(C\) and arm \(D\) occupy distinct primary coordinates.  Norm
recursion in the cubic tower cannot by itself transfer the missing order from
one arm to the other.  Their common Conway ancestry is relevant only through
the explicitly selected additive coefficients.

\begin{proposition}[common toric cubic dynamics]
\label{prop:cd-toric}
Over a field of characteristic two, for \(z\ne0\) put
\[
 \varpi(z)=z+z^{-1},\qquad D(X)=X^3+X,
 \qquad F_c(U)=U^3+cU^2+cU,
\]
where \(c^2+c=1\).  Then
\[
 D(\varpi(z))=\varpi(z^3),
 \qquad
 F_c(U)=D(U+c)+c^2,
\]
and
\[
 F_{c^2}(F_c(U))=D(D(U+c))+c.
\]
In the literal cubic tower choose alternating \(c_j\in\mathbb F_4\) with
\(c_j^2+c_j=1\) and \(c_{j-1}=c_j^2\), and put
\(u_j=\gamma_j+c_j\).  Then
\[
 F_{c_j}(u_j)=u_{j-1}.
\]
Consequently the selected \(C\) ancestry is the inversion quotient of the
\(3\)-division tree on \(\mathbb G_m\), while this auxiliary recursion used
in the analysis of \(D\) is a two-periodic affine coordinate on the same
toric tree.  This does not identify \(u_k\) with the exceptional target
\(A_k\), whose Kummer class remains the one in
Theorem~\ref{prop:d-exact}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expanding \((z+z^{-1})^3+(z+z^{-1})\) in characteristic two leaves
\(z^3+z^{-3}\).  The second identity is another direct expansion using
\(c^2+c=1\), which also gives \(c^3=1\).  Applying it first with \(c\) and
then with \(c^2\), and using \(c^4=c\), gives the third identity.
Finally, \(\gamma_j^3+\gamma_j=\gamma_{j-1}\), so the second identity gives
\[
 F_{c_j}(u_j)=D(\gamma_j)+c_j^2
 =\gamma_{j-1}+c_{j-1}=u_{j-1}.
\]
\end{proof}

This exact identification rules out direct identification with several
tempting division systems.  The map
\(D\) is not additive:
\[
 D(X+Y)-D(X)-D(Y)=XY(X+Y),
\]
and its degree is three, whereas a nonconstant Drinfeld or Carlitz
endomorphism in characteristic two is an additive polynomial of
power-of-two degree.  Nor is this a smooth elliptic Latt\`es system: infinity
is a totally invariant exceptional point of the polynomial \(D\), while a
finite quotient of a separable elliptic isogeny of degree greater than one has no
exceptional point, since the inverse images upstairs grow with every
iterate and quotient fibres stay bounded.  The relevant algebraic group is
\(\mathbb G_m\), the smooth group of a nodal cubic, not a hidden smooth
elliptic division tower.

The natural characteristic-zero Dickson lift \(X^3-3X\) has the same
semiconjugacy with cubing.  The selected \(C\)-preimages come from roots of
unity and therefore are preperiodic and have canonical height zero.  The
forward-orbit primitive-divisor theorem of Ingram and Silverman assumes an
infinite orbit and therefore does not apply \cite{ingramsilverman2009}.
More fundamentally, these viewpoints still end at the same selected values
\(\Omega_{k,\ell}\); none evaluates them, and the exceptional target remains
separate as stated above.

\section{Boundedness and the remaining theorem}\label{sec:boundedness}

Lenstra asked whether the finite excesses are absolutely bounded
\cite{lenstra1977}.  Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule} would give the bound four,
but boundedness itself is weaker than any maximal-order assertion above.

For \(a\geq0\), let
\[
 C_a=\mathbb F_{2^{2^a}}
\]
be the complete finite Conway subfield at that level.  For an odd prime
\(p\), put \(x_p=\kappa_{f(p)}\) and
\(L_{p,a}=\mathbb F_2(x_p)C_a\).

\begin{proposition}[finite-line criterion for absolute boundedness]
\label{prop:bounded}
For every \(p\) and \(a\),
\[
 m_p<2^{2^a}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{there exists }c\in C_a\text{ such that }
 x_p+c\notin L_{p,a}^{p},
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sup_p m_p<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{for some fixed }a,
 \text{ every }p\text{ has such a }c\in C_a.
\]
\end{proposition}

Here \(L^p=\{y^p:y\in L\}\) for a field \(L\).

\begin{proof}
For \(c\in C_a\), let \(F_c=\mathbb F_2(x_p+c)\).  Since
\(x_p=(x_p+c)+c\), one has \(F_cC_a=L_{p,a}\).  The degree
\([L_{p,a}:F_c]\) divides \([C_a:\mathbb F_2]=2^a\), so it is prime to the
odd prime \(p\).  Extension to \(L_{p,a}\) is therefore injective on
\(p\)-Kummer quotients: restriction followed by norm is multiplication by
this invertible degree.  Thus \(x_p+c\) is a \(p\)-th power in its defining
field exactly when it is a \(p\)-th power in \(L_{p,a}\).

The finite nimbers below \(2^{2^a}\) are exactly \(C_a\), which proves the
first equivalence from the definition of \(m_p\).  A fixed \(a\) gives the
uniform bound \(2^{2^a}-1\).  Conversely, choose \(a\) with
\(2^{2^a}\) larger than a given uniform bound.
\end{proof}

The proposition says that boundedness is uniform nonsaturation of one fixed
finite affine line.  It does not require any of the particular translates
\(0,1,4\) to have maximal primary order.  No fixed line is presently known to
work for all primes.

The universal frontier can now be stated without auxiliary machinery:
\begin{enumerate}
\item prove generation by the synchronized structural norm \(T_h\), including
the Conway--Fermat equalities \(\delta_n=F_n\);
\item prove full primary order for the marked projective unit \(y_{r,a}\) on
every ordinary odd spine;
\item prove that every selected Gaussian period \(\gamma_k\), equivalently
every selected norm-one increment \(\eta_k\), is primitive;
\item prove \(\Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k)\) for every exceptional
level.
\end{enumerate}
Each item is a one-dimensional selected Kummer nonvanishing problem after
the reductions above.  Generic degree, trace, norm, or unmarked reciprocity
data either determine a different coordinate or reconstruct the same
selected coordinate.  They do not supply the missing nonvanishing.

\section{Conclusion}

The finite excess problem is not presently a problem of discovering another
candidate sequence.  Its exact candidate is Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:four-arms} reduces it to four explicit selected-order
assertions.  The reductions explain both the strength of the numerical
pattern and the persistence of the proof gap: all coarse finite-field
invariants are compatible with more than one value of the selected Kummer
coordinate.

The most economical remaining target is therefore direct.  One must prove
nonvanishing of the recursively selected Euler quotient in each of the four
arms, with full primary valuation.  Until those four statements are proved,
the universal \(0/1/4\) rule and even absolute boundedness remain open.
