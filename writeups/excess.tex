\begin{abstract}
Lenstra's construction of ordinal nim multiplication assigns a finite excess
$m_p$ to every odd prime $p$.  We disprove the proposed $0/1/4$ rule determined
by the component support of $f(p)=\operatorname{ord}_p(2)$.  Two literal
Conway towers with the same radicand acquire equal relative quadratic norms
at synchronized levels.  Their selected sum then belongs to the product of
proper-subfield unit groups despite generating the full field.  Every
primitive prime divisor at the resulting degree is a failed zero-excess row.
No numerical search or assumption about random selected elements is needed.

The disproof concerns the piecewise rule; it does not establish an excess
outside $\{0,1,4\}$ or disprove absolute boundedness.  We retain the exact
four-arm reduction into a structural norm, a marked cyclotomic value, a
Gaussian period, and a corrected conductor-five norm.  The first universal
assertion is false; the ordinary, cubic, and exceptional assertions remain
open.  The remaining sections give selected-order bounds, exact norm and
reciprocity reductions, and the independent Conway--Fermat problem.

The proof record distinguishes the paper theorem from its kernel-checked
Lean components.  Lean checks the algebra, synchronization arithmetic,
translation rigidity, and existence of primes at every specified degree;
the literal Conway interpretation and finite-field synthesis remain
paper-level.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Conway's nim addition and multiplication make the ordinals a proper class
field of characteristic two.  Its finite subfields are canonically embedded,
and its first transfinite layers are assembled by explicit Kummer extensions
\cite{conway1976,lenstra1977,lenstra1978}.  Lenstra's description associates
to each odd prime $p$ an element
\[
  \alpha_p=[\kappa_{f(p)}+m_p],
  \qquad f(p)=\operatorname{ord}_p(2),
\]
where $\kappa_{f(p)}$ is determined by the tower and $m_p\in\mathbb N$ is
the \emph{finite excess}.  Brackets around an ordinal sum denote ordinary
ordinal addition; unbracketed addition is nim addition.  The same structure
appears in the description of the ordinal fields $\mathrm{On}_p$
\cite{dimuro2011}.

Let $\mathcal Q(h)$ be Lenstra's component set for $\kappa_h$.  The
proposed rule is the following assertion, disproved in
Section~\ref{sec:counterexample}.

\begin{conjecture}[the proposed $0/1/4$ rule]\label{conj:rule}
For every odd prime $p$,
\[
 m_p=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&\mathcal Q(f(p))\text{ is not a singleton odd prime power},\\
  4,&f(p)=2\cdot3^k\text{ for some }k\geq1,\\
  1,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
\end{conjecture}

The term \emph{selected} is essential.  A finite field contains many
elements having the same degree, trace, norm, or cyclotomic support, whereas
the Conway construction recursively marks one Frobenius orbit.  Its primary
multiplicative order is the relevant datum.  The disproof below retains this
selection at every step, including when two towers share an ancestor.

\begin{thmA}
The universal zero arm $Z$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:arms}, and hence the proposed
$0/1/4$ rule, is false.  More precisely, put
\[
 E_t=36\cdot37^{18t}109^{18t}\qquad(t\geq1).
\]
For every $t\geq1$ and every prime $p$ with
$\operatorname{ord}_p(2)=E_t$ one has
\[
 \mathcal Q(E_t)=\{37^{18t},109^{18t}\},\qquad m_p>0.
\]
Such primes exist at every $E_t$, giving infinitely many failed zero-arm
rows.  The entire primitive-support coordinate vanishes:
$\Delta_{E_t}(\kappa_{E_t})=1$.
\end{thmA}

The proof uses equality of two literal radicands and synchronization of their
norms.  It supplies an ancestry relation that is absent from arguments about
generic elements with prescribed degree, trace, or norm.  Theorem~\ref{thm:equal-norm-translates} shows that every nonzero translate
by the common base field recovers some primitive support.  This does not
settle the full primary order at a specified prime.  The ordinary,
cubic, exceptional, and singleton-even order questions remain meaningful
independently of the failed multicomponent zero arm.

After finite-field preliminaries, Section~\ref{sec:arms} gives the four-arm
equivalence, and Section~\ref{sec:counterexample} gives the disproof.
Sections~\ref{sec:z}--\ref{sec:d} develop one lossless selected
coordinate for each arm.  Section~\ref{sec:nim-reciprocity} proves the
saturation boundary for reciprocity arguments built from one marked phase.
Section~\ref{sec:bounded} separates the failed $0/1/4$ rule from absolute
boundedness, and Section~\ref{sec:verification} records the authority of every
claim class used in the paper.

\section{Finite-field preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

For a finite algebraic nimber $x$, write
\[
  d(x)=[\mathbb F_2(x):\mathbb F_2].
\]
The element $\kappa_h$ is the sum of the selected components
$\kappa_q$, $q\in\mathcal Q(h)$, and $h\mid d(\kappa_h)$.  The excess has
the equivalent form
\[
  m_p=\min\{m\geq0:[\kappa_{f(p)}+m]
      \text{ is not a $p$-th power in its defining finite field}\}.
\]
This ordinal excess need not equal a nim-translation index.  Write
$\kappa_{f(p)}=[\lambda_p+k_p]$, where $\lambda_p$ is zero or a limit
ordinal and $k_p$ is finite.  Then the nim translate giving $\alpha_p$ has
index $k_p\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}[k_p+m_p]$.  It equals $m_p$ when $k_p=0$,
as in the singleton odd-component arms.  In every case $m_p=0$ exactly when
$\kappa_{f(p)}$ itself is a nonpower.  Finite integers are identified with
their canonical finite nimbers.  We use
standard facts about finite fields and Capelli composition
\cite{lidlniederreiter1997}.

\begin{proposition}[exact power criterion]\label{prop:power}
Let $p$ be prime, let $p\mid2^E-1$, and let
$x\in\mathbb F_{2^E}^{\times}$.  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $x$ is a $p$-th power;
\item $x^{(2^E-1)/p}=1$;
\item $\operatorname{ord}(x)\mid(2^E-1)/p$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, $x$ is not a $p$-th power if and only if
\[
  v_p(\operatorname{ord}(x))=v_p(2^E-1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose a generator $g$ of the cyclic multiplicative group and write
$x=g^a$.  Solvability of $py\equiv a\pmod{2^E-1}$, the displayed power
test, and the order divisibility are equivalent.  The final statement is
their $p$-primary form.
\end{proof}

The valuation cannot be replaced by the weaker assertion
$p\mid\operatorname{ord}(x)$.  If $h=f(p)$ and $h\mid E$, lifting the
exponent gives
\[
  v_p(2^E-1)=v_p(2^h-1)+v_p(E/h).
\]
Every order assertion below retains the full right-hand valuation.

\begin{proposition}[norm reduction]\label{prop:norm}
Let $h\mid E$, let $p\mid2^h-1$, and let
$x\in\mathbb F_{2^E}^{\times}$.  Then
\[
 x^{(2^E-1)/p}
 =\operatorname N_{\mathbb F_{2^E}/\mathbb F_{2^h}}(x)^{(2^h-1)/p}.
\]
Thus $x$ is a $p$-th power in $\mathbb F_{2^E}$ if and only if its norm is
a $p$-th power in $\mathbb F_{2^h}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The norm exponent is $(2^E-1)/(2^h-1)$.  Multiply it by
$(2^h-1)/p$ and apply Proposition~\ref{prop:power}.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact four-arm reduction}\label{sec:arms}

For $h>1$, put
\[
 \Pi_h=
 \prod_{\substack{\ell\text{ odd prime}\\
                    \operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(2)=h}}
 \ell^{v_{\ell}(2^h-1)},
 \qquad
 T_h=\operatorname N_{\mathbb F_{2^{d(\kappa_h)}}/
                            \mathbb F_{2^h}}(\kappa_h).
\]
Thus $\Pi_h$ is the full primary part supported at primes first occurring at
residue degree $h$.

For an odd prime $r$, let $b_r=d(\alpha_r)$.  Put
$x_{r,0}=\alpha_r$, and for $a\geq1$ put
\[
 x_{r,a}=\kappa_{r^a}.
\]
For every $a\geq0$, define
\[
 F_{r,a}=\mathbb F_{2^{r^ab_r}},\qquad
 y_{r,a}=x_{r,a}+1,
\]
and for $a\geq1$ define
\[
 G_{r,a}=F_{r,a}^{\times}/F_{r,a-1}^{\times},
 \qquad
 L_{r,a}=|G_{r,a}|=\Phi_{r^a}(2^{b_r}).
\]

For $k\geq1$, set $h=3^k$, let $\zeta_k=\kappa_{3^k}$, and put
\[
 \gamma_k=\zeta_k+\zeta_k^{-1}\in\mathbb F_{2^h}^{\times}.
\]
Finally, let $q=2^h$, let $\omega=\zeta_k^h\in\mathbb F_4$, and define
\[
 A_k=\zeta_k^2+\zeta_k+\omega,
 \qquad M_k=A_k^{q-1},
 \qquad \Psi_k=\frac{\Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)}3.
\]

\begin{theorem}[four-arm reduction]\label{thm:arms}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule} is equivalent to the conjunction of the following
four universal assertions.
\begin{description}
\item[$Z$.] If $\mathcal Q(h)$ is not a singleton odd prime power, then
      $\Pi_h\mid\operatorname{ord}(T_h)$.
\item[$O$.] If $r\ne3$ is odd, $a\geq1$, and $p\ne r$ is a prime
      divisor of $L_{r,a}$, then
      \[
      v_p\!\left(\operatorname{ord}
       (y_{r,a}F_{r,a-1}^{\times})\right)=v_p(L_{r,a}).
      \]
\item[$C$.] The element $\gamma_k$ is primitive in
      $\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}^{\times}$ for every $k\geq1$.
\item[$D$.] One has
      $\Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k)$ for every $k\geq1$.
\end{description}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $p$ and put $h=f(p)$.  If $\mathcal Q(h)$ is not a singleton odd prime
power, Proposition~\ref{prop:z-support} below identifies $m_p=0$ with the
corresponding primary factor of $Z$.

If $\mathcal Q(h)=\{r^a\}$ with $r$ odd, Lenstra's component formulas give
$h=r^as$ with $s\mid b_r$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:o-projective} identifies
the row primes with $p\ne r$ dividing $L_{r,a}$ and identifies $m_p=1$
with assertion $O$.

When $r=3$, one has $b_3=2$.  The cases are $h=3^a$ and
$h=2\cdot3^a$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:c-half} identifies the former with
$C$, while Theorem~\ref{thm:d-target} identifies the latter with $D$.
These cases are disjoint and exhaustive.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:arms} is an equivalence, not a proof of its four assertions.
The next section disproves $Z$.  Assertions $O,C,D$ remain open.
\end{remark}

\section{Synchronized towers disprove the zero arm}\label{sec:counterexample}

The literal tower identities used here are
\[
 \kappa_{r^a}^{r^a}=\alpha_r,\qquad
 d(\kappa_{r^a})=r^a d(\alpha_r),
\]
for an odd prime $r$ and $a\geq1$.  In particular,
$X^{r^a}-\alpha_r$ is the minimal polynomial of $\kappa_{r^a}$ over
$\mathbb F_2(\alpha_r)$.  These are Conway--Lenstra tower facts; see
Lenstra's equations (1.6)--(1.8) \cite{lenstra1977} and
DiMuro's Theorem~3.1 and Lemma~3.9 \cite{dimuro2011}.  The argument never
replaces these components by roots chosen independently.

\begin{proposition}[the two common-radicand seed rows]
\label{prop:z-shared-seeds}
In the literal Conway tower,
\[
 f(37)=f(109)=36,\qquad
 \mathcal Q(36)=\{4,9\},\qquad
 m_{37}=m_{109}=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \alpha_{37}=\alpha_{109}=\kappa_9+\kappa_4,
 \qquad d(\alpha_{37})=d(\alpha_{109})=36.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $y=\kappa_9$, $c=y^3=\kappa_3$, and $x=\kappa_4=4$.
The Conway equations give
\[
 y^9=2,\qquad c^3=2,\qquad x^2+x=2=c^3.
\]
Here $2$ is the primitive cube root in $\mathbb F_4$. Thus $y$ and $c$
have multiplicative orders $27$ and $9$, respectively. Since the orders of
two modulo $27$ and $9$ are $18$ and $6$, their absolute degrees are
$18$ and $6$. In particular $c$ has minimal polynomial
\[
 C(T)=T^6+T^3+1
\]
over $\mathbb F_2$. Also $x$ has absolute degree four. The component-set
recursion therefore gives $\kappa_{36}=y+x$: the nine-component supplies
degree $18$, which is not divisible by four, so the four-component is added.

Let $K=\mathbb F_{64}$ and put $q=64$. Then $K=\mathbb F_2(c)$,
$K(x)=\mathbb F_{q^2}$, $K(y)=\mathbb F_{q^3}$, and their intersection is
$K$. Their compositum is $L=\mathbb F_{q^6}=\mathbb F_{2^{36}}$.
The products
\[
 1,\ y,\ y^2,\ x,\ xy,\ xy^2
\]
form a $K$-basis of $L$. The degree of $z=x+y$ is exactly $36$:
the prime-power factor $4$ of $d(x)$ is absent from $d(y)=18$, and the
prime-power factor $9$ of $d(y)$ is absent from $d(x)=4$; the degree-sum
lemma forces both factors into $d(z)$, while $d(z)$ divides $36$.

Consider the multiplicative subgroup
\[
 H=K(x)^\times K(y)^\times\subset L^\times.
\]
Its index is
\[
 [L^\times:H]
 =\frac{(q^6-1)(q-1)}{(q^2-1)(q^3-1)}
 =q^2-q+1=4033=37\cdot109.
\]
Every element of $H$ has a rank-one coefficient matrix in the displayed
product basis: multiplication of $a_0+a_1x$ by
$b_0+b_1y+b_2y^2$ gives the outer product
$(a_0,a_1)^{\mathsf T}(b_0,b_1,b_2)$.

Reduction using only
$c^6+c^3+1=0$, $x^2+x=c^3$, and $y^3=c$ gives the following coefficient
matrices, with rows indexed by $1,x$ and columns by $1,y,y^2$:
\[
 [z^{37}]=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1+c^2+c^3 & c^3+c^4 & 0\\
 c^2 & c+c^3+c^4 & c
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
 [z^{109}]=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1+c+c^3 & c+c^3+c^4+c^5 & 1+c^2+c^3+c^5\\
 1+c+c^2+c^4 & c^2+c^3+c^4 & c^2+c^3+c^4+c^5
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The minors in their first two columns reduce, respectively, to
\[
 1+c^3+c^4,\qquad c^2+c^3+c^4+c^5.
\]
Both are nonzero, since they are values at $c$ of nonzero binary
polynomials of degree less than six. Hence neither $z^{37}$ nor
$z^{109}$ belongs to $H$. The cyclic quotient $L^\times/H$ has order
$37\cdot109$, so $zH$ has that full order.

For completeness, the fixed elementary congruences
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{37}(2)=\operatorname{ord}_{109}(2)=36
\]
show that neither seed prime divides $|K(x)^\times|=2^{12}-1$ or
$|K(y)^\times|=2^{18}-1$. Their valuations in $|L^\times|$ are therefore
exactly their valuations in $[L^\times:H]$, namely one. The full order of
$zH$ proves that $z$ is neither a $37$-th nor a $109$-th power in its
defining field $L$. The defining excess criterion, together with
$\kappa_{36}=z$, gives $m_{37}=m_{109}=0$ and the stated equality of the
two radicands.

The two matrix expansions and minor reductions are the ordinary
kernel-checked identities \texttt{z36\_expansions},
\texttt{z36\_expansion\_109}, \texttt{z36\_minor\_37}, and
\texttt{z36\_minor\_109} in \texttt{Excess.lean}. The fixed seed primality
and residue-degree statements are checked in \texttt{ExcessSeed.lean}.
The finite-field compositum, tensor-basis, subgroup-index, and Conway
component identifications in this proof remain paper-level arguments.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[odd binomials prevent cancellation of the shared base]
\label{lem:equal-norm-degree}
Let $b\geq1$, let $K=\mathbb F_{2^b}$, and let $R,S>1$ be coprime odd integers, and let
$x,y$ have respective degrees $bR,bS$ over $\mathbb F_2$.  Suppose
$x^R=y^S=\alpha\in K$ and $X^R-\alpha$ is the minimal polynomial of $x$
over $K$.  Then $d(x+y)=bRS$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two component fields intersect in $K$.  If an absolute Frobenius
$\sigma$ fixes $x+y$, then
\[
 c=\sigma(x)+x=\sigma(y)+y\in K.
\]
The polynomial
\[
 (X+c)^R+X^R+\sigma(\alpha)+\alpha\in K[X]
\]
vanishes at $x$ and has degree less than $R$, so it is zero.  Its coefficient
of $X^{R-1}$ is $Rc=c$, since $R$ is odd.  Thus $c=0$ and $\sigma$ fixes
both components.  A Frobenius power fixing the sum therefore has exponent
divisible by both $bR$ and $bS$, hence by $bRS$.  The reverse divisibility
follows from the compositum.  In particular $x+y\ne0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[equal half-norms erase primitive support]
\label{lem:equal-norm-support}
Let $E>0$ be even, let $Q=2^{E/2}$, and let $x,y,w$ be nonzero elements of
$\mathbb F_{2^E}$, with $x\ne y$, satisfying
\[
 x^{Q+1}=y^{Q+1}=w.
\]
If $x$ and $y$ belong to proper subfields, then $x+y$ belongs to the product
of the multiplicative groups of proper subfields.  In particular every prime
$p$ with $\operatorname{ord}_p(2)=E$ is absent from
$\operatorname{ord}(x+y)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $\rho=x/y$.  Equal norms give $\rho^Q=\rho^{-1}$, so
$u=\rho+\rho^{-1}$ is a nonzero element of $\mathbb F_{2^{E/2}}$.
Characteristic two gives
\[
 (x+y)^2=xyu.
\]
Each factor on the right belongs to a proper-subfield unit group.  Squaring
is an automorphism of every such group, and the square root in
$\mathbb F_{2^E}$ is unique.  Hence $x+y$ itself belongs to their product.
A prime of residue degree $E$ divides no proper-subfield unit-group order,
proving the last assertion.  Equivalently, the same calculation yields
\[
 (x+y)^{Q-1}=\frac{w}{xy}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[literal synchronized counterexample family]
\label{thm:equal-norm-counterexample}
For every positive integer $t$, put $a=18t$, $R=37^a$, $S=109^a$, and $E=36RS$.  Then
\[
 \mathcal Q(E)=\{R,S\},\qquad d(\kappa_E)=E,\qquad
 \kappa_E\in
 H_E:=\prod_{\substack{q\mid E\\q\text{ prime}}}
              \mathbb F_{2^{E/q}}^\times.
\]
For every prime $p$ with $\operatorname{ord}_p(2)=E$ one has $m_p>0$,
although the proposed rule requires $m_p=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The seed primes satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{37}(2)=\operatorname{ord}_{109}(2)=36,
 \qquad \mathcal Q(36)=\{4,9\}.
\]
Indeed $2^{18}=-1$ and $2^{12}\ne1$ modulo either prime.  The element
$\zeta=\kappa_9$ has order $27$ and degree $18$, while $\kappa_4=4$ has
degree $4$.  Their sum
\[
 \alpha=\zeta+4
\]
has degree $36$: the prime-power factors $9$ and $4$ cannot cancel under
addition, because each is absent from the other summand's degree
(Lenstra's Lemma~2.5 \cite{lenstra1977}, also DiMuro's Lemma~4.2
\cite{dimuro2011}).  Proposition~\ref{prop:z-shared-seeds} therefore
gives $\alpha_{37}=\alpha_{109}=\alpha$ and $b_{37}=b_{109}=36$.

Put $M=2^{18}-1=27\cdot7\cdot19\cdot73$.  One has
\[
 37^{18}\equiv109^{18}\equiv1\pmod M,
 \qquad R\equiv S\equiv1\pmod M.
\]
For completeness these congruences have a short proof.  Modulo $27$, the
two bases are $10$ and $1$, and $10^3=1$.  Modulo $7$ they are $2$ and $4$,
both cube roots of one.  Modulo $19$, Fermat's theorem gives the eighteenth
powers.  Modulo $73$ they are $2^{-1}$ and $-2^{-1}$, and $2^9=1$.
The Chinese remainder theorem and then powering by $t$ give the claims.

Let $x=\kappa_R$ and $y=\kappa_S$.  Their literal Kummer relations and
degrees are
\[
 x^R=y^S=\alpha,\qquad d(x)=36R,\qquad d(y)=36S.
\]
Define the common base half-norm
\[
 w=\alpha^{2^{18}+1}\in\mathbb F_{2^{18}}^\times.
\]
The half-norm
$v_x=x^{2^{18R}+1}$ belongs to $\mathbb F_{2^{18R}}^\times$ and satisfies
\[
 v_x^R=\alpha^{2^{18R}+1}=w,
\]
because $R$ is odd.  Since $36\nmid18R$, the prime $37$ does not divide
$2^{18R}-1$, so the $R$-th power map on this half-field is bijective.
On the other hand $w^R=w$, by $R\equiv1\pmod M$.  Uniqueness of the
$R$-th root gives $v_x=w$.  The identical argument gives
$y^{2^{18S}+1}=w$.

In the compositum $\mathbb F_{2^E}$, set $Q=2^{18RS}$.  Since the other
tower degree is odd, its half-Frobenius restricts to the half-Frobenius of
each component field.  Consequently
\[
 x^{Q+1}=y^{Q+1}=w.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:equal-norm-degree} gives $d(x+y)=E$.  The greedy
component formula (Lenstra's Theorem~2.1 \cite{lenstra1977}, or DiMuro's
Theorem~4.3 \cite{dimuro2011}) now selects $S$, then
$R$, and omits $9$ and $4$, which already divide the degree of the selected
sum.  Thus $\mathcal Q(E)=\{R,S\}$ and $\kappa_E=x+y$.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:equal-norm-support}.  More precisely, its three factors
$x,y,u$ lie in subfields of degrees $E/109$, $E/37$, and $E/2$, respectively,
so $\kappa_E\in H_E$.  Every prime $p$ of residue degree $E$ is therefore
absent from the order of $\kappa_E$.  It follows that $\kappa_E$ is a
$p$-th power in its own defining field $\mathbb F_{2^E}$ and $m_p>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[prime divisors at the specified degrees]
\label{lem:equal-norm-primitive}
For $a\geq1$ and $E=36\cdot37^a109^a$, the integer $\Phi_E(2)$ is greater
than one, and every prime divisor $p$ of $\Phi_E(2)$ satisfies
$\operatorname{ord}_p(2)=E$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every primitive complex $E$-th root $\xi$, one has $|2-\xi|>1$;
thus $\Phi_E(2)>1$.  The value is odd.  The cyclotomic divisor criterion
says that a prime divisor $p$ either has residue degree $E$, or divides
$E$ and satisfies $E=\operatorname{ord}_p(2)p^j$ for some $j\geq1$.
In the second case $p$ is $3$, $37$, or $109$.  Their respective orders are
$2$, $36$, and $36$, and none of $2\cdot3^j$, $36\cdot37^j$, or
$36\cdot109^j$ equals $E$.  The second case is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[disproof of the universal rule]
\label{cor:rule-false}
The universal assertion $Z$ and Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule} are false,
with infinitely many failing zero-arm rows.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:equal-norm-counterexample} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:equal-norm-primitive} supply a failing prime at every $E_t$.
These primes are distinct for distinct $t$, because their residue degrees
are distinct.  Each of these rows directly contradicts the proposed zero-excess clause.
\end{proof}

This is an existence proof for the literal Conway tower.  It requires no
factorization of $2^{E_t}-1$ or enumeration of rows; the two seed equalities
have the symbolic proof above.  The conclusion $m_p>0$ does
not determine the first successful translate and does not imply
$m_p\notin\{0,1,4\}$ or unbounded excess.

Four Lean modules check these steps:
\texttt{ExcessEqualNorm}, \texttt{ExcessKummerDegree},
\texttt{ExcessSeed}, and \texttt{ExcessPrimitive}. They verify the equal-norm
identity and power transfer, odd Kummer norm synchronization, binomial
translation obstruction, arithmetic for every level, and existence of the
required row primes.  The
finite-field subfield synthesis and literal Conway identification in this
theorem are paper-level arguments, not one end-to-end Lean theorem.

\subsection{Nonzero translates and the remaining primary-order question}

\begin{theorem}[nonzero translates recover primitive support]
\label{thm:equal-norm-translates}
Retain the synchronized family of
Theorem~\ref{thm:equal-norm-counterexample}, and put
$K=\mathbb F_{2^{36}}$.  For every $c\in K^\times$,
\[
 \kappa_E+c\notin H_E.
\]
Thus the zero translate is the unique point of this base-field affine line
whose entire primitive-support coordinate vanishes.  Nonzero support does
not assert full primary order at every prime of residue degree $E$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $x=\kappa_R$, $y=\kappa_S$, and $L=\mathbb F_{2^E}$.  We have
$x^R=y^S=\alpha\in K^\times$, and the monomials
$x^iy^j$, $0\leq i<R$, $0\leq j<S$, form a $K$-basis of $L$:
the component degrees over $K$ are the coprime integers $R,S$.
For $q\in\{2,3,37,109\}$ set
\[
 \tau_q=\operatorname{Frob}^{E/q},\qquad
 \delta_q(z)=z/\tau_q(z).
\]
These multiplicative operators commute.  Every factor of $H_E$ is killed
by its corresponding $\delta_q$, so membership in $H_E$ implies
annihilation by their product.  Only this implication is needed here.

The top Kummer actions are
\[
 \tau_{37}(x)=\lambda x,\quad \tau_{37}(y)=y,
 \qquad
 \tau_{109}(x)=x,\quad \tau_{109}(y)=\mu y,
\]
where $\lambda,\mu\in K$ have orders $37,109$, respectively.
Indeed $[K(x^{37}):K]=R/37$, and $\tau_{37}$ restricts to an
automorphism of order $37$ on $K(x)$; all $37$-th roots of unity lie in
$K$.  The other tower is identical.  Equal half-norms give
$\tau_2(x)=w/x$ and $\tau_2(y)=w/y$ with $w\in K^\times$.
Put $d=\tau_2(c)\in K^\times$ and introduce the rational function
\[
 r(X,Y)=\frac{D(X,Y)}{N(X,Y)},\qquad
 D=X+Y+c,\qquad N=w(X+Y)+dXY.
\]
Then $\delta_2(x+y+c)=xy\,r(x,y)$, and the factor $xy$ disappears
under $\delta_{37}\delta_{109}$.  Consequently
\[
 \delta_{37}\delta_{109}\delta_2(x+y+c)=F(x,y),\qquad
 F(X,Y)=\frac{r(X,Y)r(\lambda X,\mu Y)}
                  {r(\lambda X,Y)r(X,\mu Y)}.
\]
Writing $D_{ij}=D(\lambda^iX,\mu^jY)$ and similarly for $N$, we have
\[
 F=P/Q,\qquad
 P=D_{00}D_{11}N_{10}N_{01},\qquad
 Q=N_{00}N_{11}D_{10}D_{01}.
\]
Both $P$ and $Q$ have degree at most four in each variable.  All their
factors evaluate nontrivially at $(x,y)$ by the displayed tensor basis.
If $x+y+c\in H_E$, then, with $\sigma=\tau_3$,
\[
 \sigma(F(x,y))=F(x,y).
\]

Set $n=2^{E/3}$ and choose $u=n\bmod R$, $v=n\bmod S$.  The literal
Kummer equations give
\[
 \sigma(x)=Ax^u,\qquad \sigma(y)=By^v,\qquad A,B\in K^\times.
\]
Moreover
\[
 u^2+u+1\equiv0\pmod R,\qquad
 v^2+v+1\equiv0\pmod S.
\]
For the first congruence, the standard order bound
$\operatorname{ord}_{37^a}(2)\mid36\cdot37^{a-1}$ implies $u^3=1$
modulo $R$.  But $u\ne1\pmod{37}$, since $36\nmid E/3=12RS$.
Thus $u-1$ is invertible modulo $R$, and the cubic factors as required.
The argument for $S$ is the same.  On coefficients, $\sigma$ acts as
$\operatorname{Frob}^{12}$ on $K$, since $RS\equiv1\pmod3$.

The map $(i,j)\mapsto i+uj\pmod R$ is injective for $0\leq i,j\leq4$.
In fact a collision gives $h+uk\equiv0\pmod R$ with $|h|,|k|\leq4$,
and hence
\[
 h^2-hk+k^2\equiv(h+uk)(h+u^2k)\equiv0\pmod R.
\]
The last integer lies between zero and $48<R$, so it vanishes and
$h=k=0$.  The same holds for $v,S$.  Therefore the monomials
\[
 x^{i+uj}y^{k+v\ell},\qquad 0\leq i,j,k,\ell\leq4,
\]
are $K$-linearly independent: reduction modulo $R,S$ only multiplies
distinct tensor-basis vectors by nonzero powers of $\alpha$.

Write $P=\sum p_{ik}X^iY^k$ and $Q=\sum q_{ik}X^iY^k$.
Clearing denominators in this equality and retaining
the coefficient action of $\sigma$ gives
\[
 P(x,y)Q^\sigma(Ax^u,By^v)
   =Q(x,y)P^\sigma(Ax^u,By^v).
\]
The preceding independence implies, for all indices,
\[
 p_{ik}\sigma(q_{j\ell})A^jB^\ell
   =q_{ik}\sigma(p_{j\ell})A^jB^\ell.
\]
Choosing a nonzero coefficient of $Q$ shows that $P$ and $Q$ are
proportional.  Thus $F$ must be constant as a rational function.

This is impossible.  The prime polynomial $D_{00}=X+Y+c$ divides $P$
but none of the four factors of $Q$.  To check the latter claim, substitute
$Y=X+c$.  The factors $D_{10}$ and $D_{01}$ become
$(\lambda+1)X$ and $(\mu+1)(X+c)$, both nonzero.  The restrictions of
$N_{00}$ and $N_{11}$ have nonzero quadratic coefficients $d$ and
$d\lambda\mu$, respectively.  Thus $D_{00}\nmid Q$, contradicting
constancy and proving the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[a first-translate or Wieferich alternative]
\label{cor:equal-norm-first-translate}
At every synchronized degree $E$, either some prime $p$ with
$\operatorname{ord}_p(2)=E$ has $m_p=1$, or there is a prime of that
residue degree satisfying $p^2\mid2^{p-1}-1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply the theorem to $c=1$.  The nonidentity class
$(\kappa_E+1)H_E$ has order divisible by some prime $p$ of residue
degree $E$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:equal-norm-primitive} and the index
$[L^\times:H_E]=\Phi_E(2)$.  The finite tail of $\kappa_E$ is zero,
so its ordinal first translate is its nim sum with $1$.
This translate has degree $E$, since every proper-subfield unit lies in
$H_E$.
If $m_p\ne1$, the already-proved $m_p>0$ means that this first translate
is still a $p$-th power.  Its positive $p$-primary order is then strictly
smaller than that of $L^\times$, forcing $p^2\mid2^E-1$.
Since $E\mid p-1$, this implies $p^2\mid2^{p-1}-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[primitive support in both coprime factors]
\label{thm:equal-norm-two-factors}
Fix $t\geq1$ and put $a=18t$, $R=37^a$, $S=109^a$, $E=36RS$,
$K=\mathbb F_{2^{36}}$, and $F=\mathbb F_{2^E}$. Let $x,y\in F$ satisfy the
synchronized-family identities
\[
 x^R=y^S=\alpha\in K^\times,\qquad
 [K(x):K]=R,\quad [K(y):K]=S,\qquad
 x^{2^{E/2}+1}=y^{2^{E/2}+1}=w\in K^\times.
\]
In particular these hypotheses hold for the literal components
$x=\kappa_R$, $y=\kappa_S$ with $\alpha=\kappa_9+4$.
Define
\[
 d=E/4=9RS,\quad q=2^d,\quad \theta=2^{(d+1)/2},\quad
 A_\pm=q+1\pm\theta.
\]
Put $\tau_p(u)=u^{2^{E/p}}$ for primes $p\mid E$, and let $\Delta_E=\prod_{p\in\{2,3,37,109\}}(\tau_p-1)$ act
multiplicatively, so $(\tau_p-1)(u)=\tau_p(u)/u$.
For every $b\in K^\times$, put $h=b+x+y$. Then $h\ne0$, and for
$D=\Delta_E(h)$ one has
\[
                    D^{A_-}\ne1,\qquad D^{A_+}\ne1.
\]
Consequently $h$ has nonzero primitive-degree multiplicative support in
both coprime factors $A_-$ and $A_+$ of $q^2+1$. This asserts neither full
primary order at any specified prime nor the value of the excess.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coprime extension degrees make $\{x^i y^j:0\leq i<R,\ 0\leq j<S\}$
a $K$-basis of $F$. In particular $h\ne0$, and $x^n\in K$ implies
$R\mid n$ (reduce $n$ modulo $R$ and use the binomial basis); likewise
for $y,S$. Thus $R,S\mid q^2+1$. Since $R,S\equiv1\pmod8$,
$d\equiv9\pmod{72}$; using the binary order $36$ of both seed primes gives
\[
 (q,\theta)\equiv(-6,-5)\pmod{37},\qquad
 (q,\theta)\equiv(76,32)\pmod{109}.
\]
Now $A_-A_+=q^2+1$ and $\gcd(A_-,A_+)=1$: their difference is the power
of two $2\theta$, and both are odd. Hence $R\mid A_-$ and $S\mid A_+$.

Put $X=x^q$, $Y=y^q$, $W=w^q$, $T=w^\theta$, and
\[
 \lambda=x^{-A_-}\in K^\times,\qquad
 \mu=y^{A_+}\in K^\times.
\]
Thus $x^\theta=\lambda xX$ and $y^\theta=\mu/(yY)$. Write the six Frobenius images as
\begin{align*}
 H_0&=b+x+y,& H_1&=b^q+X+Y,\\
 H_2&=b^{q^2}+w/x+w/y,& H_3&=b^{q^3}+W/X+W/Y,\\
 J_0&=b^\theta+\lambda xX+\mu/(yY),&
 J_2&=b^{q^2\theta}+T/(\lambda xX)+(T/\mu)yY.
\end{align*}
For $\sigma=\tau_2$ and $v=\sigma(h)/h$, we obtain
\[
 L_-:=v^{A_-}=\frac{H_3H_2J_0}{H_1H_0J_2},\qquad
 L_+:=v^{A_+}=\frac{H_3H_2J_2}{H_1H_0J_0}.
\]
Indeed $H_j=h^{q^j}$, $J_0=h^\theta$, and $J_2=\sigma(h)^\theta$;
the last identity uses
$(x^\theta)^{q^2+1}=(y^\theta)^{q^2+1}=T$.

We first treat $x,X,y,Y$ as independent Laurent variables over $K$.
The automorphism $\tau_{37}$ fixes $K(y)$ and has order $37$ on $K(x)$.
Since $K(x^{37})/K$ has degree $R/37$, it fixes $x^{37}$. Therefore
$\tau_{37}(x)=\xi x$ for a nonidentity $37$th root $\xi\in K$.
Similarly $\tau_{109}(y)=\eta y$ for a nonidentity $109$th root
$\eta\in K$, and $\tau_{109}$ fixes $K(x)$. Their formal actions are
\[
 g_{37}(x,X,y,Y)=(\xi x,\xi^qX,y,Y),\qquad
 g_{109}(x,X,y,Y)=(x,X,\eta y,\eta^qY).
\]
For either $L=L_-$ or $L=L_+$, set
\[
             J=\frac{L\,g_{37}g_{109}(L)}{g_{37}(L)g_{109}(L)}.
\]
The Laurent prime $P=b+x+y$ has valuation $-1$ in $J$. To see this,
substitute $y=b+x$. A scaled copy of $h$ becomes
$b(1+\eta^j)+(\xi^i+\eta^j)x$, which vanishes identically only for
$i=j=0$. A scaled copy of $\sigma(h)$, after clearing its monomial
denominator, has nonzero $x^2$ coefficient $b^{q^2}$. The two factors involving
only $X,Y$ are nonzero, and each remaining factor has a nonzero
$X$ or $X^{-1}$ coefficient. Thus the sole occurrence of $P$ is the
unscaled denominator $h$, with multiplicity one.

Set $s=2^{E/3}$. Introduce four further independent variables
$u,U,z,Z$ representing $x^s,X^s,y^s,Y^s$, and let $J_s$ be obtained by
raising every coefficient of $J$ to the $s$th power and making that
variable replacement. Then $G=J_s/J$ has $P$-valuation $1$, hence is
not identically one. Upon evaluation it is respectively $D^{A_-}$ or
$D^{A_+}$. This uses the actual four Frobenius differences; no unsynchronized
choice of conjugates is made.

It remains to justify that evaluation cannot erase this rational
nonidentity. Each $L$ has three factors on each side, and each factor
has every individual Laurent exponent in $\{-1,0,1\}$. Hence $J$ has
at most $12$ factors per side and $G=N/M$ at most $24$. Without cancelling,
$N-M$ is a nonzero Laurent polynomial whose exponent vectors lie in
$[-24,24]^8$; differences of support vectors have all coordinates of
absolute value at most $48$. Its evaluated denominators are nonzero,
being actual Frobenius images of $h$.

We prove injectivity on this support. The substitutions are
\[
 X=x^q,\quad u=x^s,\quad U=x^{qs},\qquad
 Y=y^q,\quad z=y^s,\quad Z=y^{qs}.
\]
We have $q^2\equiv-1$ modulo both $R,S$. Also $s^3\equiv1$, while
$s\equiv2^{12}\not\equiv1$ modulo both seed primes, because
$RS\equiv1\pmod3$. Thus $s^2+s+1\equiv0$ modulo $R,S$.
Suppose
\[
 b_0+b_1q+b_2s+b_3qs\equiv0\pmod R,\qquad |b_j|\leq48.
\]
Let $i^2=-1$ and let $\omega$ be a primitive complex cube root.
The algebraic integer
$\beta=b_0+b_1i+b_2\omega+b_3i\omega$ has four conjugates, given
by independently replacing $i$ with $\pm i$ and $\omega$ with
$\omega,\omega^2$. Their product is an integer divisible by $R$:
substitute $q,s$ modulo $R$ in that product, using their two defining
relations, and the displayed congruence annihilates one factor.
If $(b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3)\ne0$, the basis
$1,i,\omega,i\omega$ of $\mathbb Q(i,\omega)$ shows that $\beta\ne0$, whereas
\[
 0<|N_{\mathbb Q(i,\omega)/\mathbb Q}(\beta)|
 \leq\Bigl(\sum_j|b_j|\Bigr)^4\leq192^4<37^{18}\leq R,
\]
a contradiction. The same argument holds modulo $S$. Distinct Laurent
support vectors therefore reduce to distinct basis monomials $x^i y^j$,
so $N-M$ cannot vanish. This proves both asserted inequalities.

Finally, $\Delta_E$ has kernel
$\prod_{p\mid E}\mathbb F_{2^{E/p}}^\times$ and image of order $\Phi_E(2)$,
by the cyclic-group order calculation for maximal-subfield differences.
Every prime divisor $\ell$ of $\Phi_E(2)$ has binary order $E$: the
cyclotomic exception would require
$E=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)\ell^j$ with $\ell\in\{3,37,109\}$, whose respective
orders are $2,36,36$, and none is possible here. Thus $D$ has only
primitive-degree primary support. Its order divides $q^2+1=A_-A_+$.
If that support lay wholly in either factor, the corresponding power
of $D$ would be one, contrary to the inequalities. This proves the
stated interpretation without asserting full primary valuations.
\end{proof}

\section{The zero arm: structural norms and Fermat packets}\label{sec:z}

\subsection{Synchronized structural norm}

\begin{proposition}[exact support criterion]\label{prop:z-support}
Let $E=d(\kappa_h)$.  For an odd prime $p$ with $f(p)=h$,
\[
 m_p=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_p(\operatorname{ord}(T_h))=v_p(2^h-1).
\]
Consequently every such row has excess zero if and only if
$\Pi_h\mid\operatorname{ord}(T_h)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the defining excess formula, $m_p=0$ exactly when $\kappa_h$ is not a $p$-th
power.  Proposition~\ref{prop:norm} moves the test to $T_h$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:power} gives the primary valuation.
\end{proof}

When $\kappa_h$ has several components, $T_h$ is not determined by separate
component norms.  Their common embedding carries a synchronized Frobenius
phase.  The following quotient removes every proper-subfield ambiguity while
retaining that phase.

\begin{proposition}[cubical primitive-support coordinate]
\label{prop:cube}
Let $S$ be the set of prime divisors of $h$, let
$M=\mathbb F_{2^h}$, and write $\varphi(x)=x^2$ and
$\sigma_r=\varphi^{h/r}$.  For $A\subseteq S$, put
$\sigma_A=\prod_{r\in A}\sigma_r$, and define
\[
 H_h=\prod_{r\in S}\mathbb F_{2^{h/r}}^{\times},
 \qquad
 \Delta_h(x)=
 \frac{\prod_{|A|\text{ even}}\sigma_A(x)}
      {\prod_{|A|\text{ odd}}\sigma_A(x)}.
\]
Then
\[
 \ker\Delta_h=H_h,
 \qquad |\operatorname{im}\Delta_h|=\Phi_h(2),
\]
and $\Delta_h$ induces an isomorphism
$M^{\times}/H_h\simeq\operatorname{im}\Delta_h$.  Arm $Z$ at degree $h$
is equivalent to
\[
  \Pi_h\mid\operatorname{ord}(\Delta_h(T_h)).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The operator is the power map
\[
  x\longmapsto x^{\prod_{r\mid h,\ r\text{ prime}}(1-2^{h/r})}.
\]
In the cyclic group of order $2^h-1$, lifting the exponent prime by prime
shows that its kernel has order
$\operatorname{lcm}_{r\in S}(2^{h/r}-1)$, exactly the order of $H_h$.
The cyclotomic product formula gives quotient order $\Phi_h(2)$.  A prime
with residue degree $h$ divides no proper-subfield order, so the quotient
preserves its full primary valuation.  Proposition~\ref{prop:z-support}
gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The cubical coordinate is canonical, but nonidentity is weaker than full
primary order.  Moreover, an additive cube made from independently shifted
components is not the synchronized norm of their selected sum.  Thus neither
linear disjointness nor a nonzero raw cross-ratio proves $Z$.

\subsection{The singleton-even Conway--Fermat chain}

The zero arm also contains the singleton-even chain.  Put
\[
 h_n=2^{n+1},\qquad c_n=\kappa_{h_n}=2^{2^n},\qquad
 E_n=\mathbb F_{2^{h_n}},\qquad F_n=2^{2^n}+1,
\]
and $E_{-1}=\mathbb F_2$.  The tower relation is
\[
 c_n^2+c_n=\prod_{j<n}c_j.
\]
Let
\[
 \delta_n=\operatorname{ord}
   (c_nE_{n-1}^{\times})
 \quad\text{in }E_n^{\times}/E_{n-1}^{\times}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Conway--Fermat selected order]\label{thm:fermat}
One has $1<\delta_n\mid F_n$, and the zero arm at every prime divisor of
$F_n$ is equivalent to
\[
  \delta_n=F_n\qquad(n\geq0).
\]
All compatible binary root choices form one absolute-Frobenius orbit, so
they have the same order vector $(\delta_0,\ldots,\delta_n)$.  The remaining
assertion is therefore intrinsic to the selected tower rather than to an
extra choice of root.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The quotient group has order
$(2^{2^{n+1}}-1)/(2^{2^n}-1)=F_n$, and $c_n\notin E_{n-1}$.  Every prime
$p\mid F_n$ has $f(p)=2^{n+1}$, with its full primary part in this quotient.
Proposition~\ref{prop:power} gives the displayed equality for $\delta_n$.

A compatible prefix has two extensions at each level, so there are
$2^{n+1}$ compatible tuples.  Frobenius preserves the defining equations,
and the $2^{n+1}$ Frobenius conjugates are distinct because $c_n$ has that
degree.  They exhaust the tuples and preserve all relative orders.
\end{proof}

The quotient in Theorem~\ref{thm:fermat} is a torus.  Put
\[
 q=2^{2^n}=|E_{n-1}|,\qquad
 T_n=\ker\bigl(N_{E_n/E_{n-1}}\colon E_n^{\times}\to E_{n-1}^{\times}\bigr),
 \qquad
 u_n=\frac{c_n+1}{c_n},
\]
and $a_{n-1}=\prod_{j<n}c_j$, with $a_{-1}=1$.

\begin{proposition}[Fermat torus coordinate]\label{prop:z-torus}
The group $T_n$ is cyclic of order $F_n$, and $z\mapsto z^{q-1}$ induces an
isomorphism $E_n^{\times}/E_{n-1}^{\times}\simeq T_n$ sending
$c_nE_{n-1}^{\times}$ to $u_n$.  Moreover
\[
 u_n+u_n^{-1}=a_{n-1}^{-1},
 \qquad
 a_{n-1}=\frac{u_n}{(u_n+1)^2}.
\]
Consequently $\operatorname{ord}(u_n)=\delta_n$, and the Conway--Fermat
equality $\delta_n=F_n$ says exactly that $u_n$ generates $T_n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The group $E_n^{\times}$ is cyclic of order $q^2-1$ and
$N_{E_n/E_{n-1}}(z)=z^{q+1}$, so $T_n$ is its subgroup of order $q+1=F_n$.
The $(q-1)$-st power map has kernel $E_{n-1}^{\times}$ and image $T_n$.  The
generator $z\mapsto z^q$ of $\operatorname{Gal}(E_n/E_{n-1})$ fixes
$a_{n-1}$, hence permutes the two roots $c_n$ and $c_n+1$ of
$X^2+X+a_{n-1}$; since $c_n\notin E_{n-1}$, it exchanges them.  Thus
$c_n^{q-1}=(c_n+1)/c_n=u_n$.  Finally
\[
 u_n+u_n^{-1}=\frac{(c_n+1)^2+c_n^2}{c_n(c_n+1)}
 =\frac1{c_n^2+c_n}=\frac1{a_{n-1}},
\]
and $u_n/(u_n+1)^2=(u_n+u_n^{-1})^{-1}$ in characteristic two.
\end{proof}

The recurrence has a deterministic packet form.  For each divisor $e>1$ of
$F_n$,
the inverse pairs of primitive $e$-th roots give a squarefree packet
polynomial
\[
 C_e(X)=
 \prod_{[z]\in\mu_e^{\mathrm{prim}}/(z\sim z^{-1})}
 \left(X-\frac{z}{(z+1)^2}\right)\in\mathbb F_2[X].
\]
Every irreducible factor has degree $2^n$, and
\[
 \delta_n=e
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{the selected minimal polynomial of $a_{n-1}$ divides $C_e$}.
\]
If $P$ is such a factor, its unique irreducible child is
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_X(P(X),Y^2+XY+X^3).
\]
Thus the literal chain is one deterministic orbit beginning at $X+1$.
Full parent conductor does not imply full child conductor: exact degree-$16$
packet examples have identical full parent order but deterministic children
of different conductor.  Consequently any induction must use the complete
marked orbit, not only the integer conductor.

The packet degree nevertheless gives an unconditional restriction on every
possible loss of conductor.

\begin{proposition}[packet-degree defect bound]
\label{prop:z-packet-degree}
For every $n\geq0$ one has
\[
 \varphi(\delta_n)\geq2^{n+1}.
\]
For $n\geq2$ the sharper bound is
\[
 \varphi(\delta_n)\geq2^{n+2}.
\]
If a prime $p\mid F_n$ is absent from the selected order at its full
valuation, then
\[
 p\leq1+2^{2^n-n-1}.
\]
For $n\geq2$ this improves to
\[
 p\leq1+2^{2^n-n-2}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The packet $C_{\delta_n}$ has degree $\varphi(\delta_n)/2$, while it
contains the selected minimal polynomial of $a_{n-1}$, of degree $2^n$.
This proves the first inequality.  Put $H=2^{n+1}$.  If $n\geq2$, choose a
prime $q\mid\delta_n$; such a prime exists because the first inequality
makes $\delta_n>1$.  Since $q\mid F_n$, the order of $2$ modulo $q$ is
exactly $H$.  Thus $q-1=kH$ for some $k$.  Moreover $q\equiv1\pmod8$, so
the supplementary law for $2$ and Euler's criterion give
\[
 1=2^{(q-1)/2}=\bigl(2^{H/2}\bigr)^k=(-1)^k\pmod q.
\]
Consequently $k$ is even, every prime divisor of $F_n$ is congruent to
$1$ modulo $2H=2^{n+2}$, and
$\varphi(\delta_n)\geq\varphi(q)=q-1\geq2^{n+2}$.

Write
$a=v_p(F_n)$ and $b=v_p(\delta_n)<a$.  Since $\delta_n\mid F_n$, the ratio
\[
 \frac{\varphi(F_n)}{\varphi(\delta_n)}
\]
is an integer.  The factor in its prime-by-prime product indexed by $p$ is
$p^{a-1}(p-1)$ if $b=0$, and $p^{a-b}$ if $b>0$; in either case the ratio is
at least $p-1$.  Hence
\[
 (p-1)2^{n+1}
 \leq\varphi(F_n)
 \leq F_n-1=2^{2^n},
\]
which is the asserted bound.
Replacing $2^{n+1}$ by the sharper lower bound $2^{n+2}$ gives the final
claim when $n\geq2$.
\end{proof}

The smallest missing prime of each Fermat packet gives a useful sharpening
of this bound.  Congruence spacing separates a repeated missing coordinate
from a defect containing a second prime.

\begin{corollary}[Fermat congruence regimes for the first missing prime]
\label{cor:z-one-packet}
Let $n\geq2$, suppose $\delta_n<F_n$, and let $p$ be the smallest prime
divisor of $D=F_n/\delta_n$.  Put
\[
 M=2^{n+2},
 \qquad
 A_{<p}=\prod_{\substack{q<p\\q\mid F_n}}q^{v_q(F_n)},
 \qquad G=\max\{M,\varphi(A_{<p})\}.
\]
Every prime divisor of $D$ is at least $p$ and is congruent to $1$ modulo
$M$.  Moreover
\[
 \varphi(F_n)<G p(p-1)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 D=p.
\]
In the larger range
\[
 \varphi(F_n)<G(p-1)(p+M-1)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 D=p\quad\hbox{or}\quad p^2\mid D.
\]
If $v_p(F_n)=1$, the second alternative is impossible and the conclusion
in the larger range is again $D=p$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By the choice of $p$, every prime divisor of $D$ is at least $p$ and
$p\mid D$.  Thus $D$ is $p$-rough.

The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:z-packet-degree} shows that every prime
divisor of $F_n$ is $1$ modulo $M$.  Minimality within this packet forces the
full $q$-primary part of $F_n$ into $\delta_n$ for every $q<p$.  Hence
$A_{<p}\mid\delta_n$, so
\[
 G\leq\varphi(\delta_n).
\]

Put $t=\varphi(F_n)/\varphi(\delta_n)$.  If $D\ne p$, the prime-by-prime
factorization of the totient quotient has either a repeated missing
$p$-factor or a distinct missing prime $q>p$.  In the repeated case its
local factor is at least $p(p-1)$.  In the distinct case congruence spacing
gives $q\geq p+M$, and the two local factors give
\[
 t\geq(p-1)(q-1)\geq(p-1)(p+M-1)\geq p(p-1).
\]
Multiplication by $\varphi(\delta_n)\geq G$ proves the first implication.
The larger strict bound excludes the distinct-prime case and leaves only
$D=p$ or $p^2\mid D$.  The valuation-one assertion is immediate.

The square survivor is a precise classical boundary: $p^2\mid F_n$ implies
$2^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod {p^2}$, so $p$ is a base-$2$ Wieferich prime.  Thus
the congruence sieve isolates, rather than silently assumes, the unresolved
repeated-factor obstruction.
\end{proof}

 S_{F_n/\ell}(a_{n-1})\ne0\qquad(\ell\mid F_n\text{ prime}).

Equivalently, one applies the same polynomial to multiplication by

 S_{F_{n+1}/\ell}(M_n)\ne0
 \qquad(\ell\mid F_{n+1}\text{ prime}).

At the lower index one instead has the automatic exclusion

 S_{F_n/\ell}(a_n)\ne0,
 \quad\text{equivalently}\quad

It is not the selected-order conjecture.  The block formula remains an exact

At the genuine index there is nevertheless a sharper exact residual.  Let
$d=F_{n+1}/\ell=2m+1>1$ and put $x=a_n$.  Characteristic-two doubling and
Cassini give
\begin{align*}
 S_m(x)^4+x^{d-2}&=S_d(x)S_{d-2}(x),\\
 S_{m+1}(x)^4+x^d&=S_d(x)S_{d+2}(x).
\end{align*}
Consequently, over a field,
\[
 S_d(x)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 S_m(x)^4=x^{d-2}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 S_{m+1}(x)^4=x^d.
\]
The converse is literal: squaring
$S_d=S_{m+1}^2+xS_m^2$ makes the two displayed powers cancel.  The pair is
strictly sharper than either equation separately.  Indeed, in
$\mathbb F_2[X]$ its two left-hand residual polynomials have monic gcd exactly
$S_d$.  To see the only needed coprimality, any common divisor of
$S_{d-2}$ and $S_{d+2}$ divides $X^{d-2}S_4=X^{d-2}$ by the continuant
determinant identity, while the positive odd-index polynomial $S_{d-2}$ has
constant coefficient one.  Thus $S_{d-2}$ and $S_{d+2}$ are coprime, and
the two factorizations give the stated gcd.

This exact branch selector still does not close the Conway--Fermat target.
If $d-1=2^s g$ with $g$ odd, its lower equation is

 S_g(x)^{2^{s+1}}=x^{d-2}.

Writing $q=|E_n|$, choose $K$ by the exact congruence
$2^{s+1}K\equiv d-2\pmod{q-1}$.  Since $x\ne0$, $x^{q-1}=1$, and the
$2^{s+1}$-power Frobenius is injective, the equation becomes
$S_g(x)=x^K$.  This is precisely the existing AE4 inverse-exponent seam.
The upper equation removes the adjacent Fibonacci branch but, after
$S_d(x)=0$ is imposed, follows by squaring the same endpoint relation twice
and using the lower equation; no present ancestry identity contradicts the
resulting monomial equality.

In fact the inverse-exponent seam is not merely compatible with deficient
packets: it is a universal identity on every such packet.

\begin{proposition}[packet-universal Fermat residual]
\label{prop:z-packet-universal}
Let $s,g\geq1$, let $d=Rg+1$ with $R=2^s$, and let $u\ne0,1$ satisfy
$u^d=1$ in a field of characteristic two.  Put
\[
 \phi(u)=\frac{u}{(u+1)^2},\qquad x=\phi(u),\qquad
 f=S_g(x),\qquad h=S_{g+1}(x),\qquad
 B=\frac{x^g}{f^2}.
\]
Then $f\ne0$ and
\[
 B=\phi(u^g),\qquad B^R=x,
 \qquad \frac hf=T_s(B),
\]
where $T_s(z)=z+z^2+\cdots+z^{2^{s-1}}$.
Consequently both fourth-power residual coordinates produced by a zero of
$S_d$ are identities for every nontrivial $d$-th root---equivalently, on
every packet $C_e$ with $1<e\mid d$---before the Conway parent is selected.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The characteristic-two Binet identity is
\[
 S_j(\phi(u))=\frac{u^j+1}{(u+1)^j}.
\]
It first gives $S_d(x)=0$.  Since $\gcd(g,d)=1$ and $u\ne1$, it also gives
$f\ne0$, and direct substitution gives
\[
 \frac{x^g}{f^2}=\frac{u^g}{(u^g+1)^2}=\phi(u^g).
\]
The map $\phi$ commutes with every binary Frobenius power and satisfies
$\phi(v^{-1})=\phi(v)$.  Since $u^{Rg}=u^{d-1}=u^{-1}$,
this proves $B^R=x$.

Put $t=(u^g+1)^{-1}$.  Then $B=t+t^2$, so the partial Frobenius trace
telescopes:
\[
 T_s(B)=t+t^R
 =\frac1{u^g+1}+\frac{u}{u+1}.
\]
The last equality uses
$(u^g+1)^R=u^{Rg}+1=u^{-1}+1$.  A second application of the Binet identity
gives
\[
 \frac hf
 =\frac{u^{g+1}+1}{(u+1)(u^g+1)}
 =\frac1{u^g+1}+\frac{u}{u+1},
\]
proving the assertion.  Thus these residual coordinates characterize the
ambient torsion packet but do not distinguish its recursively selected
member.
\end{proof}

The Binet identity in this proof is kernel-checked as
\texttt{fibPolyValue\_torus\_binet}.  The proposition sharpens the failure
boundary: any successful induction must use the deterministic parent label
in
\[
 u_{n+1}(u_n+1)^3=u_n(u_{n+1}+1)^2,
\]
not only the fourth-power residual, inverse Frobenius, trace, or norm of the
child packet.

This isolates the part not settled by the existing Conway-tower literature.
Popovych proves lower bounds and verifies the required relative full orders
through level eleven using the known Fermat factorizations
\cite{popovych2018}.  Cagliero, Herman, and Szechtman prove an exact product
formula for global order in terms of the relative orders, as well as a normal
basis theorem for the associated tower elements
\cite{caglierohermanszechtman2025}.  Neither result forces every relative
factor $\delta_n$ to equal $F_n$; that all-level equality is precisely the
remaining selected assertion here.

The limitation of normality and finitely many absolute traces can be made
unconditional, independently of the selected tower.

\begin{proposition}[finite-trace order separation]
\label{prop:z-trace-separation}
For every finite set $S\subset\mathbb Z_{>0}$, there are arbitrarily large
powers $d=2^s$ and normal full-degree elements
$\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb F_{2^d}$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^j)=\operatorname{Tr}(\beta^j)
 \quad(j\in S),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{ord}(\alpha)=2^d-1,
 \quad
 \operatorname{ord}(\beta)=\frac{2^d-1}{3}.
\]
Consequently no fixed finite tuple of absolute power traces, even together
with degree and normality, distinguishes full multiplicative order from a
missing factor three in characteristic two.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Absolute trace is invariant under squaring, so replace every $j\in S$ by its
odd core, remove repetitions, and adjoin the exponent one.  The resulting
odd monomials $X^{u_1},\ldots,X^{u_r}$ are strongly linearly independent in
the sense of Cohen: a nonconstant Artin--Schreier polynomial $H^2-H+b$ has
even leading degree, whereas every nonzero binary linear combination of the
chosen monomials has odd leading degree.  A rational $H$ cannot evade this
argument, since any finite pole would survive in $H^2-H$.

Apply Cohen's specified-order, specified-functional-trace theorem
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{cohen2005specified} twice with $q=2$, first with defect
$l=1$ and then with defect $l=3$, prescribing the same trace values and
prescribing one for the coordinate $X$.  Its explicit degree bound holds for
all sufficiently large $d=2^s$, and $3\mid2^d-1$ for even $d$.  This gives
the displayed exact orders and trace equalities.

For an extension of degree a power of the characteristic, an element is
normal exactly when its absolute trace is nonzero.  By the normal-basis
theorem the Frobenius module is free of rank one over the local algebra
$\mathbb F_2[T]/((T-1)^d)$, and trace is $(T-1)^{d-1}$.
Thus both elements are normal.  The primitive element has full degree.  For
$d\ge4$, so does the element of order $(2^d-1)/3$, since this order exceeds
$2^{d/2}-1$, the largest possible multiplicative order in a proper subfield.
\end{proof}

This proposition is not a counterexample to the Conway--Fermat assertion:
it controls generic field elements, whereas $\delta_n$ is the order of the
torus coordinate attached to the literal recursively selected ancestry.
It proves, however, that a successful all-level argument must use more than
any fixed finite absolute-trace tuple or the normal-basis theorem.  Across a
single Conway quadratic edge the entire power-sum recursion is nevertheless
explicit: if $y^2+xy+x^3=0$, then
\[
 y^m+(y+x)^m=D_m(x,x^3),
\]
where $D_m(S,B)$ is the signed Dickson--Lucas recurrence with initial values
$2,S$ and step $D_{m+2}=S D_{m+1}-B D_m$.  This identity is kernel-checked as
\texttt{conwayChild\_powerSum}; it transports trace data but does not recover
the missing conductor coordinate.

There is an exact supersingular realization of the selected ancestry.

\begin{proposition}[supersingular realization and pairing boundary]
\label{prop:z-elliptic}
On
\[
 \mathcal E:\quad y^2+y=x^3+x^2
\]
put $P_n=(c_n,c_{n+1})$.  Then $P_n\in\mathcal E$, and for $n\geq2$,
\[
 [F_n]P_n=O.
\]
More precisely, if $\pi$ is absolute Frobenius and $\iota=\pi+[1]$, then
\[
 \iota(x,y)=(x+1,y+x+1),\qquad
 \iota^2=[-1],\qquad \pi^4=[-4].
\]
For $g=(y+1)/y$, one has
\[
 g(P_n)=\frac{c_{n+1}+1}{c_{n+1}},
 \qquad \operatorname{ord}(g(P_n))=\delta_{n+1}.
\]
If $T=(0,0)$, then $T$ has order five and
\[
 \operatorname{div}(g)
 =2[-T]+[2T]-2[T]-[-2T].
\]
Thus $g$ is the quotient of the two fixed doubling Miller functions at
$-T$ and $T$, but it is not a Weil or reduced-Tate pairing function attached
to the $F_n$-torsion point $P_n$ when $n\geq2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Conway recursion gives
\[
 c_{n+1}^2+c_{n+1}
 =c_n(c_n^2+c_n)=c_n^3+c_n^2,
\]
so $P_n$ lies on $\mathcal E$.  The coordinate formula for
$\iota(P)=\pi(P)+P$ follows from the chord law.  Applying it twice gives the
inverse $(x,y)\mapsto(x,y+1)$, so
$\pi^2+2\pi+2=0$ in $\operatorname{End}(\mathcal E)$ and hence
$\pi^4=[-4]$.

Put $H=2^{n+1}$.  The $2^H$-Frobenius fixes $c_n$ and sends $c_{n+1}$ to
$c_{n+1}+1$, hence sends $P_n$ to $-P_n$.  For $n\geq2$,
\[
 \pi^H=(\pi^4)^{2^{n-1}}=[2^{2^n}],
\]
which gives $[2^{2^n}+1]P_n=O$.

The formula for $g(P_n)$ is immediate from the coordinates, and its order is
$\delta_{n+1}$ by the definition of $u_{n+1}$.  The five rational points of
$\mathcal E/\mathbb F_2$ show that $T$ has order five.  Direct intersection
with the two horizontal lines gives
\[
 \operatorname{div}(y)=2[T]+[-2T]-3[O],\qquad
 \operatorname{div}(y+1)=2[-T]+[2T]-3[O],
\]
and subtraction gives the divisor of $g$.  Equivalently,
$g=f_{2,-T}/f_{2,T}$ for functions normalized at $O$.  Its divisor is
supported on fixed rational five-torsion, with coefficients $2,1,-2,-1$.
For $n\geq2$, these valuations are not divisible by $F_n\geq17$, whereas the
divisor of an $F_n$-Miller function and every $F_n$-th-power correction have
all coefficients divisible by $F_n$.  Thus $g$ cannot be such a function
attached to $P_n$, even modulo an $F_n$-th power.
A Weil or reduced Tate pairing on $\mathcal E[F_n]$ takes values in
$\mu_{F_n}$, whereas $g(P_n)$ has order dividing $F_{n+1}$.  Pairwise
coprimality of the Fermat numbers and $\delta_{n+1}>1$ exclude equality.
\end{proof}

The elliptic curve is only the first stage of a recursive
Artin--Schreier tower.  Its ramification remains completely rigid at every
depth, but that rigidity occurs away from every finite torsion packet.

\begin{proposition}[all-iterate ramification boundary]
\label{prop:z-iterate-ramification}
Let $\mathcal X_r$ be the smooth projective normalization of the affine
curve over $\mathbb F_2$ with coordinates $c_0,\ldots,c_r$ and equations
\[
 c_{j+1}^2+c_{j+1}=c_j^3+c_j^2
 \qquad(0\leq j<r).
\]
Then $\mathcal X_r$ is geometrically connected, the projection
$\mathcal X_r\to\mathbb P^1_{c_0}$ has degree $2^r$, and it is unramified
over the finite $c_0$-line.  There is a unique point $\infty_r$ above
$c_0=\infty$, and
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{\infty_r}(c_j)
 =-2^{r-j}3^j\qquad(0\leq j\leq r).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 g(\mathcal X_r)=\frac{3^{r+1}-2^{r+2}+1}{2}.
\]
Consequently no invariant determined only by the branch locus, ramification
indices, different, or inertia filtration of the iterated correspondence can
distinguish the literal Conway specialization from a deficient finite
torsion packet.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $K_j=\overline{\mathbb F}_2(\mathcal X_j)$.  On
$\mathcal X_0=\mathbb P^1$, the function $c_0$ has one pole, of order one.
Suppose inductively that $c_j$ has a unique pole $\infty_j$, of order
$3^j$.  The Artin--Schreier driver
\[
 f_j=c_j^3+c_j^2
\]
then has the same unique pole, of odd order $m_j=3^{j+1}$; the cubic term
has strictly larger pole order than the quadratic term.  It cannot equal
$w^2+w$ in $K_j$: if $w$ has a pole, the leading pole order of $w^2+w$ is
even, and if $w$ has no pole, neither does $w^2+w$.  Thus
$Z^2+Z=f_j$ is irreducible even after extending the constants, proving
geometric connectedness and degree two at the next stage.

The reduced Artin--Schreier ramification criterion gives a unique point
$\infty_{j+1}$ above $\infty_j$, with ramification index two and different
exponent
\[
 m_j+1=3^{j+1}+1.
\]
At every other place $f_j$ is integral, and the derivative of
$Z^2+Z-f_j$ is one, so the extension is unramified there.  With the
normalized valuation upstairs one has
$\operatorname{ord}_{\infty_{j+1}}(f_j)=-2m_j$.  The defining equation
therefore forces
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{\infty_{j+1}}(c_{j+1})=-m_j=-3^{j+1}.
\]
Every later quadratic stage doubles the valuation of an earlier coordinate,
which proves
$\operatorname{ord}_{\infty_r}(c_j)=-2^{r-j}3^j$.

Put $h_j=2g(\mathcal X_j)-2$.  Riemann--Hurwitz gives
\[
 h_0=-2,
 \qquad
 h_{j+1}=2h_j+3^{j+1}+1.
\]
Hence
\begin{align*}
 h_r
 &= -2^{r+1}
    +\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}2^{r-1-j}\bigl(3^{j+1}+1\bigr)\\
 &=3^{r+1}-2^{r+2}-1,
\end{align*}
which is the asserted genus formula.

Finally, every nontrivial torsion coordinate $u$ has
$c=(u+1)^{-1}$ finite.  The literal place $c_0^2+c_0+1=0$ and every finite
packet specialization therefore meet the same etale locus.  The literal
place is totally inert through the Conway tower, but that is decomposition
data at one marked place, not a consequence of the ramification just
computed.  The proposition consequently supplies no generator or
nonvanishing assertion.
\end{proof}

The anti-invariant generalized Jacobian can also be computed exactly.  It is
large, but the Kummer function occupies only one tame boundary direction.

\begin{proposition}[generalized-Prym saturation boundary]
\label{prop:z-prym-boundary}
For $r\geq1$, let
\[
 \pi_r:\mathcal X_r\longrightarrow\mathcal X_{r-1},\qquad
 \tau_r(c_r)=c_r+1,\qquad
 u_r=\frac{c_r+1}{c_r}.
\]
For $\epsilon=(\epsilon_0,\ldots,\epsilon_{r-1})\in\{0,1\}^r$ and
$\delta\in\{0,1\}$, write $P_{\epsilon,\delta}$ for the corresponding
binary point of $\mathcal X_r$, and put
$z(\epsilon)=\#\{i:\epsilon_i=0\}$.  Then
\[
 \operatorname{div}(u_r)
 =\sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^r}2^{z(\epsilon)}
   \bigl([P_{\epsilon,1}]-[P_{\epsilon,0}]\bigr).
\]
In particular its reduced support $S_r$ consists of $2^{r+1}$
$\mathbb F_2$-points, and $B_r=\pi_r(S_r)$ consists of $2^r$
$\mathbb F_2$-points.

Let $J(\mathcal X,S)$ denote the reduced-modulus generalized Jacobian.  The
identity component of the generalized norm kernel
\[
 \ker\bigl(J(\mathcal X_r,S_r)\longrightarrow
                 J(\mathcal X_{r-1},B_r)\bigr)
\]
has dimension $3^r$.  Its ordinary Prym quotient has dimension
$3^r-2^r$, while its boundary-torus kernel has dimension $2^r$.

For every odd prime $\ell$, with $U_r=\mathcal X_r\setminus S_r$, the
Kummer class
\[
 \delta_\ell(u_r)\in H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(U_r,\mu_\ell)
\]
comes from the unit term in the Kummer sequence, has tame residue vector
given by the displayed divisor formula modulo $\ell$, and satisfies
$\tau_r^*\delta_\ell(u_r)=-\delta_\ell(u_r)$.  This vector is nonzero for
every odd $\ell$, but its Frobenius orbit spans only one line, up to the
cyclotomic Tate twist.  The ray character defined by $u_r$ is nontrivial on
the anti-invariant boundary torus and is geometrically surjective onto
$\mathbb G_m$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The affine equations are triangular with derivative one in each new
coordinate.  Hence the binary tuples are distinct smooth points.  Conversely,
$c_r\in\{0,1\}$ forces
\[
 c_{r-1}^2(c_{r-1}+1)=c_r(c_r+1)=0,
\]
and induction forces every earlier coordinate to be binary.  At a binary
point the same identities give
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(c_i-\epsilon_i)
 =
 \begin{cases}
  2\operatorname{ord}(c_{i-1}),&\epsilon_{i-1}=0,\\
  \operatorname{ord}(c_{i-1}+1),&\epsilon_{i-1}=1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Starting with order one on $\mathcal X_0$ proves
the divisor formula.  There is no contribution at infinity, since
$u_r=1+c_r^{-1}$ there.  As a check, each of the zero and pole divisors has
degree
\[
 \sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^r}2^{z(\epsilon)}=(2+1)^r=3^r,
\]
the pole degree of $c_r$.

For a reduced modulus of $s$ geometric points, the boundary torus has
dimension $s-1$.  The norm on the two boundary points above each point of
$B_r$ is pairwise multiplication, so its toric kernel has dimension $2^r$.
The ordinary Prym dimension is, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-iterate-ramification},
\[
 g(\mathcal X_r)-g(\mathcal X_{r-1})=3^r-2^r.
\]
The generalized norm is surjective: its composite with pullback is
multiplication by two, which is a finite surjective morphism of the
semiabelian target even in characteristic two.  Subtracting dimensions now
gives $3^r$ for the identity kernel.  On prime-to-two Tate modules, the
toric norm kernel is exactly the $(-1)$-part of the boundary-torus module for
$\tau_r$; this formulation avoids any false semisimplicity assertion at the
characteristic prime.

The Kummer exact sequence gives
\[
 0\longrightarrow
 \mathcal O(U_r)^\times/\mathcal O(U_r)^{\times\ell}
 \longrightarrow H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(U_r,\mu_\ell)
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Pic}(U_r)[\ell]
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
The class of $u_r$ lies in the first term, and its tame residues are its
valuations modulo $\ell$.  All displayed multiplicities are powers of two,
so none vanishes modulo an odd $\ell$; in particular the reduced modulus
loses no conductor information.  The coefficient belonging to the all-one
prefix is one, proving nonvanishing.  Since
$\tau_r(u_r)=u_r^{-1}$, the class is anti-invariant and its corestriction is
zero.

Every point of $S_r$ is rational over $\mathbb F_2$, so Frobenius has no
permutation action on the residue vector.  Only the scalar cyclotomic twist
remains, and the orbit therefore spans one line.  Finally, for a degree-zero
divisor $D$ disjoint from $S_r$, put $\chi_{u_r}([D])=u_r(D)$.  If
$D$ is changed by $\operatorname{div}(f)$ with $f=1$ on $S_r$, Weil
reciprocity gives
\[
 u_r(\operatorname{div}(f))=f(\operatorname{div}(u_r))=1,
\]
so this is a character of the generalized Jacobian.  Its restriction to the
toric norm kernel has the nonzero anti-invariant exponent vector
displayed above; a nonzero character of a torus is geometrically
surjective onto $\mathbb G_m$.
\end{proof}

This surjectivity concerns geometric generalized-divisor classes, not the
literal totally inert Conway place.  It therefore supplies no full-order
claim.  Rather, it shows sharply why the ordinary Prym and lower norm data
cannot evaluate the selected phase: the relevant ray class is one
Frobenius-stable line inside a $2^r$-dimensional anti-invariant boundary
torus, and selecting its value requires decomposition data at the marked
place.

\begin{corollary}[fixed-five-torsion rank-one boundary]
\label{cor:z-fixed-s-rank-one}
Let $n\geq2$, put $A=2^{2^n}$, $q=A^2$, and let
$\ell$ be an odd prime dividing $q+1=F_{n+1}$.  Set
$L=\mathbb F_{q^2}$ and $S=\mathcal E(\mathbb F_2)$.  If $[z]_\ell$
denotes the class of $z\in L^\times$ in
$L^\times/L^{\times\ell}$, then
\[
 \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_\ell}
 \bigl\{[F(P_n)]_\ell:
 F\in\mathbb F_2(\mathcal E)^\times,\,
 \operatorname{supp}\operatorname{div}(F)\subseteq S\bigr\}
 =
 \mathbb F_\ell[g(P_n)]_\ell .
\]
Thus no $\mathbb F_2$-rational unit supported on the fixed five-torsion
supplies an independent Kummer detector for the selected endpoint.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 A_0=[-T]-[T],\qquad B_0=[-2T]-[2T].
\]
Every anti-invariant degree-zero divisor supported on $S$ is $aA_0+bB_0$.
Its class in $\operatorname{Pic}^0(\mathcal E)\simeq\mathcal E$ is
$(3a+b)T$, so the principal lattice is
$3a+b\equiv0\pmod5$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:z-elliptic} gives
$\operatorname{div}(g)=2A_0-B_0$.  With
\[
 r=f_{5,T}=\frac{y^2(y+x)}{(x+1)^2},
 \qquad s=\frac{r\circ[-1]}r,
\]
one has $\operatorname{div}(s)=5A_0$; these two divisor vectors have
determinant five and hence form a basis of the principal lattice.

For an $S$-unit $F$, put $R=(F\circ[-1])/F$.  Since
$\mathbb F_2^\times=\{1\}$, the lattice calculation gives
$R=g^m s^k$ for some integers $m,k$.  Direct substitution in the displayed
functions gives
\[
 s=\frac{g^2}{g\circ\iota}.
\]
Now $c_n^A=c_n+1$, so $P_n^A$ and $\iota P_n$ have the same
$x$-coordinate.  Hence $P_n^A=\epsilon\iota P_n$ for some
$\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$, and
\[
 s(P_n)=g(P_n)^{\,2-\epsilon A}.
\]
As $P_n^q=-P_n$, it follows that
$F(P_n)^{q-1}=R(P_n)$, placing every displayed class on the line generated
by $[g(P_n)]_\ell$ because $\gcd(\ell,q-1)=1$.

Conversely, for $h=y/(x+1)$ one has
$h\circ[-1]/h=g$.  Therefore
$h(P_n)^{q-1}=g(P_n)$, and $[h(P_n)]_\ell$ spans the same line.  This proves
the reverse inclusion.
\end{proof}

The fixed support can be enlarged to the complete top Frobenius orbit without
losing the literal selector.  The resulting divisor lattice has two explicit
function generators after evaluation, but the value quotient still has only
one Kummer direction.

\begin{proposition}[selected top-orbit $S$-unit reduction]
\label{prop:z-top-orbit-units}
Let $n\geq2$ and put
\[
 H=2^{n+1},\qquad A=2^{H/2}=2^{2^n},\qquad
 q=2^H=A^2,\qquad d=2H,\qquad L=\mathbb F_{q^2}.
\]
Let $P=(x_P,y_P)\in\mathcal E(L)$ satisfy
\[
 P^q=-P,\qquad [\mathbb F_2(x_P):\mathbb F_2]=H,
 \qquad x_P^A=x_P+1.
\]
These are the top-orbit and half-Frobenius properties of $P_n$.  Let
$M\in\mathbb F_2[X]$ be the minimal polynomial of $x_P$, and define the
pointwise translated divisors
\[
 D_0=\sum_{0\leq i<d}[P^{2^i}],\qquad
 D_\pm=\sum_{0\leq i<d}[P^{2^i}\pm T],\qquad
 C_0=D_+-D_-.
\]
Put
\[
 S'=\mathcal E(\mathbb F_2)\cup\operatorname{supp}(D_+)
       \cup\operatorname{supp}(D_-),
 \qquad f(Q)=M(x(Q+T)),
 \qquad U=\frac{f\circ[-1]}f,
\]
and
\[
 \Theta_P=U(P)
 =\frac{M(x(P-T))}{M(x(P+T))}
 =M(x(P+T))^{q-1}.
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{div}(U)=C_0+dA_0,
 \qquad A_0=[-T]-[T],\quad B_0=[-2T]-[2T].
\]
The $\mathbb F_2$-rational, inversion-anti-invariant, degree-zero divisors
supported on $S'$ are exactly
\[
 aA_0+bB_0+kC_0\qquad(a,b,k\in\mathbb Z).
\]
Such a divisor is principal if and only if
\[
 3a+b+2dk\equiv0\pmod5.
\]
In the coordinates $(A_0,B_0,C_0)$, the principal lattice has the basis
\[
 \operatorname{div}(g)=(2,-1,0),\qquad
 \operatorname{div}(s)=(5,0,0),\qquad
 \operatorname{div}(U)=(d,0,1),
\]
where $s$ is the fixed-support function in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:z-fixed-s-rank-one}.

More precisely, if $F\in\mathbb F_2(\mathcal E)^\times$ has divisor supported
on $S'$, there are $m,k,r\in\mathbb Z$ and
$\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$, with $P^A=\epsilon\iota P$, such that
\[
 F(P)^{q-1}=g(P)^{\,m+k(2-\epsilon A)}\Theta_P^r.
\]
Consequently, for every odd prime $\ell\mid q+1$,
\[
 \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_\ell}
 \bigl\{[F(P)]_\ell:
       F\in\mathbb F_2(\mathcal E)^\times,
       \operatorname{supp}\operatorname{div}(F)\subseteq S'\bigr\}
 =\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_\ell}
   \{[g(P)]_\ell,[\Theta_P]_\ell\}.
\]
This names two function/divisor generators, not two independent value
directions: the cyclic quotient $L^\times/L^{\times\ell}$ has
$\mathbb F_\ell$-dimension one.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The point $P$ has Frobenius orbit of length $d$: its $x$-coordinate has orbit
length $H$, while $P^q=-P\ne P$.  The divisors $D_+$ and $D_-$ are single
$\mathbb F_2$-orbits.  They are disjoint from each other, from
$\mathcal E(\mathbb F_2)$, and from $D_0$; in particular, $P\notin S'$ and
all evaluations below are defined.  The orbit $D_0$ is disjoint from
$\mathcal E(\mathbb F_2)$ by its length, and the same follows for $D_\pm$
after translating by $\mp T$.  Any remaining collision would give a nonzero
translation of one orbit point by rational five-torsion.  Iterating the
corresponding Frobenius shift five times would fix that point; since
$\gcd(d,5)=1$, the original shift would already be trivial, a contradiction.

The roots of $M$ are the $x$-coordinates of
$P,P^2,\ldots,P^{2^{H-1}}$.  The two points over each root are interchanged by
inversion and together give $D_0$.  Since $x$ has a double pole at $O$,
\[
 \operatorname{div}(f)=D_--d[-T],\qquad
 \operatorname{div}(f\circ[-1])=D_+-d[T].
\]
Their difference proves the formula for $\operatorname{div}(U)$.  Moreover,
$P^q=-P$ and rationality of $M,T$ give
\[
 M(x(P+T))^q=M(x(-P+T))=M(x(P-T)),
\]
which proves the formula for $\Theta_P$.

Inversion swaps $T$ with $-T$, $2T$ with $-2T$, and the two rational orbits
$D_+$ and $D_-$.  It fixes $O$, whose coefficient in an anti-invariant
integral divisor must therefore vanish.  Frobenius invariance makes the
coefficient constant on each of $D_+$ and $D_-$, proving the asserted
rank-three description.  Under
$\operatorname{Pic}^0(\mathcal E)\simeq\mathcal E$ one has
\[
 A_0\longmapsto3T,\qquad B_0\longmapsto T,
 \qquad C_0\longmapsto2dT.
\]
This proves the congruence criterion.  The three displayed divisor vectors
belong to its kernel and have determinant five.  Since the congruence kernel
itself has index five in $\mathbb Z^3$, they form a basis.

For an $S'$-unit $F$, set $R=(F\circ[-1])/F$.  Its divisor belongs to the
principal anti-invariant lattice, so
\[
 R=g^m s^k U^r
\]
for some integers $m,k,r$; no scalar remains because
$\mathbb F_2^\times=\{1\}$.  The selected half-Frobenius equation gives
$x(P^A)=x(\iota P)$, hence $P^A=\epsilon\iota P$ for some
$\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$.  Using
\[
 s=\frac{g^2}{g\circ\iota},\qquad g(-Q)=g(Q)^{-1},
\]
one obtains $s(P)=g(P)^{2-\epsilon A}$.  Finally,
\[
 F(P)^{q-1}=\frac{F(P^q)}{F(P)}
 =\frac{F(-P)}{F(P)}=R(P),
\]
which proves the explicit evaluation.  Since
$\gcd(\ell,q-1)=1$, exponentiation by $q-1$ is invertible on
$L^\times/L^{\times\ell}$.  This gives one inclusion in the span equality;
the reverse follows because $g$ and $U$ are themselves $S'$-units and
$U(P)=\Theta_P$.
\end{proof}

The half-Frobenius selector in this proposition is essential.  The exact
finite certificate in the \texttt{experiments} directory is
\[
 \texttt{fermat\_top\_orbit\_countermodel.py}.
\]
It constructs a generic
$H=32$ point with $P^{2^{32}}=-P$ and $x_P$ of exact degree $32$ in
\[
 \mathbb F_2[X]/(\mathtt{1c019c923}),\qquad
 x_P=\mathtt{2bf5b2fb},\qquad
 M=\mathtt{1b3e069a5}.
\]
For $\ell=641$ and $2^{32}+1=641\cdot6700417$, it verifies
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(g(P))=6700417,
 \qquad \operatorname{ord}(\Theta_P)=2^{32}+1.
\]
Thus $[g(P)]_{641}=0$ while $[\Theta_P]_{641}\ne0$.  The same certificate
finds that the fixed-support value $s(P)$ has full order $2^{32}+1$, so even
the elimination of $s$ fails without the selector.  Crucially,
\[
 x_P^{2^{16}}=\mathtt{5e024149}\ne
 \mathtt{2bf5b2fa}=x_P+1.
\]
This is an exact countermodel to any deduction from unmarked top-orbit
geometry, not a Conway specialization and not evidence against arm $Z$.
The nonzero classes of $s(P)$ and $\Theta_P$ are the same sole
$641$-direction in the cyclic value quotient; they are not independent
Kummer coordinates.

Proposition~\ref{prop:z-elliptic} supplies exact CM annihilator control but
not generator status.  For $n\geq3$, no elliptic endomorphism can carry $P_n$ to
$P_{n-1}$: the image order would divide both coprime numbers $F_n$ and
$F_{n-1}$, although $P_{n-1}\ne O$.  For $n=2$, the same conclusion follows
from $\operatorname{ord}(P_1)=3$ and $\operatorname{ord}(P_2)\mid17$.
Thus the relation
$x(P_n)=y(P_{n-1})$ is not an isogeny division tower, and pairing functoriality
along this proposed tower cannot propagate primitivity.  A perfect alternating
pairing likewise guarantees some detecting partner, not nonvanishing on a
prescribed line.

The tempting cubic curve $Y^2+Y=X^3$ is not a coordinate rewrite of
$\mathcal E$: the two curves have respectively three and five rational points
over $\mathbb F_2$.  For $u=(Y+1)/Y$ on that auxiliary curve,
$u/(u+1)^2=X^3$; every positive-level Fermat-torsion value is already a cube
because $\gcd(F_n,3)=1$.  Its natural cubic Kummer class is therefore trivial
and cannot replace the selected five-torsion function above.  The remaining
statement is still exclusion of every proper-conductor packet along the
complete resultant chain.  Trace, relative norm, irreducibility, and the
etale Jacobian hold for proper packets as well as the full packet.

\subsection{Wiedemann's sibling tower}\label{sec:z-wiedemann}

Proposition~\ref{prop:z-torus} places the Conway--Fermat assertion beside an
older open problem.  Wiedemann's iterated quadratic tower is
\[
 x_{-1}=1,\qquad x_m+x_m^{-1}=x_{m-1}\qquad(m\geq0).
\]
Wiedemann proves that every step is quadratic, so that $E_m=E_{m-1}(x_m)$
and $x_m^{-1}$ is the conjugate of $x_m$ over $E_{m-1}$, and that
$x_m^{F_m}=1$ \cite[Theorem~1 and Corollary]{wiedemann1988}.  He verifies
$\operatorname{ord}(x_m)=F_m$ for $m\leq8$, asks whether it holds for every
$m$, and, setting his tower beside Conway's, records that no explicit
isomorphism between the two presentations was known
\cite[Sections~4--5]{wiedemann1988}.  Seyfarth and Ranade show that the
conjecture is equivalent to the assertion that, for every $m$, the minimal
polynomial of $x_{m-1}$---the $m$-fold self-reciprocal transform
$f\mapsto X^{\deg f}f(X+X^{-1})$ of $X+1$---has Fibonacci index $F_m$
\cite[Theorem~2]{seyfarthranade2012}.  The question remains open.  Since
$x_{n-1}^{-1}=x_n/(x_n+1)^2$, the Binet identity in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-packet-universal} turns it into the residue test
\[
 S_{F_n/\ell}(x_{n-1}^{-1})\ne0
 \qquad(\ell\mid F_n\text{ prime}),
\]
the Conway target $S_{F_n/\ell}(a_{n-1})\ne0$ evaluated at a different
selected point.

Both towers have one shape.  Call $(b_m)_{m\geq-1}$, with $b_{-1}=1$, a
\emph{trace-compatible quadratic tower} if $b_m\in E_m\setminus E_{m-1}$ and
\[
 b_m^2+b_{m-1}b_m+\nu_m=0,
 \qquad \nu_m\in E_{m-1}^{\times}\qquad(m\geq0).
\]
Then $\operatorname{Tr}_{E_m/E_{m-1}}(b_m)=b_{m-1}$ and
$N_{E_m/E_{m-1}}(b_m)=\nu_m$, and with $q=|E_{m-1}|$ the norm-one coordinate
$t_m=b_m^{q-1}$ satisfies
\[
 t_m+t_m^{-1}=\frac{(b_m^q+b_m)^2}{b_m^{q+1}}=\frac{b_{m-1}^2}{\nu_m},
 \qquad\text{that is,}\qquad
 \frac{t_m}{(t_m+1)^2}=\frac{\nu_m}{b_{m-1}^2}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Wiedemann sibling]\label{prop:z-wiedemann}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The sequences $(a_m)$ and $(x_m)$ are trace-compatible quadratic
  towers, with $\nu_m=a_{m-1}^3$ and $\nu_m=1$, and with norm-one
  coordinates $u_m$ and $x_m^{-2}$.  Thus $x_n\in T_n$, and Wiedemann's
  conjecture at level $n$ says that $x_n$ generates $T_n$.
\item[(b)] For each $m$, the compatible tuples $(x_0,\ldots,x_m)$ form one
  absolute-Frobenius orbit.
\item[(c)] The elements $u_n$ and $x_n$ are Frobenius-conjugate if and only
  if $n\leq2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) The nontrivial automorphism of $E_m/E_{m-1}$ sends $c_m$ to $c_m+1$,
hence $a_m=a_{m-1}c_m$ to $a_{m-1}(c_m+1)$, and it sends $x_m$ to $x_m^{-1}$.
Taking sums and products, and using $c_m(c_m+1)=a_{m-1}$, gives
$\operatorname{Tr}(a_m)=a_{m-1}$, $N(a_m)=a_{m-1}^3$,
$\operatorname{Tr}(x_m)=x_{m-1}$, and $N(x_m)=1$.  Moreover
$a_m\notin E_{m-1}$, because $a_{m-1}\in E_{m-1}^{\times}$ and
$c_m\notin E_{m-1}$.  The norm-one coordinates are
$a_m^{q-1}=(c_m+1)/c_m=u_m$ and $x_m^{q-1}=x_m^{-2}$.

(b) A compatible tuple is determined by its top entry, and there are
$2^{m+1}$ of them.  Every proper subfield of $E_m$ lies in $E_{m-1}$, which
does not contain $x_m$, so $x_m$ has $2^{m+1}$ distinct Frobenius conjugates.
Frobenius preserves the defining relations, so these conjugates are the top
entries of $2^{m+1}$ distinct compatible tuples, which are all of them.

(c) The map $z\mapsto z+z^{-1}$ commutes with every field automorphism, and
its fibres are the pairs $\{z,z^{-1}\}$; on $T_n$ one has $z^{-1}=z^q$, a
Frobenius conjugate of $z$.  Since $u_n+u_n^{-1}=a_{n-1}^{-1}$ and
$x_n+x_n^{-1}=x_{n-1}$, and every automorphism of $E_{n-1}$ extends to
$E_n$, the elements $u_n$ and $x_n$ are conjugate exactly when
$a_{n-1}^{-1}$ and $x_{n-1}$ are.  As $x_{n-1}^{-1}$ is a conjugate of
$x_{n-1}$, this happens exactly when $a_{n-1}$ and $x_{n-1}$ are conjugate.

Let $n\geq3$, and suppose that an automorphism $\sigma$ of $E_{n-1}$ sends
$a_{n-1}$ to $x_{n-1}$.  It preserves $E_{n-2}$ and commutes with
$N_{E_{n-1}/E_{n-2}}$, so (a) gives
\[
 1=N(x_{n-1})=\sigma\bigl(N(a_{n-1})\bigr)=\sigma(a_{n-2})^3.
\]
Hence $a_{n-2}^3=1$ and $a_{n-2}\in\mathbb F_4\subseteq E_{n-3}$, contrary to
$a_{n-2}\notin E_{n-3}$.

For $n\leq2$: one has $a_{-1}=x_{-1}=1$; next, $a_0=c_0$ and $x_0$ are both
roots of $X^2+X+1$; finally, $a_1$ has trace $a_0$ and norm $a_0^3=1$, so it
is a root of $X^2+a_0X+1$, which is the Wiedemann relation for the
compatible choice $x_0=a_0$.  By (b), $a_1$ is conjugate to $x_1$.
\end{proof}

For instance, $a_2^{17}=N(a_2)=a_1^3$ has order five, because $a_1$ is a
root of $X^2+a_0X+1$ and hence of $X^4+X^3+X^2+X+1$, while the image of
$a_2$ in $E_2^{\times}/E_1^{\times}$ is that of $c_2$, of order
$\delta_2=17$.  Thus $a_2$ has order $85$, whereas $x_2$ lies in $T_2$, of
order $17$.

In the trace-compatible coordinates the two recursions therefore share their
trace prescription and differ in their norm prescription.  On the selected
torus elements the parent laws are
\[
 \frac{x_n}{(x_n+1)^2}=\frac1{x_{n-1}},
 \qquad
 \frac{u_n}{(u_n+1)^2}=\frac{u_{n-1}}{(u_{n-1}+1)^3}
 \qquad(n\geq1),
\]
the second by Proposition~\ref{prop:z-torus} and
$c_{n-1}=(u_{n-1}+1)^{-1}$.  The first is the genus-zero correspondence of
iterated preimages of $z\mapsto z+z^{-1}$: every entry of a Wiedemann tuple
is a rational function of the top entry, so the analogue of the curve
$\mathcal X_r$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:z-iterate-ramification} is the
projective line at every depth.  The second is birational to the
supersingular curve of Proposition~\ref{prop:z-elliptic}, and its iterates
have the growing genus of Proposition~\ref{prop:z-iterate-ramification}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-packet-universal} is independent of the selected
ancestry and holds equally for Wiedemann's torus elements, whereas
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-trace-separation} concerns generic field elements
and gives no separation theorem inside the Fermat tori or along either
selected sequence.  The two selections differ only through their parent
laws, and part~(c) shows that they are distinct from level three on: no
isomorphism between Conway's and Wiedemann's presentations of $E_n$ carries
$u_n$ to $x_n$ when $n\geq3$.

\begin{remark}[ordinary realization]\label{rem:z-ordinary}
Wiedemann's tower is an isogeny division tower, on a curve of the opposite
type.  On the ordinary curve
\[
 \mathcal E'\colon\quad y^2+xy=x^3+1
\]
over $\mathbb F_2$, let $\pi$ be absolute Frobenius and $V$ its dual, so
that $V\pi=[2]$ and $V=[2]\circ\pi^{-1}$ on geometric points.  Duplication
gives $x([2]Q)=x(Q)^2+x(Q)^{-2}$, so
\[
 x(VQ)=x(Q)+x(Q)^{-1}\qquad(x(Q)\ne0).
\]
Voloch uses this identity to place Wiedemann's elements on $\mathcal E'$
\cite[Section~4]{voloch2010}, and Ugolini studies the dynamics of
$z\mapsto z+z^{-1}$ through the same abscissa action of the dual Frobenius
\cite{ugolini2012}.  Since $\pi$ preserves
orders, $\operatorname{ord}(VQ)=\operatorname{ord}(Q)/\gcd(2,\operatorname{ord}(Q))$.
The rational points $(1,0)$ and $(1,1)$ have order four, because
$[2](1,0)=(0,1)$ and $[2](0,1)=O$, and they are the only points with
abscissa one.  Every point of order at least eight has nonzero abscissa, so
$V^{n+1}$ carries a geometric point with abscissa a level-$n$ Wiedemann
element to a point of order four, and conversely.  Hence the level-$n$
Wiedemann elements are exactly the abscissas of the geometric points of
exact order $2^{n+3}$ on $\mathcal E'$.  Such a point is rational over
$E_n$: with $z=y/x_n$, its ordinate solves $z^2+z=x_n+x_n^{-2}$, whose
absolute trace is
$\operatorname{Tr}_{E_n/\mathbb F_2}(x_n+x_n^{-1})
 =\operatorname{Tr}_{E_n/\mathbb F_2}(x_{n-1})=0$.

Thus Wiedemann's tower is the abscissa tower of the $2$-primary torsion of
an ordinary curve, whereas the selected points of
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-elliptic} are annihilated by the odd number $F_n$ on
a supersingular curve and, as shown above, do not form a division tower.
Ordinary and supersingular curves are not isogenous, even over
$\overline{\mathbb F}_2$.  This separates the two geometric realizations; it
does not exclude other relations between the two multiplicative-order
questions.
\end{remark}

The ordinary realization carries a published asymptotic lower bound for
Wiedemann's tower.  Voloch proves $\operatorname{ord}(x_n)\geq\exp(2^{\delta n})$
for an unspecified $\delta>0$ and all large $n$ \cite{voloch2010}, and
Popovych proves the effective bound
$\operatorname{ord}(x_n)\geq7\cdot2^{n+2}+1$ for $n\geq12$ together with
$\operatorname{ord}(x_n)=F_n$ for $n\leq11$
\cite[Theorem~3]{popovych2015}.  For the Conway--Fermat chain the published
lower bound is the Euler--Lucas congruence bound
$\delta_n\geq2^{n+2}+1$ for $n\geq2$
\cite{popovych2018,caglierohermanszechtman2025}, which
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-packet-degree} recovers.  Voloch's theorem concerns
a fixed function $y$ on an elliptic curve evaluated at a point $P$ whose
cyclic group is Frobenius-stable and whose order $r$ is below
$[\mathbb F_q(P):\mathbb F_q]^{3/2-\epsilon}$
\cite[Theorem~4.1]{voloch2010}.  Its proof chooses many small integers $k$
in one coset $\gamma\langle a\rangle$ of the image of Frobenius in
$(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^{\times}$; with $C=[\gamma^{-1}]P$, the values
$y([k]C)$ are Frobenius conjugates of $y(P)$, and products of them are
separated by the multiplicative independence of the functions $y\circ[k]$.
On $\mathcal E'$ the image of Frobenius is everything up to sign: if $Q$ has
exact order $2^{n+3}$ and abscissa $x_n$, the $2^{n+1}$ conjugates of $x_n$
are abscissas of points of exact order $2^{n+3}$, and these are the
$2^{n+1}$ abscissas $x([k]Q)$ with $k$ odd.  In this specialization the
argument is explicit.

\begin{proposition}[explicit Wiedemann bound]\label{prop:z-wiedemann-bound}
For every $n\geq0$,
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(x_n)\geq2^t,
 \qquad t=\bigl\lfloor(3\cdot2^{n-2})^{1/3}\bigr\rfloor.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $Q$ be as above and put $f_k=x\circ[k]$ for odd $k\geq1$, a function of
degree $2k^2$ on $\mathcal E'$.  Each $f_k(Q)$ is a conjugate of $x_n$, hence
lies in $\langle x_n\rangle$.  The functions $f_k$ are multiplicatively
independent: $\operatorname{div}(x)=2[T]-2[O]$ with $T=(0,1)$, and for the
largest $k$ in a putative relation, a geometric point $R$ of exact order $2k$
is a zero of $f_k$, whereas for odd $\ell<k$ the point $[\ell]R$ is neither
$T$ nor $O$.  If two subset products of $f_1(Q),f_3(Q),\ldots,f_{2t-1}(Q)$
coincide, the quotient of the corresponding functions minus one is a nonzero
function over $\mathbb F_2$ vanishing at $Q$, hence at its $2^{n+1}$
conjugates, while its degree is at most
\[
 2\sum_{j=1}^{t}(2j-1)^2=\frac{8t^3-2t}{3}<2^{n+1}.
\]
Thus the $2^t$ subset products are distinct elements of
$\langle x_n\rangle$.
\end{proof}

This beats Popovych's effective bound from $n=13$ on, and it is far from
$F_n$.  The selected Conway element also has a fixed-function realization,
$u_{n+1}=g(P_n)$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:z-elliptic}, but its Frobenius
image is thin.

\begin{proposition}[Frobenius-thin Conway torsion]
\label{prop:z-voloch-boundary}
Let $n\geq2$, put $h=2^{2^{n-1}}$, and let $r$ be the order of the point
$P_n$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:z-elliptic}.  Then $r\mid F_n$, the cyclic
group $\langle P_n\rangle$ is stable under $\iota$ and absolute Frobenius,
$\iota$ acts on it as multiplication by $h$ or by $-h$, and Frobenius acts
through the subgroup
\[
 H_n=\langle2\rangle\cup(h+1)\langle2\rangle
 \subseteq(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^{\times},
 \qquad |H_n|=2^{n+2}=[\mathbb F_2(P_n):\mathbb F_2].
\]
In particular $r>2^{n+2}$.  Suppose $r=F_n$.  Then no odd integer $k$ with
$1<|k|<h-1$ lies in $H_n$, and for distinct odd positive integers $k,k'$
with $kk'<h/2$ there is no congruence $k'\equiv\alpha k\pmod{F_n}$ with
$\alpha\in H_n$; when both are units, they lie in distinct cosets of
$H_n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-elliptic} gives $[F_n]P_n=O$.  Let
$\sigma=\pi^{2^n}$ be the $|E_{n-1}|$-power Frobenius.  It sends $c_n$ to
$c_n+1$, hence $a_n=a_{n-1}c_n$ to $a_n+a_{n-1}$, so it sends $c_{n+1}$ to a
root of $Z^2+Z=a_n+a_{n-1}$, namely $c_{n+1}+c_n$ or $c_{n+1}+c_n+1$.  By the
coordinate formula for $\iota$ and $-(x,y)=(x,y+1)$, this says
$\sigma(P_n)=\pm\iota P_n$.  On the other hand $\pi^4=[-4]$ gives
$\sigma=[(-4)^{2^{n-2}}]=[\pm h]$.  Hence $\iota P_n=\pm hP_n$, and since
$\pi=\iota-[1]$, Frobenius acts on $\langle P_n\rangle$ as multiplication by
$m=\pm h-1$.

Modulo $r$ one has $h^2=2^{2^n}\equiv-1$, so $-1$ and $h$ lie in
$\langle2\rangle$, which has order $2^{n+1}$.  Since
$(h+1)^2\equiv2h\in\langle2\rangle$, the set $H_n$ is a subgroup of order
at most $2^{n+2}$.  Moreover $(h+1)h=h-1$, so $\pm h-1\in(h+1)\langle2\rangle$
and $\langle m\rangle\subseteq H_n$.  The group $\langle m\rangle$ has the
order $2^{n+2}$ of the Frobenius orbit of $P_n$, which is the degree of
$c_{n+1}$.  Hence $\langle m\rangle=H_n$, and $\varphi(r)\geq2^{n+2}$ gives
$r>2^{n+2}$.

Now let $r=F_n=h^2+1$, and note that
$\langle2\rangle=\{\pm2^b:0\leq b<2^n\}$ modulo $F_n$.  Since
$(h+1)2^{b+2^{n-1}}\equiv(h-1)2^b$, the residues of $H_n$ in $(0,F_n)$ are
$2^b$ and $F_n-2^b$ for $0\leq b<2^n$, together with $(h\pm1)2^b$ and
$F_n-(h\pm1)2^b$ for $0\leq b<2^{n-1}$.  The odd ones are $1$, $h\pm1$, and
residues $F_n-y$ with $y\leq(h+1)2^{2^{n-1}-1}$, which are at least
$2^{2^n-1}+1-h/2>h$.  Since $-1\in H_n$, the first claim follows.

For the second, suppose $k'\equiv\epsilon k2^b$ or
$k'\equiv\epsilon k(h+1)2^b$ with $\epsilon=\pm1$ and $0\leq b<2^n$.  In the
first case, if $k2^b<F_n/2$, then $k'-\epsilon k2^b$ is a nonzero integer of
absolute value below $F_n$: it is odd when $b\geq1$, and $k'\ne\pm k$ when
$b=0$.  If $k2^b\geq F_n/2$, multiplication by $2^{2^n-b}<2k$ gives
$2^{2^n-b}k'+\epsilon k\equiv0$, an odd integer of absolute value below
$2kk'+k<F_n$.  In the second case, multiplication by $h-1$ and
$(h+1)(h-1)\equiv-2$ give $k'(h-1)+\epsilon k2^{B}\equiv0$ with $B=b+1$.  If
$k2^B<F_n/2$, the left side is an odd integer of absolute value below
$k'h+F_n/2<F_n$.  Otherwise multiplication by $2^{2^n-B}<2k$ gives
$k'(h-1)2^{2^n-B}-\epsilon k\equiv0$, where the left side has absolute value
below $2kk'(h-1)+k<F_n$ and is nonzero: by parity if $B<2^n$, and because
$k'(h-1)\geq h-1>k$ if $B=2^n$.  Each case contradicts $F_n$ dividing a
nonzero integer of smaller absolute value.
\end{proof}

For $2\leq n\leq6$ a direct computation gives $r=F_n$; whether
$\operatorname{ord}(P_n)=F_n$ for every $n$ is itself a selected-order
question.  When $r=F_n$ and $n\geq3$, Theorem~4.1 of \cite{voloch2010} does
not apply to $g(P_n)$, because $F_n>2^{3(n+2)/2}$.  Under $r=F_n$ the usual
odd-multiplier argument also meets a counting obstruction.  Its standard
degree estimate separates products when the sum of their function degrees,
proportional to $\sum k^2$, is below the degree $d=2^{n+2}$ of $P_n$.  For
$n\geq4$, any two distinct odd multipliers within this budget satisfy
$kk'<d\leq h/2$, so each Frobenius coset contains at most one, and this
sufficient budget cannot supply the growing family of multipliers that
Voloch's argument uses.  The first nontrivial odd multiplication map
representing Frobenius itself, $[h-1]$, has degree $(h-1)^2>2^{2^n-1}$.
None of this excludes sharper degree estimates that exploit cancellation,
even multipliers, other counting schemes, non-integer endomorphisms, or other
realizations of $u_n$.  A realization satisfying Voloch's
hypotheses would be an independent ancestry input in the sense of
Section~\ref{sec:nim-reciprocity}; none is known.  Popovych's effective
exclusions \cite[Theorem~2]{popovych2015}, valid for $i\geq2$, use trivial
intersections between the cyclic groups of parent coordinates, such as
$\langle x_{i-1}\rangle\cap\langle x_{i-2}\rangle=1$.  For the trace-compatible Conway parents
that intersection is nontrivial when $i\geq3$: by
Proposition~\ref{prop:z-wiedemann}(a),
$a_{i-2}^3=N(a_{i-1})=a_{i-1}^{F_{i-1}}$ lies in
$\langle a_{i-1}\rangle\cap\langle a_{i-2}\rangle$ and is not $1$.

\section{The ordinary arm: a marked cyclotomic value}\label{sec:o}

Fix an odd prime $r$, write $b=b_r$, and abbreviate
$x_a=x_{r,a}$, $y_a=x_a+1$, and $F_a=F_{r,a}$.

\begin{proposition}[projective order criterion]\label{prop:o-projective}
For $a\geq1$,
\[
 \operatorname N_{F_a/F_{a-1}}(y_a)=y_{a-1},
 \qquad
 |F_a^{\times}/F_{a-1}^{\times}|=L_{r,a}.
\]
If $p\ne r$ divides $L_{r,a}$, then the corresponding singleton row has
\[
 m_p=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_p\!\left(\operatorname{ord}
 (y_aF_{a-1}^{\times})\right)=v_p(L_{r,a}).
\]
Every singleton row with component $r^a$ arises this way.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The relative conjugates of $x_a$ are $\xi x_a$ for $\xi\in\mu_r$;
evaluating their binomial at one gives the norm identity.  The quotient order
is the quotient of the two finite-field unit orders.  The cyclotomic divisor
criterion gives $\operatorname{ord}_p(2^b)=r^a$ for $p\ne r$ dividing
$L_{r,a}$, and Lenstra's component theorem identifies precisely the
singleton rows.  The lower unit group has no $p$-part, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:power} gives the last equivalence.
\end{proof}

The full recursive ancestry can also be retained in one binary polynomial.
Let $P_r\in\mathbb F_2[T]$ be the minimal polynomial of
$x_0=\alpha_r$.

\begin{proposition}[bottom-ancestor divisibility]
\label{prop:o-bottom-divisibility}
Put $q=|F_{a-1}|$, $N=L_{r,a}$, and let $p\ne r$ divide $N$.
Then $\gcd(p,q-1)=1$.  Write $d=N/p$, and choose $e,t\geq0$ such that
\[
 pe=1+t(q-1).
\]
Then the current $p$-coordinate of $y_aF_{a-1}^{\times}$ is deficient if
and only if
\[
 P_r(T^{r^a})\mid
 (1+T)^d+(1+T^r)^e
 \qquad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[T].
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, $N\equiv r\pmod {q-1}$, so
$\gcd(N,q-1)=r$.
The polynomial $P_r(T^{r^a})$ is the minimal polynomial of $x_a$.
The coordinate is deficient exactly when $c=(1+x_a)^d$ lies in
$F_{a-1}^{\times}$.  In that case the norm identity gives
\[
 c^p=(1+x_a)^N=1+x_a^r.
\]
Since $c^{q-1}=1$ and $pe=1+t(q-1)$,
$c=(c^p)^e=(1+x_a^r)^e$.  Conversely this equality puts $c$ in
$F_{a-1}$.  In characteristic two the equality is precisely the vanishing
at $x_a$ of the displayed binary polynomial, which is equivalent to
divisibility by its minimal polynomial.
\end{proof}

This is a lossless selector-retaining reduction, not a proof of arm~$O$:
the remaining assertion is that the displayed divisibility never occurs for
the literal polynomial $P_r$ and every current factor $p$.

For a polynomial $B(T)=\sum_m b_mT^m$ and $0\leq j<n$, define its
$j$-th $n$-section by
\[
 \operatorname{Sec}_{n,j}(B)(U)=\sum_{k\geq0}b_{j+kn}U^k.
\]

\begin{lemma}[polynomial section criterion]
\label{lem:o-section}
Let $k$ be a field, let $0\ne P\in k[U]$, and let $n\geq1$.  Then
\[
 P(T^n)\mid B(T)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 P(U)\mid\operatorname{Sec}_{n,j}(B)(U)
 \quad(0\leq j<n).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every polynomial has the unique decomposition
$B(T)=\sum_{j<n}T^j\operatorname{Sec}_{n,j}(B)(T^n)$.
Multiplication by $P(T^n)$ preserves each residue class of exponents modulo
$n$.  Applying the decomposition to the quotient proves the forward
implication, and summing the sectionwise quotients proves the converse.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Lucas-section ordinary case]
\label{cor:o-large-factor}
Put $n=r^a$, let $p\ne r$ divide $N=L_{r,a}$, and put $d=N/p$.
If some $0\leq j<n$ satisfies
\[
 r\nmid j,\qquad \binom dj\equiv1\pmod2,
 \qquad d-j<bn,
\]
then the current $p$-coordinate of $y_aF_{a-1}^{\times}$ has full primary
order.  In particular this holds if $d\leq bn$, and hence when $N/r$ is a
prime distinct from $r$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the polynomial of Proposition~\ref{prop:o-bottom-divisibility}, the
$j$-th section receives no term from $(1+T^r)^e$, since $r\mid n$ but
$r\nmid j$.  Its other summand has constant coefficient one and degree at
most $\lfloor(d-j)/n\rfloor<b$.  It is therefore a nonzero polynomial of degree
less than $\deg P_r=b$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:o-section} rules out the required
divisibility.  Finally, $d$ is odd, so when $d\leq bn$ one may take $j=1$.
If $p=N/r$, then $d=r\leq br^a$.
\end{proof}

There is a selector-sensitive strengthening obtained by retaining the two
opposite nonzero sections at the top Kummer step.  For a monic polynomial
$H$ with constant coefficient one, write
\[
 H^*(U)=U^{\deg H}H(U^{-1})
\]
for its reciprocal.

\begin{proposition}[reciprocal doubling in the ordinary arm]
\label{prop:o-reciprocal}
Put $n=r^a$, let $p\ne r$ divide $N=L_{r,a}$, and put $d=N/p$.
For $0\leq j<r$ define the top $r$-sections
\[
 A_j(W)=\operatorname{Sec}_{r,j}((1+T)^d)(W)
       =\sum_{k\geq0}\binom d{j+kr}W^k.
\]
Set $s=r^{a-1}$ and
\[
 M(W)=P_r(W^s),\qquad M^*(W)=P_r^*(W^s).
\]
Then $r\mid d$, and, writing $h=d/r$, one has the exact section reciprocity
\[
 A_{r-1}(W)=W^{h-1}A_1(W^{-1}).
\]
If the current $p$-coordinate is deficient, then both $M$ and $M^*$ divide
$A_1$.  Consequently, if $P_r\ne P_r^*$, deficiency forces
\[
 d\geq 2br^a+r.
\]
Equivalently, when $P_r\ne P_r^*$, every current factor satisfying
$d\leq2br^a$ has full primary order.

This non-self-reciprocity condition is independent of the tower level.  More
explicitly, if $f=\operatorname{ord}_r(2)$, then $f\mid b$, and it holds
automatically whenever $f$ is odd or $b/f$ is even.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The relative Kummer construction has $x_a^r=x_{a-1}$ and degree $r$; as
above, its nonpower radicand gives $r\mid2^b-1$.  Therefore
\[
 N=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(2^b)^{ir^{a-1}}
   \equiv r\equiv0\pmod r.
\]
As $p\ne r$, this gives $r\mid d$.

Put $K=F_{a-1}$, $z=x_a$, and $w=z^r=x_{a-1}$.  The polynomial $M$ is the
minimal polynomial of $w$ over $\mathbb F_2$.  If the coordinate is
deficient, then $(1+z)^d\in K$.  Expanding in the $K$-basis
$1,z,\ldots,z^{r-1}$ shows that
\[
 A_j(w)=0\qquad(1\leq j<r),
\]
so $M$ divides every nonzero top section.  Since $r\mid d$, also
$z^d=w^{d/r}\in K$, and therefore
\[
 (1+z^{-1})^d=z^{-d}(1+z)^d\in K.
\]
Expansion in the inverse basis gives $A_j(w^{-1})=0$ for $1\leq j<r$.
The minimal polynomial of $w^{-1}$ is $M^*$, so $M^*$ divides $A_1$.

The displayed section reciprocity follows directly from
$\binom d\ell=\binom d{d-\ell}$ and $d=rh$: the coefficient indexed by
$1+kr$ is paired with the one indexed by
$(r-1)+(h-k-1)r$.  The polynomial $P_r$ has constant coefficient one
because $x_0\ne0$, and hence $M$ and $M^*$ are monic irreducibles.  If
$P_r\ne P_r^*$, then
\[
 M^*(W)=P_r^*(W^s)\ne P_r(W^s)=M(W),
\]
so they are coprime.  Thus $MM^*\mid A_1$ under deficiency.  But $A_1$ is
nonzero, since its constant coefficient is $\binom d1=1$, and
\[
 \deg A_1\leq h-1=\frac dr-1,
 \qquad \deg(MM^*)=2bs.
\]
It follows that $d/r-1\geq2br^{a-1}$, which is the claimed lower bound.
Because $r\mid d$, its contrapositive is equivalently $d\leq2br^a$.

It remains to prove the arithmetic sufficient condition.  The first Kummer
step shows that $x_0$ is not an $r$-th power in
$F_0^\times$: otherwise, since $\mu_r\subset F_0$, the chosen $r$-th root
$x_1$ would already lie in $F_0$.  Hence $r\mid\operatorname{ord}(x_0)$.
If $P_r=P_r^*$, then $x_0^{-1}=x_0^{2^j}$ for some $0<j<b$.  Since the
Frobenius orbit has length $b$, this forces $b$ even, $j=b/2$, and
\[
 2^{b/2}\equiv-1\pmod {\operatorname{ord}(x_0)},
 \quad\text{hence}\quad
 2^{b/2}\equiv-1\pmod r.
\]
Writing $f=\operatorname{ord}_r(2)$, the last congruence requires $f$ even
and $b/f$ odd.  The stated alternatives are the contrapositive.
\end{proof}

The apparent self-reciprocal exception in
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-reciprocal} cannot be the first failure of the
$0/1/4$ rule.  The algebraic input is the following affine Kummer lemma.

\begin{lemma}[affine odd-Kummer nonunitarity]
\label{lem:o-affine-kummer}
Let $E$ be a field of characteristic two, let $q>1$ be odd, and let
$a,u\in E^\times$.  Suppose that $t$ has minimal polynomial $X^q-a$ over
$E$.  Then, for every $k\geq0$,
\[
 (t+u)^{2^k+1}\ne1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $Q=2^k$ and write $Q=Aq+v$ with $0<v<q$; the residue is nonzero because
$q$ is odd.  If the displayed equality held, then Frobenius additivity would
give
\[
 0=(t+u)^{Q+1}+1
  =t^{Q+1}+u t^Q+u^Qt+u^{Q+1}+1.             \tag{\(*\)}
\]
Reduce this expression in the $E$-basis $1,t,\ldots,t^{q-1}$ using
$t^q=a$.  If $v=1$, the coefficient of $t^2$ is $a^A\ne0$.  If
$2\leq v\leq q-2$, the coefficient of $t^{v+1}$ is $a^A\ne0$.  If
$v=q-1$, the coefficient of $t^{q-1}$ is $ua^A\ne0$.  Each contradicts
\((*)\), and these cases exhaust the possible nonzero residues.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[bottom reciprocity cannot be a first failure]
\label{prop:o-bottom-not-first}
Let $r\ne3$ be an odd prime.  If the $0/1/4$ rule holds at the row $r$
itself, then the literal bottom polynomial is not self-reciprocal:
\[
 P_r\ne P_r^*.
\]
The excluded prime really is exceptional: $\alpha_3=2$ and
$P_3(X)=X^2+X+1=P_3^*(X)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $f=\operatorname{ord}_r(2)$ and use the Conway--Lenstra component
decomposition
\[
 \alpha_r=\kappa_f+m_r,
 \qquad
 \kappa_f=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q(f)}\kappa_q.
\]
If $P_r=P_r^*$ and $b=d(\alpha_r)$, then $b$ is even and
\[
 \alpha_r^{2^{b/2}+1}=1.                     \tag{\(**\)}
\]

Suppose first that $\mathcal Q(f)$ contains more than one component.  The
rule gives $m_r=0$.  Choose the largest odd component $s^c$ and put
$t=\kappa_{s^c}$.  Let $E$ be generated by $\alpha_s$, all the other-prime
components, and the finite translate, all at once.  The element $\alpha_s$
lies strictly below the $s$-component, and every other component prime is
smaller than $s$ by the choice of the largest odd component.  Thus none of
these generators contributes any $s$-primary field degree.  The full
$s^c$-Kummer component therefore remains after passing to $E$, and the
component theorem gives
\[
 t^{s^c}=\alpha_s,
 \qquad [E(t):E]=s^c;
\]
in particular $1,t,\ldots,t^{s^c-1}$ is a power basis.  The sum $u$ of the
remaining components is a nonzero element of $E$, by uniqueness of the
Conway component normal form.  Lemma~\ref{lem:o-affine-kummer}, with
$Q=2^{b/2}$, contradicts \((**)\).

If $\mathcal Q(f)=\{s^c\}$ is a singleton odd prime power, the rule gives
$m_r=1$, except when $f=2\cdot3^j$, when it gives $m_r=4$.  In the first
case take $t=\kappa_{s^c}$ and $u=1$.  In the exceptional case take
$t=\kappa_{3^j}$ and $u=4$; adjoining the finite field containing $4$ to
the coefficient field leaves the odd relative degree $3^j$.  The same
lemma again contradicts \((**)\).

The remaining possibility is a singleton even component.  Then the rule
gives $m_r=0$, and $f=2^j$.  The selected Artin--Schreier generator
$c=\kappa_{2^j}$ has degree $2^j$ and its nontrivial conjugate over the
half field is $c+1$.  Since $r\ne3$, one has $2^j>2$.  Equation \((**)\)
would give $c(c+1)=1$, so $c$ would satisfy $X^2+X+1$, contradicting its
degree.  This proves the assertion.  For $r=3$, the displayed polynomial
is the defining quadratic and is self-reciprocal.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[reciprocal sieve from a valid bottom row]
\label{cor:o-minimal-reciprocal}
Let $r\ne3$ be an odd prime at which the proposed $0/1/4$ rule holds,
let $a\geq1$, and let $p\ne r$ be a prime divisor of $L_{r,a}$.
Then $P_r\ne P_r^*$.  Consequently, with $d=L_{r,a}/p$, a deficient
current $p$-coordinate must satisfy
\[
 d\geq2b_r r^a+r;
\]
thus every such coordinate with $d\leq2b_r r^a$ has full primary order.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The explicit hypothesis at $r$ gives $P_r\ne P_r^*$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-bottom-not-first}.  Apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-reciprocal}.  No hypothesis about other rows is
needed.
\end{proof}

The full Kummer orbit gives a second sieve which is substantially stronger
when the tower level is large.

\begin{proposition}[Kummer-ratio packing over minimal coefficient fields]
\label{prop:o-ratio-packing}
Let $r$ be any odd prime, $b=b_r$, and $a\ge1$, and let
$o$ be the order of the literal selected class
$(1+x_{r,a})F_{r,a-1}^{\times}$.  For $1\le m\le a$ put
\[
 t_m=\operatorname{ord}_{r^m}(2),\qquad
 D_m=r^{m-1}(r-1),\qquad
 R_m=\frac{br^a}{t_m}.
\]
These are positive integers, and $R_m\ge r$.  Write
\[
 \mathcal R(D,s)=\sum_{t=0}^s2^t\binom Dt\binom st
\]
and define the parity-sensitive bound
\[
 \mathcal B_r(D,R)=
 \begin{cases}
  \mathcal R(D,R/2),&R\text{ even},\\[2pt]
  \displaystyle\mathcal R(D,(R-1)/2)+
  \left\lceil\frac{
   \mathcal R(D,(R+1)/2)-\mathcal R(D,(R-1)/2)}r\right\rceil,
   &R\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
 B_{r,a}:=
 \max_{1\le m\le a}\mathcal B_r(D_m,R_m)\le o.
\]
Consequently, a deficient current prime $p\ne r$ must satisfy
$L_{r,a}/p\ge B_{r,a}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $v=v_r(2^b-1)\ge1$, $K=F_{r,a-1}$, $L=F_{r,a}$,
$x=x_{r,a}$, and $y=1+x$.  Fix $m$.  LTE gives
\[
 v_r(2^{br^{a-1}}-1)=v+a-1\ge a\ge m.
\]
Thus $t_m\mid br^{a-1}$ and the smallest finite field containing
$\mu_{r^m}$ is
\[
 H_m=\mathbb F_{2^{t_m}}\subset K.
\]
Since $x$ generates $L$ over $\mathbb F_2$, its minimal polynomial over
$H_m$ has degree $[L:H_m]=R_m\ge r$ and nonzero constant coefficient.

Let $\sigma$ be the $|K|$-Frobenius on $L$, and put $u=\sigma(y)/y$.
The Hilbert--90 isomorphism
$L^\times/K^\times\longrightarrow\ker\operatorname N_{L/K}$
gives $\operatorname{ord}(u)=o$: its kernel is $K^\times$ and both
finite groups have order $L_{r,a}$.
Choose representatives $\mathcal C_m$ for $\mu_{r^m}/\mu_r$.
For $c\in\mathcal C_m$ and $\zeta\in\mu_r\setminus\{1\}$ set
\[
 u_{c,\zeta}=\frac{1+\zeta cx}{1+cx}.
\]
There are exactly $D_m$ such ratios, all with coefficients in $H_m$.
Their numerators and denominators are nonzero: otherwise $x\in H_m$,
contrary to $R_m>1$.

Every ratio belongs to $\langle u\rangle$.  Indeed, $m\le a$ makes
$cx$ a root of the literal irreducible polynomial $P_r(T^{r^a})$,
so $cx=x^{2^e}$ for some $e$.  The automorphism $\sigma$ fixes $c$,
and $\sigma(x)/x$ generates $\mu_r$.  Choose $k$ such that
$\sigma^k(cx)=\zeta cx$.  Commutativity of absolute and relative
Frobenius gives
\[
 u_{c,\zeta}
 =u^{2^e(1+|K|+\cdots+|K|^{k-1})}.
\]

Index the ratios by $I$, with $|I|=D_m$, and evaluate integer vectors by
$U_w=\prod_{i\in I}u_i^{w_i}$.  A signed ball
$\sum_i|w_i|\le s$ has exactly $\mathcal R(D_m,s)$ elements:
choose its $t$ nonzero coordinates, their signs, and their positive
absolute values with sum at most $s$.

Suppose first that $R_m$ is even.  Include the entire signed ball of
radius $R_m/2$.  If $U_w=U_{w'}$, put $g=w-w'$ and clear denominators
without cancelling common factors.  This gives $A(x)=B(x)$ with
$A,B\in H_m[T]$, both constant coefficients one, and both degrees
$\sum_i|g_i|\le R_m$.
The minimal polynomial of $x$ divides $A-B$.  A nonzero difference
would therefore be a scalar multiple of that minimal polynomial,
contradicting its zero constant coefficient.  Hence $A=B$.

If $R_m$ is odd, put $s=(R_m-1)/2$ and include the inner ball of
radius $s$ together with a largest fibre, in the shell of radius $s+1$,
of the leading-phase character
\[
 \chi(w)=\prod_{(c,\zeta)}\zeta^{w_{c,\zeta}}\in\mu_r.
\]
The chosen family has cardinality at least $\mathcal B_r(D_m,R_m)$.
For a collision with at least one inner-ball vector, the same
cross-product degree is at most $2s+1=R_m$.  For two shell vectors,
it is at most $2s+2=R_m+1$, but equality of leading phases cancels
the leading coefficient.  Thus $\deg(A-B)\le R_m$ again, and the
same zero-constant argument gives $A=B$.

In either parity, different $c$-cosets have disjoint stars of linear
factors.  Within each star, valuation at $1+\zeta cT$ recovers the
corresponding signed exponent.  Unique factorization makes $A=B$
force $g=0$.  The chosen vectors therefore inject into the same cyclic
group $\langle u\rangle$.  The normalized rational evaluation injection
and the resulting bound by $\operatorname{ord}(u)$ are kernel-checked in
\texttt{ExcessOrdinaryPacking}; finite-field ancestry, the disjoint-factor
comparison, and the lattice count remain paper-level inputs.  Taking the
maximum over $m$ proves the bound.
Under a deficient current $p$-coordinate, $o\mid L_{r,a}/p$, proving
the stated sieve.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[intermediate-field recovery and explicit growth]
\label{cor:o-ratio-growth}
Put $v=v_r(2^b-1)$.  For $0\le j<a$, set
\[
 m_j=\min\{a,j+v\},\quad
 D'_j=r^{m_j-1}(r-1),\quad R'_j=r^{a-j},\quad s_j=(R'_j-1)/2.
\]
Then $B_{r,a}$ is at least
\[
 \mathcal R(D'_j,s_j)+
 \left\lceil\frac{\mathcal R(D'_j,s_j+1)-\mathcal R(D'_j,s_j)}r
 \right\rceil
\]
for every $j$.  The case $j=a-1$ uses
$D=r^{a-1}(r-1)$ and signed radius $(r-1)/2$.  Moreover,
\[
 \log_2 o\ge
 (\log_2 3)(1-1/r)\sqrt{r^a}-\tfrac12\log_2 3.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
LTE puts $\mu_{r^{m_j}}$ in $F_{r,j}$, so
$t_{m_j}\mid br^j$ and hence $R_{m_j}\ge R'_j$.
If these degrees are equal, they are odd and the displayed term is
exactly $\mathcal B_r(D_{m_j},R_{m_j})$.
If they are unequal, $R_{m_j}\ge R'_j+1=2(s_j+1)$, so the larger
degree bound contains the entire ball of radius $s_j+1$, which already
dominates the displayed inner-ball-plus-partial-shell term.

For the growth assertion choose $j=\lfloor(a-1)/2\rfloor$ and put
$h=\min\{D'_j,s_j\}$.  The inner ball contains $\{-1,0,1\}^h$,
so its size is at least $3^h$.  Since $v\ge1$,
$D'_j\ge(r-1)r^j$.  If $a=2k$, then
$h\ge(r-1)r^{k-1}$.  If $a=2k+1$, then
$h\ge(r^{k+1}-1)/2$.  In both cases
$h\ge(1-1/r)r^{a/2}-1/2$, already for $r\ge3$.
\end{proof}

For fixed $r$ and bottom ancestry this packing bound is exponential in
$\sqrt{r^a}$, whereas $L_{r,a}$ is exponential in $r^a$.  To see the upper
scale as well, put $v_0=v_r(2^{t_1}-1)$.  Order lifting gives
$t_m=t_1r^{\max(0,m-v_0)}$, so $D_mR_m=O(r^a)$ uniformly in $m$.
The elementary estimate
\[
 \mathcal R(D,s)\le\sum_{t\ge0}\frac{(2Ds)^t}{(t!)^2}
 \le\exp(2\sqrt{2Ds})
\]
and $\mathcal B_r(D,R)\le\mathcal R(D,\lceil R/2\rceil)$ therefore give
$\log B_{r,a}=O(\sqrt{r^a})$.  Thus the theorem proves a selected
large-factor range, not universal arm~$O$.  Bounds from different coefficient
fields cannot simply be multiplied: they count subsets of the same cyclic
group, and a mixed collision must be tested over the largest coefficient
field present.  The statement includes $r=3$; its cubic interpretation is
given in Proposition~\ref{prop:c-ratio-packing}.

\begin{corollary}[ordinary congruence-gap regimes]
\label{cor:o-one-primary}
Fix an odd prime $r\ne3$ and $a\geq1$.  Let $N=L_{r,a}$ and let $o$ be
the selected projective order.  Write $N=o\Delta$, and let
$\Delta^{(r')}$ be the prime-to-$r$ part of $\Delta$.  Suppose
$\Delta^{(r')}>1$, and let $p$ be its smallest prime divisor.
Let $B_{r,a}$ be the minimal-coefficient-field maximum in
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-ratio-packing}, and put
\[
 M=2r^a,\qquad
 A_{<p}=\prod_{\substack{q<p,\ q\ne r\\q\mid N}}q^{v_q(N)},
 \qquad B_*=\max\{B_{r,a},A_{<p}\}.
\]
Then every prime divisor of $\Delta^{(r')}$ is at least $p$ and is
congruent to $1$ modulo $M$, and
\[
 N<B_*p^2
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Delta^{(r')}=p.
\]
In the larger range
\[
 N<B_*p(p+M)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Delta^{(r')}=p\quad\hbox{or}\quad p^2\mid\Delta^{(r')}.
\]
If $v_p(N)=1$, the second alternative is impossible.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By definition $p\mid\Delta^{(r')}$, and its minimality among the deficient
coordinates of this quotient makes $\Delta^{(r')}$ $p$-rough.  For every such
prime $q\ne r$, the cyclotomic divisor theorem gives
$\operatorname{ord}_q(2^{b_r})=r^a$.  Since $q$ is odd and $r^a$ is odd,
this implies $q\equiv1\pmod {2r^a}$.

The unconditional part of Proposition~\ref{prop:o-ratio-packing} gives
$B_{r,a}\le o$.  Minimality also forces the full $q$-primary part of $N$
into $o$ for every $q<p$, $q\ne r$, so $A_{<p}\mid o$ and therefore
$B_*\le o$.  Hence
\[
 B_*\Delta^{(r')}\le B_*\Delta\le o\Delta=N.
\]
If $\Delta^{(r')}\ne p$, its quotient by $p$ has a prime divisor at least
$p$, proving the first implication.  If $p^2\nmid\Delta^{(r')}$, that prime
is distinct from $p$ and congruence spacing makes it at least $p+M$; hence
$\Delta^{(r')}\ge p(p+M)$.  This proves the second implication and the
valuation-one conclusion.  A surviving square factor similarly makes $p$
a base-$2^{b_r}$ Wieferich prime, since $r^a\mid p-1$ and
$(2^{b_r})^{r^a}\equiv1\pmod {p^2}$.
\end{proof}

Let $N_a=\operatorname{ord}(x_a)$.  The Kummer relation
$x_a^r=x_{a-1}$ and the relative degree $r$ give
$N_a=rN_{a-1}$.  Choose the roots and primes recursively:
$\xi_{N_a}$ is a primitive complex $N_a$-th root satisfying
\[
 \xi_{N_a}^r=\xi_{N_{a-1}},
\]
and $\mathfrak P_a\mid2$ is the prime whose residue sends
$\xi_{N_a}$ to $x_a$.  Since $x_a^r=x_{a-1}$, this makes
$\mathfrak P_a$ restrict to $\mathfrak P_{a-1}$.  Put
$\varepsilon_a=1-\xi_{N_a}$.
For a row prime $p$, use the intrinsic residue-field notation
\[
 \left(\frac{\varepsilon_a}{\mathfrak P_a}\right)_p
 :=\overline{\varepsilon_a}^{\,(|F_a|-1)/p}\in\mu_p(F_a).
\]
This is an Euler symbol in the selected residue field.  It agrees with the
usual local power-residue/Artin symbol after adjoining $\mu_p$ and choosing
the prime above $\mathfrak P_a$.

\begin{theorem}[selected cyclotomic-value form]\label{thm:o-symbol}
For $p\ne r$ dividing $L_{r,a}$, arm $O$ at $(r,a,p)$ is equivalent to
\[
  \left(\frac{\varepsilon_a}{\mathfrak P_a}\right)_p\ne1.
\]
The compatible primes $\mathfrak P_a$ form a unique inert ray above two:
$\mathfrak P_a$ is the unique prime above $\mathfrak P_{a-1}$, with
relative residue degree $r$, and
\[
 \operatorname N_{\mathbb Q(\xi_{N_a})/
                     \mathbb Q(\xi_{N_{a-1}})}(1-\xi_{N_a})
 =1-\xi_{N_{a-1}}.
\]
Nevertheless, residue-field corestriction kills the new $p$-coordinate and
does not determine the displayed residue symbol.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The reduction of $\varepsilon_a$ is $y_a$.  The unramified power-residue
formula gives
\[
 \left(\frac{\varepsilon_a}{\mathfrak P_a}\right)_p
 =y_a^{(|F_a|-1)/p},
\]
so Proposition~\ref{prop:power} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-projective} give the equivalence.

The Kummer steps increase the $r$-primary part of $N_a$ by one and give
$\operatorname{ord}_{N_a}(2)=br^a$.  The relative cyclotomic degree and
the residue-degree ratio are therefore both $r$, forcing one prime above the
preceding selected prime.  The global norm identity is the product over the
$r$-th roots of unity.  Finally,
$p\mid\Phi_{r^a}(2^b)$ but $p\nmid2^{br^{a-1}}-1$, so the lower residue
field has trivial $p$-Kummer quotient.  Its norm receives no information
about the new selected class.
\end{proof}

The word \emph{value} in the theorem is deliberate.  The element
$1-\xi_{N_a}$ is a cyclotomic number and is an $S$-unit for the primes
dividing $N_a$; it is a global unit exactly when $N_a$ is not a prime power.
For example, in the $r=3$ chain one has $N_a=3^{a+1}$ and its absolute norm
is $3$.  This distinction does not alter reduction modulo the selected prime
over two or the relative norm identity, but any global Kummer formulation
must retain the conductor primes in $S$ (or in the ray modulus).

The selected symbol has an exact Jacobi--Fourier expansion.  Let
$Q=|F_a|$, let $H=\langle x_a\rangle\subset F_a^\times$ have order $N_a$,
put $M=(Q-1)/N_a$, and let $\chi$ be a complex-valued order-$p$ power
character.  Extend complex-valued multiplicative characters by zero at zero
and define
\[
 f(h)=\chi(1-h),\qquad
 \widehat f(\theta)=\sum_{h\in H}f(h)\theta(h)^{-1}
 \quad(\theta\in\widehat H).
\]
For an extension $\psi_\theta$ of $\theta$ to $F_a^\times$, and with
$H^\perp$ the character group of $F_a^\times/H$, one has
\[
 \widehat f(\theta)
 =\frac1M\sum_{\lambda\in H^\perp}
 J(\psi_\theta^{-1}\lambda,\chi),
 \qquad
 f(x_a)=\frac1{N_a}\sum_{\theta\in\widehat H}
 \theta(x_a)\widehat f(\theta).
\]
Indeed, the character average
$M^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in H^\perp}\lambda$ is the indicator of $H$;
inserting it in the first Fourier sum gives the Jacobi sums, and Fourier
inversion gives the second identity.  This is a fully explicit reciprocity
formula, but the selected label survives in $\theta(x_a)$ and the Jacobi
unit phases.  Ideal factorization or valuation formulas for the Jacobi sums
do not by themselves determine this inverse-DFT entry.  This separation of
the ideal and $p$-adic unit conditions is also visible in Uehara's
cyclotomic-unit criterion \cite{uehara1985}; it supplies an exact test, not a
uniform nonvanishing theorem for the Conway-selected entry.

After adjoining $\mu_p$ and retaining the conductor primes in the $S$-ray
modulus, this residue-field condition becomes a marked Artin-symbol
assertion, not merely a statement about decomposition or conductor.  Its
ray character splits into a ramified local logarithm and an unramified
ideal-class character evaluated at $[\mathfrak P_a]$.  Stickelberger
annihilation reduces the latter to an explicit decomposition-invariant
quotient, but does not make it vanish uniformly.  This is a genuine boundary:
at the actual Conway conductor
$r=11$, $a=1$, $p=23$, one contributing Stickelberger scalar vanishes with
exact $23$-adic valuation one.  The corresponding unramified degree-$23$
class line is nontrivial, and every prime over two has nontrivial Artin symbol
on it, although the literal row still has $m_{23}=1$.  Thus successful rows
need not have a zero unramified term; the open assertion is noncancellation in
the actual selected sum.

\section{The cubic arm: a selected Singer generator}\label{sec:c}

Let $h=3^k$, $\zeta_k=\kappa_{3^k}$, and
$\gamma_k=\zeta_k+\zeta_k^{-1}$.

\begin{proposition}[half-angle reduction]\label{prop:c-half}
The element $\zeta_k$ is a primitive $3^{k+1}$-st root of unity,
\[
 \mathbb F_2(\zeta_k)=\mathbb F_{2^{2h}},
 \qquad
 \mathbb F_2(\gamma_k)=\mathbb F_{2^h}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\zeta_k+1)
 =3^{k+1}\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k),
\]
and the translates by $2$ and $3$ have the same order.  Hence the rows with
$f(p)=3^k$ have excess one precisely when $\gamma_k$ has the full relevant
primary order; over all levels this is equivalent to primitivity of every
$\gamma_k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The defining Kummer equation gives $\zeta_k^{3^k}=\kappa_2$, a primitive
cube root.  The order of two modulo $3^{k+1}$ is $2h$, and the least exponent
giving $\pm1$ is $h$, proving the field degrees.  If $s$ is the inverse of
two modulo $3^{k+1}$, then
\[
 \zeta_k+1=\zeta_k^s(\zeta_k^s+\zeta_k^{-s}),
 \qquad
 (\zeta_k^s+\zeta_k^{-s})^2=\gamma_k.
\]
The factors lie in the coprime circle and real parts of the unit group,
giving the order identity.  Frobenius gives the other translates, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:power} finishes the reduction.
\end{proof}

The half-angle test also has a lossless group-algebra form that removes all
denominators.  Put
\[
 J_h(X)=X^{2h}+X^h+1,
 \qquad B_d(X)=(X^2+1)^d+X^d\in\mathbb F_2[X].
\]
Since $J_h$ is the minimal polynomial of $\zeta_k$ and
$\gamma_k=\zeta_k^{-1}(\zeta_k^2+1)$, one has
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_k^d=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 J_h(X)\mid B_d(X).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, in
$\mathbb F_2[X,X^{-1}]/(X^{3h}-1)$ the parity vector of
$X^d(1+X^{-2})^d$ belongs to the affine coset $1+(J_h)$, not to the ideal
$(J_h)$ itself.  Thus arm $C$ is exactly the universal assertion that, for
every prime $\ell\mid 2^h-1$,
\[
 J_h(X)\nmid B_{(2^h-1)/\ell}(X).
\]
Lucas's theorem makes the support of $B_d$ explicit from the binary digits of
$d$, but it does not by itself prove these selected nondivisibilities.  The
denominator-free equivalence above is also kernel-checked as
\texttt{halfAngle\_pow\_eq\_one\_iff\_minpoly\_dvd}; it is a reduction of
$C$, not a proof of $C$.

There is also an exact stable-polynomial formulation.  Put $P_0(X)=X$ and
\[
 P_{j+1}(X)=P_j(X)^3+P_j(X)^2+1,
\]
so that $P_j=f^{\circ j}$ for $f(X)=X^3+X^2+1$.  In characteristic two,
$t(X)=X+1$ conjugates $g(X)=X^3+X$ to $f$: $f=t\circ g\circ t$.
The triple-angle identity gives $g(\gamma_j)=\gamma_{j-1}$ with
$\gamma_0=1$, hence $b_j=\gamma_j+1$ is a root of $P_j$.  Since $b_j$ has
degree $3^j$, $P_j$ is its minimal polynomial.  Lin and Wang prove that $f$
is stable, equivalently that all these iterates are irreducible
\cite{linwang2024}; their theorem does not assert multiplicative primitivity.

Indeed, this missing strengthening is exactly arm $C$.  With
$q=2^{3^{j-1}}$, both $\gamma_j$ and $b_j$ have relative norm
$\gamma_{j-1}$ to $\mathbb F_q$, and
\[
 \gamma_j b_j^2=\gamma_{j-1}.
\]
Because $\gcd(q-1,q^2+q+1)=1$, the norm propagates every old primary part.
For a new prime $\ell\mid q^2+q+1$, the base-field factor
$\gamma_{j-1}$ is an $\ell$-th power, while the displayed identity gives
$[\gamma_j]+2[b_j]=0$ in the $\ell$-Kummer quotient.  Since $\ell$ is odd,
$\gamma_j$ is an $\ell$-th power exactly when $b_j$ is.  Induction therefore
shows that all $P_j$ with $j\geq1$ are primitive polynomials exactly when
all $\gamma_j$ with $j\geq1$ are primitive.  Stability supplies the degrees;
primitive preservation is the original selected-order problem, not a known
solution of it.

Put $q_k=2^{3^{k-1}}$ and
\[
 U_k=\ker\!\left(
 \operatorname N_{\mathbb F_{q_k^3}/\mathbb F_{q_k}}
 \right),
 \qquad
 \eta_k=\gamma_k^{q_k-1},
 \qquad \eta_0=0.
\]

\begin{theorem}[selected norm-one coordinate]\label{thm:c-eta}
One has $|U_k|=q_k^2+q_k+1=\Phi_{3^k}(2)$.  The element $\eta_k$ generates
$\mathbb F_{2^{3^k}}$ and is the selected root of
\[
 X^3+\eta_{k-1}X^2+(\eta_{k-1}+1)X+1.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 C_k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C_{k-1}\text{ and }\operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)=|U_k|,
\]
where $C_j$ denotes primitivity of $\gamma_j$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The definition puts $\eta_k$ in the norm-one group.  Frobenius calculation
from $\zeta_k$ gives the displayed cubic; its coefficients are respectively
the trace, second symmetric function, and norm.  A base-field element of
$U_k$ has order dividing $\gcd(q_k-1,3)=1$, so the nontrivial cubic is
irreducible.  Its quadratic coefficient recursively recovers
$\eta_{k-1}$, proving the field degree.  Finally, the finite-field unit group
splits into its base-field and norm-one factors, whose orders are coprime.
The norm propagates the old part of $\gamma_k$ and $\eta_k$ detects exactly
the new factor.
\end{proof}

The recursion selects one Frobenius orbit inside a Singer difference set.
Indeed, for
\[
 R(x)=\frac{x^3+x+1}{x^2+x},
 \qquad
 W(x)=\frac{x+\omega}{x+\omega^2},
\]
one has
\[
  R(\eta_k)=\eta_{k-1},
  \qquad W(R(x))=W(x)^3,
  \qquad W(\eta_k)=\zeta_k^2.
\]
Thus the complete lower ancestry, not merely degree or trace, marks the
orbit.  The displayed conjugacy exposes its cyclotomic phase but
does not evaluate the multiplicative order of $\eta_k$.

\begin{proposition}[selected Singer sieve]\label{prop:sieve}
Let $N=|U_k|$ and $h=3^k$.  Then
\[
  \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)\geq h(h-1)+1.
\]
If a prime $\ell\mid N$ is missing from the selected order at its full
valuation, then
\[
  \frac N\ell\geq h(h-1)+1.
\]
Hence every prime $\ell>N/(h(h-1)+1)$ occurs with full valuation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The selected Frobenius orbit has $h$ points in the Singer
$(N,q_k+1,1)$ difference set.  Its $h(h-1)$ ordered off-diagonal quotients
are distinct nonidentity elements of $\langle\eta_k\rangle$, giving the
first bound.  If the full $\ell$-part is missing, the orbit lies in the
subgroup of order $N/\ell$, which must contain all these quotients.
\end{proof}

The sieve is polynomial in $h$, while $N$ is exponential.  It proves a
genuine selected large-factor range but not arm $C$.

\begin{proposition}[intermediate-field cubic ratio packing]
\label{prop:c-ratio-packing}
Let $k\geq1$, $h=3^k$, $H=3^{k-1}$, $q=2^H$, and
$N=q^2+q+1$.  Let $x=\zeta_k$ be the selected primitive
$3^{k+1}$-st root, put $\gamma_k=x+x^{-1}$, and put
$\eta_k=\gamma_k^{q-1}$.  For $0\leq j<k$ define
\[
 D_j=2\cdot3^j,\qquad R_j=3^{k-j},\qquad s_j=(R_j-1)/2,
\]
\[
 B_j=\mathcal R(D_j,s_j)+
 \left\lceil\frac{\mathcal R(D_j,s_j+1)-\mathcal R(D_j,s_j)}3\right\rceil,
 \qquad B_{3,k}=\max_{0\leq j<k}B_j.
\]
These are the $r=3$, $b_3=2$ parameters of
Proposition~\ref{prop:o-ratio-packing}, with $m=j+1$.  Then
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)\geq
 \left\lceil\frac{B_{3,k}}3\right\rceil.
\]
In particular, if a prime $\ell\mid N$ is missing from the order of
$\eta_k$ at its full valuation, then
\[
 \frac N\ell\geq\left\lceil\frac{B_{3,k}}3\right\rceil.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $L=\mathbb F_{q^6}=\mathbb F_{4^{3^k}}$ and
$K=\mathbb F_{q^2}=\mathbb F_{4^{3^{k-1}}}$.  We first identify exactly
the order $o$ of $(1+x)K^\times$ in $L^\times/K^\times$:
\[
 o=3\operatorname{ord}(\eta_k).
\]
Indeed, write $A=\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k)$.  The half-angle identity
gives $\operatorname{ord}(1+x)=3^{k+1}A$, with
$A\mid q^3-1$ and $3\nmid A$.  Since $H$ is odd,
$\gcd(q^3-1,q^2-1)=q-1$, while LTE gives
$v_3(q^2-1)=k$.  Consequently
\[
 \gcd(3^{k+1}A,q^2-1)=3^k\gcd(A,q-1),
\]
and the order in the cyclic quotient is
\[
 o=\frac{3^{k+1}A}{\gcd(3^{k+1}A,q^2-1)}
   =3\frac A{\gcd(A,q-1)}
   =3\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k^{q-1}).
\]
The coprime circle/real order splitting and transfer of a lower bound
through this factor of three are kernel-checked in
\texttt{ExcessCubicPacking}; the finite-field half-angle, valuation, and
component-gcd inputs remain paper-level.

Since $\operatorname{ord}_{3^m}(2)=2\cdot3^{m-1}$, the minimal
coefficient fields in Proposition~\ref{prop:o-ratio-packing} are exactly
$\mathbb F_{4^{3^{m-1}}}$.  Its unconditional bound gives
$o\geq B_{3,k}$, and the identity $o=3\operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)$ gives the first
assertion.  A deficient full $\ell$-part implies
$\operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)\mid N/\ell$, proving the second.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[cubic square-root exponential bound]
\label{cor:c-ratio-growth}
As $k\to\infty$, with $h=3^k$,
\[
 \log_2\operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)
 \geq\left(\log_2 3+\frac56\right)\sqrt h-O(\log h).
\]
The coefficient $\log_2 3+5/6>2.4182$ improves the partition coefficient
$\pi/(3\log 2)$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:c-partition}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to retain one summand
$2^t\binom{D_j}t\binom{s_j}t$ of the signed ball.
Put $u=\sqrt h$ and $t=\lfloor u/2\rfloor$.
If $k=2m$ is even, take $j=m-1$.  Then
\[
 D_j=\frac23u,\qquad s_j=\frac32u-\frac12.
\]
Writing $H_2(z)=-z\log_2z-(1-z)\log_2(1-z)$, the elementary
Stirling estimate gives
\[
 \log_2\left(2^t\binom{D_j}t\binom{s_j}t\right)
 =\left[\frac12+\frac23H_2\left(\frac34\right)
              +\frac32H_2\left(\frac13\right)\right]u-O(\log h)
 =\left(\log_2 3+\frac56\right)u-O(\log h).
\]
If $k=2m+1$ is odd, take $j=m$.  Then
\[
 D_j=\frac2{\sqrt3}u,\qquad
 s_j=\frac{\sqrt3}2u-\frac12,
\]
giving the coefficient
\[
 c_{\rm odd}=\frac12+
 \frac2{\sqrt3}H_2\left(\frac{\sqrt3}4\right)
 +\frac{\sqrt3}2H_2\left(\frac1{\sqrt3}\right).
\]
This is at least the even coefficient, as can be seen without numerical
comparison.  The function
$g(x)=e^xH_2(\tfrac12e^{-x})$ is strictly concave on $x>-\log2$:
with $z=\tfrac12e^{-x}\in(0,1)$,
\[
 g''(x)=\frac{e^x}{\log2}
 \left(-\log(1-z)-\frac z{1-z}\right)<0.
\]
Thus $g(x)+g(-x)$ decreases for $0<x<\log2$.
The even coefficient uses $x=\log(3/2)$, whereas the odd coefficient
uses $x=\log(2/\sqrt3)$, which is smaller.
Finally, dividing the order bound by three costs only $\log_2 3$,
which is absorbed into $O(\log h)$.
\end{proof}

The bound remains subexponential in $h$, while $N$ is exponential in $h$.
It therefore leaves the smaller current prime factors unresolved.

The cyclotomic selector also permits a complementary partition bound.  This
is the usual low-degree collision argument for Gaussian periods, specialized
to the composite conductor $3^{k+1}$ and therefore retaining the literal
selected element rather than replacing it by an arbitrary normal generator.

\begin{proposition}[selected norm-one partition bound]
\label{prop:c-partition}
Let $n=3^{k+1}=3h$.  For an integer $m\geq1$ put
\[
 \mathcal A_m=\{1\leq j\leq m:3\nmid j\},\qquad
 t_m=|\mathcal A_m|,\qquad s_m=\sum_{j\in\mathcal A_m}j.
\]
Let
\[
 \mathcal U_h=\{1\leq j<n:3\nmid j\},
\]
choose $0\leq z_j<2h$ with $2^{z_j}\equiv j\pmod n$, and, for
$E\subseteq\mathcal U_h$, put
\[
 Q_E=\sum_{j\in E}2^{z_j},\qquad s_E=\sum_{j\in E}j.
\]
For distinct $E,F\subseteq\mathcal U_h$, set
\[
 A=E\setminus F,\quad B=F\setminus E,\quad
 W=s_A+s_B,\quad j_0=\min(A\cup B).
\]
Then $\eta_k^{Q_E}\ne\eta_k^{Q_F}$ if either
\[
 s_A\not\equiv s_B\pmod3\ \text{ and }\ W<h,
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 s_A\equiv s_B\pmod3\ \text{ and }\ W-2j_0<h.
\]
In particular, weighted symmetric-difference sum below $h$ rules out every
collision.

Put
\[
 R_h=\frac{h-1}{2},\qquad
 \mathcal P_h=
 \sum_{r=0}^{R_h}[X^r]
 \prod_{\substack{j\geq1\\3\nmid j}}(1+X^j).
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)\geq\mathcal P_h,
 \qquad
 \log_2\mathcal P_h=
 \left(\frac{\pi}{3\log 2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt h.
\]
If $s_m<h$, then also
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)\geq2^{t_m}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \log_2\operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)
 \geq \left(\frac{\pi}{3\log 2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt h,
\]
and the elementary initial-segment subfamily alone gives
\[
 \log_2\operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)
 \geq \sqrt{\frac{4h}{3}}-O(1).
\]
If a prime $\ell\mid N=|U_k|$ is absent from the order of $\eta_k$ at its
full valuation, then $N/\ell\geq\mathcal P_h$ and, whenever $s_m<h$, also
$N/\ell\geq2^{t_m}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $w=\zeta_k^2$.  The identity $W(\eta_k)=w$ gives
\[
 \eta_k=\frac{\omega+\omega^2w}{1+w}.
\]
The order of two modulo $n$ is $2h$, so the exponents $z_j$ in the statement
exist.  They satisfy
\[
 \eta_k^{2^{z_j}}
 =\omega^j\frac{1+(\omega w)^j}{1+w^j}.
\]
A collision $\eta_k^{Q_E}=\eta_k^{Q_F}$ first permits cancellation of all indices in
$E\cap F$: the relevant numerator and denominator factors are nonzero at
$w$, because both $w$ and $\omega w$ are primitive $n$-th roots.  Put
$A=E\setminus F$, $B=F\setminus E$, and $W=s_A+s_B$ as in the statement.
Clearing the remaining
denominators makes $w$ a root of the difference between
\[
 \omega^{s_A}
 \prod_{j\in A}(1+(\omega X)^j)\prod_{j\in B}(1+X^j)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \omega^{s_B}
 \prod_{j\in B}(1+(\omega X)^j)\prod_{j\in A}(1+X^j).
\]
Each polynomial has degree $W$.  The element $w$ is a
primitive $n$-th root and the order of four modulo $n$ is $h$, so its minimal
polynomial over $\mathbb F_4$ is $X^h+\omega^2$.  Thus a collision with
$W<h$ would make the two polynomials identical.  Their constant coefficients
first force $s_A\equiv s_B\pmod3$.  Cancel that common scalar and let $j_0$
be the least index in $A\cup B$.  If, say, $j_0\in A$, then the coefficients
of $X^{j_0}$ on the two
sides are respectively $\omega^{j_0}$ and $1$.  They differ because
$3\nmid j_0$; the other case is symmetric.  More sharply, if
$s_A\not\equiv s_B\pmod3$, the
difference has span $W$, while if $s_A\equiv s_B\pmod3$, its constant and
leading terms cancel but its coefficients at degrees $j_0$ and $W-j_0$ are
nonzero, so its span is $W-2j_0$.  A nonzero multiple of $X^h+\omega^2$ has
span at least $h$.  This proves both pairwise criteria in the statement.

When $s_m<h$, necessarily $m<n$, so $\mathcal A_m\subseteq\mathcal U_h$.
For $E,F\subseteq\mathcal A_m$, their symmetric difference has weighted sum
at most $s_m$.  Hence all $2^{t_m}$ displayed powers are distinct.

Every subset $E$ with $s_E\leq R_h$ belongs to a family whose pairwise
weighted symmetric differences have sum at most $2R_h<h$.  The number of
such subsets is exactly $\mathcal P_h$, proving the first finite bound.  For
its asymptotic, put
\[
 G(t)=\prod_{j\geq1}(1+e^{-tj}),\qquad
 F(e^{-t})=\prod_{3\nmid j}(1+e^{-tj})=\frac{G(t)}{G(3t)}.
\]
The standard $q$-Pochhammer asymptotic gives
\[
 G(t)\sim2^{-1/2}\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi^2}{12t}\right),
 \qquad
 \frac{F(e^{-t})}{1-e^{-t}}
 \sim t^{-1}\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi^2}{18t}\right).
\]
The coefficients of $F(X)/(1-X)$ are the nonnegative, nondecreasing
cumulative counts in $\mathcal P_h$.  Ingham's exponential Tauberian theorem
\cite{ingham1941} therefore gives
\[
 \log\mathcal P_h
 =2\sqrt{\frac{\pi^2R_h}{18}}+o(\sqrt h)
 =\frac\pi3\sqrt h+o(\sqrt h).
\]
Finally, $t_m=2m/3+O(1)$ and $s_m=m^2/3+O(m)$; taking the largest $m$ with
$s_m<h$ gives the elementary initial-segment estimate.  The final assertion
follows because a missing full $\ell$-part would force
$\operatorname{ord}(\eta_k)\mid N/\ell$.
\end{proof}

There is a second, sharp consequence of the same selected formula.  It
classifies the first possible one-sided relation rather than merely packing
many pairwise-distinct powers.

\begin{proposition}[sharp selected first-relation threshold]
\label{prop:c-first-relation}
Retain the notation above.  For every nonempty $E\subseteq\mathcal U_h$,
put $S=s_E$ and decompose
\[
 B_E(X)=\prod_{j\in E}(1+X^j)
 =B_0(X^3)+XB_1(X^3)+X^2B_2(X^3),
 \qquad B_a\in\mathbb F_2[Y].
\]
If $a_0\equiv-S\pmod3$, then the exact all-weight criterion is
\[
 \eta_k^{Q_E}=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Y^{2H}+Y^H+1\mid B_a(Y)
 \quad\text{for both }a\ne a_0,
 \qquad H=h/3.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \eta_k^{Q_E}=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s_E\geq2h+1.
\]
The threshold is sharp: with $H=h/3$,
\[
 E_0=\{1,h-1,h+1\},\qquad
 Q_{E_0}=1+2^H+2^{2H}=|U_k|,\qquad s_{E_0}=2h+1.
\]
Consequently, if a prime $\ell\mid N=|U_k|$ is absent from the selected
order at its full valuation, write
\[
 \frac N\ell=\sum_{0\leq z<2H}\epsilon_z2^z,
 \qquad j_z\equiv2^z\pmod{3h},\quad1\leq j_z<3h.
\]
Then necessarily
\[
 \sum_{\epsilon_z=1}j_z\geq2h+1.
\]
For this quotient word put
$S=\sum_{\epsilon_z=1}j_z=3T+1$.  Its two surviving sections are padded
reciprocals:
\[
 B_1(Y)=Y^T B_0(Y^{-1}).
\]
Thus the two divisibility conditions are equivalent, and the exact failure
criterion is the single condition
\[
 \eta_k^{N/\ell}=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Y^{2H}+Y^H+1\mid B_0(Y).
\]
Equivalently, define $\mathcal T_m\in\mathbb F_2[Y]$ by
\[
 \mathcal T_0=\mathcal T_1=\mathcal T_2=1,
 \qquad
 \mathcal T_m=\mathcal T_{m-1}+\mathcal T_{m-2}
 +(1+Y)\mathcal T_{m-3}\quad(m\geq3).
\]
Then, for $d=N/\ell$,
\[
 \eta_k^d=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Y^{2H}+Y^H+1\mid\mathcal T_d(Y).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 P_E(X)=\omega^S B_E(\omega X)+B_E(X).
\]
The selected formula shows that $\eta_k^{Q_E}=1$ exactly when
$M(X)=X^h+\omega^2$, the minimal polynomial of $w$ over $\mathbb F_4$,
divides $P_E$.  If $[X^r]B_E=b_r\in\mathbb F_2$, then
\[
 [X^r]P_E=b_r(1+\omega^{S+r}).
\]
Thus the scalar multiplier vanishes precisely on the section
$r\equiv-S\pmod3$.  Write
the three sections as in the statement.
Since $h=3H$, division by
$M(X)=(X^3)^H+\omega^2$ respects these three sections.  Divisibility by $M$
therefore forces each of the two surviving $B_a(Y)$ to be divisible by
$Y^H+\omega^2$.  Because its coefficients lie in $\mathbb F_2$, conjugation
also forces divisibility by $Y^H+\omega$, and hence by
\[
 (Y^H+\omega^2)(Y^H+\omega)=Y^{2H}+Y^H+1.
\]
The converse is immediate because the displayed polynomial contains
$Y^H+\omega^2$ as a factor.  This proves the exact all-weight criterion.

If $S<2h$, every surviving section has degree below $2H$.  If $S=2h$, the
top term lies in the vanished residue-zero section, so the same strict bound
holds for the two surviving sections.  Hence both are zero.  The constant
term of $B_E$ then forces the sole supported section to be residue zero, but
the least $j\in E$ contributes coefficient one at $X^j$, in a nonzero residue
class.  This contradiction proves $S\geq2h+1$.

The congruence $2^H\equiv h-1\pmod{3h}$ follows by induction from
$2\equiv2\pmod9$ after cubing; squaring gives
$2^{2H}\equiv h+1\pmod{3h}$.  Hence $E_0$ has the asserted exponent, and
$\eta_k^N=1$ proves sharpness.  Finally, $N$ is odd and prime to three, so
$\ell\geq5$ and $N/\ell<2^{2H}$.  Its binary indices lie below $2H$ and
have distinct residues modulo $3h$.  A missing full $\ell$-part gives
$\eta_k^{N/\ell}=1$, so the first assertion gives the displayed residue-weight
bound.

For the quotient word, every current prime satisfies
$\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(2^H)=3$, hence $\ell\equiv1\pmod3$, while
$N\equiv1\pmod3$; therefore $d\equiv1\pmod3$ and
$S\equiv d\equiv1\pmod3$.  The product $B_E$ is self-reciprocal of degree
$S=3T+1$.  If $[X^m]B_E=b_m$, then $b_m=b_{S-m}$, so comparison of the
residue-zero and residue-one coefficients gives
\[
 B_1(Y)=Y^TB_0(Y^{-1}).
\]
The polynomial $G(Y)=Y^{2H}+Y^H+1$ is self-reciprocal.  Since
$\deg B_0\leq T$, reversing a factorization by $G$ shows
$G\mid B_0$ if and only if $G\mid B_1$; the zero and subdegree cases are
included.  The generic padded-mirror divisibility step is kernel-checked as
\texttt{selfMirror\_dvd\_paddedMirror\_iff}.  The vanished section is
$a_0\equiv-S\equiv2\pmod3$, proving the single-section criterion.

For the recurrence formulation, let $y$ be a root of $G$, choose $w$ with
$w^3=y$, and let $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$.  The three elements
$1+w,1+\omega w,1+\omega^2w$ are the roots of
\[
 Z^3+Z^2+Z+(1+y),
\]
so their power sums obey the displayed recurrence and have initial values
$1,1,1$.  Lucas's theorem gives
$(1+X)^d=\prod_{\epsilon_z=1}(1+X^{2^z})$, and this product is congruent to
$B_E(X)$ modulo $X^{3h}-1$.  Summing its values at the three cube roots of
$y$ projects onto the residue-zero section.  Therefore
$\mathcal T_d(y)=B_0(y)$.  Since $y$ has order $h=3^k$ and
$\operatorname{ord}_h(2)=2H$, its minimal polynomial is $G$; the last
divisibility criterion follows.
\end{proof}

The recurrence also admits a full symmetric composition law.  This explains
why the forced norm-triple relation at exponent $N$ cannot simply be
descended through $N=d\ell$.

\begin{proposition}[cubic power-state composition saturation]
\label{prop:c-composition}
Let $K$ be a characteristic-two field containing
$\omega^2+\omega+1=0$.  Let $L/K$ be a cyclic extension of degree three
with generator $\sigma$, and let $w\in L^\times$ satisfy
$\sigma(w)=\omega w$.  Put $y=w^3\in K$ and
\[
 a=1+w,\qquad c=1+y,\qquad b=\frac ca=1+w+w^2,
\]
and
\[
 A_m=\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(a^m),\qquad
 B_m=\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(b^m),\qquad C_m=c^m.
\]
For universal variables define the sequence
\[
 D_0=1,\quad D_1=A,\quad D_2=A^2,\qquad
 D_n=AD_{n-1}+BD_{n-2}+CD_{n-3}\quad(n\geq3).
\]
Then, for all $m,n\geq0$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A_{mn}&=D_n(A_m,B_m,C_m),\\
 B_{mn}&=D_n(B_m,A_mC_m,C_m^2),\\
 C_{mn}&=C_m^n.
 \end{aligned}
\]
If $m\equiv1\pmod3$ and, for some positive integer
$H$, the minimal polynomial of $y$ over $\mathbb F_2$ is the
self-reciprocal polynomial $G(Y)=Y^{2H}+Y^H+1$, then
\[
 A_m=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B_m=0.
\]
Consequently, for every $n\equiv1\pmod3$,
\[
 (A_m,B_m)=(0,0)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 (A_{mn},B_{mn})=(0,0).
\]
The converse cannot hold uniformly: already at $H=1$ one has
$G\mid\mathcal T_7$ but $G\nmid\mathcal T_1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The eigenvalue relation makes $w\notin K$, so $K(w)=L$.  It also gives
$a\ne0$; and $c=0$ would make $w$ a cube root of unity already contained in
$K$, so $c\ne0$.  Thus all displayed quotients are defined.
The three conjugates of $a^m$ have elementary invariants
$(A_m,B_m,C_m)$.  Newton's identities in characteristic two give the
displayed recurrence for their power sums.  The pairwise products have
elementary invariants $(B_m,A_mC_m,C_m^2)$, which proves the second
composition formula; the third is immediate.

Write $m=3t+1$ and decompose
\[
 (1+X)^m=P_0(X^3)+XP_1(X^3)+X^2P_2(X^3).
\]
Self-reciprocity gives $P_1(Y)=Y^tP_0(Y^{-1})$.  Since $G$ is
self-reciprocal, $A_m=P_0(y)=0$ also gives $P_1(y)=0$; hence
$a^m\in w^2K$.  Therefore $b^m=c^m/a^m\in wK$ and $B_m=0$.
Conversely, decompose
\[
 (1+X+X^2)^m=Q_0(X^3)+XQ_1(X^3)+X^2Q_2(X^3).
\]
This polynomial is self-reciprocal of degree $6t+2$, so
$Q_2(Y)=Y^{2t}Q_0(Y^{-1})$.  The equality $B_m=Q_0(y)=0$ therefore puts
$b^m$ in $wK$, and $a^m=c^m/b^m$ lies in $w^2K$, giving $A_m=0$.

When $A=B=0$, the recurrence $D_n=CD_{n-3}$ has zero values in residue
classes one and two modulo three.  This proves the saturation statement.
Finally, for $H=1$ the recurrence gives
$\mathcal T_1=1$ and $\mathcal T_7=Y^2+Y+1=G$.
\end{proof}

The first infinitesimal neighborhood of the selected factor is also exact.
It does not contradict failure; instead it shows that any failure is
transverse and recovers the unique Kummer preimage already forced by the
projective equation.

\begin{proposition}[selected cubic first-jet transversality]
\label{prop:c-first-jet}
Use the notation of Proposition~\ref{prop:c-composition}.  Specialize to
$k\geq1$, $h=3^k$, and $H=3^{k-1}$, let $y$ be the selected primitive
$h$-th root whose minimal polynomial over $\mathbb F_2$ is
$G(Y)=Y^{2H}+Y^H+1$, and put
\[
 q=2^H,\qquad N=q^2+q+1,\qquad e=\frac{q+1}{3},
\]
and let $\ell\mid N$ be prime, with
$d=N/\ell$ and $s=(\ell-1)/3$.  Choose $w^3=y$, and put
$a=1+w$ and $c=1+y$.  Then
\[
 \mathcal T_N(Y)=1+Y^e+Y^{qe},
 \qquad \mathcal T_N'(y)=y^{e-1}\ne0.
\]

Suppose hypothetically that $G\mid\mathcal T_d$, and write
$\mathcal T_d=GQ_d$.  There is a unique $\lambda$ with
$a^d=w^2\lambda$, necessarily with $\lambda\in K^\times$, and it is
forced to be
\[
 \lambda=\frac{y^{e-1}}{c^{ds-1}}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \mathcal T_d'(y)=\frac{y^{e-1}}{c^{ds}}\ne0,
 \qquad Q_d(y)=c^{-ds}.
\]
In particular $G^2\nmid\mathcal T_d$.  Equivalently, in the quotient by
$G^2$ one has the exact first-order lift
\[
 \mathcal T_d(Y)\equiv
 G(Y)(1+Y)^{-ds}\pmod {G(Y)^2},
\]
where the negative power denotes the inverse of the unit $1+Y$ in that
quotient.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lucas projection of
\[
 (1+X)^N=(1+X)(1+X^q)(1+X^{q^2})
\]
onto exponents divisible by three gives the displayed formula for
$\mathcal T_N$.  The integer $e$ is odd and $qe$ is even, so its derivative
at $y$ is $y^{e-1}$.

Under the hypothetical zero state, the reciprocal-section argument in
Proposition~\ref{prop:c-composition} puts $a^d$ in $w^2K$, giving the unique
$\lambda$.  In the generic cubic algebra $w^3=Y$, formal differentiation
gives $dw/dY=w^{-2}$.  Hence
\[
 \mathcal T_d'(y)
 =\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(a^{d-1}w^{-2})
 =\lambda\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(a^{-1})
 =\frac{\lambda}{c}.
\]

Since $\ell=3s+1$ and $\ell$ is odd, $s$ is even.  The universal cubic
power sum has linearization
\[
 D_\ell(A,B,C)=AC^s+O((A,B)^2).
\]
Differentiate the composition identity
$\mathcal T_N=D_\ell(A_d,B_d,c^d)$ at the zero state.  This gives
\[
 y^{e-1}=c^{ds}\mathcal T_d'(y),
\]
which proves both formulas for $\lambda$ and $\mathcal T_d'(y)$.  Since
$G'(y)=y^{H-1}$ and $e\equiv H\pmod h$, differentiating
$\mathcal T_d=GQ_d$ at $y$ gives $Q_d(y)=c^{-ds}$.  The nonvanishing and
the congruence modulo $G^2$ follow.
\end{proof}

For $k\geq2$, the next Hasse jet also saturates.  If
$\epsilon_m=\binom m2\bmod2$, direct differentiation under the same
hypothetical failure gives
\[
 \mathcal T_d^{[2]}(y)=\frac{\lambda}{yc}
       +\epsilon_d\frac{\lambda}{c^2},
 \qquad B_d'(y)=\frac{y\lambda^2}{c},
\]
while
\[
 D_\ell(A,B,C)=AC^s+\epsilon_\ell B^2C^{s-1}
                 +O((A,B)^3).
\]
Because $N\equiv1\pmod4$ and $d\ell=N$, one has
$d\equiv\ell\pmod4$ and hence
$\epsilon_d=\epsilon_\ell$.  The two quadratic terms cancel using
$\lambda^3=c^d/y^2$, leaving exactly
$\mathcal T_N^{[2]}(y)=y^{e-2}$.  Thus neither the ordinary derivative nor
the second Hasse derivative supplies a character contradiction.

The two-base-$q$-digit degree route is arithmetically vacuous as well.  Write
$d=Aq+B$ with $0\leq B<q$.  Every current quotient satisfies
\[
 B>A,\qquad B-A\equiv1\pmod h,
 \qquad d>1\Longrightarrow B-A\geq h+1\text{ and }B\geq h+2.
\]
Indeed every prime factor of $d$ is $1$ modulo $2h$, while
$q\equiv h-1\pmod {2h}$; hence $d\equiv1\pmod {2h}$ and
$B-A\equiv1\pmod h$.  Also $d\mid N$ gives
$d\mid A+q(A-B)$.  Here $d<q^2$ gives $A<q$, while oddness of $d$ gives
odd $B\geq1$.  If $A\geq B$, then
$0<A+q(A-B)<Aq+B=d$, a contradiction.  Thus $B>A$.  If $d>1$ and
$B-A=1$, then $A\geq1$, and the same divisibility gives $d\mid q-A$ with
$0<q-A<d$, again a contradiction.  Meanwhile the failure
equation gives the nonzero polynomial
\[
 \mathcal Q(X)=(X+1)^B+
  \omega^{B+1}(X+\omega)^A(X+\omega^2)^{B-A},
 \qquad \mathcal Q(w)=0.
\]
Its exponent span is $B$ unless $A\equiv1\pmod3$, when it is $B-2$.
Divisibility by the degree-$h$ selected factor therefore requires only
$B\geq h$ or $B\geq h+2$, respectively.  The digit span excludes the
already-settled $d=1$ prime-complement case directly; for every nontrivial
current quotient $d>1$, the quotient arithmetic already gives the stronger
bound in both cases.

Thus every current $\ell\equiv1\pmod3$ carries a hypothetical deficient
$d$-state to the forced $N=d\ell$ state.  In the finite-field specialization
used below, $K=\mathbb F_{q^2}$ and $L=\mathbb F_{q^6}$.  The relevant cyclic
projective quotient has order
\[
 |L^\times/K^\times|=q^4+q^2+1
 =(q^2+q+1)(q^2-q+1),
\]
and the two displayed factors are coprime.  Hence for
$\ell\mid q^2+q+1$ its $\ell$-power map has kernel exactly $\ell$.
The forced projective equation
\[
 X^\ell=[w^{2\ell}]
\]
therefore has exactly $\ell$ solutions, one of which is the deficient target
$[w^2]$.  This exact fibre belongs to the projective power map, not to the
symmetric pair $(A_d,B_d)$ itself.  It proves that the symmetric recurrence
and projective raising equation alone do not select the literal preimage;
any resolvent or determinant refinement must supply an additional
selector-sensitive ancestry input.

The all-weight criterion has an equivalent projective-torsion form which
pinpoints why Hilbert--90 does not close it.  This retains the marked root,
rather than replacing it by a generic norm-one element.

\begin{proposition}[marked projective obstruction and cohomological blindness]
\label{prop:c-projective}
Put $H=h/3$, $q=2^H$, $N=q^2+q+1$, and let
$\ell\mid N$ be prime, with $d=N/\ell$.  In
\[
 L=\mathbb F_{2^{2h}},\qquad K=\mathbb F_{q^2},
\]
write $w=\zeta_k^2$ and
\[
 Z=\frac{(1+w)^d}{w^2}.
\]
Then
\[
 \eta_k^d=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Z\in K.
\]
Moreover, with $\alpha=w^3$,
\[
 Z^\ell
 =\omega^2(1+\alpha)\alpha^{-(2\ell+1)/3}\in K.
\]
Thus $[Z]\in L^\times/K^\times$ is an explicitly marked
$\ell$-torsion class, and arm~$C$ asks that this class be nontrivial for
every current $\ell$.

Let $\sigma(x)=x^{q^2}$ generate $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$.  On
$\mu_\ell$ one has
\[
 1+\sigma+\sigma^2=0,
 \qquad \sigma-1\text{ is bijective},
 \qquad H^1(\langle\sigma\rangle,\mu_\ell)=0.
\]
Consequently the norm and Hilbert--90 conditions on the displayed torsion
class are automatic; they do not distinguish $[Z]=1$ from a nontrivial
marked class.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The congruences
\[
 2^H\equiv h-1,\qquad 2^{2H}\equiv h+1\pmod{3h}
\]
give
\[
 w^q=\omega^2w^{-1},\qquad w^{q^2}=\omega^2w.
\]
The selected formula in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:c-partition} gives
\[
 \frac{1+\omega^2w}{1+w}=\omega\eta_k^{q+1}.
\]
Since every prime divisor $\ell$ of $N$ satisfies
$\ell\equiv1\pmod3$, also $d\equiv1\pmod3$.  Therefore
\[
 Z^{q^2-1}
 =\omega^2\left(\frac{1+\omega^2w}{1+w}\right)^d
 =\eta_k^{d(q+1)}.
\]
As $\gcd(q+1,N)=1$, this is one exactly when $\eta_k^d=1$,
which proves the fixed-field equivalence.

The three conjugates under $q$-powering satisfy
\[
 (1+w)^N
 =(1+w)(1+\omega^2w^{-1})(1+\omega^2w)
 =\frac{\omega^2(1+w^3)}w.
\]
Hence
\[
 Z^\ell=\frac{(1+w)^N}{w^{2\ell}}
 =\omega^2(1+\alpha)\alpha^{-(2\ell+1)/3};
\]
the exponent is integral because $\ell\equiv1\pmod3$.  The left side is
fixed by $q^2$-powering (equivalently, this also follows by direct use of
$\alpha^{q^2}=\alpha$), so it lies in $K$.

Finally put $Q=q^2$.  Modulo $\ell$,
\[
 1+Q+Q^2=1+q^2+q^4\equiv1+q^2+q=0,
\]
because $q^3\equiv1$.  On the other hand
$\gcd(Q-1,\ell)=1$: any common divisor of $q^2-1$ and
$q^2+q+1$ divides three, whereas $\ell\ne3$.  Thus the norm operator is
zero on $\mu_\ell$ and the coboundary operator $\sigma-1$ is an
automorphism.  The standard cyclic formula
\[
 H^1=\ker(1+\sigma+\sigma^2)/(\sigma-1)\mu_\ell
\]
is therefore zero.  This proves the claimed blindness, not the desired
pointwise nontriviality of $[Z]$.

More explicitly, $c=\sigma(Z)/Z$ lies in $\mu_\ell$ because $Z^\ell\in K$.
There is a unique $\rho\in\mu_\ell$ with
$c=\sigma(\rho)/\rho$, and then $Z/\rho\in K$, so $[Z]=[\rho]$ in
$L^\times/K^\times$.  Every possible marked projective class is therefore
represented by a coboundary; the zero cohomology class does not say whether
its representing root $\rho$ equals one.
\end{proof}

Together, the partition and first-relation bounds strengthen the polynomial
Singer sieve to an exponential-in-$\sqrt h$ exclusion and a sharp
weight-$2h$ exclusion for the actual norm-one coordinate.  They remain
subexponential and do not classify relations above the norm triple, so they
do not force primitivity.

There is an exact number-field factorization of the remaining index.  Put
$L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{3^{k+1}})^+$ and
$K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{3^k})^+$, reduce global units $E_F$ and circular units
$C_F$ modulo the inert prime over two, and write
\[
 R_F=(\mathcal O_F/2)^{\times}/\overline E_F,
 \qquad T_F=\overline E_F/\overline C_F.
\]
Then
\[
 [U_k:\langle\eta_k\rangle]
 =|\ker(\operatorname N:R_L\to R_K)|
  \frac{[\overline E_L:\overline C_L]}
       {[\overline E_K:\overline C_K]}.
\]
The first factor is a relative principal $2$-ray kernel; the second divides
the relative real class-number quotient.  Even class number one would leave
the principal-ray factor.  This is not a formal possibility only: analogous
prime-conductor periods can have inert two and class number one while their
finite circular-unit reduction has nontrivial defect
\cite{hoechsmann1995}.  Arm $C$ is exactly the assertion that both factors
in the displayed index formula vanish at every level.

\section{The exceptional arm: corrected norm and Capelli}\label{sec:d}

Let $h=3^k$, $q=2^h$, $\zeta=\zeta_k$, and
$\omega=\zeta^h\in\mathbb F_4$.

\begin{proposition}[first possible translate]\label{prop:d-lower}
If $f(p)=2\cdot3^k$, then $m_p\geq4$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Such a prime divides $2^h+1$ but neither $3^{k+1}$ nor $2^h-1$.  The
half-angle decomposition in Proposition~\ref{prop:c-half} shows that each of
$\zeta,\zeta+1,\zeta+2,\zeta+3$ has order prime to $p$.  All four are
$p$-th powers in their defining fields, so the excess definition gives the
claim.
\end{proof}

The translate by four lies in a compositum with $\mathbb F_{16}$.  The
nontrivial relative Frobenius over $\mathbb F_4$ exchanges the nimbers $4$
and $5$, which are the roots of $Y^2+Y+\omega$.

\begin{proposition}[corrected norm]\label{prop:d-norm}
In $\mathbb F_{2^{4h}}/\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}$,
\[
 \operatorname N(\zeta+4)
 = (\zeta+4)(\zeta+5)
 = \zeta^2+\zeta+\omega=A_k.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \mathbb F_2(\zeta+4)=\mathbb F_{2^{4h}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One has $4+5=1$ and $4\cdot5=\omega$.  Expanding the product gives the
formula.

Put $v=\zeta+4$ and suppose $v^{2^j}=v$.  Then
\[
 c:=\zeta^{2^j}+\zeta=4^{2^j}+4
\]
belongs both to $\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}$ and to $\mathbb F_{16}$.  Since $h$ is
odd, their intersection is $\mathbb F_4$, so
$c\in\{0,1,\omega,\omega^2\}$.  If $c\ne0$, the Frobenius conjugate
$\zeta^{2^j}=\zeta+c$ would have order
$3^{k+1}\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k)>3^{k+1}$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:c-half}, whereas every Frobenius conjugate of $\zeta$
has order exactly $3^{k+1}$.  Hence $c=0$.  It follows simultaneously that
$2h\mid j$ and $4\mid j$.  Their least common multiple is $4h$, so the
Frobenius orbit of $v$ has size $4h$, proving the generation assertion.
\end{proof}

Define
\[
 R_k(Y)=\operatorname{Res}_X
 \left(X^{2h}+X^h+1,\;Y+X^h+X^2+X\right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[exact exceptional target]\label{thm:d-target}
The polynomial $R_k$ is the irreducible degree-$2h$ minimal polynomial of
$A_k$.  The prime divisors of
$\Psi_k=\Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)/3$ are exactly the primes
$\ell\ne3$ with $\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)=2h$, with their full
valuations.  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every row with $f(\ell)=2\cdot3^k$ has $m_\ell=4$;
\item $\Psi_k\mid\operatorname{ord}(M_k)$;
\item $A_k$ is not an $\ell$-th power in $\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}$ for every
      prime $\ell\mid\Psi_k$;
\item $R_k(X^\ell)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb F_2$ for every prime
      $\ell\mid\Psi_k$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The element $\zeta$ has minimal polynomial $X^{2h}+X^h+1$.  To justify the
degree of $A_k$, suppose $A_k^{2^j}=A_k$, put
$t\equiv2^j\pmod{3h}$, and write $u=\zeta^t$.  If $j$ is even, then
$\omega^t=\omega$ and
\[
 (u+\zeta)(u+\zeta+1)=0,
\]
so $u$ is either $\zeta$ or $\zeta+1$.  If $j$ is odd, then
$\omega^t=\omega^2$ and
\[
 (u+\zeta+\omega)(u+\zeta+\omega^2)=0,
\]
so $u$ is either $\zeta+\omega$ or $\zeta+\omega^2$.  The three translated
possibilities are impossible: Proposition~\ref{prop:c-half} gives each the
same order as $\zeta+1$, namely
$3^{k+1}\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k)>3^{k+1}$; here
$\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_k)>1$ because
$\mathbb F_2(\gamma_k)=\mathbb F_{2^h}$ and $h>1$.  On the other hand, $u$ is a
$3^{k+1}$-st root of unity.  Hence $u=\zeta$, so
$2^j\equiv1\pmod{3h}$.  Since the order of two modulo $3h=3^{k+1}$ is
$2h$, the Frobenius orbit of $A_k$ has size $2h$.  Thus $A_k$ has degree
$2h$ and the displayed resultant is its minimal polynomial.

The cyclotomic divisor criterion applied to
$\Phi_{2\cdot3^k}(2)$ gives the stated prime support after removing its
single factor of three.  Proposition~\ref{prop:d-lower} and the corrected
norm, now taken from the actual defining field of $\zeta+4$, give
\[
 m_\ell=4
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A_k\notin(\mathbb F_{2^{2h}}^{\times})^\ell.
\]
Raising to $q-1$ is invertible on the current $\ell$-Kummer quotient, so
this is equivalent to the full $\ell$-primary part occurring in
$\operatorname{ord}(M_k)$.  Finally, $\mu_\ell$ lies in the defining field;
Kummer theory and Capelli's lemma identify nonpower with irreducibility of
$R_k(X^\ell)$.
\end{proof}

The exceptional Euler test also has a lossless sparse form parallel to the
three-block criterion in the cubic arm.

\begin{proposition}[sparse exceptional block criterion]
\label{prop:d-block}
Let $n=3h$, let $\ell\mid\Psi_k$ be prime, put
$d=(q+1)/\ell$, and work in
$\mathcal R_h=\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^n-1)$.  With $x$ the class of $X$, set
\[
 \mathcal A=x^2+x+x^h,\qquad
 \iota(x)=x^{-1},\qquad C=\mathcal A^d.
\]
Write the unique cyclic representative of $V=C+\iota(C)$ as
\[
 V=V_0+X^hV_1+X^{2h}V_2,
 \qquad \deg V_i<h.
\]
Then
\[
 M_k^{(q+1)/\ell}=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 V_0=V_1=V_2.
\]
If $d=\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}\epsilon_i2^i$, then, losslessly in
$\mathcal R_h$,
\[
 C=\prod_{\epsilon_i=1}
 \left(x^{2^{i+1}}+x^{2^i}+x^{h2^i}\right),
\]
where exponents are read modulo $n$.  Thus arm $D$ is equivalent to failure
of this three-block equality for every current prime $\ell$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The order of two modulo $3^{k+1}=n$ is $2h$, so
$q=2^h\equiv-1\pmod n$.  Hence $q$-powering on $\mathcal R_h$ is
$\iota$.  In the selected field
$\mathbb F_2[X]/(J_h)$, where $J_h=X^{2h}+X^h+1$, the image of
$\mathcal A$ is $A_k$, and
\[
 M_k^d=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (A_k^d)^q=A_k^d
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 J_h\mid C+\iota(C).
\]
The first equivalence is kernel-checked abstractly as
\texttt{exceptional\_euler\_fixed\_iff}.  Modulo $J_h$, characteristic two
gives $X^{2h}=X^h+1$, and therefore
\[
 V\equiv(V_0+V_2)+X^h(V_1+V_2).
\]
The right side has degree less than $2h=\deg J_h$, so it vanishes exactly
when $V_0=V_1=V_2$.  The product formula is the binary-power expansion of
$\mathcal A^d$ in characteristic two.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[primitive-only palindromicity]
\label{cor:d-palindromic}
In the notation of Proposition~\ref{prop:d-block}, one always has
\[
 V_0+V_1+V_2=0.
\]
Consequently the following conditions are equivalent:
\[
 M_k^d=1,
 \qquad V_0=V_1=V_2,
 \qquad V_0=V_1=V_2=0,
 \qquad C=\iota(C)\text{ in }\mathcal R_h.
\]
In particular, every proper cyclic fold through
$\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^h-1)$ vanishes identically on $V$; no Fourier test on a
proper $3$-power quotient can detect arm~$D$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By lifting the exponent of three,
\[
 v_3(2^h+1)=k+1,
\]
so $n=3^{k+1}$ divides $q+1$.  Since $\ell\ne3$, it follows from
$q+1=\ell d$ that $n\mid d$.  Modulo $X^h-1$ one has
\[
 \mathcal A=X^2+X+1,
 \qquad \iota(\mathcal A)=X^{-2}\mathcal A.
\]
Hence
\[
 \iota(C)=X^{-2d}C=C
       \pmod {X^h-1}.
\]
The image of $V_0+X^hV_1+X^{2h}V_2$ in this quotient is
$V_0+V_1+V_2$, proving the first assertion.  If the three blocks are equal
to $W$, their sum is $3W=W$ in characteristic two, so $W=0$.  The converse
is immediate, and every quotient $X^m-1$ with $m\mid h$ factors through
$X^h-1$.
\end{proof}

There is a second, complementary compression which shortens the binary
product by one third before testing this palindromicity.

\begin{proposition}[one-third Frobenius compression]
\label{prop:d-one-third}
Put
\[
 H=3^{k-1},\qquad h=3H,\qquad Q=2^H,
 \qquad S=Q^2-Q+1=3\Psi_k,
\]
and, for a prime $\ell\mid\Psi_k$, put $e=S/\ell$.  Then
\[
 Q\equiv h-1\pmod {3h},\qquad
 S\equiv3\pmod {3h},\qquad
 e\equiv3\pmod {3h},
\]
and
\[
 d=(Q+1)e.
\]
In $\mathcal R_h$, define
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal B}=\mathcal A^{Q+1}.
\]
It has the lossless seven-term representative
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal B}
 =1+x^{-2}+x^h+x^{h+1}+x^{2h}+x^{2h+1}+x^{2h+2},
\]
and $C=\widetilde{\mathcal B}^{e}$.  In the primitive component
$\mathbb F_2[X]/(J_h)$ this reduces further to the trinomial
\[
 \mathcal B=x^{-2}+x+x^{2h+2}.
\]
Thus
\[
 M_k^d=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal B^e=\iota(\mathcal B)^e
       \text{ in }\mathbb F_2[X]/(J_h),
\]
while arm~$D$ asks for the opposite inequality.  The exponent now has at
most $2H+1=2h/3+1$ binary places, rather than $h+1$.

If $e=3$---equivalently, if $\Psi_k=\ell$ is prime---then the opposite
inequality always holds.  Hence every prime-complement level satisfies
arm~$D$; in particular this gives proof-level, non-enumerative certificates
for $k=2$ and $k=3$.

More generally, put $r=\Psi_k/\ell$ and
\[
 \varepsilon_k=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&k\text{ even},\\
  \omega^2,&k\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]
For the reciprocal ratio
\[
 P=\frac{\iota(\mathcal B)}{\mathcal B}
   =M_k^{Q+1}
\]
one has the exact identities
\[
 P^{\Psi_k}=\varepsilon_k,
 \qquad
 M_k^d=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P^r=\varepsilon_k.
\]
Thus, after removing its explicitly known three-primary phase, the unresolved
composite target has exponent $r=\Psi_k/\ell\equiv1\pmod {2h}$ rather than
$e=3\Psi_k/\ell$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The congruence for $Q$ follows by induction.  The base case is
$2\equiv3-1\pmod9$.  If
$Q+1=h(1+3t)$, write $h=3s$ and cube.  Then
\[
 Q^3+1
 =3h\left(1+3\left[
 t-s(1+3t)^2+s^2(1+3t)^3
 \right]\right),
\]
which is the required congruence at the next level.  Substitution gives
\[
 S\equiv(h-1)^2-(h-1)+1\equiv3\pmod {3h}.
\]
Since $S=3\Psi_k$ and $\ell\mid\Psi_k$, the integer $e$ is divisible by
three.  Moreover $\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)=2h$ implies
$h\mid\ell-1$.  Therefore $3h\mid(\ell-1)e$, and from $\ell e=S$ one gets
$e\equiv S\equiv3\pmod {3h}$.

The factorization $Q^3+1=(Q+1)S$ gives $d=(Q+1)e$.  Frobenius and the first
congruence give
\[
 \mathcal A^Q
 =x^{2h-2}+x^{h-1}+x^{2h}.
\]
Multiplication by $\mathcal A=x^2+x+x^h$ cancels the two copies of
$x^{2h-1}$ and gives the displayed seven terms.  Modulo $J_h$, the three
terms $1+x^h+x^{2h}$ vanish and
$x^{h+1}+x^{2h+1}=x$, leaving $\mathcal B$.  The fixed-field equivalence is
then Proposition~\ref{prop:d-block} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:d-palindromic} applied to
$C=\widetilde{\mathcal B}^{e}$.

We next determine the three-primary phase.  Since
$(Q+1)\Psi_k=(q+1)/3$,
\[
 P^{\Psi_k}
 =M_k^{(q+1)/3}
 =A_k^{(q^2-1)/3}
 =\operatorname N_{\mathbb F_{4^h}/\mathbb F_4}(A_k).
\]
The minimal polynomial of $\zeta$ over $\mathbb F_4$ is $X^h+\omega$.
If $y^2+y+\omega=0$, its other conjugate is $y+1$, so the resultant formula
for the norm gives
\[
 \operatorname N(A_k)
 =(y^h+\omega)((y+1)^h+\omega).
\]
Reduction by $y^2+y+\omega=0$ gives $y^{15}=1$ and
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cccc}
  h\bmod15&3&9&12&6\\ \hline
  y^h&\omega+\omega^2y&\omega+\omega y&1+\omega^2y&\omega y.
 \end{array}
\]
The residues of $3^k$ modulo $15$ run through $3,9,12,6$.  Using
$y(y+1)=\omega$, the displayed product is therefore $\omega^2$ in the
first and third cases and $1$ in the second and fourth.  This proves
$P^{\Psi_k}=\varepsilon_k$.

Now $e=3r$.  If $P^e=1$, then $P^r$ is a cube root of unity.  Moreover
\[
 (P^r)^\ell=P^{\Psi_k}=\varepsilon_k.
\]
Every current prime satisfies $\ell\equiv1\pmod3$, so raising a cube root
of unity to the $\ell$-th power fixes it.  Hence $P^r=\varepsilon_k$.
The converse follows from $\varepsilon_k^3=1$.  Finally, since every prime
factor of $r$ is current and hence congruent to one modulo $2h$, one has
$r\equiv1\pmod {2h}$.

It remains to prove the prime-complement assertion, where $r=1$.  Put
$z=\zeta$ and multiply numerator and denominator of $P$ by $z^2$.  Then
\[
 P=\frac{N}{D},\qquad
 N=z^4+z+\omega,\qquad
 D=1+z^3+\omega^2z^4,
\]
and direct factorization over $\mathbb F_4$ gives
\[
 N+D=\omega(z+\omega^2)(z^3+\omega^2),
 \qquad
 N+\omega^2D=\omega^2(z+1)(z^3+\omega).
\]
If $k$ is even, the first identity shows $P\ne1$; if $k$ is odd, the second
shows $P\ne\omega^2$.  Indeed $z$ has order $3h\ge27$, so neither $z$ nor
$z^3$ can equal the displayed cube root of unity.  Thus
$P\ne\varepsilon_k$, as required.  The only earlier level has
$\Psi_1=1$ and no current prime.
\end{proof}

The normalized phase has a substantially simpler Kummer representative.  It
also has an exact cubic ancestry law, although that law is provably blind to
the newly born primary coordinate.

\begin{proposition}[exceptional Cayley coboundary and norm boundary]
\label{prop:d-cayley}
Assume $k\geq2$, retain the notation of
Proposition~\ref{prop:d-one-third}, and put
\[
 V_k=\frac{z^2+\omega^2z+1}{z^2+\omega z+1}
     =\frac{\gamma_k+\omega^2}{\gamma_k+\omega}.
\]
Put also $U=\varepsilon_k^{-1}P$.
For every prime $\ell\mid\Psi_k$, the elements $M_k$, $P$, $U$, and $V_k$
have the same $\ell$-power/nonpower status.  Equivalently, arm~$D$ is the
assertion that $V_k$ retains the full current $\Psi_k$-primary order.

Under the relative cubic norm
\[
 \operatorname N_k:\mathbb F_4(z)^\times
       \longrightarrow\mathbb F_4(z^3)^\times
\]
one has
\[
 \operatorname N_k(V_k)=V_{k-1}^{-1}.
\]
Thus the elements
\[
 E_k=\begin{cases}V_k,&k\text{ even},\\V_k^{-1},&k\text{ odd}
 \end{cases}
\]
are norm coherent.  Nevertheless, at the birth level of every current
$\ell$, every fibre of $\operatorname N_k$ contains both $\ell$-th powers
and non-$\ell$-th powers.  Hence this complete norm ancestry does not
determine the selected $\ell$-symbol.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $H=3^{k-1}$ and $Q=2^H$.  The relative Frobenius
$\sigma:x\mapsto x^{Q^2}$ fixes $\mathbb F_4(z^3)$ and satisfies
$\sigma(z)=\omega z$, because $Q^2\equiv h+1\pmod {3h}$.  All divisions
below are legitimate.  The roots of either quadratic defining $V_k$ lie in
$\mathbb F_{16}^{\times}$ and therefore have order dividing $15$, whereas
each of $z$, $\omega z$, and $\omega^2z$ has order $3h\geq27$; the ratio
defining $P$ is a ratio of the nonzero elements $\iota(\mathcal B)$ and
$\mathcal B$.  Direct factorization gives
\[
 \frac{\sigma(V_k)}{V_k}=\omega^2P,
 \qquad\text{or equivalently}\qquad
 P=\omega V_k^{Q^2-1}.
\]
The scalar is essential in this literal equality.  For a current $\ell$,
$\operatorname{ord}_\ell(Q^2)=3$, so
$\gcd(\ell,Q^2-1)=1$.  Also $\ell\equiv1\pmod3$, and hence
$\omega=\omega^\ell$ is itself an $\ell$-th power.  Raising by
$Q^2-1$ acts invertibly on the $\ell$-Kummer quotient, so it preserves
power/nonpower status and the generated $\ell$-primary subgroup.  Finally
$P=M_k^{Q+1}$, and $Q$ has order six modulo $\ell$, so
$\gcd(\ell,Q+1)=1$.  Also
$U=\varepsilon_k^{-1}P$ with
$\varepsilon_k\in\{1,\omega^2\}$, and
$\varepsilon_k=\varepsilon_k^\ell$ is an $\ell$-th power.  This proves all
four power/nonpower equivalences.  The equality with
the Möbius transform of $\gamma_k=z+z^{-1}$ follows after dividing the
quadratics by $z$.

For the norm recurrence, direct multiplication in
$\mathbb F_2[\omega]/(\omega^2+\omega+1)$ gives
\[
 \prod_{i=0}^2\left((\omega^iz)^2+
        \omega^2(\omega^iz)+1\right)=z^6+\omega z^3+1,
\]
and
\[
 \prod_{i=0}^2\left((\omega^iz)^2+
        \omega(\omega^iz)+1\right)=z^6+\omega^2 z^3+1.
\]
Their quotient is $V_{k-1}(z^3)^{-1}$, proving both the norm formula and
the alternating normalization.  The polynomial cross-product, the two
three-factor products, the Möbius identity, and the scalar-corrected Kummer
step are kernel-checked as
\begin{center}
\texttt{exceptional\_cayley\_coboundary\_cross},\\
\texttt{exceptionalCayleyNum\_cubic\_product},\\
\texttt{exceptionalCayleyDen\_cubic\_product},\\
\texttt{exceptionalCayley\_eq\_traceMobius},\\
\texttt{exceptional\_scaled\_coboundary\_}\par
\texttt{isPthPower\_iff}.
\end{center}

It remains to prove the claimed blindness.  Put $B=4^H$.  Then
$\operatorname{ord}_\ell(B)=3$, so
\[
 \ell\nmid B-1,
 \qquad \ell\mid B^2+B+1.
\]
The kernel of
$\mathbb F_{B^3}^\times\to\mathbb F_B^\times$ is cyclic of order
$B^2+B+1$.  If $g$ generates the top group, the kernel is generated by
$g^{B-1}$.  Because $\ell\nmid B-1$, this generator maps nontrivially, and
hence surjectively, to the order-$\ell$ quotient
\[
 \mathbb F_{B^3}^\times/
       (\mathbb F_{B^3}^\times)^\ell.
\]
Multiplying any point of a fixed norm fibre by the norm kernel therefore
realizes every $\ell$-Kummer class in that same fibre.  Strict ancestors
have trivial $\ell$-Kummer quotient, while later cubic norm maps are
isomorphisms on that quotient because $\ell\ne3$.  Thus either birth class
extends to a fully norm-coherent tail.  This is an exact alternative-parent
countermodel to norm-only induction, not a counterexample on the literal
Conway-selected ancestry.
\end{proof}

The parity-twisted exponent reduction does not by itself settle composite
$\Psi_k$.  After putting $U=\varepsilon_k^{-1}P$, it says exactly that
$U^{\Psi_k}=1$ and that arm~$D$ asks for
$U^{\Psi_k/\ell}\ne1$ for every current prime $\ell$.  Thus it isolates the
prime-to-three current coordinate but does not prove that every one of its
prime factors occurs in $\operatorname{ord}(U)$.  Here the normalization uses
$\Psi_k\equiv1\pmod3$ and $r\equiv1\pmod3$, the latter already following from
$r\equiv1\pmod {2h}$.

The ordinary-arm ratio packing has an exceptional analogue, provided the
multiplier roots of unity and the degree of the marked root are balanced by
an intermediate constant field.

\begin{proposition}[intermediate-field exceptional packing sieve]
\label{prop:d-packing}
Assume $k\geq2$, put $h=3^k$, and let the prime $\ell$ divide $\Psi_k$.
For $0\leq j<k$, set
\[
 m_j=3^j,\qquad R_j=3^{k-j},\qquad
 s_j=\left\lfloor\frac{R_j-1}{8}\right\rfloor
\]
and define
\[
 \mathcal S_k=
 \max_{0\leq j<k}
 \sum_{t=0}^{\min(2m_j,s_j)}
 2^t\binom{2m_j}{t}\binom{s_j}{t}.
\]
If the current $\ell$-coordinate is deficient, then
\[
 \frac{\Psi_k}{\ell}\geq\mathcal S_k.
\]
Consequently arm~$D$ holds at every current factor satisfying
\[
 \frac{\Psi_k}{\ell}<\mathcal S_k.
\]
Moreover, if $H_2(x)=-x\log_2x-(1-x)\log_2(1-x)$ is binary entropy, then
\[
 \log_2\mathcal S_k
 \geq c_0\sqrt h-O(\log h),
 \qquad
 c_0=\frac14+\frac23H_2\!\left(\frac38\right)
              +\frac38H_2\!\left(\frac23\right)
 >1.2306.
\]
Thus this sieve proves an exponential-in-$\sqrt h$ large-factor range at
every exceptional level, while remaining subexponential in $h$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $z=\zeta$, $d=\Psi_k/\ell$, and regard the normalized phase as the
rational function
\[
 U(T)=\varepsilon_k^{-1}\frac{\mathcal N(T)}{\mathcal D(T)},
 \qquad
 \mathcal N(T)=T^4+T+\omega,
 \qquad
 \mathcal D(T)=1+T^3+\omega^2T^4.
\]
The polynomial $T^h+\omega$ is the minimal polynomial of $z$ over
$\mathbb F_4$.  The order of four modulo $3h$ is $h$, and the map
\[
 t\longmapsto z^{4^t-1},\qquad 0\leq t<h,
\]
is a bijection from the Frobenius orbit indices to $\mu_h$: every value is
in $\mu_h$, and injectivity follows from the orbit size.  Hence the roots of
$T^h+\omega$ are exactly $cz$, $c\in\mu_h$.  Since $U$ is defined over
$\mathbb F_4$, deficiency $U(z)^d=1$ implies
\[
 U(cz)^d=1\qquad(c\in\mu_h).
\]

Fix $j$, abbreviate $m=m_j$, $R=R_j$, $s=s_j$, and put
$E=\mathbb F_{4^m}$.  LTE gives
\[
 v_3(4^m-1)=1+v_3(m)=j+1,
\]
so $\mu_{3m}\subset E$.  Since $E\subset\mathbb F_{4^h}$,
$[E(z):E]=h/m=R$.  Also $z^R$ has order $3m$ and lies in $E$, so the
minimal polynomial of $z$ over $E$ is the binomial $T^R+z^R$.

Over $\mathbb F_4$ introduce the six monic quadratics
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
 f_1=T^2+\omega T+1,
 &f_2=T^2+\omega T+\omega,
 &f_3=T^2+\omega^2T+1,\\
 f_4=T^2+T+\omega,
 &f_5=T^2+T+\omega^2,
 &f_6=T^2+\omega^2T+\omega^2.
\end{array}
\]
They are distinct and irreducible by direct inspection in $\mathbb F_4$.
Up to nonzero scalar factors,
\[
 \mathcal N(T)=f_1(T)f_2(T),\qquad
 \mathcal D(T)\doteq f_3(T)f_4(T),
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal N(\omega T)\doteq f_4(T)f_5(T),\qquad
 \mathcal D(\omega T)=f_2(T)f_6(T),
\]
where $\doteq$ denotes equality up to a nonzero scalar.  Since $m$ is odd,
$E\cap\mathbb F_{16}=\mathbb F_4$, so all six quadratics remain irreducible
over $E$.

Choose representatives $\mathcal C$ for $\mu_{3m}/\mu_3$.  Scaled factors
belonging to different representatives are disjoint.  Indeed, a common root
of $f_a(cT)$ and $f_b(c'T)$ would give
$c'/c\in\mathbb F_{16}^{\times}\cap\mu_{3m}=\mu_3$, hence $c=c'$ by the
choice of representatives, and then $a=b$ because the six factors are
distinct.  In particular, for each $c\in\mathcal C$ the two elements
\[
 u_{c,0}=U(cz),\qquad u_{c,1}=U(\omega cz)
\]
have, up to scalars, the factor patterns
\[
 u_{c,0}\doteq\frac{f_1(cz)f_2(cz)}{f_3(cz)f_4(cz)},
 \qquad
 u_{c,1}\doteq\frac{f_4(cz)f_5(cz)}{f_2(cz)f_6(cz)}.
\]
Their denominators are nonzero: otherwise a conjugate of $z$ would be a
root over $\mathbb F_4$ of the degree-four polynomial $\mathcal D$, whereas
$z$ and all its conjugates have degree $h\geq9$.  All $2m$ displayed
elements are $d$-th roots of unity.

Index them by a set $I$ of size $2m$.  For
$v\in\mathbb Z^I$ with $\sum_i|v_i|\leq s$, put
$V_v=\prod_i u_i^{v_i}$.  Suppose $V_v=V_w$ and set $g=v-w$.  Separating
positive and negative exponents and cross-multiplying gives an equality at
$z$ between two polynomials in $E[T]$, each of degree at most
\[
 4\sum_i|g_i|\leq8s<R.
\]
The degree-$R$ minimal polynomial therefore forces equality of the two
polynomials.  Unique factorization now recovers every exponent: for a fixed
$c$, the valuation at $f_1(cT)$ gives $g_{c,0}=0$, and the valuation at
$f_5(cT)$ gives $g_{c,1}=0$.  Cross-star disjointness handles all
$c\in\mathcal C$.  Thus the $V_v$ are distinct.

The signed $\ell^1$ ball in $\mathbb Z^{2m}$ has exactly
\[
 \sum_{t=0}^{\min(2m,s)}
 2^t\binom{2m}{t}\binom{s}{t}
\]
points.  Since their images are distinct roots of $X^d-1$, this number is
at most $d$.  Taking the maximum over $j$ proves the exact bound.

For the asymptotic statement, first let $k$ be even and take
$j=k/2-1$.  Then, with $x=\sqrt h$,
\[
 2m=\frac23x,\qquad s=\frac38x+O(1).
\]
The summand with $t=\lfloor x/4\rfloor$ and Stirling's formula give
\[
 \log_2\mathcal S_k\geq
 \left[\frac14+\frac23H_2\!\left(\frac38\right)
                  +\frac38H_2\!\left(\frac23\right)\right]x
 -O(\log h).
\]
If $k$ is odd, take $j=(k-3)/2$.  Then
\[
 2m=\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}x,
 \qquad s=\frac{3\sqrt3}{8}x+O(1),
\]
and the same choice $t=\lfloor x/4\rfloor$ gives coefficient
\[
 \frac14+\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}H_2\!\left(\frac{3\sqrt3}{8}\right)
 +\frac{3\sqrt3}{8}H_2\!\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}\right)
 >1.2341>c_0.
\]
The $O(\log h)$ errors are the standard logarithmic errors in the two
binomial-coefficient estimates.  This proves the uniform claim for all
sufficiently large $k$; enlarging the implicit constant absorbs the finitely
many smaller levels.
\end{proof}

This packing theorem is a genuine composite-$\Psi_k$ range, not a universal
proof: its lower bound is exponential in $\sqrt h$, whereas $\Psi_k$ is
exponential in $h$.  Logarithmic or Hasse
derivatives do not add an intrinsic constraint in this finite etale quotient:
$\mathbb F_2[X]/(J_h)$ has no nonzero $\mathbb F_2$-derivations, while the
formal polynomial derivative does not descend because
$J_h'=X^{h-1}$ is a unit modulo $J_h$.  A derivative argument would therefore
need genuinely new lift or multiplicity information.  Proposition~
\ref{prop:d-one-third} and Proposition~\ref{prop:d-packing} therefore prove
the prime-complement case and a large-factor range, not universal arm~$D$.

\begin{proposition}[effective exceptional bound]\label{prop:d-bound}
Let $k\geq2$, $h=3^k$, and let $\ell\mid\Psi_k$ be prime.  If $t$ is the
least power of two satisfying
$t\geq\lceil\log_2(h-1)\rceil$, then
\[
  m_\ell\leq4^t-1<16h^4.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $K=\mathbb F_{4^h}$ and extend constants to
$L=K\mathbb F_{4^t}$.  For a character $\chi$ of exact order $\ell$, the
sum
\[
  S=\sum_{c\in\mathbb F_{4^t}}
      (\chi\circ\operatorname N_{L/K})(\zeta+c)
\]
equals $4^t$ precisely when every translate is an $\ell$-th power.  The
associated primitive even function-field character has $L$-polynomial
$(1-T)\prod_{j=1}^{h-2}(1-\alpha_jT)$ with $|\alpha_j|=2$.  Constant-field
extension therefore gives
\[
  |S|\leq1+(h-2)2^t<4^t
\]
when $2^t\geq h-1$; see \cite{rosen2002}.  Some translate is a nonpower,
and norm injectivity on the $\ell$-Kummer quotient returns the statement to
its defining field.  The elements of $\mathbb F_{4^t}$ are the finite
nimbers below $4^t$.  The displayed polynomial estimate follows from the
minimal choice of $t$.
\end{proof}

This bound is level-dependent and does not prove absolute boundedness or the
value four.

The exceptional coordinate also has an exact circular-ray factorization.
In the real field generated by roots of orders $3^{k+1}$ and $5$, let
$\mathfrak P$ be the prime selected by $\zeta\mapsto\zeta_k$ and the
conductor-five coordinate $\mapsto\omega$.  Let $E_L$ and $C_L$ be the
global and circular units and let a subscript $\ell$ denote the primary
projection after reduction.  Then
\[
 \overline C_{L,\ell}
 =\langle A_{k,\ell}\rangle
 =\langle M_{k,\ell}\rangle,
\]
and
\[
 [k_{\mathfrak P,\ell}:\langle M_{k,\ell}\rangle]
 =\left|\ker\bigl(
   \operatorname{Cl}_L(\mathfrak P)_\ell\to
   \operatorname{Cl}_L{}_{\ell}\bigr)\right|
  |(\overline E_L/\overline C_L)_\ell|.
\]
The second factor is controlled by the real class number, but the first is a
principal ray kernel.  The marked conductor-five cyclotomic unit is globally
not an $\ell$-th power; the height theorem of Amoroso--Dvornicich gives this
directly \cite{amorosodvornicich2000}.  Global nonpower does not determine
its Frobenius at the selected prime.  Arm $D$ is exactly the assertion that
both factors in the displayed ray-class index vanish.

The current $C$ and $D$ coordinates are orthogonal.  If
$q_0=2^{h/3}$ and $q=q_0^3$, their norm-one tori have orders
\[
 q_0^2+q_0+1
 \quad\text{and}\quad q+1,
\]
which are coprime.  The exceptional cubical operator kills the cubic
subfield, while the local norm kills the exceptional torus.  Hence the two
principal-ray factors cannot force one another through the shared prime over
two.  Each requires its own marked unit-image calculation.

Even a perfect one-dimensional Kummer--Artin pairing does not show that a
prescribed prime has nonzero image in the Artin quotient.  The obstruction
already occurs arithmetically.

\begin{proposition}[perfect-pairing no-go]\label{prop:pairing-no-go}
There exist a number field $K$ containing $\mu_3$, a global unit $u$ which is
not a cube in $K$, and exactly one prime $\mathfrak q$ of $K$ above two; this
prime splits completely in the nontrivial cyclic Kummer extension
$K(\sqrt[3]{u})/K$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take
\[
 K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3,\sqrt3),
 \qquad u=2+\sqrt3.
\]
The Pell equation gives
$\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)}^\times=\{\mathord\pm u^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}$,
so $u$ is not a cube in the real quadratic subfield.  If $X^3-u$ acquired a
root in its quadratic extension $K$, its cubic factorization over the real
subfield would also have a linear factor.  Hence $u$ is not a cube in $K$,
and adjoining a cube root gives a nontrivial cyclic cubic extension.

Locally at two, $\mathbb Q_2(\zeta_3)/\mathbb Q_2$ is unramified quadratic
and adjoining $i$ is ramified quadratic.  The two local quadratics are
linearly disjoint, so $K\otimes\mathbb Q_2$ is a degree-four field; hence two
has a unique prime $\mathfrak q$ in $K$.  In its residue field
$\sqrt3\equiv1$, so $u\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak q}$.  The polynomial $X^3-u$
has the simple root $1$ modulo $\mathfrak q$, because its derivative at one
is the unit $3$.  Hensel's lemma makes $u$ a cube in $K_{\mathfrak q}$; since
$\mu_3\subset K_{\mathfrak q}$, the polynomial splits completely there and
$\mathfrak q$ splits completely in $K(\sqrt[3]{u})$.
\end{proof}

Thus nontriviality of the Kummer line, one-dimensionality of its dual, and
even uniqueness of the prime above two are compatible with zero marked
pairing.  In the four arms, perfect duality reduces the target to
nonvanishing of the Conway-selected prime class; it does not prove that
nonvanishing.

\section{Nim reciprocity saturation and marked holonomy}
\label{sec:nim-reciprocity}

The residue-symbol reductions suggest using reciprocity to evaluate the
remaining phase.  There is a precise saturation boundary for that program.
Let $\ell$ be an odd prime, let $s\in\mu_\ell$, and put
$E=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)$.  The two-axis Frobenius--cyclotomic family
is $s_{j,c}=s^{2^j c}$ for $0\leq j<E$ and
$c\in\mathbb F_\ell^\times$.

\begin{proposition}[reciprocity saturation]\label{prop:reciprocity-saturation}
The following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every multiplicative word in the $s_{j,c}$ is $s^\lambda$, where
\[
 \lambda=\sum_{j,c}w_{j,c}2^jc\pmod\ell.
\]
It is blind when $\lambda=0$ and, when $\lambda\ne0$, equals one exactly when
the original marked phase equals one.
\item For multiplicative characters $\chi,\psi$ of a finite field and
$a\ne0$, the Jacobi convolution
\[
 J_{\chi,\psi}(a)=\sum_x\chi(x)\psi(a-x)
\]
satisfies
\[
 J_{\chi,\psi}(a)=\chi(a)\psi(a)J_{\chi,\psi}(1).
\]
In particular, if both characters are powers of one order-$\ell$ character,
the marked dependence is again one power of the same phase.
\item In the affine group on $\mathbb F_\ell$, write $(t,q)$, with
$q\in\mathbb F_\ell^\times$, for the map $x\mapsto qx+t$.  If $q\ne1$, all
$(t,q)$ are conjugate.  Consequently every class function is independent of
$t$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first statement follows by collecting exponents.  For the second,
substitute $x=ay$ and factor $\chi(a)\psi(a)$ from every summand.  For the
third, conjugation by the translation $x\mapsto x+a$ changes $t$ to
$t+(1-q)a$; taking $a=t/(q-1)$ makes this zero.
\end{proof}

Thus norm products, weighted Stickelberger words, and Gauss or Jacobi
transforms either erase or re-encode one phase.  A class function on the
single fixed-multiplier affine coset is blinder still: it forgets the
translation coordinate in part~3.  None of these operations independently
evaluates the selected phase.

Ordinary cross-arm multiplicative words face the same boundary.  If a strict
ancestor lies in a field $\mathbb F_{2^d}$ with $f(\ell)\nmid d$, exactness
of the residue degree gives
$\ell\nmid2^d-1$; the $\ell$-power map is therefore an automorphism on that
ancestor group and its $\ell$-Kummer image is trivial.  Thus every cross-arm
word built from strict lower-level coordinates in such degrees separates its
coprime-primary torsion.  Nor does the next $\ell$-component give an induction:
its defining radicand is
$\alpha_\ell=\kappa_{f(\ell)}+m_\ell$, so irreducibility proves only that the
actual winning translate is a non-$\ell$-th power.  Using that fact to identify
$m_\ell$ with the conjectural $0$, $1$, or $4$ would already assume the target.

The intrinsic coordinate is a full Frobenius-orbit holonomy, not a one-step
affine translation.  Let $\beta\in\mathbb F_{2^E}^\times$ be the selected
element, put $s=\beta^{(2^E-1)/\ell}$, choose $\alpha^\ell=\beta$ in an
algebraic closure, fix a primitive $\zeta\in\mu_\ell$, and let
$\varphi(z)=z^2$.  Then
\[
 \frac{\varphi^E(\alpha)}{\alpha}
 =\alpha^{2^E-1}
 =\beta^{(2^E-1)/\ell}=s.
\]
This ratio is unchanged when $\alpha$ is replaced by $\zeta^a\alpha$,
because $\varphi^E$ fixes $\mu_\ell$.  If $s=\zeta^T$, the $E$-step action
on the labeled roots is the pure translation $e_x\mapsto e_{x+T}$.  Hence on
the Fourier lines
\[
 v_r=\sum_x\zeta^{-rx}e_x,
 \qquad \varphi^E(v_r)=\zeta^{rT}v_r.
\]
The diagonal eigenvalue at $r=1$ is exactly the selected Kummer--Artin
symbol.

Equivalently, choose a root frame over each conjugate
$\beta_j=\beta^{2^j}$ and write the one-step maps as
$e_{j,x}\mapsto e_{j+1,2x+t_j}$.  Their cyclic holonomy is
\[
 T=\sum_{j=0}^{E-1}2^{E-1-j}t_j\pmod\ell.
\]
Changing frames by $\alpha_j\mapsto\zeta^{a_j}\alpha_j$ sends
$t_j$ to $t_j+2a_j-a_{j+1}$; with $a_E=a_0$, these changes telescope because
$2^E=1$ modulo $\ell$.  Thus $T$ is intrinsic, while the individual
off-diagonal coefficients
\[
 \rho(t_j,2)v_r=\zeta^{r2^{-1}t_j}v_{r2^{-1}}
\]
are gauge-dependent.  Every proper edge can be normalized away recursively,
but a closed gauge cannot erase the cyclic holonomy: all but one edge may be
made trivial, with the remaining edge carrying $T$.

This also explains the cocycle no-go.  A homomorphic splitting on a
commutative multiplier subgroup obeys
$t(qr)=t(q)+q t(r)$.  Fixing $q_0\ne1$ and comparing
$t(qq_0)$ with $t(q_0q)$ gives
\[
 t(q)=(1-q)a,
 \qquad a=\frac{t(q_0)}{1-q_0}.
\]
Every such cocycle is a coboundary and has trivial full-period transfer.
Powers of one affine lift give this law on the subgroup generated by its
multiplier.  A general labeled section has a factor set, but changing root
frames changes that factor set by a coboundary; only its cyclic holonomy is
gauge-invariant.

Affine ancestry between two such torsor paths cannot create a missing phase.
If the vertex maps are $x\mapsto\lambda x+b_j$, then the target translations
have the form
\[
 u_j=\lambda t_j+b_{j+1}-2b_j,
\]
and the path holonomies satisfy
\[
 U_n=\lambda T_n+b_n-2^n b_0.
\]
On a closed $E$-cycle the endpoint term telescopes, so $U=\lambda T$.
Consequently an affine recursion from a strict ancestor with trivial
$\ell$-holonomy cannot prove a nonzero current phase; a successful relation
must contain a genuinely non-affine or independently nonzero closing source.

A successful nim-reciprocity proof must therefore retain and independently
evaluate the marked holonomy $T$, equivalently $s$, along the literal Conway
ancestry.  Constructing the holonomy from the selected phase is an exact
reformulation, not yet its evaluation.

\section{Boundedness and the remaining theorem}\label{sec:bounded}

Lenstra asked whether the finite excesses are absolutely bounded
\cite{lenstra1977}.  The disproof of Conjecture~\ref{conj:rule} does not
answer that question, or even show that an excess lies outside $\{0,1,4\}$.

For $a\geq0$, put $B_a=2^{2^a}$ and let $C_a=\mathbb F_{B_a}$, the
finite nimbers below $B_a$.  For an odd prime $p$, put
$x_p=\kappa_{f(p)}=[\lambda_p+k_p]$ as above and
$L_{p,a}=\mathbb F_2(x_p)C_a$.

\begin{proposition}[finite-line criterion]\label{prop:bounded}
For every $p$ and $a$,
\[
 m_p<B_a-(k_p\bmod B_a)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{some }c\in C_a\text{ has }x_p+c\notin L_{p,a}^p.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sup_p m_p<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{one fixed $a$ works for every $p$}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $F_c=\mathbb F_2(x_p+c)$, one has $F_cC_a=L_{p,a}$ and
$[L_{p,a}:F_c]\mid2^a$, which is prime to $p$.  Extension therefore
preserves $p$-power status for nonzero candidates, while zero is already a
$p$-th power.

Nim addition of $c<B_a$ changes only the lowest $2^a$ binary digits of the
finite tail.  Thus $x_p+C_a$ consists exactly of the ordinals
$[\lambda_p+n]$ with
\[
 B_a\left\lfloor\frac{k_p}{B_a}\right\rfloor
 \leq n<
 B_a\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k_p}{B_a}\right\rfloor+1\right).
\]
Every point below $x_p$ is a $p$-th power: by the defining minimality of
$\kappa_h$, its degree is not divisible by $h=f(p)$, so its finite-field
unit group has order prime to $p$.  The first nonpower in ordinal order is
$[\lambda_p+k_p+m_p]$.  It belongs to the displayed block exactly when
$m_p<B_a-(k_p\bmod B_a)$, proving the first equivalence.

One fixed successful line gives $m_p<B_a$ for every $p$.  Conversely, the
component normal form shows that $k_p$ is either zero or a single even
component $\kappa_{2^j}=2^{2^{j-1}}$, with $j\geq1$.  It is therefore zero
or a power of two.  Since $B_a$ is also a power of two, either
$k_p\bmod B_a=0$ or $k_p\bmod B_a\leq B_a/2$.  Given a uniform bound on
$m_p$, choose $a$ with $B_a/2>\sup_p m_p$.  The exact threshold then
guarantees a successful line for every $p$.
\end{proof}

No fixed finite affine line is known to work for every prime.  The remaining
selected-order questions include the singleton-even Conway--Fermat equalities
$\delta_n=F_n$ and assertions $O,C,D$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:arms}.  Their
coordinates retain the literal Conway ancestry.  The false universal
multicomponent assertion $Z$ must instead be replaced by a description of its
failure set and of the first nonpower translate there.  The family in
Theorem~\ref{thm:equal-norm-counterexample} determines neither those excess
values nor the least counterexample prime.

\section{Conclusion}

The proposed piecewise $0/1/4$ rule is false.  Two actual Conway towers share
the radicand $\kappa_9+4$; compatible high levels have equal quadratic norms,
and their selected sum loses every primitive multiplicative-order factor.
The sum still generates its full field, so the construction respects the
literal component selector.  This produces infinitely many failed zero-arm
rows without searching for or factoring any counterexample prime.

Every nonzero common-base translate recovers primitive support in both
coprime factors of the half-field torus order.  The total cancellation at
zero is therefore isolated on this affine line.  The remaining question is
the full primary order at each individual row prime, which aggregate
nonvanishing does not determine.

The ordinary, cubic, exceptional, and singleton-even questions remain open.
So do the exact excesses in the new family, the assertion that all excesses
belong to $\{0,1,4\}$, and Lenstra's absolute boundedness question.  The
four-arm reduction and the remaining arithmetic bounds describe those
separate questions; they do not restore the disproved multicomponent rule.
