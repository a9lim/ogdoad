\title{The Impartial Realizer Problem:\\
One Mover Bit from Symmetry}
\author{a9lim}

\begin{abstract}
The weighted-source \textsc{witt-fifo} theorem of the companion flagship
paper realizes $\{Q=0\}$ as a normal-play $P$-set for every quadratic
refinement $Q$ of every alternating form on $\F_2^n$, under a bounded
$(w_0,c)=(1,1)$ access contract.  The compiled arena is partizan through
exactly one bit: the terminal claim move is keyed to the accumulated
charge \emph{and the mover phase}, so which seat may claim depends on
whose turn it is; every other move is seat-symmetric.  This note isolates
the flagship's first residual open question: does an \emph{impartial}
rule -- identical move sets for both players, ordinary normal play --
meet the same access contract and realize the same $P$-sets uniformly?
We collect the proved fence (the coin-turning linear ceiling, the abelian
obstruction, the transcript-span bound), record the trivial rigid-tempo
compilation lemma, and reduce the design problem to a pass-free or
pass-parity-pinned analogue of the matching forcing theorem, which is the
open technical content.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem}

Throughout, $V=\F_2^n$, $B$ is an alternating bilinear form on $V$, and
$Q$ is a quadratic refinement of $B$, i.e.\ $Q(u+v)=Q(u)+Q(v)+B(u,v)$
with $Q(0)=0$.  The access contract F1--F3/N1--N2, the loading map, and
the realization theorem are those of the flagship
paper~\cite{goldarf}: statics are refinement-blind (F1), each move
predicate and transition observes $Q$ through at most $c$ oracle queries
at points of Hamming weight at most $w_0$, with $(w_0,c)$ independent of
dimension (F2), the semantics is one declared outcome convention (F3),
and exactness holds uniformly over the full torsor of refinements of
every form in the family (N1) within the stated budget (N2).

The \textsc{witt-fifo} arena satisfies the contract at $(w_0,c)=(1,1)$
and has root outcome $P$ iff $Q(x)=0$ for every loaded input
$x$~\cite[weighted-source theorem]{goldarf}.  Its state carries a coin set, a
FIFO queue of open coins, a Boolean ko bit, the accumulated charge
$\sigma$, and the mover phase $\phi$; the moves are OPEN, CLOSE, PASS,
and one terminal claim.  OPEN, CLOSE, and PASS are available to whichever
player moves.  Only the claim is partizan: at a terminal board of charge
$\sigma$, the unique claim exists exactly when the player to move is the
seat designated by $\sigma$.  The arena is therefore partizan through the
single bit $\phi$, consumed at one move.

\begin{question}[impartial uniform realizer]\label{q:impartial}
\OPEN\ Does there exist an impartial rule -- every legal move available
to both players, outcomes read by ordinary normal play (last player to
move wins) -- satisfying F1--F3/N1--N2 at some bounded $(w_0,c)$, whose
root outcome at loaded input $x$ is $P$ exactly when $Q(x)=0$, uniformly
over every quadratic refinement $Q$ of every alternating form $B$ in
every finite dimension?
\end{question}

The question is aesthetically loaded for the motivating instance: the
Gold trace data $Q_a(x)=\Tr(x\,x^{2^a})$ is built entirely from
\emph{impartial} game arithmetic (nim addition, the Turning-Corners
product, its Frobenius diagonal), yet the only known realizer consumes
one partizan bit.  An affirmative answer would close the loop --
impartial data realized by impartial play; a negative answer would prove
the mover bit is the price of the quadric.

\section{The proved fence}

Three flagship results bound any candidate from below; none decides
Question~\ref{q:impartial}.

\begin{theorem}[coin-turning linear ceiling]\label{thm:ceiling}
\PROVED~\cite[Thm.~A]{goldarf}
Every Winning Ways coin-turning game has configuration $P$-set equal to
the kernel of an $\F_2$-linear nimber-valued map; no coin-turning $P$-set
is a quadric of positive polar rank.
\end{theorem}

An impartial realizer therefore cannot be a disjunctive sum of
coin-turning heaps, and more generally cannot present its positions as
configurations whose Grundy value is XOR-additive over independent
components.  Whatever forces the quadric must entangle the coordinates
dynamically, as the FIFO queue does.

\begin{theorem}[abelian obstruction]\label{thm:abelian}
\PROVED~\cite[Lem.~abelian, Thm.~B]{goldarf}
In any commutative monoid, squaring is an endomorphism, so any quadratic
datum read through homomorphic coordinates has polarization identically
zero.  In particular no commutative game-value monoid -- Sprague--Grundy
values under disjunctive sum, mis\`ere quotients, turn-parity counters --
carries a quadratic refinement with $B\neq0$.
\end{theorem}

This does \emph{not} exclude impartial realizers.  It excludes
factorizations: an impartial candidate cannot read $Q$ off any
commutative value quotient of its positions.  The refinement must enter
intensionally, through the move relation.  Impartial play retains the one
asymmetry the flagship identifies as structurally
available~\cite[Rem.~screen]{goldarf} -- the first-/second-player
asymmetry of whose turn it is -- even though its move \emph{sets} are
equal.  Normal play is precisely the reading of that asymmetry.

\begin{theorem}[transcript-span bound]\label{thm:span}
\PROVED~\cite[transcript-span theorem]{goldarf}
Any transcript-stable exact rule observes refinement values at directions
spanning the input; in the original frame, weight-$\leq w$ observations
number at least $\wt(x)/w$.
\end{theorem}

The bound is seat-blind: an impartial realizer at $w_0=1$ still needs the
full singleton support $\{e_j : x_j=1\}$.  No information discount is
available for symmetry.

\section{Tempo compilation}

The elementary mechanism by which impartial normal play converts a bit
into an outcome is play-length parity.

\begin{lemma}[rigid tempo]\label{lem:tempo}
\STANDARD\
Let $A$ be a finite impartial arena under normal play in which every
maximal play from the root has the same length parity $p\in\F_2$.  Then
the root outcome is strategy-independent: $N$ if $p=1$ and $P$ if $p=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The winner of a maximal play of length $\ell$ is the player who made move
$\ell$, namely the first player iff $\ell$ is odd.  If every maximal play
has $\ell\equiv p$, the winner's identity does not depend on either
player's choices.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[tempo compilation]\label{cor:tempo}
\STANDARD\
Suppose for each $(B,Q,x)$ an impartial arena with refinement-blind
statics and $(w_0,c)$-metered transitions has the property that every
maximal play from the loaded root has length $\equiv Q(x) \pmod 2$.
Then it is an impartial uniform realizer in the sense of
Question~\ref{q:impartial}.
\end{corollary}

The content is thus a \emph{forced-length} theorem: the total move count
parity must equal the charge under both players' arbitrary play, while
transitions stay inside the oracle meter.  The flagship arena is close
to length-rigid already -- every loaded coin is touched exactly twice
(one OPEN, one CLOSE), contributing a fixed even base -- and its charge
$\sigma$ is public state accumulated during play, so a terminal tail of
forced length $\sigma\bmod 2$ is a legal impartial gadget (its legality
reads the position, not the oracle).  Two mechanisms break rigidity:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{PASS.}  The flagship strategy uses PASS at opponent
checkpoints and as ko relief; each PASS flips play-length parity.  The
partizan claim is precisely the device that makes pass parity harmless:
keying the claim to the mover phase $\phi$ hands the last move to the
correct seat regardless of how many passes occurred.
\item \emph{The claim itself.}  As a partizan right it consumes $\phi$;
as an impartial move (``claim legal iff $\sigma=1$, for whoever moves'')
it hands the win to whichever player \emph{reaches} the terminal board on
their turn, which reintroduces the tempo battle the claim was compiled to
avoid.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[the crux]\label{rem:crux}
\OPEN\
The open technical content of Question~\ref{q:impartial} is therefore a
pass-controlled analogue of the flagship's matching forcing theorem
\cite[matching FIFO theorem]{goldarf}: a both-seat strategy theorem on
matching-plus-isolates boards in which either (i) PASS is eliminated and
the safe-front forcing survives with rigid length, or (ii) pass parity is
itself strategically pinned, so that total length parity, not claim
rights, compiles the charge.  The ko rule is the immediate obstruction to
(i): in the FIFO transition system a forced pass is what clears ko, and
the kernel-checked strategy semantics of the matching theorem uses it.
Any replacement must re-prove termination, the queue invariants, and the
overlap-forcing lemma under the modified move set -- the analogues of
what \path{formal/Ogdoad/FifoMatching.lean} and
\path{formal/Ogdoad/GoldSemantics.lean} check for the partizan compilation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[weaker targets]\label{rem:weaker}
Two retreats would still be informative.  A realizer at larger but
bounded $(w_0,c)$ would separate the impartial question from the
weight-one optimality question.  A negative result -- no impartial rule
in the declared access class realizes the torsor uniformly -- would
prove the mover bit necessary and give the flagship's access contract a
genuinely partizan floor; by Theorem~\ref{thm:abelian} such a proof
cannot proceed through value quotients and must engage the move relation
directly, plausibly through a parity-transport invariant of impartial
transition systems.
\end{remark}

\section{Status}

Everything in Section~2 is proved in the flagship paper; the lemmas of
Section~3 are trivial and included to fix the compilation target.
Question~\ref{q:impartial} is open in both directions.  This note is the
authoritative statement of the problem and will be folded into the
flagship paper when resolved.
